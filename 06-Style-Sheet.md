# 06 — Style Sheet
**Status:** THE RULE SIDE — how the prose is written; invariant, amended in place. Spend state (which departures and glimpses are used): `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md`. Vocabulary and naming registers: `02-World-Bible/lexicon.md` and `naming.md`.

## POV
- **Default (the vast majority of every book):** close third limited, single POV (Kael). Within Kael-scenes the strict information rule holds: the reader never knows more than he does, except by inference.
- **Sanctioned departures** — each rationed; interludes and slips argued per use like a heavy-canon beat, none before Part III of Book One unless argued:
  1. **EXTERNAL-CAMERA PAN-AWAYS** (established, author s29 — exempt from the per-use argument; not counted): short scenes without Kael — dialogue and observable action ONLY; NO interiority for secret-holding heads (Severin, Vask, Neris, the Gardener — entering them detonates live valves). The cut-before-the-answer is the device: the reader overhears until the cliffhanger, never past it. Native seal available — characters shifting into the downhill language lock the reader out in-world. Show machinations MOVING, never sealed answers. Set roman, never block-italic.
  2. **INTERLUDES / SPLIT-GROUP BITS — SPENT ON THE ISLAND (author, s41; the *post-island acts* scoping is retired).** Third limited in non-secret-holding heads (Valeria the safest); adult-cold register from the first line (contrast with Kael's maturing voice is a feature). Character flashbacks allowed for OTHER characters — the parents' backstory exception stands (below): their past stays archaeology, never flashback. **THE FIRST ONE IS CH. 31, WHOLE-CHAPTER, INSIDE THE FIRE**, argued per use and ratified: it is the only instrument that can render what Kael can never witness (04 beat 20's unwritten hole — how Valeria gets out of that district). The per-use argument is UNCHANGED for every later one; the count is still the rule (`07/standing-rules` §DEPARTURE LEDGER). **An interlude may be time-displaced** — Ch. 31 runs concurrent with Ch. 29–30 — provided the reader is anchored in its first lines.
  3. **FIRST-PERSON REFLECTIVE SLIPS (Kael only):** rare retrospective "I" surfacing at critical emotional turning points — sentences, not scenes. **May carry future FEELING, JUDGMENT and FACTS (author, s37 — the never-future-facts clause is retired).** The replacement fence is the only one: **a slip may reach forward to anything except a beat the book has not yet paid for** — it may say *I looked for years*; it may not name an event, a place or a date the story still owes (the s37 test case: *three days later, in a boat* was written and cut as a spoiler). The telling-frame stays DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED. Implies a telling-frame (told to whom, from when — braids toward the witness structure) that stays DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED; do not commit without a dedicated session. **Typographic convention: slips are set in italics** — block-italic for overtures, italic sentences mid-scene; distinct from word-level emphasis italics.
- Spend state — slips 2 spent, slip 3 firmly reserved for the fire; which chapters pan away: `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §DEPARTURE LEDGER.

## The Maturing Voice
- Early childhood: short sentences, concrete senses, literal child-logic. The village lights are "the stars that fell down the mountain."
- Middle childhood: questions sharpen; sentences lengthen; pattern-noticing.
- Adolescence+: complexity, irony, self-awareness.
- **THE BLOOMING.** From Part II on, his wits bloom visibly as he grows — quicker in dialogue, drier and faster in thought, pattern-jumps arriving sooner. The gradient includes intelligence made audible, not just sentence complexity.

## Dread by Inference
- Menace is shown through what a child half-notices; the reader assembles it. Never explain.
- Assassin-craft is presented as normal chores/lessons — wrongness exists only in the reader's head.

## Hard Rules
- **The parents' backstory is never told, only inherited.** No flashbacks, no confession scenes. Kael learns who they were like an archaeologist: objects, scars, reflexes, other people's fear — across the whole series. Pan-aways may show the parents in the PRESENT, external camera only; their PAST stays archaeology. This rule outranks the flashback allowance for other characters.
- **The parents are never seen at true capacity until the fire.** Training scenes are constant and welcome — sparring, drills, corrections — but they always operate at Kael's ceiling, never their own (concealment is their resting state, even at home). Glimpses only: a strike blocked without looking up; one blur of motion the mother seems embarrassed by. Kael never beats either of them, and eventually realizes he has never once seen them *try* — a beat to land mid-book, once the Temple gives him a reference class. **Amended (the braid school):** from Ch. 12 on, DELIBERATE TEACHING DEMONSTRATIONS at demonstration scale are licensed (the trunk demo — recorded in 07). Re-scoped, not weakened: Kael has still never seen either parent in CONTEST or at EFFORT — "never seen them try" means *tried*, not *demonstrated* — and the reflex-glimpse ledger (07) is untouched by demonstrations. The fire remains the only full answer.
- **Love is embedded in the teaching, never stated.** Every stern lesson must carry the signature of protection in its stitching (the hand before the ground; the lie that makes a horizon). If a teaching scene reads stern without detectable love, revision flag.
- **DO NOT SOFTEN THE HARSHNESS (counterweight).** The parents are not good parents. Their teaching is at times result-oriented to the point of genuine, reader-discomforting harshness — procedural, calm, total; never rage, never cruelty for its own sake. Kael normalizes and defends it on the page (he is proudest when the harshness "works"), which makes it worse, not better. The love is real AND the harm is real; neither cancels the other. Occasionally a parent glimpses what they are doing — and does not change. They rebuilt the shape of the thing that unmade them. If every harsh scene resolves into reassurance, revision flag.
- **Kael's social blindness is shown, not diagnosed.** He misreads on the page; the narration never labels it.
- **THE WHAT/WHY SPLIT (governs Kael's people-reading for the series).** His physical reading is extraordinary — ten years of training; his social reading is near-nonexistent — and the gap must NOT close quickly. Render the split explicitly: he may correctly detect the WHAT (he's angry; she's lying; the room turned) while completely misreading the WHY (the motive; whether a lie is cruel, protective, embarrassed, playful, or kind). Let him make real mistakes, on the page, uncorrected — the loom-glance ("they are checking I did it correctly") is the model. The long development is series-scale, and it is an ARC, not a fixed trait: he eventually discovers that people are not patterns to solve — they have intentions, contradictions, private histories, and the ability to deliberately change the pattern — and past that discovery his social reading grows into EXTREME PROFICIENCY: the pattern-mind, once it accepts what people are, turns fully onto them (the Requiem is its endpoint's proof — 00). The rule constrains PACE, not destination: competence is earned late, through real mistakes, and arrives fully.
- **Fights are puzzles.** Kael wins by legible technique and decision-making, never by power-ups; the reader should understand every victory. **In a fight chapter the thinking happens BEFORE and AFTER; the fight itself is bodies** — immersive, fast cuts, the reader in it; relative distances, never surveyed ones; no floating callbacks. Do not justify what Kael understands, and never compare him to what he was at nine: he knows what he is looking at — write that.
- **Staging rules for sealed truths (evicted from 00 at s33 — how a truth reaches the page):**
  - The Sanctum (00) is a bible-level term ONLY: it never appears in Book One, and the parents can never say it. Their magic and combat arrive as METHOD, never as sourced doctrine; the diaspora between the Temple's teaching and the mountain's is SHOWN (Ch. 9's two-schools-one-candle beat is its first rendering).
  - Vask says only "Mine as well." — he NEVER claims blood inheritance in words; Kael, the Temple and the world infer it. The false paternity floor is built without the father laying one false word, so the eventual reveal lands as his monument, never his lie.
  - The chest may be planted as a locked chest; its CONTENTS stay sealed until the fire opens it — the archaeology object made literal.
  - The throw is staged under the paternity rule (00 §THE PATERNITY): whenever the reveal lands, it must land as the LARGEST proof of Vask's fatherhood, never as its undoing. Every earlier Vask beat (the tally-marks, the carved wasters, the audit, the seed-bread, the carried-across-ice hand) points here. Stage it as the monument.
  - **THE PARENTS AT TRUE CAPACITY IS SPENT (Ch. 30 — Vask only).** The rule held for twenty-nine chapters and the fire paid it. Rendered through a boy on the ground with broken ribs who can see only part of it and knows exactly what the part is worth; the narration never compares him to anything and never explains him. **NERIS AT CAPACITY IS STILL OWED AND STILL BANKED.**
  - **THE CHEST'S STEEL REACHES THE PAGE BEFORE THE CHEST DOES (author, s39).** Vask arrives already carrying the longsword, so the chest is no longer the visible signal that the ceiling is off — the man is. The chest is therefore paid at the house as ARCHAEOLOGY: the board up, the empty hole, and two things gone out of it. Do not stage a chest-opening retrospectively, and do not let anybody explain the sword.
  - **Never remark on these:** Severin's fern (Ch. 10) was worked from his chair across the room — mastery on display for any reader who later holds the law · Ch. 4's night-watch blade lying *unsheathed* across Neris's knees pre-echoes the word · the old enforcer went and learned to mend (the atonement doctrine, never stated) · **Halden takes a Vel through a gap by the arm (Ch. 29) and she sets herself and it does not matter** — he is genuinely strong in body and Art (00 §HALDEN IS THE HARVEST); the page keeps it deniable as a grown man against a girl of thirteen, and nothing anywhere points at it.

## Character & Scene Rules
- **KAEL'S DEFINING AXIS.** His defining features are intelligence, technique, and practical field knowledge (enumerated in 03 and 02) AND exceptional fighting — all of it his parents' teaching in him. The deficiency is ONE axis only: keep a HEAVY, persistent emphasis on his lack of magic capacity, and on the empire/Temple wrongly treating capacity as the measure of a Resonant. The world keeps saying the loud wrong thing about him; the page keeps showing the quiet true thing. Neither half is allowed to fade: as magic's stakes escalate, the axis the capacity-worshipping world undervalues is the axis that wins. **COUNTERWEIGHT: the critique is of capacity-WORSHIP as the sole measure — never of capacity, mass, leadership, or social gravity themselves.** Those are real powers, valued by the story and by Kael's own honest pricing (Aurelian's count was "well given" and Kael filed it): command moves six backs where the pry-bar moves none, and Kael's lack of it is a REAL deficit, not a hidden virtue. Render the complementarity; never hand-wave the other column away.
- **RELATIONSHIP TIME IS LOAD-BEARING.** Every chapter carries at least one scene whose only job is a relationship moving — warmth, friction, play, pride — not plot, not clue-work. Rendered in the established registers (tells, shared work, jokes, the body) but allowed to BREATHE — real dialogue, real laughter that isn't threat. The never-stated rule holds; the fix is TIME, not sentiment. The Oss cooling and the fire only devastate in proportion to the warmth banked beforehand.
- **DIALOGUE AND EXPOSITION MIX.** Exposition and interiority mixed and matched with spoken scene — lessons in the teacher's actual voice, conflicts in the room's actual words; emotional conflict on the surface of scenes as well as under them. Not a quota; an ear. **The instruction is HOLD, not push** (the manuscript's early dialogue starvation was corrected by Part III): a chapter that wants to be interior is allowed to be; the flag is drift back toward the teens in a chapter built out of scenes.
- **The supporting cast shines.** Every significant character gets moments that are THEIRS — voice, body, presence; Kael is sometimes the camera, not the subject. Show, don't tell (some tell allowed). Physical descriptions come from 03's APPEARANCE LEDGER, filtered through Kael's eye; others' reactions carry conventional beauty.

## Model-Tic Self-Awareness
- Claude has default rhythms it falls into when not watching itself: standalone em-dashes, "not X but Y" constructions, sentence fragments deployed for gravity, triadic lists, AND-CHAINS ("and x and y because z" accumulations), default similes ("the way…" / "like"). None are banned — the book's voice uses all of them — but every instance must be a CHOICE, not a default. While drafting, Claude self-flags passages where its own patterns are steering the sentence instead of the voice; revision passes report per-chapter tic counts beside the word count: dashes, not-buts, fragments, triads, and/1k, similes/1k, and **narration words + words-per-paragraph** (`wc -w` is the budget instrument, not a reading-experience one — a dialogue-heavy chapter carries far less prose bulk per word; check narration bulk before calling a chapter long, and never cut fast dialogue to protect a total). AND-chains and simile density are awareness instruments, not thresholds. **Known default: first drafts run 51–55 and/1k uniformly across every section** — check and/1k per section at the end of every first draft, not per chapter. Upward drift without intent = revision flag. The test is always: would this sentence survive being read aloud in the author's voice, or does it sound like a model being writerly?

## Genre Register
- Epic, gritty, historical-flavored fantasy. Intimate over spectacular. Earned over destined.

## Things to Avoid
- Info-dumps; POV breaks; telling the reader what to feel; characters as plot functions (Character Law); hidden-puppet-master vibes for the leader — he stays human, limited, doctrine-driven.

## Author taste notes (calibration, not rules)
- **Interiority is the seasoning, scene is the meal.** Kael's deep head is the story's depth — keep it — but favor dialogue and real-time action over narrated summary whenever both can carry the beat. A lot of descriptive/interior text over action and dialogue reads boring to the author.
- **"Telling the reader what to feel" is a FLAG, not a verdict** (author ruling, session 19). The §Things to Avoid entry points at prose that substitutes an emotion-label for the scene that would have earned it. It does not ban the naked statement of feeling: a line that names what a character feels, in his own register, at a moment the book has already paid for, is allowed and is sometimes the right line. Weigh each instance; never auto-fail one on the rule's name alone.
- **The causal chain stops at the first why.** The child's-logic chains earned their density in Ch. 1–3; from Part II, links that re-derive established canon ("the why of the why") get cut — trust the plant. Not a flag system; an ear.

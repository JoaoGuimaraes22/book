# 06 — Style Sheet
**Status:** Mostly stable. Last updated: 2026-08-20, session 12 (POV amended to governed flexibility — pan-aways, interludes, first-person slips; relationship-time + dialogue-mix rules; Kael defining-axis + blooming). Prior: 2026-08-09.

## POV (AMENDED session 12 — hard single-POV rule replaced by governed flexibility, author-ratified)
- **Default (the vast majority of every book):** close third limited, single POV (Kael). Within Kael-scenes the strict information rule is UNCHANGED: the reader never knows more than he does, except by inference.
- **Sanctioned departures** — each rationed, each argued per-use like a heavy-canon beat, none before Part III of Book One unless argued:
  1. **EXTERNAL-CAMERA PAN-AWAYS:** short scenes without Kael — dialogue and observable action ONLY; NO interiority for secret-holding heads (Severin, Vask, Neris, the Gardener — entering them detonates live valves). The cut-before-the-answer is the device (the reader overhears until the cliffhanger, never past it). Native seal available: characters shifting into the downhill language locks the reader out in-world. Departures show machinations MOVING, never sealed answers — they relocate dread; 00 stays sealed by camera placement as much as by silence.
  2. **INTERLUDES / SPLIT-GROUP BITS (post-island acts):** third limited in non-secret-holding heads (Valeria the safest); adult-cold register from the first line (contrast with Kael's maturing voice is a feature). Character flashbacks allowed for OTHER characters — THE PARENTS' BACKSTORY EXCEPTION STANDS (below): their past stays archaeology, never flashback.
  3. **FIRST-PERSON REFLECTIVE SLIPS (Kael only):** rare retrospective "I" surfacing at critical emotional turning points — sentences, not scenes. May carry future FEELING and JUDGMENT, never future FACTS or events. Implies a telling-frame (told to whom, from when — braids toward the witness structure); the frame stays DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED — do not commit without a dedicated session. **FIRST INSTANCE SPENT (argued and author-ratified, session 13): the Ch. 1 micro-overture** — five sentences of future feeling/judgment ("I was happy...") ahead of "Before anything else, there was the fire," which now works as the hinge into the child's head. Zero future facts; frame unresolved (no addressee, no when). Retroactively legitimizes Ch. 6's "later, much later" line as the same voice surfacing. Deliberate echo logged: "both were true" runs adjacent to "both things were real" (the family trade, in the future voice). NEXT SLIP RESERVED: the fire — page one and the fire are a designed pair (the voice returning); no slip between them without a dedicated argument. **TYPOGRAPHIC CONVENTION (ratified session 13): all first-person slips are set in italics** — block-italic for overtures, italic sentences when a slip surfaces mid-scene; distinct from word-level emphasis italics, which continue unchanged.

## The Maturing Voice
- Early childhood: short sentences, concrete senses, literal child-logic. The village lights are "the stars that fell down the mountain."
- Middle childhood: questions sharpen; sentences lengthen; pattern-noticing.
- Adolescence+: complexity, irony, self-awareness.

## Dread by Inference
- Menace is shown through what a child half-notices; the reader assembles it. Never explain.
- Assassin-craft is presented as normal chores/lessons — wrongness exists only in the reader's head.

## Hard Rules
- **The parents' backstory is never told, only inherited.** No flashbacks, no confession scenes. Kael learns who they were like an archaeologist: objects, scars, reflexes, other people's fear — across the whole series. (SURVIVES the session-12 POV amendment: pan-aways may show the parents in the PRESENT, external camera only; their PAST stays archaeology — this rule outranks the flashback allowance for other characters.)
- **The parents are never seen at true capacity until the fire.** Training scenes are constant and welcome — sparring, drills, corrections — but they always operate at Kael's ceiling, never their own (concealment is their resting state, even at home). Glimpses only: a strike blocked without looking up; one blur of motion the mother seems embarrassed by. Kael never beats either of them, and eventually realizes he has never once seen them *try* — a beat to land mid-book, once the Temple gives him a reference class. The fire answers the question once, completely.
- **Kael's social blindness is shown, not diagnosed.** He misreads on the page; the narration never labels it.
- **THE WHAT/WHY SPLIT (author-ratified session 13 — governs Kael's people-reading for the series).** His physical reading is extraordinary — ten years of training; his social reading is near-nonexistent — and the gap must NOT close quickly. Render the split explicitly: he may correctly detect the WHAT (he's angry; she's lying; the room turned) while completely misreading the WHY (the motive; whether a lie is cruel, protective, embarrassed, playful, or kind). Let him make real mistakes, on the page, uncorrected — the loom-glance ("they are checking I did it correctly") is the model. The long development is series-scale, and it is an ARC, not a fixed trait (author clarification, session 13): he eventually discovers that people are not patterns to solve — they have intentions, contradictions, private histories, and the ability to deliberately change the pattern — and past that discovery his social reading grows into EXTREME PROFICIENCY: the pattern-mind, once it accepts what people are, turns fully onto them (the Requiem is, structurally, the endpoint's proof — engineering how a whole world will read him). The rule constrains PACE, not destination: no deterministic "he can never read people"; competence is earned late, through real mistakes, and arrives fully.
- **Fights are puzzles.** Kael wins by legible technique and decision-making, never by power-ups. The reader should understand every victory.

## Genre Register
- Epic, gritty, historical-flavored fantasy. Intimate over spectacular. Earned over destined.

## Naming Conventions
- Three registers (see World Bible): village-plain / imperial-ornate / guard-hard. Names carry sociology; commoners lack surnames.

## Things to Avoid
- Info-dumps; POV breaks; telling the reader what to feel; characters as plot functions (Character Law); hidden-puppet-master vibes for the leader — he stays human, limited, doctrine-driven.

## Author taste notes (session 8 — not rules, calibration)
- **Interiority is the seasoning, scene is the meal.** Kael's deep head is the story's depth — keep it — but favor dialogue and real-time action over narrated summary whenever both can carry the beat. A LOT of descriptive/interior text over action and dialogue reads boring to the author.
- **The causal chain stops at the first why.** The child's-logic chains earned their density in Ch. 1–3; from Part II, links that re-derive established canon ("the why of the why") get cut — trust the plant. Not a flag system; an ear.
- **KAEL'S DEFINING AXIS (author-ratified session 11 — a rule, kept here for adjacency).** His defining features are intelligence, technique, and practical knowledge of the field — hunting, growing, weather, terrain, the body, the sciences of his mother's schools — AND exceptional fighting: martial skill, balance, physical prowess, hand-to-hand and weapons, all of it his parents' teaching in him. The deficiency is ONE axis only: keep a HEAVY, persistent emphasis on his lack of magic capacity — and on the empire/Temple wrongly over-emphasizing capacity as the measure of a Resonant. The world keeps saying the loud wrong thing about him; the page keeps showing the quiet true thing. Neither half is allowed to fade. And as magic grows more and more important across the series, his TECHNIQUE AND CONTROL remain his greatest asset — the axis the capacity-worshipping world undervalues is the axis that wins; every escalation of magic's stakes re-proves it. **COUNTERWEIGHT (author-ratified session 13): the critique is of capacity-WORSHIP as the sole measure — never of capacity, mass, leadership, or social gravity themselves.** Those are real powers, valued by the story and, on the page, by Kael's own honest pricing (Aurelian's count was "well given" and Kael filed it): command moves six backs where the pry-bar moves none, the empire's armies run on it, and Kael's lack of it is a REAL deficit, not a hidden virtue. Render the complementarity — perhaps equal in weight to his economy, timing, perception, and leverage; never hand-wave the other column away.
- **THE BLOOMING (author-ratified session 11).** From Part II on, his wits and intelligence bloom visibly as he grows — quicker in dialogue, drier and faster in thought, pattern-jumps arriving sooner. The maturing-voice gradient explicitly includes intelligence made audible in speech and interiority, not just sentence complexity.
- **RELATIONSHIP TIME IS LOAD-BEARING (author-ratified session 12).** Every chapter carries at least one scene whose only job is a relationship moving — warmth, friction, play, pride — not plot, not clue-work. Rendered in the established registers (tells, shared work, jokes, the body), but allowed to BREATHE: real dialogue, real laughter that isn't threat. The never-stated rule holds; the fix is TIME, not sentiment. The Oss cooling and the fire only devastate in proportion to the warmth banked beforehand.
- **DIALOGUE AND EXPOSITION MIX (author direction, session 12).** The manuscript runs dialogue-starved (audit: 0–14% by chapter against fiction's typical 25–50%). Exposition and interiority must be mixed and matched with spoken scene — lessons in the teacher's actual voice, conflicts in the room's actual words. Not a quota; an ear. Emotional conflict belongs on the surface of scenes as well as under them.
- **The supporting cast shines.** Every significant character gets moments that are THEIRS — voice, body, presence — good or bad; Kael is sometimes the camera, not the subject. Show, don't tell (some tell allowed). Physical descriptions come from 03's APPEARANCE LEDGER, filtered through Kael's eye; others' reactions carry conventional beauty.

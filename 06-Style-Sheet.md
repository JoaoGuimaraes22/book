# 06 — Style Sheet
**Status:** THE RULE SIDE — how the prose is written; invariant, amended in place. Spend state (which departures and glimpses are used): `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md`. Vocabulary and naming registers: `02-World-Bible/lexicon.md` and `naming.md`.

## POV
- **Default (the vast majority of every book):** close third limited, single POV (Kael). Within Kael-scenes the strict information rule holds: the reader never knows more than he does, except by inference.
- **Sanctioned departures** — each rationed; interludes and slips argued per use like a heavy-canon beat:
  1. **EXTERNAL-CAMERA PAN-AWAYS** (established, author s29 — exempt from the per-use argument; not counted): short scenes without Kael — dialogue and observable action ONLY, no interiority for anyone — the camera is the device. The cut-before-the-answer is the device: the reader overhears until the cliffhanger, never past it. Native seal available — characters shifting into the downhill language lock the reader out in-world. Show machinations MOVING, never sealed answers. Set roman, never block-italic.
  2. **INTERLUDES / SPLIT-GROUP BITS:** a whole chapter or a bit, third limited in another head; adult-cold register from the first line (contrast with Kael's maturing voice is a feature); set roman. Character flashbacks allowed for other characters; the parents' past arrives in its own register (§Hard Rules, archaeology). Argued per use, every time — whose head, and what it may hold, is settled in the argument — and the count is the rule (`07/standing-rules` §DEPARTURE LEDGER). **An interlude may be time-displaced**, provided the reader is anchored in its first lines.
  3. **FIRST-PERSON REFLECTIVE SLIPS (Kael only):** rare retrospective "I" surfacing at critical emotional turning points — sentences, not scenes — or, as a book's OVERTURE, a short block of them before the close third begins (Ch. 1, Ch. 37); either way argued per use. **May carry future FEELING, JUDGMENT and FACTS (author, s37 — the never-future-facts clause is retired).** The replacement fence is the only one: **a slip may reach forward to anything except a beat the book has not yet paid for** — it may say *I looked for years*; it may not name an event, a place or a date the story still owes (the s37 test case: *three days later, in a boat* was written and cut as a spoiler). The slips imply a telling-frame (told to whom, from when — braids toward a structure that is in 00) that stays DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED; do not commit without a dedicated session. **Typographic convention: slips are set in italics** — block-italic for overtures, italic sentences mid-scene; distinct from word-level emphasis italics.
- Spend state — how many slips and interludes are spent, and which chapters pan away: `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §DEPARTURE LEDGER.

## The Maturing Voice
- Early childhood: short sentences, concrete senses, literal child-logic. The village lights are "the stars that fell down the mountain."
- Middle childhood: questions sharpen; sentences lengthen; pattern-noticing.
- Adolescence+: complexity, irony, self-awareness.
- **THE BLOOMING.** From Part II on, his wits bloom visibly as he grows — quicker in dialogue, drier and faster in thought, pattern-jumps arriving sooner. The gradient includes intelligence made audible, not just sentence complexity.

## Dread by Inference
- Menace is shown through what Kael notices and does not weigh; the reader assembles it. Never explain. The instrument moves with him: in Part I it was the child half-noticing; from the fire on it is the WHAT/WHY split (below) — he reads the room right and the motive wrong, uncorrected, and the reader holds the difference.
- What is wrong is presented as normal — assassin-craft as chores and lessons was Part I's form — and the wrongness exists only in the reader's head; the narration never names it.

## Hard Rules
- **The parents' past reaches the page as archaeology** — objects, scars, reflexes, other people's fear, other people's mouths — and Kael reads it the way he reads everything, as evidence, across the whole series. What the page holds of it: `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §STANDING FACTS THAT OUTLAST BOOK ONE.
- **Love reads in the stitching of the work, not in declarations** — the hand before the ground; the lie that makes a horizon. For thirty-one chapters it was never stated at all; the concealment ended in the house (Ch. 32; `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §WHAT THE FIRE LEFT ON THE PAGE) and the family behave like people who know. A teaching scene that reads stern without detectable love is a revision flag.
- **The harshness stands (counterweight).** The parents were not good parents: their teaching was at times result-oriented to the point of genuine, reader-discomforting harshness — procedural, calm, total; never rage, never cruelty for its own sake — and Kael normalized and defended it on the page (proudest when the harshness "works"), which made it worse, not better. The love is real AND the harm is real; neither cancels the other. They rebuilt the shape of the thing that unmade them. Neither a harsh scene nor a harsh memory resolves into reassurance — grief does not sand the harm off the dead father, and a lesson remembered keeps its stitching and its cost. If it resolves, revision flag.
- **Kael's social blindness is shown, not diagnosed.** He misreads on the page; the narration never labels it.
- **THE WHAT/WHY SPLIT (Kael's people-reading).** His physical reading is extraordinary — ten years of training; his social reading is near-nonexistent. Render the split explicitly: he may correctly detect the WHAT (he's angry; she's lying; the room turned) while completely misreading the WHY (the motive; whether a lie is cruel, protective, embarrassed, playful, or kind). Let him make real mistakes, on the page, uncorrected — the loom-glance ("they are checking I did it correctly") is the model. It is an ARC, not a fixed trait; its pace is the author's, scene by scene, and its destination is 00's.
- **Fights are puzzles.** Kael wins by legible technique and decision-making, never by power-ups; the reader should understand every victory. **In a fight chapter the thinking happens BEFORE and AFTER; the fight itself is bodies** — immersive, fast cuts, the reader in it; relative distances, never surveyed ones; no floating callbacks. He knows what he is looking at — write that, in the present: not a justification of it, and not a callback to the boy he was.
- **Sealed truths on the page — the spends are STATE, not staging:** how the throw, the chest, *"Mine as well."* and the parents' capacity reached the page is in `07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §THE PARENTS AT CAPACITY, AND THE STEEL; Neris's ceiling is Book Two's and her spend-state is there too. What a page may not yet point at is 00's, and the author carries it.

## Character & Scene Rules
- **KAEL'S DEFINING AXIS.** His defining features are intelligence, technique, and practical field knowledge (enumerated in 03 and 02) AND exceptional fighting — all of it his parents' teaching in him. The deficiency is ONE axis only: keep a HEAVY, persistent emphasis on his lack of magic capacity, and on the empire/Temple wrongly treating capacity as the measure of a Resonant. The world keeps saying the loud wrong thing about him; the page keeps showing the quiet true thing. Both halves stay in play: as magic's stakes escalate, the axis the capacity-worshipping world undervalues is the axis that wins. **COUNTERWEIGHT: the critique is of capacity-WORSHIP as the sole measure — not of capacity, mass, leadership, or social gravity themselves.** Those are real powers, valued by the story and by Kael's own honest pricing (Aurelian's count was "well given" and Kael filed it): command moves six backs where the pry-bar moves none, and Kael's lack of it is a REAL deficit, not a hidden virtue. Render the complementarity.
- **RELATIONSHIP TIME IS LOAD-BEARING.** Every chapter carries at least one scene whose only job is a relationship moving — warmth, friction, play, pride — not plot, not clue-work. Rendered in the established registers (tells, shared work, jokes, the body) but allowed to BREATHE — real dialogue, real laughter that isn't threat. Love stays in the stitching (§Hard Rules); the fix is TIME, not sentiment. A loss only devastates in proportion to the warmth banked beforehand.
- **DIALOGUE AND EXPOSITION MIX.** Exposition and interiority mixed and matched with spoken scene — lessons in the teacher's actual voice, conflicts in the room's actual words; emotional conflict on the surface of scenes as well as under them. Not a quota; an ear. **The instruction is HOLD, not push** (the manuscript's early dialogue starvation was corrected by Part III): a chapter that wants to be interior is allowed to be; the flag is drift back toward the teens in a chapter built out of scenes.
- **The supporting cast shines.** Every significant character gets moments that are THEIRS — voice, body, presence; Kael is sometimes the camera, not the subject. Show, don't tell (some tell allowed). Physical descriptions come from 03's APPEARANCE LEDGER, filtered through Kael's eye; others' reactions carry conventional beauty.

## Model-Tic Self-Awareness
- Claude has default rhythms it falls into when not watching itself: standalone em-dashes, "not X but Y" constructions, sentence fragments deployed for gravity, triadic lists, AND-CHAINS ("and x and y because z" accumulations), default similes ("the way…" / "like"). None are banned — the book's voice uses all of them — but every instance must be a CHOICE, not a default. While drafting, Claude self-flags passages where its own patterns are steering the sentence instead of the voice; revision passes report per-chapter tic counts beside the word count: dashes, not-buts, fragments, triads, and/1k, similes/1k, and **narration words + words-per-paragraph** (`wc -w` is the budget instrument, not a reading-experience one — a dialogue-heavy chapter carries far less prose bulk per word; check narration bulk before calling a chapter long, and never cut fast dialogue to protect a total). AND-chains and simile density are awareness instruments, not thresholds. **Known default: first drafts run 51–55 and/1k uniformly across every section** — check and/1k per section at the end of every first draft, not per chapter. Upward drift without intent = revision flag. The test is always: would this sentence survive being read aloud in the author's voice, or does it sound like a model being writerly?

## Genre Register
- Epic, gritty, historical-flavored fantasy. Intimate over spectacular. Earned over destined.

## Things to Avoid
- Info-dumps; POV breaks; telling the reader what to feel; characters as plot functions (Character Law); hidden-puppet-master vibes for the leader — he stays human, limited, doctrine-driven.

## Author taste notes (calibration, not rules)
- **Interiority is the seasoning, scene is the meal.** Kael's deep head is the story's depth — keep it — but favor dialogue and real-time action over narrated summary whenever both can carry the beat. A lot of descriptive/interior text over action and dialogue reads boring to the author.
- **"Telling the reader what to feel" is a FLAG, not a verdict** (author ruling, session 19). The §Things to Avoid entry points at prose that substitutes an emotion-label for the scene that would have earned it. It does not ban the naked statement of feeling: a line that names what a character feels, in his own register, at a moment the book has already paid for, is allowed and is sometimes the right line. Weigh each instance; never auto-fail one on the rule's name alone.
- **The causal chain stops at the first why.** The child's-logic chains earned their density in Ch. 1–3; from Part II, links that re-derive established canon ("the why of the why") get cut — trust the plant. Not a flag system; an ear.

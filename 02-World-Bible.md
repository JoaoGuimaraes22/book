## The Empire — THE VESSANTINE EMPIRE
- **Name (CANON):** the Vessantine Empire; ruling dynasty House Vessantine.
- Once great: generations ago it used its power for good; it has rotted generation by generation into depravity — greed, pride, blood, lust. The powerful rule over the weak.
- Corrupt to the core at the ROYALTY level; but good people and good rulers still exist within it. (Character Law applies to civilizations.)
- Conquered much of the continent; its subjugated nations have suffered for generations.

## The Coalition
- Recent alliance of the continent's remaining/conquered forces, formed to resist the empire.
- Founding grievances: subjugation, exploitation — and (unrevealed) stolen children taken by the program's harvests.
- Carries out the raid on the island (end of Book One). To Kael, at first, THEY are the enemy — his first truth, written in fire. The saga slowly rotates this compass.
- Contains good and desperate people; commits atrocities. Neither side of the war is clean.

## The Program (mostly unrevealed — see 00 Author Canon)
- On-page in Book One: only fragments. An insignia the father recognizes with horror; a forbidden-language word in a raider's mouth; the mother's face when she understands what has come back.
- Reader's takeaway at end of Book One: the parents thought something was dead, and it isn't.

## Magic (system canon — 2026-08-09)
- **Elemental — as far as the known world knows.** Six elements: FIRE, AIR, WATER, EARTH, LIGHTNING, NATURE. The taxonomy is the known world's MODEL of magic, not necessarily its truth (origin/nature remain a world-level mystery; braids into the far-continent thread — see 00). The mother's "way" is pedagogy BENEATH the elements — perception-first, threads, structure, cost — deniably closer to the deeper grammar.
- **The Temple's natural philosophy (public/scholarly layer — on-page from Part II):** the Temple teaches a particulate theory: magic moves through **"the minima"** — the smallest grains of the world, present in all matter. A mage feels the minima (attunement) and works through them. The Temple's science is old, learned, honest — and incomplete (the deep truth: see 00, MAGIC — DEEP LAYER). Kael learns the word at enrollment; NEVER in Part I. Neris's craft register never uses it.
- **Proficiency structure:** every mage can use all elements at varying talent, and is always extremely more proficient in ONE. Rare cases: two or even three proficiencies.
- **Capacity vs. control:** raw capacity (output) and control (precision, threading, creativity) are separate axes. (Mechanical truth of both: see 00.)
- **Drain (publicly known — NOW TAUGHT ON-PAGE, Ch. 9):** magic exhausts the mage; it is never infinite. Deep drain is painful; drain near the bottom whitens the hair PERMANENTLY (folk talk: "witch-white"); full drain kills. The whitening never reverses — an old mage's hair is a readable ledger. Ch. 9 canon delivery (Ruthen's doctrine, HIS vocabulary — protect): magic is SPENDING; every mage carries a PURSE no eye can see into; effort → pain → the whitening ("the body keeps the receipt") → death past the bottom, every time, however beloved the house. Taught by the streaked master without one reference to himself; the room's collective not-looking = the lesson's second half. (The world's other body-mark — the red eyes — is folklore, not science; the two get garbled together in village talk. Ch. 9 establishes both marks in their clean registers: white as taught science by day, red as fireside legend by dark — the garbling now has its two sources on the page.)
- **THE LIE (public doctrine):** the empire teaches that only select bloodlines can be mages. FALSE — an old imperial lie preserving the power dynamic between royal/noble and citizen (see 00).
- **Enforcement — the pedagogy monopoly:** magic almost never surfaces untrained; attunement stays dormant without teaching (flickers dismissed as luck, superstition). The empire doesn't suppress commoner mages — it is simply the only teacher. The Temple is the fence around fire. Anomalies are absorbed as "noble blood somewhere in the line" — the lie self-seals.
- **Declaration (AMENDED session 8 — the leaning-tint):** a mage's dominant element SETTLES with maturity and training (soft window: ~12–14; "when it settles it settles"). Early magic manifests small and formless for most — but high capacity TINTS early: a strong leaning already shows its color in a child's first workings (Aurelian's wax-sheet fire at the candles, Ch. 9). Capacity-worship's pageantry is built on exactly this. Kael's declaration (LIGHTNING) remains a reserved beat (beat 11 window).
- **Pre-declaration cohort design (SEALED session 8 — resolves the Temple/beat-18 tension):** the young cohorts are pre-declaration BY DESIGN — their curriculum is theory, attunement, and COST (nobody hands fire to ten-year-olds); actual casting lives in the older students' precincts, which Kael's cohort never enters. Magic exists all around him behind walls and schedules — distance-texture only. The tournament's reserve is thus a POV-ACCESS rule, not a world rule: beat 18 = first magic seen CLOSE and WHOLE, as spectacle.
- **First workings (Ch. 9 canon):** candle-bench exercises under the yard-master, taught only after cost doctrine ("before I let any of you spend a grain, you will learn what spending is"). The leanings on parade in miniature. KAEL'S PUBLIC RESULT (sealed): formless near-nothing — a prickle, a half-breath's taste in the air, over before the bench is sure it happened. Load-bearing three ways: the lie self-seals around weakness ("someone's bastard, then" — the whisper is now a live cruelty vector); the declaration's revelation is protected; the golden children get their one true victory (the order of the world partially restores in the chapter that breaks it). Reader-irony: under the deep layer his profile is the favored one.
- **Kael's profile (canon):** proficiency LIGHTNING; unusually even generalist spread across all elements beneath it; LOW capacity; exceptional control, threading, precision, creativity — low-capacity lightning is not bolts but small, exact, fast: sparks, timing, nerve-and-thread work.
- **Dueling culture + the one-handed convention (SEALED session 8):** the empire fights in a GRAMMAR — salutes, the measure, guard-forms, honorable arms sold by paid fencing-masters. Mage-craft doctrine fights ONE-HANDED: longsword, rapier, or spear in the weapon hand, the off-hand kept EMPTY FOR THE ART — capacity-worship expressed as posture (the free hand is a boast). Vask's school is the antithesis: no forms because no audience; the way of the instruments is route-irrelevant, win-only; a fight is a problem, not a performance. THE DEEP JOKE (canon, rereaders): to eyes trained on form, formlessness reads as NO STYLE — the concealment does itself; the Temple watches Kael win and sees a scuffling herder with luck ("no language at all," Ch. 9). THE JOKE'S MIRROR (session 13, Ch. 9 spar rebuild): to Kael's function-trained eye, form first reads as WASTE — until he decodes it as a system "built to fight its own graduates" and begins ACQUIRING it (a second body-language; his tournament-rules literacy seeds here). Kael's endgame kit direction: see 03.
- **Consequences held in reserve:** teaching magic to a commoner-passing child (Neris to Kael) is, in imperial terms, subversive. The coalition discovering the truth and fielding commoner mages: banked as a later-books war revelation.

## The Mountain Curriculum (the parents' schools — Ch. 3 canon)
- **Register rule (AMENDED session 6 — now a GRADIENT):** the permanent ban is on OUT-OF-WORLD vocabulary only — no modern terminology, no notation, nothing that couldn't exist in this world. The knowledge, method, and precision deepen WITHOUT CEILING across the series.
  - **Part I (holds as written):** craft-lore — appetite and keeping, the lazy place, far-counting; a child's inherited vocabulary.
  - **Part II onward (the honesty pivot — canonized Ch. 7):** the parents teach with precision where they can afford it (the crossing proved the boy dismantles ambiguity); Kael's register matures into genuine period natural philosophy — substance, principle, proportion, trial; the rigor of real early natural philosophers, precise without being modern. The Temple's terms arrive where honest ("the minima"); Kael COINS HIS OWN where the world has none (far-counting precedent — the personal-lexicon habit is a canonized trait, 03).
  - **The experimental turn (Part II):** Kael moves from receiving method to running it — soil plots, reagent trials past the taught curriculum, controlled comparison he'd call "asking twice with one thing changed." The method true; the vocabulary his.
  - **The narrative engine:** his empiricism + survival pragmatism vs. the golden children's capacity-worship (magic as unexamined birthright). Sealed convergence in 00: his method is the deep layer's native epistemology — he is right, invisibly, all along.
  - Revision flags: a lesson that reads MODERN fails; a lesson that reads merely precise is now correct.
- **Father (body):** scree in the dark, breath, snares, the knife (the lesson is everything the cutting is not), stillness, terrain. **Ch. 5 additions:** loaded scree with counts; the grammar of falling; knife-work moved to the body ("where a man carries; where he is lazy"). **Ch. 7 addition — THE WEAPONS SCHOOL:** sword, spear, bow, on Vask-carved ash wasters (steel banked); opened as his answer to the night ("The body held. We will build the rest."); runs mornings at home in parallel with the Temple years — the concealment thread powered from both ends. **Ch. 5 addition (session 13) — THE HAND-TO-HAND SCHOOL:** the school with no first day (arm-snare play before memory, folded into work with no line crossed); escape-facing by design — every lesson begins with the boy CAUGHT, the one lesson "Get loose" (never explained on-page; points at the Harvest by inference only); the tap-sign (two taps = instant release, always honored; "The hand talks").
- **Mother (mind) — subject names are Kael's own or hers:**
  - **Far-counting** = mathematics. Heights by shadow, position by stars, counting what you cannot walk to.
  - **The standing game** = mechanics/engineering. Loads, levers, why things stand and fall. ("Everything heavy is lazy.")
  - **"What fire eats"** = chemistry. **SPENT (Ch. 5):** transformation as APPETITE — fire the picky eater (fat/bone, spirit/water); cold the other mouth, the slow one; salt the KEEPER (meat, barrel water, and the cold's shut mouth). The two-pail frost lesson; "the more salt the water carries, the deeper the cold must bite to hold it." Kills the promise (see 05).
  - **Herb-lore / mending** = biology/anatomy. Includes reagent-craft (vinegar, salt, hot iron: "things that cannot lie make the best witnesses"). Butchery is the anatomy classroom (father-overlap, deliberate).
  - **Languages:** four by age 8 — the bird one (first), the stone-click one ("Traders"), the soft "downhill" one (she is strictest in it; sings in it), and one more unspecified. Ends of the languages: never explained.
  - **The new school (magic — begun Ch. 4, unnamed by her design):** perception-only, rationed in drops, lessons deliberately SMALL. He is asked only to feel where things carry — the pail, the wind, the fire's lazy place. She answers until SHE stops (unique among her schools). Sealed logic: the leash (see 00).
  - **History:** deliberately RATIONED — scraps only, never on asking. The Temple carries real history education (Part II). Their secrecy about history is the same reflex as the boat-counting: every thread of the world's story leads back to them.
- Why the mother's curriculum is so deep: see 00 (Doctrine of Cultivated Inheritance — sealed).

## Geography
- **The archipelago:** rich in resources; home to wealthy noble merchant families.
- **The island (Book One):** its differentiator is the TEMPLE — a training ground where the empire sends talented young royals and nobles for a few years to be forged into future leaders, generals, mages (battle, politics, knowledge, magic, history).
  - The village: small; its lights visible from the mountain at night ("the stars that fell down the mountain"). **Ch. 5 canon: keeps the WINTER FEAST on the dark of the year — the longest night, lamps everywhere, everyone down at the water till morning.** **Ch. 6 aftermath (world-level, sealed causality in 00): the village acquires a ghost story — a crew of men found dead on the feast night, no survivors; "something came down off the mountain on the longest night." Wrong in details, true in center, pointing up. Available as village/Temple talk from Part II on (the folklore valve — see 00/05).**
  - **Temple geography (pinned Ch. 7):** the Temple stands SOUTH along the coast on a headland — visible from the mountain Kael's whole life as "a pale shape," a filed fact. The lawful road runs down the mountain past the split rock, BENDS HIGH ABOVE the village (skirts it — within sight and sound, never through it; the settlement's roofs visible from the road), then out along the coast's shoulder to the headland. Kael commutes daily: home to temple, temple to home ("Evening" = the deadline). The daily pass above the village keeps the world at the exact distance of his whole childhood — seen, heard, unentered — and gives the folklore valve a vector to reach him sideways.
  - The family's isolated home: in the mountains, sea approaches watchable.
  - Why THIS island: the master placed the parents here — hiding in the empire's own shadow, inside the blind spot of the man who runs the light.
- **The continent (Book Two+):** empire heartland, subjugated nations, coalition territory. Map: TBD.
- **The far continent:** officially doesn't exist. Clues only (see 00).

## The Temple
- **THE THREE SCHOOLS (SEALED session 8):** the empire keeps three great mage schools — the TEMPLE in the southern archipelago (serving the rich merchant-noble houses of the isles), one in the CAPITAL, one in the empire's NORTHWEST. Names of the other two reserved. The pedagogy monopoly runs through all three: three schools, one fence. Open texture axis (logged, undecided): archipelago money vs. capital blood vs. northwest frontier as student-status geography.
- Master — SEVERIN (CANON): head of the Temple; the family's secret patron — saved the parents in the past, placed them on the island, keeps their secret. The one visitor the mountain house ever receives. On-page mostly "the master."
- Students: the empire's golden children — multi-part names, house pride, casual cruelty toward the charity case. **Cohorts by age, fixed rooms; the YARD takes the young cohorts together** (Ch. 9 — the one shared arena; war-training scales where classrooms split).
- **THE BOARDS (Ch. 9 canon):** twice a year the Temple writes its children in order — midwinter and spring examinations (four days: letters, sums, the chronicle, natural philosophy, a day in the yard), standings nailed to the colonnade of the first court, every name in earned order. The machine that converts Kael's excellence into public standing whether he wants it or not. Kael's first boards: four lines from the top; the fouled-slate mark pulls him one line down at spring (permanent, official).
- The Grand Tournament: periodic grand event; **INTER-SCHOOL (SEALED session 8) — the three schools convene**, which is why the island fills, why a royal princess attends, and why the harvest screens there: the one event concentrating the empire's most exceptional children from all three schools in a single place (slots into 00's harvest logic; also sharpens the raid's causality — the coalition strikes the empire's future officer corps in one building). Students compete for a great prize (TBD). Beneath the pageantry (unrevealed): see 00.

## Time & Term Lexicon (ratified session 11 — coined register terms are CONTROLLED vocabulary)
- **Rule:** the book's coined terms for seasons, calendar, and institutions are a controlled lexicon. Each term is CONSISTENT (never improvise a synonym for a logged term) and OWNED by a place and culture — when geography or culture changes (Book Two's continent, coalition lands, the far continent), each locale mints its own terms rather than inheriting the island's. New coinages are logged here at chapter commit.
- **Village register (island):** the dark of the year (longest night) · the winter feast / feast night.
- **Temple register:** the cold term (winter term) · the turn toward spring · the boards (twice-yearly standings) · first workings · the roll · the yard / the yard-master · storm-weeks (the drowned-yard weeks of deep winter, children's vernacular — coined Ch. 10).
- **Mountain / Kael's narration register:** winter's turn (the seam of the year) · "Evening" (the daily deadline — Vask's word) · the long game · his personal lexicon (far-counting, the standing game, what fire eats, what being seen costs [Ch. 10] — see 03, the lexicon habit) · "a crop of laughter" (the group-laughter register's term of art — coined Ch. 8, ratified as deliberate coinage session 13; instances Ch. 8 ×2 / Ch. 9 / Ch. 10; capped — further uses deliberate-only, like any protected echo).
- **Scholarly register:** see Naming Registers ("the minima") · **water-wright / air-wright** (the Temple/common word for an elemental practitioner — coined Ch. 9; extends per element; never improvise synonyms like "water-mage" beside it).

## THE HONEST MEN — THE NAMED FIVE: TIRZAH · SELIS · ORAN · TALO · AMBROS
**Glance:** five of Borren's fifteen (`../02-World-Bible/factions.md` §THE HONEST MEN) · each from a different corner of the continent, each with the war's story · **THE CREW IS DESTROYED (Ch. 59). Of the fifteen, three live — TIRZAH, ORAN and TALO, out with the trio in two wagons. SELIS is dead in the traces; AMBROS was the betrayer and Borren killed him; the warehouse man died getting Kael clear.**

### STATIC — the group
- **Role:** the crew's faces. Character Law applies to each; what the page has is a name, a place and one story each, told once. Nothing below is a personality yet.
- **Naming register:** each in the register of where they came from (`../02-World-Bible/naming.md` §THE ROUND). One name each, no house — a steward is not a house.
- **How they were introduced:** Borren's *you should know what they are called*, then a round that nobody announced — *it went round anyway, the way the bread had* — and the not-asking the whole crew does, one face at a time.

### TIRZAH — the woman with the basket
- **ALIVE — one of the six out (Ch. 59), on the far wagon with the twins, looking back at Borren in the fire.** She saw the last look and knows what he never said. **She told the fire what Borren was the next night (Ch. 60):** the crew was the thrown-away he picked up off the roads; he was a general of the empire in the east who stopped and swore never to swear or kill again — *the only truly sorry man I have ever met* — said once and not again; and what he said of Kael the first night, *I want him.* **She keeps what is left of his word** — *nobody at that fire works again* — holds most of Ambros's board in her head, **stays with the twins** (*you have nobody, and I have a basket*), and gave the six their shape. She has the princess's name and rank from the girl's own mouth, put the scarf back over the hair herself, and took a promise off her; she called Kael in from the dark — *I am not him.* At the last feast she would buy cloth, a stall, *blue ribbon, all of it, and I will give it away*; the princess put her head on her shoulder. She held the princess on the wagon when Selis died — *you do not get down.*
- About thirty; of the coast three days west of Qeshet, a town she will not name; a stall of cloth and thread. The sharpest questioner in the rope-walk (Ch. 49); the one who carried the token up the stair; the one who arms Kael (Ch. 50); greeted by more of the crew than anybody.
- **Her story:** a governor's son took a length of blue ribbon off her stall and did not pay; her husband went after him to be paid, and the watch had him hanging from the gate by evening. *That is the basket off my stall. It is what there was of it that I could carry.*
- **On the road:** sits with the girls in the boat *the way a woman sits with her daughters*; puts them beside her at the fire; gives Kael the one-hand job and looks at the hand once and never mentions it; decides the trio's tent and nobody argues. Speaks the Koine with the coast under it. **Taught Kael the seller's walk** (*Sellers are not going anywhere. They are already there. Slower. No. That is a soldier being slow. Again.*) and **is the noise on a bridge** — met the officer at the third mule with a price in her mouth, three mules short of the princess. The princess does camp work having watched her once. At the fire where the job is told: *\"How many.\"*

### SELIS — the younger one
- **DEAD (Ch. 59) — a spear from the dark, folded over the mule's back in the traces of the near wagon; the princess made a sound no one had heard her make, and Tirzah held her on the wagon.**
- Eighteen perhaps; thin; hair cut off short at the jaw; says her name to the fire and not to anybody. Of **AMMARETH**.
- **Her story:** her father carried the old lord's cup for thirty years and nothing else in his life; when the empire *took the top off* and *the new top went on*, the men came to every house that had carried anything for the old one. *That is my story. It is a short one.*
- The princess sat beside her through it and did not move. **She and the princess have a tongue between them** that is neither Koine nor Ivrit, and Kael does not have it.

### ORAN and TALO — the twins
- **ALIVE — the near wagon out (Ch. 59): Oran on the box, Talo loading the chests.** The next night Talo dug the fire down and set the stones on the east side without a word; Oran hobbled the mules in Avestan and **did not sing**. They will take *enough* of the coin and are not going to buy a valley; Tirzah goes on with them. **They have the princess's name and rank** — Oran put the hair together, a boy whose own people burned his mother because the empire's column was coming — *Adah watered the mules. Every night.* At the last feast Oran sang in Avestan a road and a house at the end of it, and **Talo, who does not talk, mouthed every word without a sound while Oran sang for the both of them**; earlier Talo caught the thrown bread out of the air without looking, the funniest thing in a year. They now hold the trio's real names (said in the fold).
- *Plainly not of this country* (Ch. 49); twins, from a valley *across the water, a long way up it*. Oran talks; **Talo does not** — *He did once.* Oran's Koine is a learned thing with Avestan under it where the word will not come; Kael heard it and kept that he has the language. **Oran hears what drivers say from a cut above a road** (*he said her name twice and nobody laughed*), which Kael cannot.
- **Their story:** ahead of the empire's column *our own people came* — grain, animals, every man who could walk (the two of them at fourteen), and fire in the rest so the column would find nothing; their mother would not leave the house and was left in it, *because the sum did not have her in it*. They walked off from the column in the second week and have been walking since.

### AMBROS — the man of sixty in the good coat
- **DEAD (Ch. 59), and he was THE BETRAYER — killed by Borren, with understanding.** At Ishareth in the winter a man showed him proof his daughters had been taken and told him what would happen; he wrote everything down for a winter and a year (the nightly writing, *nobody reads it*, was it), and his letters gave the coalition the crew's movements and the fold. His handler is the coalition's architect of the island and Qeshet (00). *They took my daughters … I wrote it all down. I do not know if it kept them alive.* Borren: *I know what they do. I have done it. But you must understand me too … It is not all right. But I know.* He sewed nothing; **the princess sewed the blue**, and his last word was for her: *I am sorry to the girl. She sewed the blue.*
- Of the capital, then of a house forty miles north of it, then of nowhere; the empire's tongue *as it is spoken in the empire*. Steward forty-one years — *everything that came into it or went out of it passed under my hand and I wrote it down*. The coat is buttoned to the throat and was the house's: *the only part of that house that is still standing up.*
- **His story:** the house had land; *a cousin of the Emperor* wanted it and asked *by being told no and waiting*; a charge was found, the head went to the wall with his sons, the land went where it was always going, the people with the furniture. *A steward of sixty is not a thing anybody buys.* He names neither the house nor the cousin. **The princess knows which house it was and has said so only in the dark.** **Taught Kael the voice** — the Koine *as it was spoken forty miles north of the capital*, nine words for an hour, *say where you are from as though you were bored of it* — and **sits the box with a made seal in his coat** and pays a toll short by a copper. **Kept the crew's winter house** as he kept the old one — food, beds, the gate, the lane — *as though the house had never fallen down*, with the princess watching from the kitchen door. At the fire: *\"Who is paying us.\"*

### THE OTHER NINE — unnamed
- **The warehouse man of Ishareth is DEAD (Ch. 59) — he died getting Kael over the lip**, a spear through his back, *Go on, then* (Borren's word), and shoved the boy clear with the spear in him; **Kael never learned his name.** At the last feast he would buy a stone warehouse and a chair in the door of it, every paper real. He taught Kael the lock: wet twine under the shackle, worked round the tongue, leaned on; not one word, and *a thing worth more than a warehouse* given without saying so; at the fire where the job is told he says *\"How much.\"* — his first words on the page since the eleven. **The talker** drilled Kael's sentence at the bridge-house and made the circle laugh on purpose at the fire.

### FORWARD
- **The crew is destroyed.** Three survive with the trio, bound for Solvenna with half the coin on the tracks: **Tirzah**, who has told what Borren was and keeps his word; **Oran and Talo**, who hold the trio's names and the princess's rank. **The shape (Ch. 60):** the coin into the ground outside Solvenna, the three in at the gate, the three on up the water, and no meeting after. Whether it holds is open.

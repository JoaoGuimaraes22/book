## AELIANA CASSIVARA VESSANTINE (the princess)
**Glance:** royal princess; the raid's prime target · House Vessantine · **14 — one year above Kael and Valeria** · **in the governor's box a day east of Qeshet, gagged, in iron on her mended hands and her wrists opened on it; she pulled twice and stopped when a herder's boy said *Don't*; the governor opened the door and looked at her**

### STATIC
- **Name:** Aeliana Cassivara Vessantine; "Cassivara" is an honor-name in the formal chain.
- **Role:** Attends the Grand Tournament; escapes with Kael and Valeria. Function: the empire at close range — the Trojan horse into the imperial storyline.
- **Naming register:** *the princess* by default, the full ceremonial chain where the empire is being invoked, and **the bare given name in narration, on the page from Ch. 34** (author-ruled, s49) — it arrived when she stopped being an institution in that party and became one of eight people on a mountain. The device is in 00 and its later steps are untouched and still reserved.
- **Character (author ruling, s29): ARROGANT, PRIDEFUL, NOT EVIL** — the arrogance of someone who has never once been contradicted and does not know that is a condition. Every branch of her arc (00) survives that.
- **Appearance (SEALED at her chapter — Ch. 22):** **unmatched, radiant, royal beauty.** She is the most beautiful person in the book and is never in competition with anyone for it; the cast is kept deliberately plain (appearance-ledger) and she is the exception the plainness exists to frame. Not tall. **Long SILVER hair — the VESSANTINE HOUSE TRAIT, not a drain-mark** (author ruling, s33; the mark is pure quartz white and distinct in kind). **Pale blue eyes.** Both UNSPENT: Ch. 22 gives the gown, the gold and the construction of the hair and never once its colour. Long, and **worn LOOSE by default** (author, s33); the pinned construction is the TOURNAMENT state, not her standing look. *Prose caution: the book already spends the word* silver *on the Silver league; her hair will want another word on the page, or careful placement away from it.* Blue so dark it reads black until the light moves on it; gold at shoulder and hem in a quantity with no function except to be a quantity; hair up off the neck in a construction that took more than one person more than an hour. **Pale untouched skin, with freckles across the nose and cheeks** (ruled s35; unspent on any page). **HOW IT REACHES THE PAGE (03's register): not as a narrator verdict.** It has arrived two ways. **(1) THE RADIUS** — what a room does in her presence; at the games eleven thousand people stand, and the sound underneath the standing is not a cheer and not a roar, and is the sound of a very large number of people being glad about a thing they will never be nearer to. **(2) KAEL'S INSTRUMENTS FAILING** — he looks for reach, weight, what the shoulders were taught, where the hands go unoccupied, and gets nothing; and it is explicitly NOT concealment, which he was raised inside and can watch his own mother do across a room. There is nothing under it to get — at arena distance and at conversational range alike. **Both halves are true at once and the paradox is the point:** she is genuinely radiant AND she was built from the ground up to be looked at by exactly that many people at exactly that distance. Kael's verdict — *the kind of beautiful that is also a uniform* — arrives from nowhere he can name, and stays with him for years.

### DYNAMIC
- **GAGGED, IN IRON, IN A BOX GOING EAST.** Awake before Kael, upright, looking at the slot; she pulled twice against the iron with everything she had and opened her wrists over the oil; *"Don't"* — and she put her hands down and sat as she had at the table, and he could not read whether she had stopped because he was right or because she had given up. She watched him listen and was told the count unasked — mules, riders, Ivrit, east — and could not answer. She looked at the slot while he used the straw. **What she saw or heard in the house is hers and she cannot say it.** Hazoun Sabrane opened the door at dusk and looked at her.
- **SHE RULED THE ROOMS.** *Nobody is to be by himself who does not wish to be. It is a house. It has walls. It will bear it* — and the court stopped for her the way the boat had. Then, from her doorway, *Goodnight, Lucan* — the first mouth to say the name to the boy's face; he answered to it two strides late and did not turn round, and she said nothing more.
- **SHE LIED, FOR THEM, IN WRITING.** Asked for every name at the governor's table for a rider to the capital, she saw the boy and his mother go still and gave the house as Drusane — *Lucan Drusane, the Lady Drusane, and the child* born in the fire — and told the governor to *write it so*; Aurelian and Elarine backed it with true sentences about the dead boy, and the governor wrote. **Nobody at the table named what it was**, and she and Kael did not look at each other after. Her oath to the First Master — *nothing I have seen in this room leaves it* — is the reader's key to why; Kael has decided it was pride.
- **SHE WAS KNOWN BY HER HAIR.** A month of salt and sun took the gown's colour, the skin off her hands and the flesh off her face and did nothing to it; the governor's face changed on it, he said the whole chain, and he knelt to the girl where the watch had knelt to the name. **The colour is on the page: silver, once, and nothing else in the world that colour** (author, s55: unique). Her eyes' colour is still unspent.
- **She did the cloth** — every hundred count for two hours a day, palms bleeding through the strips, corrected once by Kael and right the second time — and told him why, looking at him for the first time in six days: *I was told on that mountain that I should be extremely good at this sort of thing … I should like to know which.* Her cup went down the boat to his mother on the nineteenth day. She inclined her head the width of a finger for the watch's water and told a kneeling governor *Get up … There are eight of us and we have not eaten. Do that first.*
- **Her guard are all dead behind her** — the last five went at a door without a mark on them, the captain last — and a Vel is on her left, ordered to give her life for her; the princess has not been told and would not price it if she had. Her hands are unwrapped and mended and held a little open. Valeria carried her out of the sea.
- **She was told to her face that she was not worth it, by a boy who then came at her** — the first contradiction of her life. She did not answer. **What it did shows only in the length of the silence and in what other people did with their hands.** What she made of it is unresolved.
- **She crossed a boat to ask to hold a herder's child** — the first thing in her life she has asked for rather than sent for — sat down on wet boards to do it, got the head wrong and corrected it herself, and wanted nothing from anybody while she held him. **It is not an apology and nobody calls it one.**
- **She offered the best coin she owns and it was obscene, and she meant it entirely:** their names read at court with their houses standing, *and I shall have it written down.* She prices twelve dead royal guard as correct and refuses to be delicate about it *because that would make it smaller than it was.* **Not evil, and uncorrected on the page.**
- **She is sworn, and she does not know what she saw.** *"Nothing I have seen in this room leaves it."* — said back to the First Master, out loud, by a girl nobody has ever told to say anything. **She saw Neris's eyes come up red and has no idea what that was.** She carries it into the boat, the trio and her whole arc; what she does with it is open.
- **She held the lamp** over a delivery in a herder's house: one small backward step she did not decide on, then obedience; very thoroughly sick against the wall afterwards, and back to pick it up again; the third time it dipped, nobody had to tell her. She held a newborn for the length of a fight because a herder's wife told her she would be extremely good at it.
- **She has been thanked once in her life for something she did with her hands** — *"And you… Thank you. You did well."*, from a woman on a floor — and did not manage to say anything back.
- **She has walked six miles of goat ground in court shoes**, fallen three times and taken it on her palms, been carried at a dead run, set down, got up off scree by another child, put flat on the boards of a boat by another child and held there through a barrage with a rope coming down a hand from her head — and has not mentioned her hands, which anybody can see from fifteen feet, and nobody has thanked her for any of it.
- **Nobody said goodbye to her in the district:** every other child in that party was held on to; she and Valeria were the two nobody had hold of.
- **She has never been contradicted, and does not know that is a condition.** She spoke once in a war council about her own survival — *"And my guard would be with me the entire way." / "Then I do not see the difficulty."* — it was the wrong thing, nobody corrected her, and a room of nineteen turned its head to her and she did not notice a room doing it.
- **Her bare given name is in narration now** — it arrived when she stopped being an institution in that party and became one of eight people on a mountain. The device's later steps are in 00 and stay reserved.
- **What she has of Kael:** nothing her instruments can price, which is very annoying — a boy with no royal reflex at all, whose rudeness was absence, not defiance; who corrected her about Drusane in front of her household and asked where the food on her table comes from; who knelt when told, had no farewell, and has now blamed her to her face. The seed of a distaste for royalty is planted in him and unassembled.

## AELIANA CASSIVARA VESSANTINE (the princess)
**Glance:** royal princess; the raid's prime target · House Vessantine · **14 — one year above Kael and Valeria** · **one day inside an outlaw crew as *Adah*, a cousin off the coast whose people are dead — sat between the woman with the basket and a girl of Ammareth, and heard her own house's rot told at a fire by people who do not know who is listening**

### STATIC
- **Name:** Aeliana Cassivara Vessantine; "Cassivara" is an honor-name in the formal chain.
- **Role:** Attends the Grand Tournament; escapes with Kael and Valeria. Function: the empire at close range — the Trojan horse into the imperial storyline.
- **Naming register:** *the princess* by default, the full ceremonial chain where the empire is being invoked, and **the bare given name in narration, on the page from Ch. 34** (author-ruled, s49) — it arrived when she stopped being an institution in that party and became one of eight people on a mountain. The device is in 00 and its later steps are untouched and still reserved.
- **Character (author ruling, s29): ARROGANT, PRIDEFUL, NOT EVIL** — the arrogance of someone who has never once been contradicted and does not know that is a condition. Every branch of her arc (00) survives that.
- **Appearance (SEALED at her chapter — Ch. 22):** **unmatched, radiant, royal beauty.** She is the most beautiful person in the book and is never in competition with anyone for it; the cast is kept deliberately plain (appearance-ledger) and she is the exception the plainness exists to frame. Not tall. **Long SILVER hair — the VESSANTINE HOUSE TRAIT, not a drain-mark** (author ruling, s33; the mark is pure quartz white and distinct in kind). **Pale blue eyes.** Both UNSPENT: Ch. 22 gives the gown, the gold and the construction of the hair and never once its colour. Long, and **worn LOOSE by default** (author, s33); the pinned construction is the TOURNAMENT state, not her standing look. *Prose caution: the book already spends the word* silver *on the Silver league; her hair will want another word on the page, or careful placement away from it.* Blue so dark it reads black until the light moves on it; gold at shoulder and hem in a quantity with no function except to be a quantity; hair up off the neck in a construction that took more than one person more than an hour. **Pale untouched skin, with freckles across the nose and cheeks** (ruled s35; unspent on any page). **HOW IT REACHES THE PAGE (03's register): not as a narrator verdict.** It has arrived two ways. **(1) THE RADIUS** — what a room does in her presence; at the games eleven thousand people stand, and the sound underneath the standing is not a cheer and not a roar, and is the sound of a very large number of people being glad about a thing they will never be nearer to. **(2) KAEL'S INSTRUMENTS FAILING** — he looks for reach, weight, what the shoulders were taught, where the hands go unoccupied, and gets nothing; and it is explicitly NOT concealment, which he was raised inside and can watch his own mother do across a room. There is nothing under it to get — at arena distance and at conversational range alike. **Both halves are true at once and the paradox is the point:** she is genuinely radiant AND she was built from the ground up to be looked at by exactly that many people at exactly that distance. Kael's verdict — *the kind of beautiful that is also a uniform* — arrives from nowhere he can name, and stays with him for years.

### DYNAMIC
- **SHE IS *ADAH* NOW.** Her road name, one of the two plain ones off the coast Kael had ready; she said it herself at the fire, and *Our people are dead* was said for her. Her sleeves stay down over her wrists.
- **SHE HEARD HER FATHER'S COUSIN TAKE A HOUSE, AND HER BACK WENT STRAIGHT AND STAYED THERE.** Ambros's story — the land, *a cousin of the Emperor*, the charge found, the head and his sons to the wall — with nobody at that fire knowing what was sitting at it. Kael saw the back go *the way it had gone at the governor's table* and could not say for whom it stayed straight.
- ***"I KNOW WHICH ONE IT WAS."*** In the tent, in the dark, in a voice that *was not the voice she used for ruling things*: *He did not name the house.* / *No.* / *I know which one it was.* — and nothing after it, and Kael did not ask. She has that house and has said so to two people, and no page says what she does with it.
- **SHE SAT WITH SELIS OF AMMARETH** — the girl whose city was her house's prize — and *did not move any part of herself* through the story of the cupbearer's house. Nothing joins the two on the page.
- **SHE RULED, AND THE RULING WAS THE COUNCIL'S LAST WORD.** Given the three roads and the decision *we make together*, she did the fear-sum out loud — a man worked out what she is worth *and what he did with it was lower his voice*; *I know the size of that fear to the grain. Of him it is small* — and closed it: *"Everything left to us is a risk. You have not yet let us down Kael." … "Then I have ruled. We go."* She read Valeria's agreement off her eyes and said so.
- **SHE CUT THE LETTER OUT OF THE SUM.** Kael raised it as a thread and she declined to trust it — *"By the man who had us in a box before morning. I would not put weight on that letter. If my father's people have any of them, it reaches the capital without it. Everything they touch does."* The lie is hers and she is the one who would not lean on it.
- **SHE WENT OUT.** Nine days indoors by her own rule, and at the bottom of the stair she stopped for the length of one breath, *as though the street were cold water and she was choosing how to enter it* — then walked down the middle of a foreign city with her sleeves pushed back, the younger of two sisters on an errand, and the only person who looked twice looked because she stood too straight.
- **SHE WAS ASKED, AND SHE ANSWERED IN TWO WORDS.** Borren, last, straight at her: *"Is it your own choice, to be standing on my bank."* / *"It is. Entirely."* — the outlaw's own procedure doing what no court ever did. What she is stays unsaid by everybody, the leader enforcing the not-saying himself.
- **SHE OPENED THE DOOR, AND SHE DID NOT OPEN IT UNTIL THE TOKEN WAS FINISHED.** *"Nineteen boards … eleven long and eight short over the joist"* through the wood, answered with the knot in the fourth, and only then the bar. Two nights standing behind it, and a woman with a basket twice, and **she did not open it once.**
- **SHE TOOK HOLD OF VALERIA BEFORE SHE WAS THROUGH THE DOOR.** Both arms, her hands shut in the back of the coat, her face down on the shoulder, and nothing said. **It is the second thing she has ever done for Valeria with her hands**, after the strip off her own hem in the fold, and it lasted twenty-three seconds. Then she refused her the form: *"Highness," / "Do not."*
- **SHE WAS TOLD WHO KNOWS WHERE SHE SLEEPS, AND SHE DID NOT REPROACH HIM.** *"So somebody in this city knows which room I am in." / "How?" / "I told them."* Her answer was *"I am not arguing with you. I have spent two nights working out how frightened I ought to be. Now I know, and it is less than it was."* **She has still not been told he held that address all day in front of strangers before he gave it.**
- **SHE HEARD HER FATHER'S SOUTH FALL AND DID THE SUM OUT LOUD.** *"By men my father is at war with. In a city my father holds. Which nobody in this country has heard about yet." … "And I am four hundred miles the wrong side of it, in a room over a mule yard, with two children and a bag of somebody else's money."* — and then, unprompted, ***"Three children," she said. "I am sorry. That was badly done."***
- **SHE IS ON NO PAGE OF CH. 50 EITHER, AND THE RAID HAPPENED WITHOUT HER.** Kael held her out of the trade and then gave up her address to buy her two words under a door; the job ran on the night after that, and **the page never goes back to the room over the mule yard.**
- **SHE IS ALONE, AND SHE IS ON NO PAGE OF CH. 49.** The two people she sent out did not come back and cannot; Valeria's own read of her is that **she will not go out and will not send anybody** — she will sit and wait until they come or somebody else does. Kael gave up her address to buy her a word, having spent the whole day keeping her out of the trade.
- **THE TOKEN IS SOMETHING SHE ALONE COULD KNOW.** *Nineteen boards. Eleven long and eight short over the joist. There was a knot in the fourth one* — from the night she asked him what the count is.
- **SHE WAS HELD OUT OF THE TRADE, AND WILL NOT KNOW IT.** *"She is not part of what I am selling. She is the reason there is anything to sell. She is not going anywhere near that building."* Said to strangers, in her absence, and on no page in her hearing.
- **SHE RULED HERSELF INDOORS BEFORE ANYBODY COULD RULE HER.** *I won't go out. Not to the water, not for bread, not once. I had that before you said it.* She is the only one of the three anybody is looking for and she had done the sum herself.
- **SHE GAVE A VEL AN ORDER AND WAS OBEYED.** Valeria refused to leave her side; the princess overrode it — *There is nothing you can do about a man on that stair that a bar on the door does not do first. What keeps me alive in this city is not you standing in front of me. It is finding out where that man sleeps.* — and against *that is not how it is done*: **"I have had twelve royal guard stand beside me and they all died for me, and I won't have it again. Go when he asks you."** The first time she has ever spent her rank on somebody's behalf rather than her own.
- **SHE CORRECTED KAEL ABOUT BEING LOOKED AT, AND ONLY SHE COULD HAVE.** *Nobody looks at faces. I have had people looking at me since before I could walk and not one of them has ever looked at my face. They look at whoever is being looked after* — the shape people make round somebody who matters, which Valeria has been standing in since Qeshet without either of them noticing, and which a man on a gate reads in the time it takes to walk past him.
- **SHE WORKED A WINDOW SHE WAS NOT ALLOWED TO OPEN.** All day, over a mule yard, one word in four by the fifth day and asking at night about the ones she had kept — and she brought back the boats **off four mouths that had not spoken to each other**, tracked as one fact moving through a yard and being repeated wrong. *"I did it correctly, then." / "You did it correctly."* **She was extremely pleased with herself and hid it badly.**
- **SHE TOOK THE GROUND, AND ASKED FOR THE DANGEROUS HALF OF IT.** She took the foraging off Kael so he could throw, learned the greens and stems in two days, and argued him into teaching her the poisons — *If I am doing this, I have to know what I am walking past. I would rather know a thing and not need it* — then made him say the procedure twice and said it back. On the sixth day she flagged her own broken one before he had to.
- **SHE ASKED HIM WHAT THE COUNT IS, AND NOBODY EVER HAD.** She refused *counting* as an answer, got the whole of it, and told him it is not ordinary by failing his own test — nine men or fifteen, and one of them with a boil coming on his neck she has thought about forty times. *You have the other one. Between us there is a whole person, which is more than either of us is managing alone.* And then, without knowing what she was standing on: *"Then when you say you have put a thing down, what is it that you have actually done with it."*
- **SHE SAID NOTHING AT ALL** when he told them what he was going to do, and looked at her hands.
- **SHE SPEAKS IVRIT ON THE PAGE.** *Thank you … I continue. Please teach again more* — said to a smith who had just corrected her, in the fold's own version of the tongue and not Kael's; *She might have a knack for this kind of thing.*
- **SHE WAS GIVEN THE MOST IMPORTANT JOB ON THAT GROUND AND DID NOT LOSE IT.** Four hundred strides up a rise under a needle-tree with a cloak over her, feet first, told to count them in and not to shout — *Afterward the number is the only thing in the world that matters. Do not lose it.* She had nineteen, held it, and **her nineteen against Kael's eighteen is why the last man did not get away.** She did not ask whether she should look away. She did not look at the flat place walking back past it.
- **SHE WORKED WITH HER HANDS AND THEN USED THEM ON SOMEBODY ELSE.** She covered three holes badly for an hour and then well, laying litter brought from a long way off the way you tile a roof; and afterward she bound Valeria's arm with a strip off her own hem, wrong twice and then right — **the first thing she has ever done for her with her hands.**
- **HER ONE CURRENCY WAS SPENT FOR HER AND SHE LET IT STAND.** Kael promised her father's reward to a village without asking. *It is the only thing I have ever had … Nobody answered me in that box, tho … This is the first time such words have been truly useful. I'm glad.* — and *whatever else happens, it will be said, and I shall have it written down.* Her only objection: *You should have asked me. Not because you were wrong. Because I would have liked to be the one who said it.*
- **SHE ASKED FOR THE SAFE ROAD, PLAINLY, AND LOST IT TO THE MAP.** *I have been in a box for two months and I have been under a floor and I have been hidden in a kitchen with a cloth on my head, and I would like very much to stop. I am not going to pretend that I would not.* She had no answer to the west road — and then **she was the one who named the debt to Doresh out loud**, and said it was not the reason and would not let them leave without it being said.
- **SHE ASKED FOR HIS LIFE WITH TWO EMPTY HANDS.** With Kael down in a stranger's yard and no Ivrit between the two of them, she made a deliberate noise — a hard double knock on wood, the loudest thing in that village that night and meant to be — and then held her hands out into the lamplight, open and turned up, iron above the wrists, and did not move them. **Valeria did not stop her. Kael was on the ground and has both only as fragments he cannot attribute, and nobody has told him.** The girl who made no sound twice when it would have killed them all made one on purpose.
- **SHE ASKED TO BE TAUGHT IVRIT, AND HAD TWO DAYS TO BUILD THE ASK.** A woman washed her feet and she sat there like a dog being seen to; a child of four had more of that house than she did; and she would rather know what is said about her while it is being said than because Kael has decided to tell her. **The first word she wanted was thank you**, drilled eleven times at a wall. About forty words by the fourth day, mostly nouns, all used on Miresh at the table whether they fitted or not.
- **SHE WAS TOLD WHAT SHE IS WORTH, TO HER FACE.** Under the floor she asked what was being said and Kael gave her all of it — the description, a price worth every roof in Doresh, and *any house* — and *So they will give us up.* / *I would give us up.* **What she answered with was the child's name**, which she had needed telling twice.
- **SHE IS HIDDEN, AND NOBODY LOOKS AT HER TWICE.** The smith's wife put the scarf on her the first morning, did it herself and explained it to nobody; after that they stopped looking at her. **It came off for about four seconds** in the yard after the soldiers, because a four-year-old pulled at it and she let her, and the dust of two months made no difference to it whatever. She never knew who saw.
- **SHE IS GOOD WITH THE CHILD, AND THEY HAVE NO LANGUAGE BETWEEN THEM AT ALL.** Neshah decided about her on the first afternoon and there was no appeal; sorting beans by a rule a four-year-old invented and enforced, corrected at length in a tongue she did not have, with her sleeves pushed back over the iron.
- **SHE STABBED A MAN TO PROTECT KAEL, AND DISOBEYED TO DO IT.** Given the small knife and told *if a man reaches you, put this in him and let go and get away*, she instead came up off the ground behind the soldier grinding Kael's ruined hand and put the knife into the back of his thigh **to the guard, and did not let go** — held on with her whole weight while he turned on her with the spear-butt up. **He did not bring it down** (her hair, her face, what he was not allowed to do), and that half-second was the whole of the fight. It is the second thing she has done on her own account, after standing up (Ch. 41), and the man she stabbed became the prisoner.
- **SHE WATCHED THE QUESTIONING AND DID NOT LOOK AWAY** — from the first lie to the last breath, on her knees in a dead man's coat, and afterward she was looking at Kael, **and he could not read one thing in her face, and for once it was not because there was nothing there.** The third time she has not looked away (the hand, Ch. 40; the door, Ch. 41).
- **SHE IS WALKING AGAIN.** Carried part of the way, but on her own feet in a dead man's boots for the last of it — ten strides, then twenty, then nearly a hundred before her legs gave and Valeria caught her. She drank half the skin at the stop and did not apologise for the amount.
- **SHE STOOD UP, AND IT IS WHY THEY ARE ALIVE.** Five weeks without standing on her own account, and at the door she got her feet under her — *her hand went to the boards once and came off them* — and every man in that doorway looked at her, and the one on the bar spent his shout on a girl who had stood up when she had been told not to. **She had been told to do nothing, not to stand, not to run, not to fight whoever took her.** She heard it and overrode it. **She never says why; nobody asks; Kael does not get there.**
- **HER FIRST WORD IN TWO MONTHS IS *KAEL* (author).** Not *Lucan* — the name she gave a governor's clerk for a letter, and the only name she had ever said to his face. It came out *broken and about a third of the size of her voice*, and what followed it was *"I did not do what you told me."* and was answered *"No. You didn't."* **Nobody has remarked on the name.**
- **SHE MADE NO SOUND, TWICE, WHEN IT WOULD HAVE KILLED THEM ALL** — through the whole of the hand in the dark, and again under the box with a man's feet a hand's width from her own.
- **SHE IS CARRIED, AND SHE KNOWS IT.** She could not stand when she was put on her feet and stood anyway, holding a fistful of Valeria's sleeve the way the dead man had held hers; then she went up six shelves of stone over a Vel's shoulder. She drank half the skin and did not apologise for the amount.
- **SHE IS COMING APART, AND ONLY THE READER SEES IT AS THAT.** The back went in the fourth week — the upright she had held at the governor's table, *the single most impressive thing Kael had ever watched anybody do* — and did not come back. In the fifth she stopped reaching for the water, and Valeria has made her drink twice a day ever since. In the sixth, in the dark, she laughed through the cloth, small, and slightly too long. **Kael filed it as the heat, then the wrists, then an illness he had no training to name, and never got to the truth of it.**
- **SHE SPENT HER FOUR OR FIVE BREATHS OF MOUTH A DAY ON ONE SENTENCE, TWICE.** *"My father will surely pardon you and reward you with anything you wish, if you free me. Please. Anyone."* — the second week, and **again word for word in the fourth**, to a box holding the two people chained beside her. Nobody answered her either time. It is the only currency she has ever had, and she is offering it to the straw.
- **SHE WAS TOLD TO LOOK AWAY AND DID NOT.** She watched the whole of the hand, gagged, unable to make a sound, and her face was wet, and **she did not make one sound the whole night**. It is the most present she has been in five weeks and nobody remarks on it.
- **SHE HAS BEEN TOLD WHAT TO DO WHEN THE DOOR OPENS** — do nothing, do not stand, do not run, do not fight whoever takes you — and **whether one word of it went in is on no page.** Kael prices her as a body that will have to be carried, which is Valeria's own verdict on that mountain, arrived at independently and joined up by nobody.
- **GAGGED, IN IRON, IN A BOX GOING EAST.** Awake before Kael, upright, looking at the slot; she pulled twice against the iron with everything she had and opened her wrists over the oil; *"Don't"* — and she put her hands down and sat as she had at the table, and he could not read whether she had stopped because he was right or because she had given up. She watched him listen and was told the count unasked — mules, riders, Ivrit, east — and could not answer. She looked at the slot while he used the straw. **What she saw or heard in the house is hers and she cannot say it.** Hazoun Sabrane opened the door at dusk and looked at her.
- **SHE RULED THE ROOMS.** *Nobody is to be by himself who does not wish to be. It is a house. It has walls. It will bear it* — and the court stopped for her the way the boat had. Then, from her doorway, *Goodnight, Lucan* — the first mouth to say the name to the boy's face; he answered to it two strides late and did not turn round, and she said nothing more.
- **SHE LIED, FOR THEM, IN WRITING.** Asked for every name at the governor's table for a rider to the capital, she saw the boy and his mother go still and gave the house as Drusane — *Lucan Drusane, the Lady Drusane, and the child* born in the fire — and told the governor to *write it so*; Aurelian and Elarine backed it with true sentences about the dead boy, and the governor wrote. **Nobody at the table named what it was**, and she and Kael did not look at each other after. Her oath to the First Master — *nothing I have seen in this room leaves it* — is the reader's key to why; Kael has decided it was pride.
- **SHE WAS KNOWN BY HER HAIR.** A month of salt and sun took the gown's colour, the skin off her hands and the flesh off her face and did nothing to it; the governor's face changed on it, he said the whole chain, and he knelt to the girl where the watch had knelt to the name. **The colour is on the page: silver, once, and nothing else in the world that colour** (author, s55: unique). Her eyes' colour is still unspent.
- **She did the cloth** — every hundred count for two hours a day, palms bleeding through the strips, corrected once by Kael and right the second time — and told him why, looking at him for the first time in six days: *I was told on that mountain that I should be extremely good at this sort of thing … I should like to know which.* Her cup went down the boat to his mother on the nineteenth day. She inclined her head the width of a finger for the watch's water and told a kneeling governor *Get up … There are eight of us and we have not eaten. Do that first.*
- **Her guard are all dead behind her** — the last five went at a door without a mark on them, the captain last — and a Vel is on her left, ordered to give her life for her; the princess has not been told and would not price it if she had. Her hands are unwrapped and mended and held a little open. Valeria carried her out of the sea.
- **She was told to her face that she was not worth it, by a boy who then came at her** — the first contradiction of her life. She did not answer. **What it did shows only in the length of the silence and in what other people did with their hands.** What she made of it is unresolved.
- **She crossed a boat to ask to hold a herder's child** — the first thing in her life she has asked for rather than sent for — sat down on wet boards to do it, got the head wrong and corrected it herself, and wanted nothing from anybody while she held him. **It is not an apology and nobody calls it one.**
- **She offered the best coin she owns and it was obscene, and she meant it entirely:** their names read at court with their houses standing, *and I shall have it written down.* She prices twelve dead royal guard as correct and refuses to be delicate about it *because that would make it smaller than it was.* **Not evil, and uncorrected on the page.**
- **She is sworn, and she does not know what she saw.** *"Nothing I have seen in this room leaves it."* — said back to the First Master, out loud, by a girl nobody has ever told to say anything. **She saw Neris's eyes come up red and has no idea what that was.** She carries it into the boat, the trio and her whole arc; what she does with it is open.
- **She held the lamp** over a delivery in a herder's house: one small backward step she did not decide on, then obedience; very thoroughly sick against the wall afterwards, and back to pick it up again; the third time it dipped, nobody had to tell her. She held a newborn for the length of a fight because a herder's wife told her she would be extremely good at it.
- **She has been thanked once in her life for something she did with her hands** — *"And you… Thank you. You did well."*, from a woman on a floor — and did not manage to say anything back.
- **She has walked six miles of goat ground in court shoes**, fallen three times and taken it on her palms, been carried at a dead run, set down, got up off scree by another child, put flat on the boards of a boat by another child and held there through a barrage with a rope coming down a hand from her head — and has not mentioned her hands, which anybody can see from fifteen feet, and nobody has thanked her for any of it.
- **Nobody said goodbye to her in the district:** every other child in that party was held on to; she and Valeria were the two nobody had hold of.
- **She has never been contradicted, and does not know that is a condition.** She spoke once in a war council about her own survival — *"And my guard would be with me the entire way." / "Then I do not see the difficulty."* — it was the wrong thing, nobody corrected her, and a room of nineteen turned its head to her and she did not notice a room doing it.
- **Her bare given name is in narration now** — it arrived when she stopped being an institution in that party and became one of eight people on a mountain. The device's later steps are in 00 and stay reserved.
- **What she has of Kael:** nothing her instruments can price, which is very annoying — a boy with no royal reflex at all, whose rudeness was absence, not defiance; who corrected her about Drusane in front of her household and asked where the food on her table comes from; who knelt when told, had no farewell, and has now blamed her to her face. The seed of a distaste for royalty is planted in him and unassembled.

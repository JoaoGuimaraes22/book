## Logline

On an island in the empire's own shadow, a boy raised in secret by two ex-assassins is drawn into the temple where the empire forges its golden children — and at the height of a grand tournament, war reaches the island, his family is shattered, and he is cast onto the continent with a rival who became his first friend and a princess of the empire that was hunting him from birth.

## Pitch

Kael has never left the mountain. The rules are absolute and unexplained: never approach the village, never light a fire visible from the sea, never speak to a stranger. His parents fill his childhood with intense learning — languages no one speaks, knife-work as chores, silence as play, and in time, their way of magic. To him, this is simply life. To the reader, it is a held breath.

Because his mother and father were instruments of an imperial program that harvests children of a rare bloodline — and they didn't just flee it, they burned it down, and believe it dead. Their son, the only good thing they ever made, is also the last thing they ever stole. When his talent is seen and the island's great Temple opens its doors to him, Kael steps for the first time into the world of the empire's golden children — and meets Valeria Vel, the flawless daughter of the royal protectors, the first person who has ever been his measure.

Then the Grand Tournament comes, and with it a princess, the empire's quiet machinery — and the war. Mid-duel with Valeria, the island burns. His father dies buying time. His mother, his newborn brother, and the master who protected them vanish into the smoke. Book One ends on the water with what is left of them — a burned island behind, a broken boat, and no land anywhere. Ahead of them is a continent of courts, conspiracies, old magic and older lies, and the three who will cross it together: the mountain boy, the guard's daughter, and the princess of the empire.

Each disaster strips another illusion from him and forges what remains. To survive the world that hunted him from birth, he must become dangerous. The question the saga asks is whether he can become dangerous without becoming *them*.

## POV & Style

- **Close third limited, Kael-focused.** Single POV is the DEFAULT, not an absolute: the camera can pan away from him, a chapter or a bit can sit in another head, and a retrospective *I* can surface at an emotional turn. What each is for and how each is written: 06 §POV.
- **Prose matures with his age** (Joyce / *Portrait* technique): early chapters in a child's rhythms; complexity, irony and self-awareness grow as he does.
- **Strict information rule (scoped at amendment):** within Kael's scenes, the reader never receives information he doesn't have, except by inference. Sanctioned departures may show what Kael doesn't know — but they relocate dread, never unseal 00: machinations moving, not answers landing. All dread is still built through gaps between innocence and understanding — Kael's and now sometimes the world's.

## Structure

- **A series.** Book One = Act One = the island: childhood → the Temple → Valeria → the tournament → the fire → the escape by sea.
- **Book Two onward — the continent:** empire vs. coalition, politics, intrigue, magic, and the slow truth of his family, the program, and the bloodline.
- **Genre:** epic, gritty, historical-flavored fantasy. Grassroots start; legendary scale by the end.

## Thematic Core

- Surpassing one's limits.
- Love and friendship as forces that remake people.
- Change radiating outward — self, then others, then the world.
- A legendary tale built grassroots: no destiny handed down; everything earned and paid for.
- The ultimate enemy is human: power curdled into greed, and the war it feeds. (Full destination: see 00 — Author Canon.)

## Character Law

Every significant character — heroes, villains, everyone between — carries the good and the bad:

- Each has a **limit, a wound, an awkwardness**.
- Each **fails** at something that matters; each cries, laughs, contradicts themselves.
- Truly bad people may exist, and truly good ones — but even they are *limited*: the good are not wise in everything, the bad were not born that way.
- **No one is a function of the plot.** This law applies to factions and to the series' greatest villains, without exception.

## Emotional Promise

This is a human story wearing epic clothes. The epic scale must never cost the intimacy: when empires clash, we feel it through a hand held, a friend lost, a promise kept too late.

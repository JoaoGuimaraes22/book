### THE PARENTS AT CAPACITY, AND THE STEEL
- **THE AT-CAPACITY RULE (06 §Hard Rules) IS PAID ON VASK'S SIDE.** Vask has been seen at true capacity — Ch. 30 (the whole-body threading, the longsword, the exchanges with Pellan) and Ch. 33 (his hands) — rendered through a boy on the ground with broken ribs who could see only part of it and knew exactly what the part was worth; **the narration never compared him to anything and never explained him, and no later page may.** Nothing is left to ration on his side; the *never seen them try* beat is spent, early and by force. The pre-fire glimpse ledger (three glimpses, Ch. 1–3, and the Ch. 6 fragments) is closed history. **Neris's ceiling is Book Two's** (below).
- **VASK IS DEAD; THE SWORD WAS GIVEN, NOT THROWN (Ch. 33; author, s43)** — blade taken halfway down in his left hand, the grip turned over into his son's right, the boy's fingers closed on it by his own; then he walked into four hundred men with nothing in either hand and cut with them. It was staged as the MONUMENT every earlier Vask beat pointed at (the tally-marks, the carved wasters, the audit, the seed-bread, the carried-across-ice hand; the rule is in 00). **KAEL SAW THE DEATH, ALL OF IT, AND WAS NOT KNOCKED OUT** — never stage a later version in which he was spared any of it.
- **VASK NEVER CLAIMED THE BLOOD IN WORDS** — only *"Mine as well."* (Ch. 11) and one palm-crack; Kael, the Temple and the world inferred it. Spent; the rule behind it is in 00.
- **THE WEAPONS SCHOOL, AND ITS STEEL.** Wasters (Vask-carved; the wood's species is never named on the page — *ash* retired, s19) through Ch. 14; from Ch. 15 the school is in STEEL — blunted training blades one year out, provenance unremarked and unasked; the Temple issued its own at midsummer, so the mountain was ahead of the empire and nobody in that yard knew it. **The fire's steel was inheritance, not issue:** the longsword reached the page in Vask's hand (Ch. 30) and went into his son's (Ch. 33). Kael's own pair came out of the shed at Ch. 32 — two blades that do not match, his father's answer to the twin-shorts prohibition, put down inside his reach with not one word about the arena. **The prohibition is answered and dead; nobody adjudicates it later.**
- **THE CHEST (planted Ch. 19, locked) WAS NEVER OPENED ON ANY PAGE.** Its steel reached the page before it did — Vask arrived already carrying the longsword (Ch. 30), so the man was the signal that the ceiling was off, not the chest — and the *board up, the empty hole* archaeology designed for the house (s39) was cut from Ch. 32 by the author (s42): the archaeology and the subtraction moved forward whole and are unspent. **Do not stage a chest-opening retrospectively, and do not let anybody explain the sword.** Neris's dagger travels a different road (00; `../03-Character-Bible/neris.md`).

### THE FIRE'S SPENDS THAT BIND LATER PAGES
- **THE FLICKER IS SPENT AND STAYS UNRESOLVED (Ch. 33).** Reached THROUGH terror into the cold place, never as panic — the absorbed-state rule was the constraint the scene was built around. The page never says what happened; **only Severin saw it, and he says nothing about it, then or ever.** Nothing moved and nothing unlocked (what unlocks, and when, is in 00). No later page may explain, name or revisit it without a dedicated session.
- **THE FIRST RED EYES IN THE MANUSCRIPT ARE WREN'S (Ch. 33)**, and Kael got ONE of them for a moment before his mother put the linen back. **NERIS'S OWN WENT RED IN THE DELIVERY**, seen by Severin and the princess alone, in the pan-away — **Kael never sees them and must not.** The bloodline's name is on no page; 02's Book One ceiling was raised there and nowhere else.
- **THE CONCEALMENT ENDED IN THE HOUSE (Ch. 32).** 06's *love is embedded in the teaching, never stated* held for thirty-one chapters and is deliberately over from there: the parents behave like people who know — the mother used the Art in front of her son and took a permanent mark for it; the father sat down on his own floor, asked a question with no use in it, and armed the boy he forbade. **Nobody says why, and nobody may.** A SPEND, not a rule change: 06 is untouched and nothing retroactive follows.
- **THE CH. 32 REGISTER STANDS, AND IT IS THE POINT (author, s43).** Severin's *"You fool"* and *"I'll go check on her, do take care of these children behind me"*, Valeria's *"I hope you will let us stay by you, you know"*, Kael's *"Yes, well… I'm glad you're fine too, Valeria"*: their raw selves, in that house, that night, with the concealment ending inside it — not a general loosening of three of the book's most controlled mouths, and no retro pass.
- **NERIS'S ART IS SPENT AT MASS SCALE AND IT IS NOT HER CEILING (Ch. 34–35; author, s48).** Air that cuts, on the shelf; the same instrument turned to defence at saturation, from the bow. Kael priced her both times and found no bottom — *a number he could not even estimate the shape of.* **What she is is Book Two's to show**; what remains owed on her is a MOUTH, not a scale — the word in a raider's mouth and her face, and the Phantom. The old *steel and skill and nothing else* clause is retired.
- **HER FACE IS TURNED AWAY FOR THE WHOLE OF IT, AND THAT IS THE RULE, NOT THE STAGING** (held Ch. 34 and Ch. 35). The page never gets round in front of her during a working of that size; she does not turn round afterwards for a long time and nobody says her name; nobody remarks on the not-turning, then or ever. **Kael has never seen his mother's eyes go red and must not.**
- **KAEL'S COUNT HAS FAILED THREE WAYS** — it returned nothing at the gate (Ch. 28; the wall did not make it so, the wall made it visible), worked and returned something worse at the house (Ch. 32), and on the water returned answers faster than they were worth, losing the terms one at a time until *he did not know what number he was on* (Ch. 35). **The machine does not fail; its output stops being usable. Never write him as panicking.**

### THE BOAT, AND WHERE THE BOOK ENDS
- **THE SEPARATION IS BOOK TWO'S (author, s49).** Neris, Wren and Severin are aboard the hull at the end of Book One; the parting, its no-farewell and its instrument are Book Two's to spend — never arranged, foreshadowed as imminent, or pre-empted by anybody in a second boat. The word in a raider's mouth, the mother's face and **the Phantom asked and not answered** travel with the mouth that is still aboard.
- **HE DID NOT ASK (Ch. 36).** *Ask me on the water* came due; he had the question finished in his mouth, priced what it would cost her, and put it down; she felt him take the breath and offered him nothing. **The Phantom is unasked and unanswered, and the promise is neither broken nor kept.**
- **HE BLAMES HER, AND HE NEVER BLAMES HIMSELF (Ch. 36).** Kael told the princess to her face that the fleet came for her and that his father died of it — *they came up my father's path, four hundred of them, all night, because you were standing in his yard … and you are not worth it.* **Nobody confirms it and nobody denies it**, and the narration joins nothing up. **The fence is not the yard; the fence is his own hands** (`../00-Author-Canon-SEALED.md` §True causality): he may blame HER for the whole chain, and he may never notice that his own reconnaissance put that house in the party's road or that his own arithmetic chose the ground. **He is wrong at the level that matters, and Book One ends with him wrong.** Do not have a later page make him retract it.
- **VALERIA STOPPED THE HAND AND NOT THE MOUTH (Ch. 36).** She did not move while he said it; she was in front of him before the arm was anywhere, and it was his MOTHER's voice that ended it, not hers. **This is not the crown-vs-Kael choice and must never be read as one** — she stopped the body to avoid ever having to choose, which costs her nothing anybody can call her on. **The choice is STILL not made.** Her *Please* is spent a second time here (Ch. 27 was the first, twice) and stays rationed.
- **THE HULL'S FENCE HELD (Ch. 36).** Asked nothing, Severin said only *"It was not stocked for eight."* — true, and it answers nothing — **and nobody asked what it was stocked for.** The stores were laid in for four (Kael, Vask, Neris and the child); **that number is not on the page and Kael does not derive it**; nobody on any page knows it is the family's hull but the reader, and nothing joins the two. His *ask me the rest of it on the other side of that water* is still unspent, because they are not.
- **THE THREE WANTS ARE BOOK ONE'S LAST BEAT (author, s50):** his mother and brother alive; to know what his parents were; to kill the man who killed his father — the first wants of his life with a *because* under them (04's purpose thread pays here), and the page never says so. **NO VOW WAS SWORN AND NONE MAY BE WRITTEN:** a want, not a promise; nothing said aloud; nobody to swear it to; **he has still not named the man on any page.**
- **THE HULL DRIFTS (author, s49)** — about a month, to the south coast of the continent; `../04-Outline.md` §Book Two+ owns the landfall. No page names a course or a duration, and nobody aboard says how long.

### STANDING RULES THAT OUTLAST BOOK ONE
- **THE BLOODLINE'S ABSORBED-STATE RULE (sealed in 00 — page-facing consequence):** the blood surfaces from the absorbed/flow state, NEVER from panic or fear. Ch. 6's beating therefore correctly shows no surfacing; any future scene of Kael in mortal terror must NOT flicker; any future absorbed-state scene is live territory. Flow is necessary and insufficient (the rest of the gate is in 00). **The fire was not the unleashing**; what it carries is the FLICKER at Vask's death, rendered as him passing THROUGH terror into the cold place.
- **GROUP LAUGHTER IS A CHARGED REGISTER FOR KAEL (Ch. 6 onward, unmarked).** Every scene where people laugh around him has the settlement under it. Never labeled; render as his small physical tells only. Its term of art on the page, **"a crop of laughter"**, is capped — further uses deliberate-only (`../02-World-Bible/lexicon.md`); the register's origin runs the full night of Ch. 6 in three strikes.
- **THREE STACKED SYSTEMS.** Kael carries ignorance + settlement fear + armour at once, and others read the composite as strangeness; his threat-calibration makes their worst fail to register as danger, and his calm under mockery enrages. It ran unlabeled under every Temple-yard scene, and it is still the composite.

- **AUTHOR-DECIDED LINES — do not "fix" silently:** Ch. 16's opening comma/fragment chain stands (s21); Ch. 15's *"He'd spend two weeks looking"* stands (s20); Ch. 13's *riding steel* stands as written (s17); Ch. 30's *I'm sorry, little one.

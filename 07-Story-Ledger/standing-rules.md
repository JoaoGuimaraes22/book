- **IT WORKED BECAUSE THE DANGEROUS ONE WAS NOT HIM.** For sixty-two days every man on that road correctly identified which of the three in that box to watch, and *the man at the ring was watching Valeria. He had watched Valeria every morning since the first one.* **Nobody on the page says so.**
- **THE PRINCESS ACTED (author-approved, s63).** She stood up — the first time in five weeks, *her hand went to the boards once and came off them* — and the doorway turned to her, and the man on the bar took a fistful of her sleeve, and **the front of his shout was still the noise a man makes at a girl who has stood up when she has been told not to.** Ch. 40's *do not stand, do not run, do not fight whoever takes you* was heard and overridden. **She is gagged, so she never says why; Kael does not get there, and nothing on the page joins it up.** The governor's *She is not to be marked* (Ch. 40) is what the beat runs on and is not restated.
- **HER FIRST WORD IN TWO MONTHS IS *KAEL* (author, s63).** Not *Lucan* — the name she gave a governor's clerk, and the name she had said to his face once from a doorway (Ch. 38). Broken, *about a third of the size of her voice*, followed by *"I did not do what you told me."* and answered *"No. You didn't."* **Nobody remarks on the name, then or since**; *Kael* was not said at the governor's table (Ch. 37).
- **KAEL KILLED WITH HIS TEETH.** Both hands spent — the left a ruin, the right in iron on a chain — so the right took the hair and pulled the head back and round and the mouth did the rest: *He had thought about the front of it. It was the side.* **His whole job was the knife inside Valeria's reach**, and the same hand took it off the belt and put it in her two hands. **The ruined hand's second use is holding a mouth shut**, and Kesir bit through it.
- **VALERIA PAID IN IRONS.** The rope cut in one draw against the ring's own bracket, the ankles on the way up, **both hands together because they cannot go further than a hand's width apart**, the bar taken across the ribs without a sound, three men. **Two months of sleeping on his word and spending nothing pays here.** **Her kill is WITHHELD, not summarised** — it reaches the page as sound through an open door, because Kael is holding a mouth shut: *nine, counted without meaning to.* Her one line after it is *"Do not use it again."*
- **THE IRON IS STILL ON ALL SIX WRISTS.** The pin is peened over (Ch. 39); there is no key, and nothing on that road to cut it with. **The Art is unanswered for all three and nobody tries it.** At the stop Valeria holds her wrists up *without any particular emphasis* and lets him look, **and neither of them says anything about it.**
- **THE WAY OUT WAS THE BLIND SIDE AND THE WRONG DIRECTION.** *Behind the box there was nothing, and there had never been anything* — two months of never once hearing feet there. Frost holds a boot like wax; the churned ground goes toward the water and the pickets; the stone takes nothing and **goes uphill, back the way they came, and away from the water fifty men are sitting on.** One near-miss at the wheels, a man's feet a hand's width from the princess's, **and she did not make a sound.**
- **KAEL'S COUNT ON THE PURSUIT — RIGHT NUMBER, WRONG REASON.** *Not fifty*: **ten or twelve, mounted, with somebody who can read ground, behind them before the middle of the day** — built off the governor turning in the saddle every afternoon, which Kael still reads as a man afraid of thieves (Ch. 40). **Nothing corrects him**, and the page does not reach forward a second time.
- **WHAT THEY HAVE:** a dead man's boots, a coat, three knives, a steel and char cloth, and half a skin of water, with none in front of them. **What they do not have:** the Art, the iron off, a road, and any word of the other five.

### THE QUESTION (Ch. 42)
- **THE TRIO FOUGHT THE SEARCH PARTY TOGETHER, AND WON IN IRONS.** Twelve came off the road; **five held the horses, seven came in on foot** behind a tracker, into ground Kael set (one crossing, one gap you must bend through, a bank). **Six dead, one taken alive.** The kills were shared: Valeria out of concealment (the first two, the second's one sound cut in half), Kael the third from the bank after that man shouted (the neck, the jar up the ruined arm), Valeria the last two loose ones at the gap and the runner (a thrown spear at a dozen strides). **The party's constraint was the governor's *she is not to be marked*** (Ch. 40) — the men came in on foot and cautious and would not spear into cover, which is part of why three chained children beat seven. **Kael's Ch. 41 count of twelve held.** *Why fifty men do not catch three children* is spent (04 beat 1); the pursuit *slower and bigger* is still owed — the five who held the horses are the report that carries it, not on the page.
- **AELIANA ACTED AGAIN, AND FOR KAEL.** Given the small knife and told to stay out of it, she **came up off the ground and put it into the back of a man's thigh to get Kael out from under him, and did not let go** — the opposite of what she was told, the second thing she has done on her own account (after standing up, Ch. 41). The man she stabbed is the prisoner. **She then watched the whole of the questioning and did not look away** (the third time she has not looked away), and Kael *could not read one thing in her face, and it was not because there was nothing there.* She walked part of the way down on her own feet in a dead man's boots.
- **KAEL TORTURED A BOUND MAN — HIS FATHER'S BODY-SCHOOL TURNED OUTWARD FOR THE FIRST TIME.** Feverish, in massive pain, **keeping the count on the man's own pulse** (forty counts held first, so the truth of it could measure the rest) to stay clinical and stay himself — *he was doing it without deciding to, out of a school with no first day.* He forced the ground (**the KHAROUN**) and the nearest village (**DORESH**) out of the man against a first lie. **Then the desperation surfaced:** he overstepped the list onto the house and his mother — *what came out of that house* — read the man's flat true pulse saying he knows nothing (he was on the road, not in it), **and did it anyway for one second before he stopped** (*I believe you*, two words too late). **The page passes no verdict, sources nothing, and resolves it into neither justification nor self-horror.** The what/why holds: he read the body perfectly (the what); the thing nobody built did not stop him. **VALERIA KILLED THE PRISONER, unasked** (the good knife under the jaw), **and Kael let her** — he had the half-second and did not use it, and *knew he had not because it was the right thing and because he could not have made himself, and did not resolve it.*
- **THE PLAN IS ON THE PAGE (Kael's, in order):** where they are (the Kharoun) · a village (Doresh, half a day north, down the water, a smith) · **a smith to cut the iron** · a healer. The iron is still on all three of them (five wrists; Kael's left hand out of its band); nothing in the search party's kit opens a peened pin; the Art is unanswered and nobody tries it.
- **KAEL'S HAND IS A FEVER (Ch. 42).** Into cold running water for the first time; the back skinned and going a colour that travels, **a red line up the inside of the forearm** (his mother's lamb-lesson: *the body deciding to spend the whole animal to save part of it, and it is nearly always wrong*), hot through his shirt. **Priced at days without a healer.** Whether the hand works again is still on no page. The count is his anchor — strides, pulse, the princess's steps (*the only number he has that goes up*).
- **THE KIT CHANGED (Ch. 42).** Each of the three now carries a **plain soldier's sword** — Kael's own mismatched pair gone since Ch. 39, the father's longsword he cannot use; a soldier's sword he manages one-handed, Valeria far better with an edge than any man it came off (author, s64). Three knives kept, the spears left; skins, bread, cloaks, boots for both girls, a steel and char cloth, half a skin gone to full.

### THE WATER, AND THE DOORS (Ch. 43–44)
- **THEY WENT IN WITH A STORY AND IT IS *WAR ORPHANS WHO WERE TAKEN* (author).** Built in the grass: no name and nobody anywhere to send word to, because *a father is a name, and a name has a house on the end of it, and a house is somewhere a letter goes* — Qeshet's lesson, in Kael's own mouth. **The iron is the part of it a village believes**, because a war makes orphans and orphans get taken and are put in iron. The princess's merchant's-children version died on the father; she had never met one. **Kael says *orphans* about the three of them and the word catches him** — his father dead and never a story, his mother the sum he stopped building in the fourth week — and he goes on, and nothing is settled.
- **THE APPROACH WORKED AND KAEL DID NOT.** He read the smith off a banked fire and a shed roof and took them over the wall on the water side onto ground that holds no print; then the door was under his hand and the ground was against his face with nothing joining them. *The plan had been him.* **The two girls asked for help without a word between them** — a deliberate double knock on wood, and two hands held out open and turned up with iron above the wrists — and **Kael has both only as fragments through a narrowing hole and can attribute neither.** Valeria did not stop her. **Valeria's later report leaves both out**, as her Ch. 31 report left him out. Nobody in that house asked him a single question.
- **THE VILLAGE WAS TOLD WHAT SHE IS WORTH AND HID HER ANYWAY.** The search party described all three, including *silver, like a thing that has been made*, named a price worth every roof in Doresh, and named **any house** that had them or fed them and let them go. **That description destroyed the cover story**, and forty doors were knocked on afterwards and **not one of them opened**; Kael counted the three quarters of an hour from under a floor. **The lie did not survive and the kindness did.** Damir said it plainly and worked anyway — *she is not an orphan off a road* … *it makes no difference to the mule.*
- **THE SOUTH MENDS WITHOUT THE ART, AGAIN.** An old woman, unnamed like Qeshet's: hot water, a blade, honey, web off a rafter, a splint. **The arm is decided in two or three days; the hand she will not guess at** — *you may get a hand that shuts* — *you will find out in the spring.* Ten days needed and they had six. **Whether the hand works again is still on no page.**
- **THE IRON IS OFF ALL THREE, AND THE ART ANSWERS (Ch. 44).** Cut the peen off the pin, drift the pin, the band opens — an hour a band, five bands, two nights, and no quiet way to do it. **Forty houses lay in the dark and listened and nobody came.** The Art answers the moment the iron is off, for all three, the first since Ch. 39; Kael's is two fingers on an anvil at about the cost of a hard breath. **What the black in the grooves is is still on no page** — a smith of twenty-two years does not know it either. **The iron is in Doresh**, under the forge floor, taken as the only fee they had and kept as evidence.
- **THE PRINCESS IS LEARNING IVRIT (author).** She asked to be taught, having built the request over two days; the first word she wanted was thank you; about forty words by the fourth day. She is hidden under a scarf and nobody looks at her twice. **She was given her own price under the floor, in the Koine, and answered with the child's name.** **The scarf came off for about four seconds** in the exhale after the soldiers, because a four-year-old pulled at it and she let her.
- **TARIM SAW IT.** The smith's son, about twenty, out of his own bed for them and up the track to warn them — at the forge door with a bucket, twenty feet off, for a second and a half. **Kael read the family correctly and the person wrongly**, and priced him off six days of his kindness. **He was not in the forge on the second night, and Kael thought a man who has been up two nights running sends his son to bed.** What follows is 00's.
- **ISHARETH IS SPOKEN**, by the smith, six or seven days east where the ground goes down to a river: the last of it, held by the empire, and past the river not. **The spelling carries no accent (author, s67).** Kael took the name and the number the way he took *five months, Solvenna* from a governor.

### THE GROUND, AND THE NARROW (Ch. 45–46)
- **KAEL DID NOT STOP THE BETRAYER; HE BOUGHT HIM (author).** Tarim went up the water in the night on the same arithmetic Kael had run under that floor two days earlier and said out loud — *I would give us up.* Kael agreed with him, corrected the one term (*there is no sentence in the world where you give us up and Doresh is not in it*), and outbid him: **the Art shown as proof** (a thread the length of a soldier's sword, four seconds, at the cost of a hard breath, to a man with no word for it), **a half-truth** (*She is stronger than I am. And so is the other one* — true of Valeria past anything Tarim could understand, and a straight lie about a girl who has never spent a grain, told standing next to her), and **the princess's father's reward for the village**, promised on her without asking. In return: the confession told true, the ground named, and **a day.** One band came back out from under the forge floor as the proof he carried up.
- **DORESH WAS TOLD AND STOOD (author, s68).** Damir hears it, does the sum, and rules for the village: *These kinds of men, men who'll take children. I have no pity for them … Doresh stands by children. We are all in this together and there is nothing to decide. Anything you need, you'll have it.* **The headman is on no page of either chapter and nobody leaves.** The village is not fractured; it is frightened and behind them.
- **THE ART CAME BACK AND DID NOT SOLVE IT.** What found Tarim was fieldcraft in bad light — reading a print by looking across it, and a broken skin of ice with a hard rim giving four or five hours and *up not down*. The Art's one use in Ch. 45 is as a conjuring trick played on a frightened man. **VALERIA'S IS EXPLICITLY STILL RETURNING** (author): *My Art is still returning to me Kael* … *I'm almost embarrassed at my lack of power* — three pits properly, or four badly and no use the next day. What seventy days without the ground-sense was reaches the page in her own mouth: *As if I had lost a part of the world. And perhaps a part of myself too.*
- **KAEL SAYS *VAL* (author).** Twice in Ch. 45 and once in Ch. 46. He is the fourth mouth to use it and **nobody on any page remarks on it.**
- **AELIANA SPEAKS IVRIT ON THE PAGE (author)** — *Thank you … I continue. Please teach again more.* — corrected by Damir in the fold's own version of the tongue rather than by Kael, whose accent the author's pass stopped remarking on. She had the eye at the ambush and **the count was her job**; *This princess was not the same one he had once seen at the top of an arena.* She bound Valeria's arm afterwards with a strip off her own hem, wrong twice and then right — **the first thing she has ever done for Valeria with her hands.**
- **ALL NINETEEN DIED IN THAT FOLD AND NOBODY ESCAPED (author).** 1 under his own horse · 9 down the water side, of whom **4 were taken alive in the pits** · 3 at Kael's mouth · 6 held at the top, the officer among them. **14 dead and 5 alive, and all five were killed on the page.** Aeliana's nineteen against Kael's eighteen is what sends him two hundred strides down the shallows, where the last man is **breathing** and is killed there in front of Tarim.
- **THE KILLING OF THE HELPLESS IS EXPLICIT, AND THE FIRST ONE GOES WRONG (author).** The knife rather than the sword; the angle wrong from above the hole; the man turns the stroke so it lands over the collarbone and does nothing but make him worse; the second attempt is Kael's weight on the man's wrist through his own splint, the point under the jaw, pushed up and held. **Making sure** is two fingers on the side of the neck where the blood goes up, and *there were two he had to go back for.*
- **BOTH FIGHTERS RAN OUT.** Valeria took two spears through her own left arm to get a hand on the shafts, put a man head first into a hole to the shoulders, **visibly slowed** at sixty strides, and sat down on the horse and did not get up. **Kael could not hold the thread past a count of six** and finished his last man kneeling on his chest with none in the blade, in four goes, *because a soldier's sword is not a knife.*
- **THE MEN HAD BEEN BOUGHT, AND HE READ IT OFF THEM WITH HIS HANDS.** No two coats the same, four with the governor's mark and the rest none, boots of six makes, a mail shirt over a shepherd's coat. A child's tooth on a cord; coins sewn into three hems; a folded paper written for a man who could not read, saying **the roof had been done before the rain came and she was not to worry about it.** *He went along the whole of them and read everything there was. That part was nobody's fault but his. Nobody made him look.*
- **THE FIRST THING THAT DID NOT STAY DOWN (author).** The sum is on the page and comes out the same from every end he takes it from — *He had been right. He was still right* — against *the kind of men who sew coins into a hem*, and **both are true and neither moves the other.** *He put it down anyway, because putting things down was the only thing he had ever learned to do with them. / It did not stay down.* **This is the first failure of the mechanism** `../03-Character-Bible/kael.md` records — that a thing stays where he puts it and has not once asked him anything. **No verdict, no reassurance and no self-horror anywhere near it.**
- **THE GOVERNOR IS AT ISHARETH AND IS BUYING MEN.** Out of a dying officer: he went east four days after the escape; he is buying *as many as the letter he wrote will pay for*; **there is one bridge and it is his.** And on why three children were in a box — *He told us nothing. He has told nobody anything since the day he shut that door* — with the pulse flat on all of it, so **Kael's oldest question is still unanswered and now he knows nobody on that road has it either.**
- **DORESH KEEPS THIRTEEN HORSES AND KAEL GETS THE IRON BACK.** He would not take three east — *The gate does not have to be clever. It only has to be able to read* — and Damir prices them at one at a time down the water over four years. Damir digs the five bands and the chain out from under his forge floor at the leaving: *Because it is not evidence here. There is nobody in this fold to show it to.*
- **THE DECISION IS ISHARETH (author), AND THE MAP MADE IT.** Aeliana argued for Solvenna and did not pretend otherwise — *I have been in a box for two months … and I would like very much to stop* — and had no answer to the west road: two months in the open, wells a day apart, twenty-one of the forty still on it, no cover for eight days, and a description of her hair at every well. **Valeria never had to choose** — *I would go west if there were a way to go west. There is not* — and said once, and once only, that she does not like the rest of it, so **the crown-vs-Kael choice is still not made.** Kael said the true reason out loud when she asked for it: the governor is the only thread to his mother, his brother, the old man, **and Elarine and Aurelian**, and *there is not going to be a second one.* The princess named the debt to Doresh last, and it is **not the reason** — *That is not why we are going. / No. But it is true, and I am not going to let us leave without it having been said in this room.*

### THE CROSSING, AND THE HONEST MEN (Ch. 47–48)
- **THE COUNT IS EXPLAINED OUT LOUD, FOR THE FIRST TIME IN THE MANUSCRIPT (author, s69).** Aeliana asks, and **nobody ever has** — his whole life, two parents who built it into him, a Temple, and not one person had put the question. In his own words, with no term for it anywhere on the page: *I see what is in front of me. All of it. And it stays.* The proof is a list (nineteen boards, eleven long and eight short over the joist, the knot in the fourth, the proud nail in the seventh) and ***"Which day."*** **The numbers are amounts, time and distances**, and the useless rest stays too — *a whole wall I walked past when I was six years old* against *I could not tell you my father's mother's name, because nobody ever said it in front of me.* **HE BELIEVES IT IS ORDINARY**; she tells him it is not and proves it by failing his own test (nine men or fifteen, and one of them with a boil coming on his neck) — *You have the other one. Between us there is a whole person.* **AND IT ANSWERS THE FOLD WITHOUT EITHER OF THEM JOINING IT UP:** *"Can you stop it." / "No."* … *"Then when you say you have put a thing down, what is it that you have actually done with it." / "Stopped taking it out." / "That is not the same as it being gone." / "No."*
- **THE DESCRIPTION IS *THREE*.** Not her hair, not his arm: three children of that age walking together, which does not come off with a cloth. **The scarf stays on** (author) — the hair is hidden, not cut. **Aeliana's correction is the one only she could make:** nobody looks at faces, they look at whoever is being looked after, and there is a shape people make round somebody who matters — *she has been doing it since Qeshet* — which a man who has spent ten years on a gate reads in the time it takes to walk past him. Valeria can drop it for an hour and not a week.
- **THE PRINCESS RULES HERSELF INDOORS, AND OVERRULES HER GUARD (author, s69).** She is the only one of the three anybody is looking for and she says so before Kael can: *I won't go out. Not to the water, not for bread, not once.* **Valeria refuses to leave her** — *there is one thing I am for … I am not going to be in a street when somebody comes up that stair* — and **the only person alive who can overrule that does**: *I have had twelve royal guard stand beside me and they all died for me, and I won't have it again. Go when he asks you.* / *"Yes, Highness."* **This is the crown commanding Valeria toward Kael's work, so the crown-vs-Kael choice is still not made.** It pays inside Ch. 48: Kael and Valeria walk to the door together.
- **THE GROUND-SENSE IS A SORT OF PROJECTION (author, s69).** *Her Art flowed through the floor and walls* — a hand flat on the boards of a room over a mule yard, two bodies above and what each is doing; not long, not while asleep, and she would rather nobody in that city knew she could do it at all. `../02-World-Bible/magic.md`'s *a reading, never a projection / nothing leaves her body / perception only* clause is STRUCK; it now sits under the DISTANCE LAW with Kael's boot-thread, and its limits are consequences of spending.
- **ISHARETH IS NOT THE EMPIRE'S CITY, AND THE GRIP IS ONE BRIDGE WIDE (author, s69).** Nothing white and blue on the wall, no towers put up by somebody who arrived later, and the toll gate is the city's own with no soldier on it — *nobody in the empire cared who came into Ishareth.* The wall is Ishareth's and the watch is Ishareth's and does nothing, and **the one building the empire is standing in is the one you cannot leave the country without walking past.** So the officer's *there is one bridge and it is his* was not a boast about the difficulty: **it is the whole of the man's holdings.** A governor of a city four hundred miles west came up that road and took a bridge, and nobody gave him a town.
- **NALAR (author, s69).** The country past the river, minted in the coalition register. Kael has it off his mother, once, inside an Avestan lesson at seven or eight — *the east began at Nalar* — **the princess says the name differently from the way he does and neither of them remarks on it.** *"We are at war with Nalar." / "Then why is there a bridge."* — named by Kael as the right question, and **he has not got the answer to it**; a line of animals is crossing from the far side to the near while they say it.
- **THE GATE COUNTS WHAT IT CHARGES FOR.** Two men taking money and a third with a stick and a board, counting animals and loads; the driver says a number and the man makes it true and never looks at the height of anybody walking beside it. Kael comes in with his hand on a mule's neck in the middle of thirty-eight of them; four coppers a head for the animals and nothing at all for the people.
- **THE COIN OFF THE HEMS IS SPENT.** He cut it out of three of those coats on his knees in the flat place above Doresh with the light going, put it in the sacking with the iron, and **said nothing about it to either of them then or since.** It buys the room and the bread, and the man who takes it for the bread puts it between his teeth first.
- **THE SEARCH RAN ON HIS OWN RULE, AND THE CITY HAS TWO SILENCES OF THE SAME SHAPE.** He never asked a question he wanted the answer to — you ask the thing beside it. The yard would tell him anything about the road, and then the talk came up to the bridge and stopped and went round it, *and not one of them ever noticed himself doing it* — the same shape the talk makes round the honest men four days later.
- **THE WALL:** the bridge-house has one door on the street and one onto the bridge, a walled yard with one gate, slits made narrow and never widened, four men on the street door and two on the yard gate — **and not one of the six of this city.** A table, a clerk, a man who turns one in three away, a chest with a soldier either side; **fifty-one on the first day, sixty-three on the third**, nine of them the box's, and **RASHIM on the yard gate two strides from a boy with a barrow, in his line of sight for a slow count of forty.** *He is not spending a purse. He is spending a river.* **The sum has no door in it.**
- **THE HONEST MEN (author, s69) — A BYWORD, NOT A NAME.** They have never broken into anywhere in their lives: everything they have ever taken walked out of a gate in daylight with the paper in order and the man on the gate holding the door for it. Ishareth this month: a house on the upstream side, two on the door day and night, a load of copper out of a locked shed with the lock still on it, and every boat on the bank refusing money. The Solvenna robbery two years ago — a whole marble statue, out of a yard two hundred men stood round for a month, on a cart with the owner's own seal. **No member of the crew is on the page and the leader is unnamed** (BORREN is held in `../02-World-Bible/naming.md`). **Kael used a small version of their whole method at that gate nine days earlier and nobody says so.**
- **HE IS NOT BEGGING; IT IS A TRADE.** There is one reason to be in this city this month, and it is the chest he wants the man beside. What he brings: sixty-two days of the inside of that man's arrangements, a Vel, three Resonants, and a girl whose face is the reason the whole thing exists. ***It was a trade. That was the honest word for it, and he made himself use it.*** **And the part of him that would have argued about handing three lives to strangers did not say one word, and he noticed that it had not** — the second time the mechanism has been at issue since the fold, and the page passes no verdict.

### THE PAPER (Ch. 49)
- **THE HOUSE ON THE UPSTREAM SIDE WAS A FAKE-OUT (author, s70).** Two men on the door of a room with bedding for two, one pot and no rubbish in the corners; three questions, none about the bridge-house; twenty-two minutes while somebody walked somewhere else to decide. Then the steel on a table, a search that took the sleeves back to the elbow and looked at seventy days of iron-marks without mentioning them, and **sacks**. **Kael prices the sack before he takes it:** you do not blind a man you mean to kill, you blind him for the after. He asks for a distance and is refused one.
- **THE WALK IS COUNTED AND HE STILL DOES NOT GET THERE.** Strides, turns, surfaces, cloth over a street, a channel of running water; **the same three strides of loose board at 341 and again at 509**, after which he keeps the turns and stops believing the distances; then **twelve minutes upstream** with a bank close on the left, direction read off the catch and the recovery of the oars, because on water a count of strides is worth nothing. **He arrives with a shape on the map and not a point**, and says nothing about any of it inside that building. The real place is a **working rope-walk** with men making rope in it and a room off it holding paper, wax and seals (`../02-World-Bible/geography.md`, `factions.md`).
- **THE CREW'S LEADER IS ON THE PAGE AND UNNAMED.** Past fifty, thin, grey, *the sort of face you are given directions by*. **BORREN is still reserved and unspent** (`../02-World-Bible/naming.md`); **no member of that crew is named on any page.** He asks *what do you want* and *what have you got* and never *who are you*, overrules his own man's correct objection without arguing it (*Go and get on with the rope*), hands a child a knife, and says he will find out for himself why they cannot be seen. **His limit is spent inside twenty words:** he asks about Valeria in front of Valeria, is answered by her, and does not look at her again.
- **THE ART WAS SHOWN IN PRIVATE, AND ONE MAN SAW IT.** Kael's threading up the whole of a borrowed pen-knife, held four seconds, twice. **Valeria's is a column of the beaten floor as thick through as a man, up to her shoulder** — no noise, nothing off her, the ground for three feet round it down by a thumb's width (author, s70: a pillar, *simpler*). **THE ART IS FULLY BACK ON BOTH OF THEM (author, s70)** and neither showing is rendered as costing; Ch. 45's *my Art is still returning to me Kael* is discharged. Her ground-sense was not used and stays the thing she would rather nobody in that city knew about.
- **HE IS PRICED AS THE ESCAPEE AND DOES NOT DENY IT (author).** *You are the ones out of his box.* / silence / *You are not saying no.* / *No.* Then he is made to say aloud what the three of them are worth, in the words the soldiers used at Doresh — *every roof in a village, and the ground under them* — and told *it is more here.* **The man holds them completely at that moment and does not take it.**
- **THE TERMS.** Sixty-two days of the governor's arrangements and two Resonants' hands, for **one part in ten** of what comes out of that building — *a share is the only way to price a thing you have not been allowed to count* — **and Kael inside the building when the door opens**, on a reason that is functional, correct, useful, and *every word of it was true.* **THE PRINCESS IS HELD OUT OF THE TRADE ENTIRELY** — *She is not part of what I am selling. She is the reason there is anything to sell* — said to strangers, in her absence, and Valeria's head came round an inch.
- **HE WAS OFFERED A FIFTH AND DID NOT TAKE IT.** The road was priced against him first: **thirteen months to Solvenna**, twice the ground for people who cannot be seen, everything paid for twice, bread at four times the price *and that is what he charges for the not looking*, and a war putting men with a list on every road worth walking on. *A tenth would do it. It might be short.* **The refused fifth is the only tell the page keeps** that the money is not the thing; **no word is put on what is underneath it**, and Ch. 46's *there is not going to be a second one* stands unspent and uncontradicted (author, s70: asked twice and not ruled).
- **THEIR PLAN WAS COMPLETE FOR A MONTH AND KAEL WAS THE HOLE IN IT.** Papers, a seal, a bank, and **the boats bought before the turn of the month at a price he does not enjoy thinking about** — which is why no hull on that bank would take money (Ch. 48). *There has been exactly one thing missing since the day we came into this city, and it is the inside of that building, and it walked up the street this morning and asked for a knife.* **The job is set for the night after the meeting**, on Kael's own rate said back to him out loud. **Fifteen of them, and not one looks like anything**; the rope-walk's whole day of work stops and nobody remarks on it. *Kael had never in his life been in a room where he was the reason.*
- **NOBODY GOES HOME (author, s70).** *Nobody who knows that leaves this building until it is done. Not you. Not her. Not me* — the woman with the basket included: **it is procedure and not captivity**, and what shut the door was Kael talking well for an hour and a half at the leader's invitation. **The price of a word to the princess is the address he had held all day** — Zahir's yard, the outside stair, the last room at the end — given unpushed, after being told plainly that it changed nothing and had not been asked. **The token is theirs and nobody else's:** *nineteen boards. Eleven long and eight short over the joist. There was a knot in the fourth one* — from the night she asked him what the count is (Ch. 47). A stranger with a basket carries it up a stair; **whether the princess opens the door is on no page.**

### STANDING FACTS THAT OUTLAST BOOK ONE
- **THE BLOODLINE'S ABSORBED-STATE RULE (sealed mechanism, 00 — its page-facing consequence):** the blood surfaces from the absorbed/flow state, not from panic or fear — Ch. 6's beating shows no surfacing, and the fire's flicker came THROUGH terror into the cold place. Flow is necessary and insufficient (the rest of the gate is in 00). **The fire was not the unleashing.**
- **THE PARENTS' PAST HAS REACHED THE PAGE AS ARCHAEOLOGY ONLY** — objects (the tally-marks, the chest, the sword), scars, reflexes, other people's fear and other people's mouths (*the Phantom*, Ch. 30): no flashback and no confession on any page through Ch. 37; Ch. 12's telling gave the stakes and not the content; Vask's side closed with him (`../03-Character-Bible/vask.md`).
- **GROUP LAUGHTER IS A CHARGED REGISTER FOR KAEL (Ch. 6 onward).** Every scene where people laugh around him has the settlement under it; the page has rendered it as his small physical tells and has never labeled it. Its term of art, **"a crop of laughter"**, is used four times and capped (`../02-World-Bible/lexicon.md`); the register's origin runs the full night of Ch. 6 in three strikes.
- **THREE STACKED SYSTEMS.** Kael carries ignorance + settlement fear + armour at once, and others read the composite as strangeness; his threat-calibration makes their worst fail to register as danger, and his calm under mockery enrages. It ran unlabeled under every Temple-yard scene, and it is still the composite.
- **THE MENTOR THREAD.** Kael–Severin's private channel opened on the walk down (Ch. 7): the rehearsal — concealment taught as curriculum, the family trade in a third teacher's hands, never labeled; question-doors; the one-inch doctrine tell (*"Hold that. It is the only thing I am worried you'll lose."*); kindness in valve-form, doors with nothing written on them. The valve family — the liar's knot, the winter-feast fact, *I know what she is* — is open by design and has not tipped (`../03-Character-Bible/severin.md`).
- **CRACKED WASTER — INERT.** The waster with the seated crack (Ch. 9, Lucan's rig) is spent: the crack-up return was its resolution.
- **THE RELIEF-TELL IS SPENT** (Ch. 5, deniably).
- **AUTHOR-DECIDED LINES — the author's, and they stand as written:** Ch. 16's opening comma/fragment chain (s21); Ch. 15's *"He'd spend two weeks looking"* (s20); Ch. 13's *riding steel* (s17); Ch. 30's *I'm sorry, little one. But all of you must go.* without a comma after *But*, and its *much more of that* (s52); Ch. 29's *and it was it.*, *some of his fellow students*, and the cut of the nothing-after-the-name beat (s52); Ch. 33's *the mending Art* and *a beautiful Art* — the capital is the rule (`../02-World-Bible/lexicon.md`, s52); **Ch. 34's *You are nothing special.*, italic and in the second person** (s50) — it follows *It had now come over and told him*, so it is the sea speaking, a quoted utterance; in the first person the man merely agrees with the verdict and the personification collapses. On that page the italic is the slip's, the remembered or overheard utterance's, and word-level emphasis; 06 is untouched.
- **RESOLVES NOTHING (author, s29):** the guard who read Kael's knife at the audience (Ch. 24) — no badge, no follow, no consequence.

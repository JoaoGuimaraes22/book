- **Ch. 47 — "The Crossing" (Kael = 13, not re-pinned; eleven days east of Doresh, then six days in ISHARETH).** **I — THE ROAD.** The count restarted above Doresh and not stopped. Four rules said out loud on the first morning. Three days of bread and after that what the road had: **Kael takes hares walking** (half a second, four hit and nine missed), **Valeria out-throws him eleven to two and he stops competing and gives her the windward side**, and **the food of the ground is the princess's** — his mother's half, in his mother's words. The onion test; the mushrooms as a procedure (*you cannot tell from the top* — dig it whole, a bag at the base, a ring on the stem, white ribs, **any one and you leave it**), and what the poison does hour by hour, *the part in the middle where you feel better.* She asks for the dangerous ones and wins the argument; on the sixth day she flags her own broken one before he has to. **II — THE ARITHMETIC OF THREE.** The yard's description taken apart: *the description is three*, and it does not come off with a cloth. **The princess corrects him** — nobody looks at faces, they look at whoever is being looked after, and a Vel has been half a step off her shoulder since Qeshet; Valeria can drop it for an hour and not a week. **III — IVRIT ON THE MOVE.** Four hundred words by the ninth day; **Valeria is the worst student he has ever had** — every sound correct and nothing else about it right — *I have never in my life had to want anything out loud in front of a stranger*; Kael hears his mother in his own mouth and does not say so. **IV — THE COUNT EXPLAINED, FOR THE FIRST TIME.** She asks, and **nobody ever has.** *I see what is in front of me. All of it. And it stays* — nineteen boards in the storeroom floor, eleven long and eight short over the joist, the knot in the fourth and the proud nail in the seventh; ***"Which day."***; the numbers as amounts, time and distances, and the rest staying too (*a whole wall I walked past when I was six years old* against *my father's mother's name*). **He thinks everybody does it**; she says no and proves it by failing his own test (nine men or fifteen, and one of them with a boil coming on his neck). *"Can you stop it." / "No."* … *"Then when you say you have put a thing down, what is it that you have actually done with it." / "Stopped taking it out." / "That is not the same as it being gone." / "No."* **V — THE LIP.** The Kharoun stops; the river; the city; **one bridge**. He looks for the empire in it and finds almost nothing. **NALAR** — the country past the water, off his mother, once, inside an Avestan lesson; the princess says the name differently and nobody remarks on it; *"We are at war with Nalar." / "Then why is there a bridge."* — the right question, unanswered. He starts counting the city and stops after four seconds because the number is not one he needs. **VI — IN.** The eleventh day, two more than he said: hired onto thirty-eight mules at the last halt (the hoof named before anything is asked for), the girls four animals back with their heads covered. **The gate counts animals and loads, which is what the city charges for and therefore all it can see**; all three men on it are of this country and there is no white and blue anywhere — **nobody in the empire cares who comes into Ishareth.** **VII — THE ROOM.** ZAHIR's yard outside the old wall; a room with no window that opens, six coppers the week, paid out of coin cut from three dead men's hems above Doresh and never mentioned; the man who takes it for the bread bites it. **VIII — THE RULES.** Nobody says where we came from twice. Kael has names for both girls and none for himself. **The princess rules herself indoors** — *I won't go out. Not to the water, not for bread, not once* — **Valeria refuses to leave her**, and **the princess overrules her**: *I have had twelve royal guard stand beside me and they all died for me, and I won't have it again. Go when he asks you.* Valeria's hand on the boards, the Art through floor and walls, two above them and what each is doing. **IX — THE SEARCH.** He never asks the question he wants; he asks the one beside it. The tolls, the bridge, the hours; **and the talk comes up to the bridge and goes round it, and nobody notices himself doing it.** **X — THE WALL.** The bridge-house: one door on the street, one onto the bridge, a walled yard, slits never widened, four and two on the doors, **and not one of the six of this city.** A table, a chest, a man who turns one in three away, and coin going chest-table-hand fourteen times in a morning. **Fifty-one on the first day and sixty-three on the third**, nine of them the box's, **and Rashim on the yard gate two strides from a boy with a barrow.** *He is not spending a purse. He is spending a river.* The sum has no door in it. **FACTS:** no departure of any kind; **NALAR minted** (coalition register) and **ZAHIR** (south's); **the count is explained out loud for the first time in the manuscript** — hyper-attention plus total retention, in Kael's own words, with no term for it, and he believes it is ordinary; **the ground-sense is a projection (author, s69)**; **Ishareth is not the empire's city** — held, one bridge wide; the coin off the hems is spent; Wren, Neris, Severin, Aurelian and Elarine still on no page. **NEW NAMES:** Nalar · Zahir. **NEW WORLD FACTS:** `../02-World-Bible/geography.md` (Ishareth, the river, the bridge, Nalar) · `naming.md` (the two mints) · `magic.md` (the ground-sense ruling) · `curriculum.md` (the s69 register ruling). - **Ch. 48 — "The Honest Men" (Kael = 13, not re-pinned; continuous — the fourth to the seventh day in Ishareth).** **I — THREE FACTS THAT ARE NOT ABOUT EACH OTHER.** A load of copper gone out of a locked shed with the lock still on it and the man's own mark still on the door, and the watch came and looked at the lock and went away. **The princess's, off a window she is not allowed to open** — she sits at it all day over the yard, one word in four by the fifth day: no boat on that bank will take anybody at any price this month, and she has it **off four mouths that had not spoken to each other**, and she is extremely pleased with herself and hides it badly. **Zahir's, given for nothing**: *the soldiers are a man with a table who will be gone by the spring, and this city was here before him*; strange people since the turn of the month, a house on the upstream side, two on the door day and night. **II — THE BYWORD.** ***"They call them the honest men."*** — **because they have never broken into anywhere in their lives**; everything they have ever taken walked out of a gate in daylight with the paper in order and the man on the gate holding the door for it. The Solvenna robbery two years ago that nobody has explained: a whole marble statue, out of a yard two hundred men stood round for a month, on a cart with the owner's own seal on it. *It is a joke. It stopped being funny about six years ago and people still say it.* **III — THE CLICK.** On the floor with his back to the door: they are not passing through, and there is **one reason to be in this city this month**, and he has been standing in the yard where it is being counted onto a table three days a week out of a chest out of a river. **He does not have to sell them anything** — sixty-two days of the inside of that man's arrangements, a Vel, three Resonants, and a girl whose face is the reason the whole thing exists. ***It was a trade. That was the honest word for it, and he made himself use it.*** And he is about to hand three lives to people whose trade is that nobody knows what they are doing until it is done — **and the part of him that would have argued did not say one word, and he noticed that it had not.** **IV — THE DOOR.** Told in the morning, including the part where it is a guess. - **Ch. 49 — "The Paper" (Kael = 13, not re-pinned; the seventh day in Ishareth, continuous from Ch. 48's approach — one day, mid-morning to after dark).** **I — THE BENCH, AND A HOUSE NOBODY LIVES IN.** The man with his boots off puts them on; **neither of the two takes the half-second a face takes when it is handed something it was not expecting**, so the sentence had been rehearsed for and they had been sitting on the answer long enough to be bored of it. *"Wait,"* in the Koine, handed straight back. Inside: one room, a table, four stools, a jug and a hearth; **bedding for two, one pot, ash raked flat, and nothing in the corners** — two men on a door with nothing behind them worth two men. A third man with a face Kael could not describe an hour afterwards, whose Koine has nothing southern under it, asks three questions — *Whose · Who sent you · Who else have you said it to* — and **does not ask what the information is.** *"Sit down."* The decision is taken elsewhere: **twenty-two minutes** of a man walking somewhere and coming back. **II — THE SACKS.** *"I'll need these sacks on your heads. And the steel on the table."* Kael prices it: **a sack is not a thing you put on a man you mean to kill** — you blind him for the after, so there is an after and it is somewhere else and worth hiding. *"How far." / "Not far."* Valeria puts the cost on the record once — *Once those are on we are two people being carried. There is nothing in that I am any use for* — and does it; her *I would rather carry it and not use it* is refused. **They are searched, and the sleeves go back to the elbow and the marks of seventy days in iron are looked at and not mentioned.** Then **the counted walk**: eleven strides and a right, forty-two and a right, thirty on stone with water in a channel, two hundred and sixteen under cloth; **three strides of loose board at 341 and the same three at 509**, after which he keeps the turns and stops believing the distances. Then the river, and **twelve minutes upstream** with a bank close on the left, read off the catch and the recovery of the oars, where a count of strides is worth nothing. Boards, hemp, tar, a wheel with a squeak every second turn. **A ROPE-WALK**, longer than any building he has been inside, three men paying out hemp and a fourth at the wheel and not one of them turning round. Valeria: *"Four with us. Five off the water."* **He comes out with a shape on the map and not a point.** **III — WHAT DO YOU WANT, AND WHAT HAVE YOU GOT.** A small room with **paper** in it — three piles under a stone, a column of figures, wax and two seals and a third put down mid-use. A man past fifty, thin and grey, going bald from the front, with a close grey beard and **a plain oval of dark leather over the right eye** (author, s72) — *the rest of the face was the sort you are given directions by*, and **he is the only one in that building who could be described** — who finishes his line before looking up. *"Everybody in this city has information about the bridge-house."* / *"What do you want."* — **"To be in it when you take the chest out of the bridge-house. Then **the rate**: fifty-one on the first day, sixty-three two days later, one in three turned away. *"We have men in that yard." / "Then you have a number. You do not have two numbers with two days between them."* — *He is not sitting on that chest. It is more here."* / *"Sit down."* **V — THE TERMS.** He sells it in order: the eight who worked the box's door and the order they did it in; the man who rode at the back of fifty for two months, gave his orders in the Koine to men who did not use it with each other, and **turned in the saddle every afternoon to look at the road behind him**; sixty-two days of it, off *a slot in a door about that wide*. Two of them for the work — *a wall you want down, or a something you want cut* — **and the princess held out of the trade entirely**: *She is not part of what I am selling. She is the reason there is anything to sell.* **The price is one part in ten**, and *a share is the only way to price a thing you have not been allowed to count*, at which the man laughs for the first time. Then the road: **thirteen months to Solvenna** for a man with a paper who can use the road; twice the ground for people who cannot be seen, everything paid for twice, **bread at four times what bread costs and the surcharge is for the not looking**, and a war putting *men with a list on every road worth walking on, and the other kind on the rest.* *"A tenth would do it. It might be short." / "Then it is short." / **"I would have gone to a fifth."*** — **and Kael does not ask for it.** Then the thing that is not money: *"When that door opens, I am inside the building."* — with a reason that is functional and correct, and **"Every word of it was true."** **VI — THE BAND.** *"They are in. On a tenth. And the boy is in the building when it opens."* The clock is Kael's own number handed back to be said out loud — *six more men a day, for as long as anybody cares to sit and watch it* — and the answer is **the night after this one**, because *we have been ready for a month*: papers, a seal, a bank, and **the boats bought before the turn of the month at a price I do not enjoy thinking about**, with one thing missing, and *it walked up the street this morning and asked for a knife.* **Fifteen come in over an hour and a half and not one of them looks like anything** — a man of sixty in a good coat, two brothers not of this country, **a woman of about thirty with a basket** greeted by more of them than anybody, four who are of Ishareth; the wheel stops and the day's work stops and nobody says a word about it. *Kael had never in his life been in a room where he was the reason.* **VII — NOBODY GOES HOME.** An hour and a half of telling; thirty or forty questions, nine unanswered and said so nine times; **the best eleven from the woman with the basket, and the last two of them about the boy.** *"I am going now." / "No."* — **nobody who knows how a thing is going to be done leaves that building until it is done**, the leader and the woman included: *It is not about you. - **Ch. 51 — "The Ships" (Kael = 13, not re-pinned; the eighth day in Ishareth, the same night, continuous from Ch. 50's last line — the bridge-house, the walk out, the parting, and the room over Zahir's yard).** **I — THE ANSWER THAT IS NOT ONE.** The governor finishes looking at a face he never looked at in sixty-two days — *Sixty-two days, and this was the first of it* — and gives *"I had them. And then I did not have them. I do not know where they are."* **The instrument is denied:** the hands are behind the man's back, so Kael goes to the throat above the collar and has it in four seconds, and it is useless, because a man hit twice in the body eleven minutes ago is going to run high all night. *The way it is done is that you take forty counts of a man saying something true, so that the truth has a size* — *There was no forty in this room.* So he calibrates on two things he already owns (fifty on the road; *"Nine … With me."* inside twenty feet of the box) and *Kael knew this was truth.* **II — THE HOUSE.** Sixteen men, **four to a door, four doors** — *"Four came into my room," Valeria said*, the first thing she says inside that building — six out carrying three children, **ten never left**, and he did not wait for them.

## DUELING CULTURE (empire-wide martial culture; the Art's conventions where they touch it)
- The empire fights in a GRAMMAR — salutes, the measure, guard-forms, honorable arms sold by paid fencing-masters. Resonant doctrine fights ONE-HANDED: longsword, rapier, or spear in the weapon hand, the off-hand kept empty for the Art — capacity-worship as posture; the free hand is a boast. Vask's school is the antithesis: no forms because no audience; route-irrelevant, win-only; a fight is a problem, not a performance.
- THE DEEP JOKE (rereaders' canon): to eyes trained on form, formlessness reads as NO STYLE — the concealment does itself ("no language at all," Ch. 9). Its mirror: to Kael's function-trained eye, form first reads as waste — until he decodes it as a system built to fight its own graduates and begins acquiring it (a second body-language; his tournament-rules literacy seeds there).
- **KAEL'S BLADES ANSWER TO NO SCHOOL AND NO SELLER (s33, recorded off the plates).** The empire's honorable arms — longsword, rapier, spear — are bought from paid fencing-masters and carried as posture. Kael's two came out of a shed: *not new*, one heavier than the other by enough to feel, the grips wrapped by somebody who had been in no hurry. Their look is therefore not an imperial look, and that is correct rather than sloppy — Vask's school is the antithesis of the grammar, so the kit should not read as a cheaper version of it. **The anchor draws the pair as visually matched; the page's weight difference need not show.**
- THE VEL TWIN-BLADE HERESY (Ch. 14): the one-handed convention exists to keep the off-hand empty FOR the Art — an internal art frees both hands. Valeria fights with twin blades, both hands full: the doctrine's other defiance, the mirror of Kael's future kit (his art rides the steel, hers rides the flesh); the rhyme is never stated on-page. His endgame dual-dagger silhouette stays distinct and protected (00/03).

## GEOGRAPHY
- **The archipelago:** rich in resources; home to wealthy noble merchant families.
- **The island (Book One):** its differentiator is the TEMPLE — where the empire sends talented young royals and nobles for a few years to be forged into future leaders, generals, Resonants.
- **The village:** small; its lights visible from the mountain at night ("The stars fell down the mountain"). Keeps the WINTER FEAST on the dark of the year — the longest night, lamps everywhere, everyone down at the water till morning. Since the feast night of Ch. 6 it carries a ghost story: a crew of men found dead, no survivors — "It came down off the mountain on the longest night"; wrong in details, true in center, pointing up (the folklore valve — 00; Temple leg spent Ch. 12).
- **Temple geography (Ch. 7):** the Temple stands south along the coast on a headland — visible from the mountain all Kael's life as "a pale shape." The lawful road runs down past the split rock, bends HIGH ABOVE the village (within sight and sound, never through it), then out along the coast's shoulder. Kael commutes daily ("Evening" = the deadline); the daily pass keeps the world at the exact distance of his whole childhood — seen, heard, unentered — and gives the folklore a vector to reach him sideways.
- **The family's home:** isolated, in the mountains, sea approaches watchable. Why THIS island: the master placed the parents here — hiding in the empire's own shadow, inside the blind spot of the man who runs the light.
- **AMMARETH (Ch. 17):** a city in the EAST of the continent, taken by the empire eighteen months before Ch. 17's autumn — roughly two years before the tournament — and held undistributed since. The tournament's prize: revenues, governorship and seat, to the winner's HOUSE, held in the winner's name until he comes of age. Kael's question about who lives in it went unanswered by three children of the empire, none of whom heard it as a question.
- **THE NOBLES' DISTRICT (Ch. 27; author, s32):** the island's walled noble quarter, **a mile up from the water, INLAND** — not on the harbour and not on the Temple headland, so reaching it from the arena means crossing the populated middle of the island on foot. *"It is the only wall on this island that was built to be a wall."* Valeria's standing orders name it as the rally point, which is why she knows where to go and Kael does not. **CONTROLLED TERM: the nobles' DISTRICT** — never *quarter*, never *the walls* as a proper name (`../07-Story-Ledger/established-facts.md`). The gate beat it is owed — Valeria admitted, Kael not — is 00's (§THE NOBLES' DISTRICT, AND THE GATE).
- **THE ROUTE FROM THE ARENA (Ch. 28):** the inland road climbs off the headland between dry-stone walls, and **it goes through the VILLAGE** — so Kael, forbidden that village his whole life and routed high above it twice a day for four years, enters it for the first time on the day it is emptied. A garrison post stood at the mouth of the village lane (Ch. 22) and is gone by the time he reaches it. Past the village the ground rises to the district's flat shoulder; terraces, ditches and olive ground on both sides of the road.
- **THE GATE, AT SCALE (Ch. 28):** one gate on the landward side, and **TENS OF THOUSANDS** in front of it — the crowd has no edge, running back down the road, off both sides and over the shoulder of the hill, with people in it who cannot see the wall. The island is holding a tournament crowd (Ch. 22's count) plus its own people. **Kael's count fails on it** — the first thing in his life it could not return a number for. (The admission *rate* is not on the Ch. 28 page and never was; the s37 note claiming it was outlived its sentence and is retired here.)
- **THE GATE IS SHUT, AND WHY (Ch. 29).** It had been shut for some time before Kael reached it, and it opens exactly once, **the width of one person** — because a gap wide enough for two lets the whole hillside through and the wall falls at noon instead of tonight. That reason is TRUE, it is stated out loud by the judge, and it is correct. The district admits its houses and their households and sworn men under an officer who answers for them; there is no line for anybody else. **The houseless provision exists for the games and there is no such clause for a wall** (`temple.md` §Jurisdiction) — the empire has a provision for a houseless boy's ambition and none for his life. **The road on the far side of the district was closed at the second hour**, so passage through was never available either.
- **THE DISTRICT FROM THE INSIDE (Ch. 31).** One long spine of a street with the houses off it, full without being crowded because the households stay inside their own walls; courtyards deep enough to be turned into hospitals by carrying the tables out. **TWO GATES ONLY** (Ch. 29's *there was no third gate*): the landward gate down the road, and **THE FAR GATE** onto the road closed since the second hour — which is the one the escape party leaves by, at full dark. **THE NORTH SIDE HAS NOTHING ON IT:** the terraces come up and die against the foot of the wall — *no gate, no postern, nothing to come at* — and it is held by four men *because there is nothing there to want.* Kael read that from outside (Ch. 29); Valeria confirmed it from inside (Ch. 31). **NO SINGLE COMMAND (Ch. 31):** every house keeps its own men on its own stretch under its own orders, with nobody over the top, because whoever were chosen would be standing over everybody else's men. Six or seven sets of colours in the street.
- **THE CUT BELOW THE GOAT GROUND (Ch. 31).** On the **far side of the mountain, on the eastern water**: a cut holding a hull that takes about forty, *not on any quay and not in any ledger.* Severin's, since the autumn, and he refuses on the page to say why he has it — it is the hull he found for the family (`../03-Character-Bible/severin.md`), and nothing on any page joins the two. The route to it from the district crosses the mountain, which is where the house is.
- **THE MOUNTAIN HOUSE'S GROUND, ON THE PAGE AT LAST (Ch. 32).** One room, a loft, a fire-hole, a door whose bar goes into stone; outside it the yard, the woodpile, the block, the shed, and the pens going down the slope. **Three ways onto that ground and only one takes numbers:** the path, which is made ground for the last quarter mile and **bends twice**, the second bend having the wall above and the drop below so that two men can walk it abreast and four cannot; the **scree off the north shoulder**, which cannot be come down quietly or in a group; and **up out of the runnel on the east under the olive line**, single file, blind from the yard for the last two hundred strides and putting a man at the corner of the lower pen. The flat between the yard and the upper pen slopes toward the house about one in nine. **This is Kael's, and it is the reason professionals take a thirteen-year-old's dispositions.**
- **THE CONTINENT (author, s55; on the page Ch. 36–37):** the island lies SOUTH of it. The EMPIRE is the continent's WEST, the capital with it; the COALITION's countries are its EAST; Ammareth, in the east, is the empire's furthest reach. From the south coast the road to the capital runs *north and then west along the shoulder of the continent* (Ch. 36) — *east is a month of nothing and then it is theirs.* The rest of the map: TBD.
- **QESHET (Ch. 37):** an imperial-held city on the SOUTH COAST — a native city with a native ruler kept in place under an imperial house name (`factions.md`; `../03-Character-Bible/hazoun-sabrane.md`). A stone mole with masts behind it; a strand a mile off where the hull opened fifty strides out; walls the colour of the ground with the ground's own grain in them, tall bare-stemmed trees with all the green at the top, narrow streets roofed with cloth stretched wall to wall, water running loud in cut-stone channels at the gate and in the governor's court; behind the town nothing — the brown going back and up into haze. A watch of local men in the empire's white and blue. **THE SOUTH IS THE THIRD VISUAL REGISTER** (author, s55 — desert, oasis, markets, trade; Morocco/Egypt-inspired): loose pale cloth wound and hung on men and women alike, heads covered, brown faces, dark eyes, hands doing nothing; a market heard through walls; spice, dung, hot oil, something rotting sweetly, bread; a dark wrinkled fruit sweet enough to hurt; a low table with cushions, wine and water going round. Its healers mend with oils, a needle and strapping and no Art, and the First Master called the hands good. **Page vocabulary:** *the city* and its name, *the governor*, *the watch*; *city-state* and *oasis* are bible words and not the page's.
- **THE TWO VISUAL REGISTERS (author, s33).** The **capital and the imperial houses are Latin-Byzantine** — `naming.md`'s register, and what dress, ornament and arms look like there. **The ISLAND keeps its own material culture**, which is not the empire's and not a lesser version of it — the same fact `naming.md` rules for conquered places (the empire does not rename what it takes; the survival of somebody else's name is a small political fact nobody remarks on), carried by dress too. **Forward rule:** a character reads by WHERE THEY ARE AND WHAT THEY ARE DOING, not by their house — Valeria in training kit on the island reads island; Valeria in Vel colours at the capital reads imperial.

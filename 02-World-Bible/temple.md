## THE TEMPLE (the empire's schools; the island's fell at Ch. 27)
The institution's law is world canon — it is how an imperial school works, and it is what the Temple was for the four years of the page. The chapter-by-chapter history of the games is in the chapter records and `../archive/world-bible-book-one.md`; the day-by-day clock is `../07-Story-Ledger/timeline.md`.

### The three schools
- The empire keeps three great Resonant schools — the TEMPLE in the southern archipelago (the isles' rich merchant-noble houses), **THE COLLEGIUM** in the CAPITAL (named on the page Ch. 22; red and black), and one in the NORTHWEST, name unminted. Three schools, one fence — the pedagogy monopoly runs through all three. Open axis: archipelago money vs. capital blood vs. northwest frontier as student-status geography.

### The Temple's law
- **Head:** SEVERIN — the family's secret patron (00/03). On-page mostly "the master"; public title THE FIRST MASTER.
- **Students:** the empire's golden children — multi-part names, house pride, casual cruelty toward the charity case. Cohorts by age, fixed rooms; the YARD takes the young cohorts together — the one shared arena; war-training scales where classrooms split.
- **THE BOARDS:** twice a year the Temple writes its children in order — midwinter and spring examinations (four days: letters, sums, the chronicle, natural philosophy, a day in the yard), standings nailed to the colonnade of the first court, every name in earned order. The machine that converts a boy's excellence into public standing whether he wants it or not.
- **DECLARATION IN PRACTICE — THE DISH (Ch. 11):** declaration formalized empirically — the iron dish with its half-ring of plain media (candle, water, sand, sprig, copper rod); each student spends ONE grain and the element shows itself; the clerk enters it in the roll; held when a cohort's oldest reach the window ("What settles, settles. The dish only asks early."). Post-ceremony, resonance duels enter the yard: the taught one-handed stance; the three rules — nothing above the shoulders, nothing the healer cannot mend by supper, and the yard-master standing them.
- **Pre-declaration cohorts:** theory, attunement, and COST — nobody hands fire to ten-year-olds; casting lives in the older precincts a young cohort never enters, so magic surrounds a first-year as distance-texture. First resonances (Ch. 9) are candle-bench exercises under the yard-master, taught only after cost doctrine ("before I let any of you spend a grain, you will learn what spending is"); a formless near-nothing at the bench is absorbed by the lie ("someone's bastard, then").
- **JURISDICTION (Ch. 18 — hard):** the Temple is the First Master's; **the tournament is NOT.** The empire holds the games; the three schools convene; the island hosted because Severin asked for it four years out. Entries are made TO THE GAMES, under the games' seal, by men the games appoint, and are lodged in the capital. **THE HOUSELESS PROVISION:** a judge of the games may stand for a competitor who has no house — a small, ancient, twice-a-generation clause written so the empire never loses a boy off a farm whose father cannot write. A First Master can refuse a boy a room and the yard; he cannot refuse him the games, and cannot strike a name off a document that is not his. **There is no such clause for a wall** (`geography.md` §The gate): the empire has a provision for a houseless boy's ambition and none for his life.

### The Grand Tournament — what stays law
- **What it was:** INTER-SCHOOL — the three schools convene, which is why the island filled, why a royal princess attended, and a third reason that is in 00; the raid struck the empire's future officer corps in one building. Announced on-page Ch. 12, two springs out. **THREE FLOORS, registers kept distinct:** public glory · the market (Ch. 17 — where the nobility PRICE the empire's children and fix their standings, three schools in one place, every house that matters in the stands with a list, written once and carried for life) · **a third floor, sealed in 00 and unspent, which the market layer shelters.**
- **THE PRIZE:** the conquered city of AMMARETH — revenues, governorship and seat — to the winner's HOUSE. **THE HOUSELESS PROVISION AND THE PRIZE (author, s25 — door CLOSED):** for a boy with no house **the crown holds it in his name until he has a house to receive it** — he can win a city and be unable to take delivery of it. Consequences: **Halden gets nothing material from a Kael victory**; the entry stays clean; the SURNAME THREAD stays owed — *Kael of what?* has a legal answer, and it is *nowhere to put a city*. **The prize goes to nobody else — not Halden, not the Temple, not Severin (author, s25).** The games died with no winner; no page has awarded the prize to anybody.
- **WHAT THE GAMES MEASURE (author, s26): MARTIAL PROWESS AND NOTHING ELSE** — arms as carried, resonance without limit, one boy left standing. No trial of letters or wits, no scored discipline beside the fighting: the games are the empire's self-portrait, and what it takes its own strength to be is the officer corps it is about to inherit (00 s8). **Unstated on the page:** the Temple's BOARDS already measure the real axis twice a year, and the empire that sailed here to price its children does not read them — 06 §People on the page is served by the ABSENCE. The layer that prices more than swordwork is the nobles' market, and it is not scored.
- **THE RULES ARE LIBERAL — THE FIRST MASTER IS THE ONLY SAFETY, AND THE ARBITER (author, s25/s28).** The games permit **SHARP BLADES** and **FULLY UNLEASHED RESONANCE**; the single restriction is **"NO MORTAL INTENT"** — a clause that can only be weighed afterwards, and that most fighters disregard. **The enforcement is a man, not a rule:** the First Master sits above the sand for the whole of the games and (A) BLOCKS directly fatal attacks with EARTH, (B) MENDS even fatal wounds with NATURE — and beyond being the safety he stops bouts, interrupts attacks and declares victors; judges and heralds run the ceremony (a judge drops his hand to start a bout; the man who ends one is Severin). **Contrast, unstated on the page:** the Temple's own duels are fenced hard and the empire's games have none of that — the school protects its children; the empire posts one old man to catch what falls. He presided over the boy he could not keep out (§Jurisdiction), and nobody on the page remarks on it. Six days of it deepened the never-confirmed second reading of his grey, which stays never-confirmed (`../03-Character-Bible/severin.md`). The chair is why he was six miles away when Neris went into labour, and nobody fetched him (`../03-Character-Bible/severin.md`).
- **THE LEAGUES AND THE BRACKET.** **GOLD — 16, from three schools: 6 TEMPLE / 5 CAPITAL / 5 NORTHWEST**; the host seats six, and the extra seat is a small political fact nobody remarks on; **16 → 8 → SEMIFINALS → FINAL**, no play-in, no byes. **SELECTION:** the autumn entry sheet (Ch. 18) is the ISLAND'S ENTRY LIST in earned order off the Temple's standings; **it is NOT the Gold six** — the six are picked off the MIDWINTER BOARDS, which is how a boy ninth or tenth in autumn stood in the Gold league by spring (Ch. 21). **The autumn sheet is not the bracket.** **AFFILIATION vs RESIDENCE:** **Valeria competed for the CAPITAL**, not the Temple, though resident on the island a year. **TEMPLE SIX:** Lucan Drusane · Kael · Elarine Sedrane · Aurelian Corvane · **MELORAN** (seventeen, a spear; the surviving senior-cohort entrant; took Aurelian out in the round of 16 and sat in the other half of the bracket) · one further name, unminted. **LEDRON** was called home by a northwest house on a night boat, no letter to the First Master, and **TAVRIAN** went in the cold term — **whether his mother's illness was the reason is open**; neither departure is a mystery to be solved, both are the war arriving as an absence (Ch. 21; names in `../07-Story-Ledger/established-facts.md`). **SILVER — the Temple's remaining students against each other**, for a purse of gold and the glory of winning, set against the Gold league's city — the empire's whole class structure is in that gap, and nobody on the page needs to say so.
- **STUDENTS ONLY, AND A HOUSE DOES NOT COME TO AN ISLAND (author, s50).** The visiting schools sent their five competitors and **no other students** — no reserves, no younger cohorts, no schoolmates along to watch: four hundred Temple children and **ten** visitors, all Gold. That did not thin the island or the crowd — houses' parties, retinues, banners, officials, traders and spectators poured in for weeks; the arena seats eleven thousand. But **a student's house is overwhelmingly NOT at the games:** a parent or two travels, the seat and the rest of the name stay on the continent. So the island's dead are the schoolchildren and the parents who came, and the houses themselves survive the raid substantially intact — Corvane is at the capital and at Corvane, Sedrane four hundred miles off. **The survivors are bereaved, not orphaned of a house**, and the machine that disposes of them is untouched.
- **THE CLOCK.** One round a day; the games printed six days and **died on the third**, half the programme unspent — the day-map per chapter is `../07-Story-Ledger/timeline.md`. **THE BELLS ARE HOURS (author, s32):** a bell is an HOUR of the day, not a bout-number — *the third bell* names when a bout is called, never which bout it is on the card; the day's card is BOTH LEAGUES, the Gold round running among the Silver bouts. The page places a bout by its BELL and by what else remains on the card, not by a position in the bracket; no bell equals a bout-number, and no Gold-only running order exists (author, s32). **KAEL'S ROAD: Drusane (round of 16, Ch. 23) → SEDRANE (round of 8, Ch. 26) → VEL (the semifinal, Ch. 27 — never fought).** **THE SUMMIT WAS THE SEMIFINAL (author, s25):** the raid landed there — the empire's pageant cut off inside its own structure, one round short, never crowning anybody; no winner, unresolved for the series (`../07-Story-Ledger/open-threads.md`).

## THE TEMPLE
- **THE THREE SCHOOLS:** the empire keeps three great Resonant schools — the TEMPLE in the southern archipelago (serving the isles' rich merchant-noble houses), one in the CAPITAL — **THE COLLEGIUM** (named on the page Ch. 22; red and black) — and one in the NORTHWEST, whose name is still unminted. Three schools, one fence — the pedagogy monopoly runs through all three. Open axis (undecided): archipelago money vs. capital blood vs. northwest frontier as student-status geography.
- **Head:** SEVERIN — the family's secret patron (00/03). On-page mostly "the master"; public title THE FIRST MASTER.
- **Students:** the empire's golden children — multi-part names, house pride, casual cruelty toward the charity case. Cohorts by age, fixed rooms; the YARD takes the young cohorts together — the one shared arena; war-training scales where classrooms split.
- **THE BOARDS:** twice a year the Temple writes its children in order — midwinter and spring examinations (four days: letters, sums, the chronicle, natural philosophy, a day in the yard), standings nailed to the colonnade of the first court, every name in earned order. The machine that converts Kael's excellence into public standing whether he wants it or not (his record lives in 07).
- **JURISDICTION (Ch. 18 — hard):** the Temple is the First Master's; **the tournament is NOT.** The empire holds the games; the three schools convene; the island hosts because Severin asked for it four years out. Entries are made TO THE GAMES, under the games' seal, by men the games appoint, and are lodged in the capital. **THE HOUSELESS PROVISION:** a judge of the games may stand for a competitor who has no house — a small, ancient, twice-a-generation clause written so the empire never loses a boy off a farm whose father cannot write. Severin can refuse a boy a room and refuse him the yard; he cannot refuse him the games, and he cannot strike a name off a document that is not his.
- **TOURNAMENT STRUCTURE (author ruling, s25 — two leagues, one bracket):**
  - **THE GOLD LEAGUE — 16, drawn from three schools: 6 TEMPLE / 5 CAPITAL / 5 NORTHWEST.** The host school seats six; the two visiting schools send five each, and that extra seat is a small political fact nobody remarks on (Severin asked for these games four years out). **Bracket: 16 → 8 → SEMIFINALS → FINAL.** No play-in, no byes.
  - **SELECTION:** the autumn entry sheet (Ch. 18) is the ISLAND'S ENTRY LIST — everyone entered from here, in earned order off the Temple's standings, Valeria at its head because she is resident for the year. **It is NOT the Gold six.** The six are picked later, off the MIDWINTER BOARDS, which is how a boy who stood ninth or tenth on the autumn sheet is in the Gold league by spring: he climbed over the winter, and two northwest-house boys were called home out of the field while he did it (Ch. 21). Never let a later chapter treat the autumn sheet as the bracket.
  - **SCHOOL AFFILIATION vs RESIDENCE:** **Valeria competes for the CAPITAL**, not the Temple, though she has trained on the island a year — her coaches are the capital's grey and her house answers to the crown. Residence puts her on the island's entry sheet; affiliation puts her in the capital's five.
  - **TEMPLE SIX (canon):** Lucan Drusane · Kael · Elarine Sedrane · Aurelian Corvane · **MELORAN** · one further name (unassigned, available to mint). **MELORAN IS THE SURVIVING SENIOR-COHORT ENTRANT (decided s26, off the page's own arithmetic):** Ch. 18's sheet runs Vel, Corvane, **two of the senior cohort**, Sedrane, then four lines down Kael — so the seniors hold lines 3 and 4, Ledron was line 3 (Ch. 21: *two lines above Sedrane and six above his own*), and line 4 was never named until Ch. 21 named him. He is seventeen, entered, Temple, and good enough that the cohort's presumptive best seeks him out four days a week to be *enormously better* than him. **He is NOT one of Halden's yard pair** — Talrane's second son and the Iscane sit four lines BELOW Kael (Ch. 18), nowhere near the seniors. **AVAILABLE:** a spear, four years of reach on Kael, and an established training relationship with Aurelian — a natural round-of-8 body if the bracket wants one. **The autumn sheet's OTHER senior-cohort name was LEDRON, and he is gone** — a northwest house sent a boat for him in the turn toward spring, with no letter to the First Master (Ch. 21). **TAVRIAN**, a lower-ranked entrant of another northwest house, went in the cold term, on a reason Elarine believed at the time and states without repeating the belief now — **do not resolve whether his mother's illness was the reason.** Two names off the field, and part of how a boy who stood ninth in autumn is in the six by spring. Neither departure is a mystery to be solved; both are the war arriving as an absence.
  - **THE SILVER LEAGUE — the Temple's remaining students against each other.** Temple-only by simple logistics. **HARD RULE, and its scope is STUDENTS ONLY:** the visiting schools send their five competitors and **no other students** — no reserves, no younger cohorts, no schoolmates along to watch. Only the host has its student body present (four hundred, Ch. 18/21), so the competitors' curve is four hundred Temple children and **ten** visitors, every one of whom is in the Gold league. **This does NOT thin the island or the crowd:** houses, retinues, banners, officials, traders and spectators pour in from across the empire for weeks beforehand (Ch. 12's *biggest market this island will ever see*; 04 beat 16), the arena seats eleven thousand, and the village lets rooms it has never had to let. The empire comes; its other two schools just don't bring children who aren't fighting. Oss is here. **Prize: a purse of gold, and the glory of winning** — deliberately set against the Gold league's city, and the whole class structure of the empire is in that gap. Nobody on the page needs to say so.
  - **THE HOUSELESS PROVISION AND THE PRIZE (ruling, s25 — the door is CLOSED, deliberately):** the prize goes to the winner's HOUSE, and the provision has an answer for a boy who has none — **the crown holds it in the winner's name until he has a house to receive it.** He can win a city and be unable to take delivery of it. Consequences: **Halden gets nothing material from a Kael victory** — only what he already said out loud, that a judge who stood for a winner was right about something in front of every house in the empire; the entry stays exactly as clean as 00 s21 requires it to be; and the SURNAME THREAD stays owed rather than closing, because *Kael of what?* now has a legal answer and the answer is *nowhere to put a city*. **Do not route the prize to Halden, to the Temple, or to Severin.**
  - **THE RULES ARE LIBERAL — AND SEVERIN IS THE ONLY SAFETY THERE IS (author ruling, s25).** The games permit **SHARP BLADES** and **FULLY UNLEASHED RESONANCE**. The single stated restriction is **"NO MORTAL INTENT"** — a clause that cannot be weighed (you can only measure what a fighter did, afterwards, once it is done) and that **most fighters disregard**. **THE ENFORCEMENT IS A MAN, NOT A RULE: the FIRST MASTER sits above the sand for the whole of the games and (A) BLOCKS directly fatal attacks with EARTH, and (B) MENDS even fatal wounds with NATURE.** This is the on-page payoff of his sealed dual proficiency (00 s14) and of Ch. 19's *nature is the art of mending* + *the art alone is worth very little* — both halves, at scale, in public, at last. **CONTRAST TO HOLD (never state it):** the Temple's own duels are fenced hard — nothing above the shoulders, nothing the healer cannot mend by supper, Ruthen standing them — while the empire's games have none of that. The school protects its children; the empire posts one old man to catch what falls. **PRE-TESTS SEVERIN'S LIMIT** (00: he believes education can outrun rot) a week before the fire tests it properly, and the drain of six days of this deepens the never-confirmed second reading of his grey. **RULED s26 — THE TOURNAMENT WINS.** The s25 collision required him in two places at once; it is resolved by his chair. He is **above the sand, not at the house**, when Neris goes into labour as the semifinals begin, so the one delivery on this island that needs him happens without him and he has to be fetched (00 s26). The empire's unenforceable rule is what holds the only man who could have helped her six miles away.
  - **WHAT THE GAMES MEASURE — AND WHY (author ruling, s26).** The tournament tests **MARTIAL PROWESS AND NOTHING ELSE**: arms as carried, resonance without limit, one boy left standing. There is no trial of letters, no trial of wits, no scored discipline beside the fighting — because **the games are the empire's self-portrait, and what the empire takes its own strength to be is the officer corps it is about to inherit** (00 s8: the raid strikes that corps in one building). Three schools convene, every house that matters watches with a list, and the single question asked is *who wins a fight.* **CONSEQUENCE — never stated on the page, and the reason the ruling is a gain rather than a subtraction:** the Temple's own BOARDS already measure letters, sums, the chronicle and natural philosophy twice a year and nail the order to a colonnade, so the one instrument that has measured Kael's real axis for four years is a school ledger — and the empire that has sailed here to price its children does not read it. The instrument is wrong and nobody in that arena knows it. **06 §KAEL'S DEFINING AXIS is served by the ABSENCE, not by an event:** the world says the loud wrong thing by measuring only what it worships, and the quiet true thing has to win inside the fight (06: *fights are puzzles*) or not at all. The layer that does price more than swordwork is already canon and is not scored — the nobles' market (Ch. 17: *they are not choosing a champion, they are choosing an ORDER*).
  - **THE SUMMIT IS THE SEMIFINAL (author ruling, s25).** KAEL VS. VALERIA is a **SEMIFINAL**, and the raid lands there. The tournament therefore never reaches its own final and never crowns anybody — the empire's pageant is cut off inside its own structure, one round short. The bracket must be legible to the reader well before it breaks.
- **THE GRAND TOURNAMENT:** INTER-SCHOOL — the three schools convene: why the island fills, why a royal princess attends, and why the harvest screens there (00); the raid strikes the empire's future officer corps in one building. Students compete for a great prize (TBD). Announced on-page Ch. 12: two springs out (~Kael 13; Part IV); the island as host; "You have two years... Spend them well." **THE PRIZE + THE MARKET LAYER (Ch. 17):** the prize is the conquered city of AMMARETH, to the winner's HOUSE. Beneath the pageant, and openly known to anyone whose family writes letters, the tournament is where the nobility PRICE the empire's children and fix their standings — three schools in one place, one week, every house that matters in the stands with a list, written once and carried for life. THREE FLOORS, keep the registers distinct: public glory · the market (cynical-political, spent Ch. 17) · the HARVEST (predatory-institutional, sealed in 00, unspent). The market layer shelters the harvest: the program never had to build a net.

### Declaration in practice
- THE PROFICIENCY CEREMONY — the dish (Ch. 11): declaration formalized empirically — the iron dish with its half-ring of plain media (candle, water, sand, sprig, copper rod); each student spends ONE grain and the element shows itself; the clerk enters it in the roll; held when a cohort's oldest reach the window ("What settles, settles. The dish only asks early."). Post-ceremony, resonance duels enter the yard: the taught one-handed stance; the three rules — nothing above the shoulders, nothing the healer cannot mend by supper, and Ruthen standing them.
- Pre-declaration cohorts (design): the young cohorts learn theory, attunement, and COST — nobody hands fire to ten-year-olds; casting lives in the older precincts Kael's cohort never enters, so magic surrounds him as distance-texture. The tournament's reserve is a POV-access rule, not a world rule: beat 18 = first magic seen close and whole, as spectacle.
- First resonances (Ch. 9): candle-bench exercises under the yard-master, taught only after cost doctrine ("before I let any of you spend a grain, you will learn what spending is"). Kael's public result (sealed): formless near-nothing — the lie self-seals around weakness ("someone's bastard, then"); the declaration's revelation stays protected; under the deep layer his profile is the favored one.

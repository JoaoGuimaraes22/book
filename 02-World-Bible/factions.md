## THE VESSANTINE EMPIRE · THE COALITION · THE PROGRAM

### The Vessantine Empire
- Ruling dynasty: House Vessantine. Once great — generations ago it used its power for good; it has rotted generation by generation into greed, pride, blood, and lust. The powerful rule over the weak.
- Corrupt to the core at the royalty level; good people and good rulers still exist within it (Character Law applies to civilizations).
- Conquered much of the continent; its subjugated nations have suffered for generations. **Its spread is a few hundred years old (Ch. 38, in Neris's mouth):** before it, one tongue — Avestan, the east's — *was spoken by all for thousands of years*; the empire's own is the Vessantine Koine (`lexicon.md`). **The east has gods** — *the gods of the East*, prayed to by Neris before Kael's birth (Ch. 38); unnamed on any page.
- **THE PROTECTION TIERS (author, s25 — keep them distinct).** **(1) The imperial guard** — white and imperial blue; the empire's ordinary armed men, drilled and everywhere, unremarkable to anyone who prices bodies. **(2) The ROYAL GUARD** — a specialised body that accompanies royalty; competent and loyal, chosen and drilled; they die where they stand without needing to discuss it. Dark grey with nothing on it; they arrange themselves across the DISTANCES rather than in front of the body, each already standing where a thing would have to come from. **(3) THE ROYAL-GUARD LINEAGES — VEL, KAETH, SARN** — a **different kind entirely**, not a better grade of the same thing: old houses inherited like weapons, bred and drilled across generations. **They serve the royal LINEAGE, wherever it goes** — a Vel escorting a daughter is ordinary. **The apex: the STRONGEST VEL ALIVE stands over the KING or QUEEN**, always has, and that posting is the entire content of being the best one. The Line is small enough that **its members know each other on sight** — by a walk, at distance, in bad light. **On the page it is a LADDER, not a caste bar (author, s25):** no page says a Vel *cannot* guard a lesser royal, no ordinary escort reads as the Line's best, and the comparison has been kept light — one beat, not a meditation. **CANON (Ch. 22):** the princess's twelve are royal guard and **none of them is Vel** — Valeria would have known any of them across the arena and never turns her head. **They die in the invasion** when imperial security collapses inward around her (04 beat 19), which is why she reaches the boat with two children and no guard at all. **It starts on the mountain (Ch. 32):** four of the twelve are already gone between the district's far gate and the house, along with every other body in that party — twenty-five leave the district and fifteen arrive. **HOW THE LINEAGES ARE RAISED (author, s37):** they remove the first time — years of WATCHING killing, executions mostly, before a child is given anything sharp, so that a Vel has no first one and no number. Full entry and its rendering rules: `../03-Character-Bible/valeria.md` §THE DEATH-EDUCATION.

- **THE SOUTH IS HELD THROUGH NATIVE RULERS KEPT IN PLACE (Ch. 37).** Qeshet's governor is a man of the country under an imperial house name, who keeps the city for the Emperor; his watch are local men in the empire's white and blue who kneel on a beach to the royal name; his household keeps its own form. A governor writes every name of consequence at his table to the capital the same night, by a rider who does not stop — **and the letter of Ch. 37 carries a princess's lie** (`../07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §BOOK TWO). Sheet: `../03-Character-Bible/hazoun-sabrane.md`. **Ch. 39–40:** the governor's own box — drivers talking Ivrit, his own level Koine giving the orders — carried three of his guests east in painted iron inside **a column of fifty**, and he opened its door himself a day out and stayed on that road two months; the page has what he did and nothing of why.
- **QESHET HAS FALLEN, AND ITS GOVERNOR SOLD THE ISLAND FOR IT (author, s73; on the page Ch. 51, in Hazoun Sabrane's own mouth).** In the winter before the raid **one man came to him twice, with nothing in his hands either time**, and wanted **the coast and not the harbour**: the watch standing on the wall on certain nights looking at the water, and nothing about those nights in any letter going north. He gave it. **Three nights, and thirty-one hulls, which nobody told him and which he counted himself.** His reason is not money and is not offered as a defence — *"Because they were going to have Qeshet … What I could arrange was how it was done, and when, and to whom"* — and his own account of what he declined to know is *"I did not ask what was on it. I did not ask what they would do."* **QESHET WAS TAKEN AFTER THE ISLAND, BY THE SAME FORCE**, on a day he does not know, *and every other thing they set has been done on the day they set it.* **Nobody in Ishareth has heard it yet**, so at Ch. 51 the three children are the only people in that country who know the empire has lost its south. He has kept the city **seventeen years** and his father kept it before him, and **the house name was given to that family when the empire took the city** (`naming.md`'s conquest rule, said out loud at last).
- **THE ORIGINAL BORDERS AND THE CONQUESTS (author, s61; on the page Ch. 38).** The empire is not one ground: there is what lies inside its **ORIGINAL BORDERS** — the old core, where the empire *begins* — and the conquests held around it, some of them city-states under native rulers kept in place (above). **SOLVENNA** is the southernmost city of the original borders; **QESHET is a conquered city-state.** The distinction reaches the page in a conquered ruler's own mouth — *The empire begins there* — and **nobody remarks on it.** What the difference means in law, tax, garrison, or a subject's standing is on no page.

- **ISHARETH IS HELD, NOT OWNED, AND THE HOLD IS ONE BRIDGE WIDE (author, s69; on the page Ch. 47).** The same class as Qeshet and Ammareth — a native city the empire holds, with its own wall, its own watch and its own toll gate, and no imperial presence anywhere in it except **the bridge-house at the near end of the only bridge across the river**. Nobody in the empire cares who comes *into* Ishareth; what the empire stands on is the thing you cannot leave the country without walking past. **HAZOUN SABRANE HOLDS THAT BRIDGE AND NOT THAT TOWN** — a governor from four hundred miles west, up that road on his own business, funding what he is buying out of the tolls: *there is one bridge and it is his* is the whole of his holdings there, not a boast about the difficulty. **The city has two silences of the same shape** (Ch. 47–48): the talk goes round the bridge, and it goes round the honest men, and nobody notices himself doing either.
- **THE EMPIRE IS AT WAR WITH NALAR AND THE BRIDGE CARRIES TRAFFIC BOTH WAYS (Ch. 47).** Nalar is the country on the far bank (`geography.md`, `naming.md`). Kael names the contradiction out loud — *"We are at war with Nalar." / "Then why is there a bridge."* — **and has not got the answer to it**, and no page offers one.
- **THE ROT, IN TWO COMMONERS' MOUTHS (Ch. 53).** **AMMARETH FROM THE INSIDE:** the empire *took the top off it two years ago* — the old top gone, a new one put on, *nothing else needed to change*, and the wells and the market where they had been; then the men came to every house that had carried anything for the old lord, *because a man who has carried a cup might carry something else* (Selis). **A HOUSE OF THE ORIGINAL BORDERS SENT TO THE WALL FOR ITS LAND:** *a cousin of the Emperor* wanted it, asked *by being told no and waiting*, and in the end *a charge was found* — the head and his sons to the wall, the land where it was always going, the household sold with the furniture (Ambros, who names neither the house nor the cousin). **The south's native governors, again:** a governor's son takes a ribbon, and the stallholder's husband is hanging from the gate by evening (Tirzah). Told at a fire with the Emperor's daughter sitting at it under a false name, and nobody knows.

### THE HONEST MEN — the crew (Ch. 48)
**What this faction IS and how it works. Who its people are: `../03-Character-Bible/crew.md` and `borren.md`. What it has done, chapter by chapter: `../07-Story-Ledger/chapter-records.md`; the pre-s76 chronicle is `../archive/world-bible-book-one.md`.**
- **A BYWORD, NOT A NAME — AND IT IS A FAIRY TALE (author, s72).** What people call them, in Ivrit and the Koine alike, **because they are supposed never to have broken into anywhere in their lives**: everything they have ever taken walked out of a gate in daylight with the paper in order and the man on the gate holding the door for it. *It is a joke. It stopped being funny about six years ago and people still say it.* **THE TRUTH IS THAT THEY BREAK IN, AND ARE EXTREMELY GOOD AT IT.** **The reputation is the security, not the method:** a crew the whole continent agrees never breaks into anywhere is a crew nobody looks for when a thing has been broken into, and six years of being told where they were not is the best cover money cannot buy. **Nobody on any page says this.**
- **THE METHOD.** Cover rather than force: they arrive inside something the gate has already decided to let through, and the best version of it is to walk in the front carrying the thing the mark wants most. Papers, a seal, a bank, boats bought early. **When they do take a room they contain it and leave everybody alive** — hands and mouths, nobody shouting, four seconds.
- **HOW THEY MEET A STRANGER.** The house anyone can find is a fake-out with two men on the door and nothing behind them worth two men. A caller is answered by men bored of a rehearsed answer, then relieved of his steel, searched, hooded and walked somewhere else — **turns doubled so the same stretch of loose board is crossed twice, distances made worthless, then a stretch by water.** The real place is a working concern with a small room off it holding paper, wax and seals and nothing anybody could be arrested for.
- **THE ONE RULE, AND IT BINDS EVERYBODY INCLUDING THE LEADER:** nobody who knows how a thing is going to be done leaves until it is done. *It is not about you. It has never once been about anybody.* **On the road it is extended to the whole journey** — *nobody leaves, not at the first town, not at the worst one.*
- **HOW THEY PRICE.** **They do not leave bodies:** *a body is a hunt, and a hunt has a name in it by the third week* — a mark who must account to men he owes is worth more alive and against a wall. **A face is not a danger until somebody pays for it:** *a description is not worth the paper until somebody wants it enough to pay for it.* Work is paid in shares, and **a man on a job who is owed nothing is a man with nothing under his feet**, so nobody is allowed to forfeit his whole share.
- **FIFTEEN OF THEM, AND NOT ONE LOOKS LIKE ANYTHING** — a man of about sixty in a good coat; two who are plainly brothers and plainly not of this country; a woman of about thirty with a basket, the sharpest questioner in any room; four who are of Ishareth and could not be anything else. **The leader is the one member who can be described at all**, against a whole building of people who could have been anybody, **and nobody on any page remarks on it.** They stop a day's work without one word about the cost.
- **HOW THEY TRAVEL.** By water before first light where there is water; a landing where a track comes down; **mules nobody says the ownership of**; then tracks that go where the road does not. **The camp is a fold that cannot be seen from anywhere a man would stand**, and it makes itself one pair of hands at a time without anybody telling anybody anything: canvas, the animals to water and then a line, **fires small and dug down with flat stones on the side they came from**, and everyone eating at once out of one pot with *nobody counting anything onto anybody's plate.*
- **THEY ASK WHAT YOU WANT AND WHAT YOU HAVE GOT, AND NEVER WHO YOU ARE** — and the not-asking is the crew's, one face at a time, not just the leader's. What a person is stays unsaid even when the leader has worked it out.
- **THE TRIO ARE INSIDE, ON THE CREW'S TERMS.** Passage to Solvenna for all three: Kael's hands for any work the road asks, the two girls with the camp, their bread out of his share and the remainder his; consent asked of all three; obedience priced to his face — *on my road, being right is not a rank* — with the admission that some evenings the leader will be wrong and Kael will do it anyway. **Thirteen months, eighteen people.**
- **THE TRADE, AS IT IS TAUGHT AND DONE (Ch. 54).** Watching first, for *the thing under the count* — when guards change, who looks back at a bend, how far a man walks from his wagons to piss. **One wagon out of a convoy, never the convoy**, at the halt, with the tarpaulin retied. **The seller's walk** (weight in the front of the foot, hands never empty, eyes on hands not faces; *sellers are not going anywhere, they are already there*); **the voice** (the Koine worn down until it has a town in it, said *as though you were bored of it*); **a lock** opened with wet twine worked under the shackle; **the drunk who is drunk on purpose** — not the drunkest — priced by how often he looks at the door. **A guarded crossing is gone through in daylight, paying the toll and arguing about it**, because six men with nothing to do talk about what did not come past them; the noise on the bridge is a woman with a basket. A shipment goes out of an inn yard by the front gate under a seal made off the lord's own wax. Documents are misplaced for somebody who paid, and supplies moved between provinces that did not want them moved. Nobody on the crew is told what a drop holds, and the take goes to Borren's hand.
- **They are 04's crew** (`../04-Outline.md` §Book Two's shape, beat 4) — the beat's recorded collision is resolved there: the crew line continues (author, s74).

### The Coalition
- Recent alliance of the continent's remaining and conquered forces, formed to resist the empire.
- Founding grievances: subjugation, exploitation — and one more, unrevealed (00).
- Carries out the raid on the island at the end of Book One. To Kael, at first, THEY are the enemy — his first truth, written in fire; the saga slowly rotates the compass.
- Contains good and desperate people; commits atrocities. Neither side of the war is clean.
- **ON THE PAGE IT IS *A RESISTANCE*, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT HAS BEEN SAID (Ch. 51).** Asked *"Who's* they*?"*, the governor of Qeshet answers *"They, is what that man represented. A resistance."* — **no composition, no countries, no names, no structure.** What it is made of is on no page. **THE GREAT WAR IS NAMED, AND A CREW LEADER DOES NOT DISAGREE:** *this continent was preparing for a great war, greater than any of the past … Your island was the first spark. Qeshet was the second. The whole continent will burn* — said with *"Ask your bandit friends. They know as well as I do"*, and **Kael looks at the man from the rope-walk and he does not disagree or disavow the governor**, which is the only corroboration the page offers. **THEY WORK BY ARRANGEMENT AND THEY KEEP THE DATE** (above): a conquered ruler was approached, not conquered, and the thing was set for after the island.
- **THE COALITION'S OWN ATROCITY, IN A COALITION MOUTH (Ch. 53).** Oran, of a valley *across the water, a long way up it*: ahead of the empire's column *our own people came. Not the empire. Ours* — the grain, the animals, every man who could walk (the twins at fourteen), and fire put into the rest so the column would find nothing and have to go back down; *That was the sum. It was a good sum.* Their mother would not leave and was left in the house when the fire went in, *because the sum did not have her in it.* The two of them walked off from the column in the second week. **No name for who *ours* is** — no country, no leader; *a resistance* is still the whole of the page's word for the thing.
- **MOSTLY FIRE RESONANTS (author, s39).** The coalition's Resonants skew heavily to FIRE. Recorded as given; **why a coalition of many nations skews one element is an OPEN QUESTION** (`open-questions.md`), unexplained on the page and uninvented here.
- **THE COLUMN THAT COMES UP THE MOUNTAIN IS ORDINARY MEN (author, s42; amended s46).** The force that follows the escape party to the house is not Resonant; the Art on that ground is the five's alone (Ch. 33 — §FIVE GENERALS below). Not a contradiction of the skew above — that was said of a force that took a whole island, and this is one column of it. Nobody on the page remarks on it.
- **FIVE GENERALS (author, s39) — NAMED ON THE PAGE FROM CH. 33 (author, s43), AND NOTHING ELSE ABOUT THEM IS.** The coalition fields five, and **all five stand in the mountain yard at Ch. 33** because a princess of the empire is standing in it (`../00-Author-Canon-SEALED.md` §True causality). **PELLAN · DUNMAR · THIRO · FENNA · GALLOS.** **WHAT THE PAGE HAS OF THEM:** the names arrive **in their own mouths**, in the downhill language, in Kael's hearing — **the word *general* appears on no page of Book One**, no rank is given, the number five is never stated, and no structure, command relation or seniority is ever drawn. Kael reads five bodies and never once a command. **They carry ONE NAME EACH and no house** — against an empire whose children carry three names and a house — and nobody on any page remarks on it. **PELLAN DIES at Ch. 33** (Vask kills him); the other four survive the island. Sheet: `../03-Character-Bible/coalition-generals.md`. Sealed material: `../00-Author-Canon-SEALED.md` §THE COALITION'S GENERAL.
- **THEIR FIRE IS FIVE DIFFERENT ARTS (author, s43), and no page theorises any of them.** **PELLAN** — spent at the point and moment of contact, off knee, heel and knuckle, the instant the only warning (Ch. 30/33). **GALLOS** — mass: standing sheets the width of open ground, thrown casually, the coalition's reputation made flesh. **DUNMAR** — fire put INTO ground rather than onto anything: he does not burn men, he makes ground nobody can cross again, and he takes terrain by subtraction. **FENNA** — a finger-thick line carried out of one hand and drawn across things; she divides a working rather than opposing it, and she is the only one of them built the way Kael is built. **THIRO** — a volume of air, at ten feet, with no visible part to it at all: men reach for their own armour and then cannot breathe, and there is no mark on them afterwards. **His counter is the one Valeria found and it is the only one there is:** he cannot spend it on air he is standing in the middle of.

### The Program (mostly unrevealed — 00)
- On-page in Book One: fragments only — a forbidden-language word in a raider's mouth, and the mother's face when she understands what has come back. **THE INSIGNIA IS DEAD (author, s48).** *An insignia the father recognizes with horror* lost its father at Ch. 33 and is **struck from Book One**: it is not reassigned to Neris and it is not held in reserve. The word and the mother's face do the same work with one instrument instead of two, and Vask is dead, so that fragment is **hers or nobody's**. The object is free for a later book. Ch. 18's tail and Ch. 24's guard are unchanged — both still carry nothing that means anything.
- Reader's takeaway at the end of Book One: the parents thought something was dead, and it isn't.
- **HALDEN (author, s38):** what the judge is beyond his warrant is in 00 — not read at open or draft time. On the page: his imperial standing is real, not a costume; he is genuinely strong, body and Art, and that is unremarked (`../06-Style-Sheet.md` §Never explain); nothing about him is explained on any page of Book One. Sheet: `../03-Character-Bible/halden.md`.

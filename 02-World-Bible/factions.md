## THE VESSANTINE EMPIRE · THE COALITION · THE PROGRAM

### The Vessantine Empire
- Ruling dynasty: House Vessantine. Once great — generations ago it used its power for good; it has rotted generation by generation into greed, pride, blood, and lust. The powerful rule over the weak.
- Corrupt to the core at the royalty level; good people and good rulers still exist within it (Character Law applies to civilizations).
- Conquered much of the continent; its subjugated nations have suffered for generations. **Its spread is a few hundred years old (Ch. 38, in Neris's mouth):** before it, one tongue — Avestan, the east's — *was spoken by all for thousands of years*; the empire's own is the Vessantine Koine (`lexicon.md`). **The east has gods** — *the gods of the East*, prayed to by Neris before Kael's birth (Ch. 38); unnamed on any page.
- **THE PROTECTION TIERS (author, s25 — keep them distinct).** **(1) The imperial guard** — white and imperial blue; the empire's ordinary armed men, drilled and everywhere, unremarkable to anyone who prices bodies. **(2) The ROYAL GUARD** — a specialised body that accompanies royalty; competent and loyal, chosen and drilled; they die where they stand without needing to discuss it. Dark grey with nothing on it; they arrange themselves across the DISTANCES rather than in front of the body, each already standing where a thing would have to come from. **(3) THE ROYAL-GUARD LINEAGES — VEL, KAETH, SARN** — a **different kind entirely**, not a better grade of the same thing: old houses inherited like weapons, bred and drilled across generations. **They serve the royal LINEAGE, wherever it goes** — a Vel escorting a daughter is ordinary. **The apex: the STRONGEST VEL ALIVE stands over the KING or QUEEN**, always has, and that posting is the entire content of being the best one. The Line is small enough that **its members know each other on sight** — by a walk, at distance, in bad light. **On the page it is a LADDER, not a caste bar (author, s25):** no page says a Vel *cannot* guard a lesser royal, no ordinary escort reads as the Line's best, and the comparison has been kept light — one beat, not a meditation. **CANON (Ch. 22):** the princess's twelve are royal guard and **none of them is Vel** — Valeria would have known any of them across the arena and never turns her head. **They die in the invasion** when imperial security collapses inward around her (04 beat 19), which is why she reaches the boat with two children and no guard at all. **It starts on the mountain (Ch. 32):** four of the twelve are already gone between the district's far gate and the house, along with every other body in that party — twenty-five leave the district and fifteen arrive. **HOW THE LINEAGES ARE RAISED (author, s37):** they remove the first time — years of WATCHING killing, executions mostly, before a child is given anything sharp, so that a Vel has no first one and no number. Full entry and its rendering rules: `../03-Character-Bible/valeria.md` §THE DEATH-EDUCATION.

- **THE SOUTH IS HELD THROUGH NATIVE RULERS KEPT IN PLACE (Ch. 37).** Qeshet's governor is a man of the country under an imperial house name, who keeps the city for the Emperor; his watch are local men in the empire's white and blue who kneel on a beach to the royal name; his household keeps its own form. A governor writes every name of consequence at his table to the capital the same night, by a rider who does not stop — **and the letter of Ch. 37 carries a princess's lie** (`../07-Story-Ledger/standing-rules.md` §BOOK TWO). Sheet: `../03-Character-Bible/hazoun-sabrane.md`. **Ch. 39–40:** the governor's own box — drivers talking Ivrit, his own level Koine giving the orders — carried three of his guests east in painted iron inside **a column of fifty**, and he opened its door himself a day out and stayed on that road two months; the page has what he did and nothing of why.
- **THE ORIGINAL BORDERS AND THE CONQUESTS (author, s61; on the page Ch. 38).** The empire is not one ground: there is what lies inside its **ORIGINAL BORDERS** — the old core, where the empire *begins* — and the conquests held around it, some of them city-states under native rulers kept in place (above). **SOLVENNA** is the southernmost city of the original borders; **QESHET is a conquered city-state.** The distinction reaches the page in a conquered ruler's own mouth — *The empire begins there* — and **nobody remarks on it.** What the difference means in law, tax, garrison, or a subject's standing is on no page.

- **ISHARETH IS HELD, NOT OWNED, AND THE HOLD IS ONE BRIDGE WIDE (author, s69; on the page Ch. 47).** The same class as Qeshet and Ammareth — a native city the empire holds, with its own wall, its own watch and its own toll gate, and no imperial presence anywhere in it except **the bridge-house at the near end of the only bridge across the river**. Nobody in the empire cares who comes *into* Ishareth; what the empire stands on is the thing you cannot leave the country without walking past. **HAZOUN SABRANE HOLDS THAT BRIDGE AND NOT THAT TOWN** — a governor from four hundred miles west, up that road on his own business, funding what he is buying out of the tolls: *there is one bridge and it is his* is the whole of his holdings there, not a boast about the difficulty. **The city has two silences of the same shape** (Ch. 47–48): the talk goes round the bridge, and it goes round the honest men, and nobody notices himself doing either.
- **THE EMPIRE IS AT WAR WITH NALAR AND THE BRIDGE CARRIES TRAFFIC BOTH WAYS (Ch. 47).** Nalar is the country on the far bank (`geography.md`, `naming.md`). Kael names the contradiction out loud — *"We are at war with Nalar." / "Then why is there a bridge."* — **and has not got the answer to it**, and no page offers one.

### THE HONEST MEN (Ch. 48 — the crew)
- **A BYWORD, NOT A NAME — AND IT IS A FAIRY TALE (author, s72).** What people call them, in Ivrit and in the Koine alike, **because they are supposed never to have broken into anywhere in their lives**: everything they have ever taken walked out of a gate in daylight with the paper in order and the man on the gate holding the door for it. *It is a joke. It stopped being funny about six years ago and people still say it.* **THE TRUTH IS THAT THEY BREAK IN, AND ARE EXTREMELY GOOD AT IT** (Ch. 50). **The reputation is the security, not the method:** a crew the whole continent agrees never breaks into anywhere is a crew nobody looks for when a thing has been broken into, and six years of being told where they were not is the best cover money cannot buy. **Nobody on any page says this**, and Kael — who assembled them at Ch. 48 out of three facts — was right about *why* they were in Ishareth and wrong about *how* they work, and **has never been corrected by anybody.**
- **WHAT THE PAGE HAS OF THEM.** A house on the upstream side of Ishareth taken since the turn of the month, with two men on the door day and night who carry nothing and watch nothing; a load of copper gone out of a locked shed with the lock still on it and the owner's mark still on the door, which the watch came and looked at and went away from; and **every boat on that bank refusing money at any price for a month**. And the Solvenna robbery two years ago that nobody has explained — **a whole marble statue**, out of a yard two hundred men stood round for a month, on a cart with the owner's own seal on it. **They are not passing through.**
- **NOBODY OF THEIRS IS ON THE PAGE and their leader is unnamed** (BORREN reserved, `naming.md`). **Kael used a small version of their entire method at Ishareth's gate nine days earlier — arriving inside something the gate had already decided to let through — and no page says so.**
- **MET AT CH. 49, AND THE TRADE IS STRUCK. THE INFERENCE WAS RIGHT AND NOBODY EVER SAID SO.**
- **THE HOUSE ON THE UPSTREAM SIDE IS A FAKE-OUT.** Two on the door of a room with bedding for two, one pot, ash raked flat and no rubbish in the corners — nothing behind them worth two men. Anyone who comes up that street and says *bridge-house* is answered by men who have been sitting on a rehearsed answer long enough to be bored of it, then relieved of his steel, searched, hooded and walked somewhere else: turns doubled back so the same three strides of loose board are crossed twice, then twelve minutes upriver by boat with a bank close on the left. **The real place is a working ROPE-WALK** — a long low building open to the river on posts, with men actually making rope in it and a small room off it holding paper, wax and seals and nothing anybody could be arrested for.
- **FIFTEEN OF THEM, AND NOT ONE LOOKS LIKE ANYTHING.** A man of about sixty in a good coat; two who are plainly brothers and plainly not of this country; **a woman of about thirty with a basket**, greeted by more of them than anybody else and the sharpest questioner in the room; four who are of Ishareth and could not be anything else. They stop the day's work in that rope-walk without one word about the cost of it.
- **THE LEADER IS ON THE PAGE AND IS STILL UNNAMED** (BORREN reserved, `naming.md`). Past fifty, thin, grey, going bald from the front, with a close grey beard, and **a plain oval of dark leather over the right eye on a cord that goes up under the hair** (author, s72) — the rest of the face *the sort you are given directions by*. **He is the one member of that crew who can be described**, against a whole building of people who *could have been anybody at all*, and nobody on any page remarks on it; a pen in his hand and a sheet he finishes before he looks up. He asks **what do you want** and **what have you got**, and never once **who are you**. He overrules a correct objection from his own man without arguing it (*Go and get on with the rope*), clears the room for a boy, hands a child a knife, and laughs once, genuinely, at an answer he did not expect. **His limit is on the page inside twenty words:** he asks about Valeria in front of Valeria in the tone of a man asking about a mule, is answered by her, and never looks at her again. He tells two children he will find out for himself why they cannot be seen.
- **THE ONE RULE, AND IT BINDS EVERYBODY:** nobody who knows how a thing is going to be done leaves the building until it is done — the leader and the woman with the basket included. *It is not about you. It has never once been about anybody.*
- **THEIR PLAN WAS COMPLETE FOR A MONTH EXCEPT FOR ONE HOLE.** Papers, a seal, a bank, and **the boats bought before the turn of the month at a price he does not enjoy thinking about** (which is why no hull on that bank would take money — Ch. 48). What was missing was the inside of the bridge-house, and it walked up the street and asked for a knife. **Kael did not bring them a job; he filled the hole in theirs.**
- **THE TERMS (Ch. 49):** Kael gives sixty-two days of the governor's arrangements and two Resonants' hands; **the princess is held out of the trade entirely** and the crew are told so; the price is **one part in ten of what comes out of that building**, plus **Kael inside it when the door opens.** He is offered a fifth and does not take it. **The job is set for the night after the meeting.**
- **HOW THEY DID IT (Ch. 50) — THEY WALKED IN THE FRONT CARRYING THE THING HE WANTED MOST.** Kael's own recommendation: say you have found the ones the governor is hunting, and put two of them in irons. **The children are the paper.** Plain iron with no black in the grooves, so that if it comes apart the two of them can use the Art; **every weapon in the room walks in on the boy**, in the five places the crew themselves search a man; the captors are searched at the gate and are clean, and nobody touches the merchandise. Then five guards contained in four seconds, **choked out and left alive** — hands and mouths, nobody shouting, nobody killed — the leader taking the governor with nothing in either hand. **The chest walks out of the gate in the open as the reward for the boy**, in front of fifty men who watched two children walk in, and nobody says a word about it.
- **THE ONE RULE HELD ON THE NIGHT.** The leader went out with the job and was in the room; so was the woman with the basket, who carried her basket up a stair, through a search and into that room, and **nobody once asked her about it.**
- **HE CONCEDES TO A CHILD AND DOES NOT ASK WHY.** Twice now: the room cleared over a correct objection (Ch. 49), and the governor left conscious over another correct one (*"He can shout." / "Then see that he cannot."*), with three prepared reasons never asked for. **Unexplained on the page both times.**
- **WHY THEY ARE THERE IS KAEL'S INFERENCE AND NOBODY HAS CONFIRMED IT:** there is one reason to be in that city this month, and it is the chest at the bridge-house being counted onto a table three days a week out of the river. They are 04's crew (§Book Two's shape, beat 4); everything else about them — rules, size, leader, whether they take the trade — is open.

### The Coalition
- Recent alliance of the continent's remaining and conquered forces, formed to resist the empire.
- Founding grievances: subjugation, exploitation — and one more, unrevealed (00).
- Carries out the raid on the island at the end of Book One. To Kael, at first, THEY are the enemy — his first truth, written in fire; the saga slowly rotates the compass.
- Contains good and desperate people; commits atrocities. Neither side of the war is clean.
- **MOSTLY FIRE RESONANTS (author, s39).** The coalition's Resonants skew heavily to FIRE. Recorded as given; **why a coalition of many nations skews one element is an OPEN QUESTION** (`open-questions.md`), unexplained on the page and uninvented here.
- **THE COLUMN THAT COMES UP THE MOUNTAIN IS ORDINARY MEN (author, s42; amended s46).** The force that follows the escape party to the house is not Resonant; the Art on that ground is the five's alone (Ch. 33 — §FIVE GENERALS below). Not a contradiction of the skew above — that was said of a force that took a whole island, and this is one column of it. Nobody on the page remarks on it.
- **FIVE GENERALS (author, s39) — NAMED ON THE PAGE FROM CH. 33 (author, s43), AND NOTHING ELSE ABOUT THEM IS.** The coalition fields five, and **all five stand in the mountain yard at Ch. 33** because a princess of the empire is standing in it (`../00-Author-Canon-SEALED.md` §True causality). **PELLAN · DUNMAR · THIRO · FENNA · GALLOS.** **WHAT THE PAGE HAS OF THEM:** the names arrive **in their own mouths**, in the downhill language, in Kael's hearing — **the word *general* appears on no page of Book One**, no rank is given, the number five is never stated, and no structure, command relation or seniority is ever drawn. Kael reads five bodies and never once a command. **They carry ONE NAME EACH and no house** — against an empire whose children carry three names and a house — and nobody on any page remarks on it. **PELLAN DIES at Ch. 33** (Vask kills him); the other four survive the island. Sheet: `../03-Character-Bible/coalition-generals.md`. Sealed material: `../00-Author-Canon-SEALED.md` §THE COALITION'S GENERAL.
- **THEIR FIRE IS FIVE DIFFERENT ARTS (author, s43), and no page theorises any of them.** **PELLAN** — spent at the point and moment of contact, off knee, heel and knuckle, the instant the only warning (Ch. 30/33). **GALLOS** — mass: standing sheets the width of open ground, thrown casually, the coalition's reputation made flesh. **DUNMAR** — fire put INTO ground rather than onto anything: he does not burn men, he makes ground nobody can cross again, and he takes terrain by subtraction. **FENNA** — a finger-thick line carried out of one hand and drawn across things; she divides a working rather than opposing it, and she is the only one of them built the way Kael is built. **THIRO** — a volume of air, at ten feet, with no visible part to it at all: men reach for their own armour and then cannot breathe, and there is no mark on them afterwards. **His counter is the one Valeria found and it is the only one there is:** he cannot spend it on air he is standing in the middle of.

### The Program (mostly unrevealed — 00)
- On-page in Book One: fragments only — a forbidden-language word in a raider's mouth, and the mother's face when she understands what has come back. **THE INSIGNIA IS DEAD (author, s48).** *An insignia the father recognizes with horror* lost its father at Ch. 33 and is **struck from Book One**: it is not reassigned to Neris and it is not held in reserve. The word and the mother's face do the same work with one instrument instead of two, and Vask is dead, so that fragment is **hers or nobody's**. The object is free for a later book. Ch. 18's tail and Ch. 24's guard are unchanged — both still carry nothing that means anything.
- Reader's takeaway at the end of Book One: the parents thought something was dead, and it isn't.
- **HALDEN (author, s38):** what the judge is beyond his warrant is in 00 — not read at open or draft time. On the page: his imperial standing is real, not a costume; he is genuinely strong, body and Art, and that is unremarked (`../06-Style-Sheet.md` §Staging); nothing about him is explained on any page of Book One. Sheet: `../03-Character-Bible/halden.md`.

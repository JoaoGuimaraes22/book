## The Maturing Voice
- Early childhood: short sentences, concrete senses, literal child-logic. The village lights are "the stars that fell down the mountain."
- Middle childhood: questions sharpen; sentences lengthen; pattern-noticing.
- Adolescence+: complexity, irony, self-awareness.
- **THE BLOOMING.** From Part II on, his wits bloom visibly as he grows — quicker in dialogue, drier and faster in thought, pattern-jumps arriving sooner. The gradient includes intelligence made audible, not just sentence complexity.

## The Voice — the instruments
**THE POSITIVE HALF OF THE RULE.** §Model-Tic and §Things to Avoid say what not to do; this says what the prose DOES, named so that drafting reaches for it on purpose instead of arriving as competent fantasy narration that then has to be stripped back. Every example below is on the page. **These are instruments, not a checklist: over-use of any one of them is itself a tic**, and a paragraph built out of three at once is louder than the voice.
- **THE FLAT DECLARATIVE THAT REFUSES ITS OWN DRAMA.** The biggest beats land in the shortest, plainest sentence available, and nothing follows. *He was paid.* · *Nothing after it.* · *Then it is short.* The sentence does not rise to meet the moment; the moment is left larger than the sentence, and the reader does the rest.
- **DESCRIPTION BY ABSENCE.** The book characterises by what is not there — *not broken into, not forced, gone* · *not one of them looked like anything* · *neither of them was doing anything at all* · *He had never once seen anybody else keep it.* A negative observation is the default descriptive move; reach for it before an adjective.
- **THE NUMBER WHERE THE ADJECTIVE GOES.** Sizes, counts, prices, distances, minutes — *nine times the money* · *one word in four*. This is the narration's unit of measure, not a quirk of Kael's to be pointed at: **the count doing work is voice; the count mentioned is a tic** (§What the pass cuts).
- **THE UNATTRIBUTED COLLECTIVE.** Bodies of people act with no agent named — *one pair of hands at a time, without anybody telling anybody anything* · *Nobody had argued with her about anything all day* · *The fire moved. Nobody said anything.* It is how the book renders a crew, a village, a yard, a crowd.
- **THE GNOMIC PRESENT INSIDE PAST NARRATION.** The narration steps into a general present for a rule of the world and steps back out — *It came the way things come in a yard, which is sideways and in pieces* · *when a street is at its fullest* · *A knot wants two hands.* The telling-frame briefly audible (§POV). Sparing; the author's ear governs the rate.
- **DIALOGUE STRIPPED TO THE EXCHANGE.** Tagless, adverbless runs wherever the speakers are obvious — *"Thieves." / "No," said Zahir. / "What, then."* The social fact is carried by what is said, and is never glossed afterwards.
- **THE REACTION RENDERED AS NON-REACTION.** Where a beat expects a response, give the body continuing — *Kael's hands went on doing what they were doing* — or name the absence and stop: *The part of him that would have argued about it did not say one word. / He noticed that it had not.*
- **REPETITION AS RHYTHM, NOT EMPHASIS.** A clause returned to with one term changed — *That is what they do. That is the whole of what they do.* — where another book would reach for an intensifier.
- **THE SCENE ENDS ON THE LINE.** Chapters and sections stop on the last spoken or observed thing, with nothing added to land it: *"I want to talk to somebody about the bridge-house."*

## Model-Tic Self-Awareness
- Claude has default rhythms it falls into when not watching itself: standalone em-dashes, "not X but Y" constructions, sentence fragments deployed for gravity, triadic lists, AND-CHAINS ("and x and y because z" accumulations), default similes ("the way…" / "like"). None are banned — the book's voice uses all of them — but every instance must be a CHOICE, not a default. While drafting, Claude self-flags passages where its own patterns are steering the sentence instead of the voice; revision passes report per-chapter tic counts beside the word count: dashes, not-buts, fragments, triads, and/1k, similes/1k, and **narration words + words-per-paragraph** (`wc -w` is the budget instrument, not a reading-experience one — a dialogue-heavy chapter carries far less prose bulk per word; check narration bulk before calling a chapter long, and never cut fast dialogue to protect a total). AND-chains and simile density are awareness instruments, not thresholds. **Known default: first drafts run 51–55 and/1k uniformly across every section** — check and/1k per section at the end of every first draft, not per chapter. Upward drift without intent = revision flag. The test is always: would this sentence survive being read aloud in the author's voice, or does it sound like a model being writerly?

## What the pass cuts
**THE SUBTRACTIVE STANDARD, TAKEN FROM THE AUTHOR'S OWN PASSES** — `60ec1a2` (Ch. 53), `19d271d` and `00ab234` (Ch. 50), `0feffb5` (Ch. 48) — and named in the s70 record: *every author pass cut in the same direction, narrated Kael-conclusions out and the observable action left.* These were being made by hand every session; making them while drafting is the whole point of writing them down. **Judgement, not a checklist** — a heavy beat may argue for keeping one, the way a §Model-Tic item is argued. Struck lines below are unquoted, in italics, because they are not page canon.
- **THE GLOSS.** A stated fact is not followed by its meaning. **If the second sentence says what the first one meant, the second sentence is the cut.** Ch. 53: *Nothing after it. There was nothing after it in that city and there was nothing after it here, and the one full name he had ever owned was in a letter four hundred miles west and was not going to be said at a fire.* → **the page keeps** *Nothing after it.*
- **THE CALLBACK.** Narration that reaches back to an earlier chapter to supply weight, parallel or irony is cut to a clause or to nothing. **Write the moment so it lands on its own**; the reader who remembers is paid without being told they remembered. Ch. 53's pass cut every one of them — the Temple corridor, the governor's clerk, the rope-walk, *Valeria Vel* said to a clerk.
- **THE INSTRUMENT NAMED FOR FLAVOUR.** Kael's count, his pricing and his people-reading appear only when they do work in the scene. Struck: *He had the count on the oars. He let it run and did nothing with it.*
- **THE INVENTORY.** Lists restating possessions or facts the reader is already holding. Struck: *the sacking under his head with the iron in it, the bag inside his coat, the canvas a foot above his face.*
- **THE CLEVER DEFERRAL AT THE PEAK.** When the thing the character most wants is on the table, **he asks for the thing itself.** Technique-flex at the emotional peak is the cut: Ch. 50's *There were fifty men on that road. How many are left.* — built on Kael's own rule, *you ask the thing beside it* — was replaced by the thing itself, *"The people who were with us in Qeshet," he said. "Where are they?"*, because the rule is not what that moment is about.
- **THE RITUAL ECHO.** A line answered by its own restatement, for ceremony. Struck: *I am telling you so that it has been said. / It has been said, she said.*
- **THE COY WITHHOLDING IN DIALOGUE.** A speaker declining to say a thing in order to be interesting. Ch. 53: *West of Qeshet, three days, a town you have not heard of and I am not going to say* → **the page keeps** *Three days west of Qeshet.*

## Genre Register
- Epic, gritty, historical-flavored fantasy. Intimate over spectacular. Earned over destined.

## Things to Avoid
- Info-dumps; POV breaks; telling the reader what to feel; characters as plot functions (Character Law); hidden-puppet-master vibes for the leader — he stays human, limited, doctrine-driven.

## Author taste notes (calibration, not rules)
- **Interiority is the seasoning, scene is the meal.** Kael's deep head is the story's depth — keep it — but favor dialogue and real-time action over narrated summary whenever both can carry the beat. A lot of descriptive/interior text over action and dialogue reads boring to the author.
- **"Telling the reader what to feel" is a FLAG, not a verdict** (author ruling, session 19). The §Things to Avoid entry points at prose that substitutes an emotion-label for the scene that would have earned it. It does not ban the naked statement of feeling: a line that names what a character feels, in his own register, at a moment the book has already paid for, is allowed and is sometimes the right line. Weigh each instance; never auto-fail one on the rule's name alone.
- **The causal chain stops at the first why.** The child's-logic chains earned their density in Ch. 1–3; from Part II, links that re-derive established canon ("the why of the why") get cut — trust the plant. Not a flag system; an ear.

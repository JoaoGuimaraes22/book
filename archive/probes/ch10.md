## Q: Based only on these chapters, summarize what you KNOW and what you SUSPECT about: (a) Kael's parents' past, (b) Kael's own abilities and nature, (c) the master's motives. Separate knowledge from suspicion and cite the evidence.

### Kael’s Parents’ Past

#### 1. What is KNOWN
*   **Vask’s Combat Marks:** Kael's father, Vask, has deep scars on his body: a long pale seam under his left shoulder, a round puckered scar going through his torso (with a matching one on his chest), and forearm scars resembling tally marks [1]. He told Kael these were caused by a **"bad employer"** whom he left [2].
*   **Vask's Advanced Martial Skill:** Vask possesses unnatural speed and reflexes. He straightens tools without looking, catches a slate falling off a roof mid-storm, and catches Kael mid-fall [3-5]. He trains Kael in weapon crafting, the "grammar of falling" [6, 7], and the "standing game" of physical leverage [8, 9].
*   **Neris’s Magical Mastery & Origins:** Kael’s mother, Neris, is highly proficient in a subtle form of magic [10-12]. She was **born in no noble house** mentioned in any Temple registry [13]. 
*   **Extreme Vigilance and Seclusion:** The parents live in tight, watchful seclusion. Neris counts passing boats morning, dusk, and even at night, especially after a strange sail passes [14-16]. Now, she stands two watches a night [17]. When a boat approaches, they play a drill-like "game" where they completely mask their presence to blend into the mountain [18-20]. 
*   **A Hidden Weapon and Language:** Neris possesses a long, thin blade "older than the tools on the wall" which she keeps hidden and watches the sea with at night [21]. Kael has also overheard his parents arguing in a sharp, edged language different from the four common ones she taught him [22]. They use a flat, synchronized "third voice" when talking to the Master [23, 24].
*   **The Master’s Smuggling and Coordination:** The First Master has smuggled books and paper to Kael's parents for years, carefully coordinating his visits so Kael would be sent away up the mountain and remain unseen [25].
*   **The Settlement Deaths:** Two winters ago, Kael went down the mountain and tried to free a tied girl [26, 27]. He was beaten by a crew of men [28] until a sudden "quiet the size of several men" fell [29], after which his father picked him up [30]. Kael later learns from schoolyard gossip that **every man in that crew died that night with no marks of a fight in the yard** [31, 32].

#### 2. What is SUSPECTED
*   **Fugitive or Political Exile Status:** Their obsessive watchfulness [17], Vask's severe combat wounds from a "bad employer" [1, 2], Neris's secret sword [21], and their drill-like hiding game [18, 19] strongly indicate they are **fugitives or deserters** on the run from a powerful entity, likely the Empire. 
*   **Vask's Execution of the Crew:** Since Kael remembers the sudden "quiet the size of several men" immediately preceding his father's arrival [29, 33] and the rumors state the entire crew was killed silently with "not a mark of a fight" [31], we suspect Vask was an **elite imperial assassin or soldier** who killed the entire crew to protect Kael and keep their location secret.

### Kael's Own Abilities and Nature

#### 1. What is KNOWN
*   **Inherent Feeling of the *Minima*:** Kael does not struggle to find the carrying forces of the world (what the Temple calls the *minima*) [34, 35]. He can naturally feel where water pails carry weight, where wind pushes, and where a fire is "lazy" [35, 36]. 
*   **Controlled, Precise Magic:** Kael can manipulate magic with extreme, quiet precision. He untied the Master’s complex silk knot "before his fingers did" [37, 38], and under his mother's instruction, he made a candle flame lean exactly a hair's width by "spending a thread" [12]. He successfully replicated this in the Temple yard [39].
*   **Exceptional Physical Leverage and Reflexes:** Kael is a master of leverage. He moved a massive stone alone at age eight using a pole and log [8], moved the heavy yard roller alone [40], and easily defeated the larger Lucan Drusane in a spar by using Lucan's own momentum and a "two fingers of push" at his ankle [41]. He can also run scree in the pitch dark [42].
*   **A Stoic, Hyper-Observant Mind:** Kael is incredibly observant, reading physical "tells" in people and rooms [43, 44]. He stores his memories and observations in highly organized mental "drawers" [45-47] and maintains a perfect "stone face" to mask his internal state [48-50].

#### 2. What is SUSPECTED
*   **A Non-Standard, Forbidden Power:** While the Temple's mages brute-force their magic by "pushing" [51, 52], Kael's magic relies on "feeling first" and "spending small" [12, 39]. His style has "no name" and is in "no language at all" to onlookers [53]. This, combined with Oss's legend about a terrifying entity that "isn't a mage" whose eyes "go red as a coal" when it works [54], suggests Kael's magic shares a root with a **highly feared, legendary phenomenon** that operates completely outside the Empire's "tidy fences" [55, 56].

### The Master's Motives

#### 1. What is KNOWN
*   **Covert Assistance to Kael's Family:** The First Master has spent years smuggling books and paper to Kael's parents [25].
*   **A Shielding Cover Story:** After testing Kael with the silk knot [37, 57], the Master sponsored Kael at the Temple as a "charity case" [58, 59]. He explicitly told Kael to wear this false identity like a **"coat"** so that nobody would look too closely or investigate his true origins [60, 61].
*   **Valuing Intellectual Humility:** He specifically tells Kael to hold onto his capacity to say **"I don't know,"** calling it the only thing he is worried Kael will lose at the Temple [62].

#### 2. What is SUSPECTED
*   **Hiding a Dangerous Asset in Plain Sight:** The Master is likely protecting Kael's family out of a deep personal loyalty or a shared past with Vask and Neris. By smuggling Kael into the Temple under the guise of a poor "charity case," the Master is likely **shielding Kael from Imperial mages** who would otherwise hunt him or recognize his mother's forbidden lineage, while allowing Kael's formidable intellect and abilities to quietly develop [58, 60, 61].

### What I Expect and WANT to Happen Next

*   **What I Expect:**
    Kael is going to double down on **wearing "the coat" of his false identity** [4]. Now that he has formally named his mental drawer **"what being seen costs"** [5], he will deliberately hold himself back to avoid drawing any attention [4]. I expect him to continue playing the role of the clumsy "charity case" [4, 6], purposefully flubbing yard drills and classroom questions [7] while quietly practicing his mother's hyper-efficient "small spending" magic in absolute secret [4, 8]. 
    
    Meanwhile, **Lucan Drusane's silent math problem is going to boil over** [9]. The author notes that Lucan is watching Kael like a sum that refuses to yield its total [9]. Kael’s complete refusal to retaliate after the sums slate was fouled [10] or to react to the school's hostility is actually driving his bullies insane because they aren't getting the reaction they want [11]. 

*   **What I WANT:**
    I desperately want to see the fallout of **Elarine Sedrane’s mist experiment** [12]. During their brief walk under the colonnade, Kael gave her a brilliant, elegant methodology to test the "tidy fences" of the mages' elements: putting the same kettle over a water-wright's and an air-wright's workings, keeping everything else the same, and standing the mists side-by-side to ask what differs [12]. If Elarine actually runs this experiment, she is going to realize that Kael's understanding of the *minima* is lightyears ahead of the Temple's rigid doctrines [12-14]. I want them to form a silent, hyper-intellectual alliance. 
    
    I also want Kael to start quietly searching for **the girl from the cartwheel** [15]. The schoolyard ghost story completely erased her because "a girl loose was no use to a story about no one left" [16]. But Kael carries her memory—and her silent choice not to cry out and betray him [15]—like an open wound [16]. I want her to still be alive, perhaps hiding under her own "coat" somewhere on the island.

---

### What I FEAR the Author Might Do Wrong

*   **Failing Elarine Sedrane's Autonomy:**
    I swear, if the author turns Elarine into a generic love interest or a standard rival who exists just to get intellectually outmaneuvered by Kael, I'm going to be so disappointed. Her character is incredibly compelling because she is entirely self-contained; her face **"gives out nothing it hasn't priced first"** [17, 18]. She represents the absolute pinnacle of the Temple's logical "fences" [18], and she needs to remain a brilliant, independent force who challenges Kael on equal footing.

*   **Rushing the "Red Eyes" Lore:**
    My absolute biggest fear is that the author will rush Kael's connection to the terrifying myth Oss described: the "thing that isn't a mage" whose **eyes go "red as a coal" when it works**, leaving whole houses or crews dead with doors and walls meaning nothing [19]. The temptation in modern fantasy is to have the protagonist unleash this massive, destructive, "dark lord" power the second a bully pushes him too far. 
    
    If Kael suddenly goes red-eyed and slaughters Lucan or Aurelian in the yard, it would completely betray the beautiful, slow-burn philosophy of the book. The core theme of Kael's maternal training is that **"the ones who spend big learn what things cost. The ones who spend small learn what things are"** [20]. Kael's strength lies in finding the "lazy place"—the single, load-bearing loop of a problem—and gently slipping the thread [15, 21]. If he wins his battles through brute-force magical violence rather than this quiet, leverage-based efficiency, the novel will lose its soul.

*   **An Off-Screen Tragedy Up the Mountain:**
    I am terrified that the author will kill off Vask and Neris off-screen to force Kael into a standard, isolated "orphan protagonist" trope. The relationship between Kael and his parents is the emotional anchor of the entire book. Those beautiful, quiet evening scenes where they sit by the fire and "trade the day back and forth" [22-24] are too precious to lose.

## Q: What confused you or felt unresolved in the most recent chapter? List anything that felt like a mistake, a contradiction, or a gun that should have fired.

**Now that we finally have Chapter 10 in our hands, I am absolutely screaming.** The way the author resolved Kael's candle trial—proving that his mother’s "small spending" method works flawlessly even on the Temple's rigid tallow in the yard, and doing it so quietly that the proof was his alone [1, 2]—was brilliant. 

But oh my god, Chapter 10 did not ease the narrative tension at all. Instead, it left several massive, loaded Chekhov’s guns sitting on the mantle, along with some incredibly painful unresolved threads. Here is what is keeping me awake at night on the forums:

### 1. Elarine Sedrane's Silent Kettle Experiment
In Chapter 9, Elarine wrote exactly **five words** on her slate and hid them like a coin [3]. In Chapter 10, she finally confronts Kael under the colonnade, asking how he would make the mist "say" whose element it is [4]. Kael gives her a beautiful, scientifically elegant trial: put the same kettle over a water-wright's and an air-wright's workings, change nothing else, and stand the mists side-by-side to ask what differs [4]. She leaves without a word, and the author explicitly notes: **"What she did with it, he never saw"** [4]. 

*   **Why it's unresolved:** This is the ultimate intellectual tease! Did she run the experiment? If she did, she now possesses proof that the Temple's "tidy fences" are arbitrary [4, 5]. The fact that we don't get to see her reaction or what those original five words on her slate were [3] is a massive, lingering question mark. She is holding a piece of the puzzle, and we are just waiting for her to lay it down.

### 2. The Erased Girl (The Most Painful "Missing" Thread)
When Kael listens to the kitchen boy's rumor about the "dead crew" from two winters ago, the story claims **"no one [was] left alive... Not one"** [6]. Kael sits there, screaming inside to ask the single question he has carried for a year and a half: **"was there not a girl"** [7, 8]. 

Because asking would blow his cover, Kael forces himself to "go on being a bench" and stays silent [8]. 

*   **The Unresolved Gun:** Where is the tied girl from the cartwheel whom Kael tried to free [9]? Did "the line hold" [7, 10]? The fact that the schoolyard myth completely erased her because "a girl loose was no use to a story about no one left" [7] is a heartbreaking narrative choice. It means she is still out there, entirely unaccounted for, and Kael has no way to look for her without exposing his identity.

### 3. The Cracked Waster is Still on the Rack
Back in Chapter 9, Kael detected a deep crack in a practice sword rigged by Lucan Drusane [11]. Instead of breaking it or throwing it away, Kael put it back in the rack **"crack-up, so the next boy's hands would have a fair chance of finding it"** [11]. 

*   **Why it feels unresolved:** In Chapter 10, we learn that Lucan has done "nothing further" [12]. He is just watching Kael like "a sum that will not come right" [12]. But that compromised, cracked wooden sword is *still sitting in the yard racks* [11]. In a martial yard where students are actively sparring, leaving a rigged weapon on the shelf is a massive hazard. The author has set up a literal physical break waiting to happen, and the fact that no one has picked it up and had it shatter mid-fight yet feels like a major delayed payoff.

### 4. The "Hole" in Kael's Telling to His Parents
When Kael returns up the mountain at the end of Chapter 10, his mother asks about his day [13]. Kael deliberately gives her a version of the day that **"had no story in it"** [13]. He actively hides the fact that the village is gossiping about the dead crew and pointing their fingers up the mountain [13, 14]. Kael hears the "hole" in his own telling and feels a deep sense of relief when she doesn't seem to notice [13].

*   **The Contradiction/Tension:** We know Neris stands **two watches a night now** instead of one [15]. We know his parents are hyper-vigilant [15, 16] and "trade the day back and forth" downstairs [17]. It feels almost impossible that a mother who can read Kael well enough to cup his head "the way you hold something you are carrying across ice" [18] would completely miss the massive, terrifying "hole" in his daily report [13]. Either Kael's stone-face has become unnaturally perfect [19, 20], or his parents are quietly letting him keep his secrets while they prepare for the inevitable day his "coat" gets torn off [21].

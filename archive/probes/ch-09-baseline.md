## Q: Based only on these chapters, summarize what you KNOW and what you SUSPECT about: (a) Kael's parents' past, (b) Kael's own abilities and nature, (c) the master's motives. Separate knowledge from suspicion and cite the evidence.

### Kael's Parents' Past

#### 1. What is KNOWN
*   **Vask’s Combat History and Scars:** Kael's father, Vask, has deep physical scars: a long pale seam under his left shoulder, a round puckered scar going entirely through his torso, and numerous short, slanted forearm scars resembling tallies [1]. Vask attributes these marks to a **"bad employer"** whom he ultimately left [2].
*   **Neris’s Origins and Magic:** Kael's mother, Neris, is highly adept in magic, but she was born in **"no house any book of the Temple’s had ever heard of"** [3]. Despite this, she understands the "carrying" forces of the world and has systematically trained Kael in these methods [4-6].
*   **High-Level Vigilance:** The parents live in extreme, watchful seclusion. Neris counts passing boats morning, dusk, and night [7-10]. When a non-fishing boat approaches the island, they play "the game," using advanced physical discipline to completely blend into the mountain’s terrain and remain unseen [11-14].
*   **A Hidden Language and Weapon:** Kael overhears his parents arguing in a sharp, edged language that is not one of the four languages Neris taught him [15]. Furthermore, Neris possesses a concealed, long, thin blade "older than the tools on the wall" which she holds unsheathed on her knees in the dark while watching the sea [16].
*   **Connection to the First Master:** The Temple's First Master has secretly smuggled books and paper to them for years [17]. His visits were deliberately coordinated with Kael’s parents to occur while Kael was sent high up the mountain, ensuring Kael did not see him [17, 18].

#### 2. What is SUSPECTED
*   **Fugitive or Political Exile Status:** Their intense fear of unrecognized boats, the drill-like "game" of hiding, Vask's severe combat wounds, and Neris's secret sword strongly suggest they are **fugitives or military desertes** hiding from a powerful force (likely the Empire or Vask's former "employer") [1, 2, 10, 14, 16].
*   **Forbidden or Lost Magical Lineage:** Since Neris possesses immense magical control but belongs to no recognized house [3], she may belong to a **forbidden, foreign, or unregistered magical bloodline**, which explains why Temple students gossip that Kael is "someone's bastard" [19].

### Kael's Own Abilities and Nature

#### 1. What is KNOWN
*   **Inherent Perception of the *Minima*:** Kael can naturally feel the "carrying" forces of the world (what the Temple calls the *minima*), sensing where water pails carry weight, where wind pushes, and where a fire is "lazy" [5, 20, 21].
*   **Controlled Manipulation of Magic:** Kael can manipulate magic with extreme precision. He untied the Master’s complex, deceptive silk knot **"before his fingers did"** [4, 22], and under his mother's guidance, he successfully made a candle flame lean the precise width of a hair on command [23].
*   **Exceptional Physical Leverage and Reflexes:** Trained by Vask, Kael possesses unnatural physical timing and reflexes, such as running scree in the pitch dark [24]. He has mastered "the standing game," allowing him to use a stone's or a person's own weight against them, which he used to easily throw the larger student Lucan Drusane in a spar [25, 26].
*   **Hyper-Observant and Unreadable:** Kael naturally analyzes patterns, stores observations in mental "drawers," and can maintain a perfect "stone face" to completely mask his internal emotional and physical state [27-30].

#### 2. What is SUSPECTED
*   **He Possesses a Non-Standard, Rare Power:** Unlike the Temple's mages who brute-force their magic by "pushing" [6, 31], Kael's magic is based on feeling, precision, and finding "lazy places" [6, 20]. This suggests his magic operates entirely outside the Empire's "tidy fences" [32, 33].
*   **Potential Connection to Dangerous Lore:** Kael mentally associates his internal magic with Oss’s ghost story about things that "are not a mage" whose eyes "go red as a coal" when they work, leaving entire rooms of dead men behind [33, 34]. This implies Kael's abilities might share a root with this highly feared, legendary phenomenon.

### The Master's Motives

#### 1. What is KNOWN
*   **Complicity in Hiding Kael's Family:** The First Master has actively helped Kael’s parents maintain their isolation by smuggling materials and timing his visits so Kael would not see him [17].
*   **Sponsorship and Cover Story:** He brought the deceptive silk knot to test Kael's innate abilities [22, 35]. Once he confirmed Kael could "untie things" [36], he arranged for Kael to enter the Temple under his personal sponsorship as a "charity case," explicitly ordering Kael to wear this false background like a "coat" so nobody would investigate him [37-39].
*   **Value of Intellectual Humility:** He specifically instructs Kael to hold onto his capacity to say **"I don't know,"** calling it the only thing he is worried Kael will lose at the Temple [40].

#### 2. What is SUSPECTED
*   **Protecting a Powerful Asset:** The Master is likely protecting Kael's family out of a deep personal loyalty or a shared past with Vask and Neris [18]. 
*   **Hiding Kael in Plain Sight:** By placing Kael in the Temple as a sponsored "charity case" [37, 39] and drilling him to play a simple herder's boy [38], the Master is likely **shielding Kael from Imperial scrutiny** while allowing his unique physical and magical talents to develop in an environment where he can observe "people" [41].

*   **The "Overpowered Magic School" Trap:**

    The mystery of Vask's severe combat scars from a "bad employer" [24, 25] and Neris's hidden, ancient blade [26] is so delicious because it is rationed [27]. I fear the author might info-dump their past too quickly. Let us guess! Let us search for clues in the way Vask's "grammar of falling" [28] feels like a soldier's elite training, or how Neris's unnamed school [29] feels like a lost, forbidden art from outside the Empire [30].

## Q: What confused you or felt unresolved in the most recent chapter? List anything that felt like a mistake, a contradiction, or a gun that should have fired.

Oh, I am so glad you asked this, because **Chapter 9 left me with so much narrative whiplash and a list of unanswered questions** that I’ve been frantically scribbling in my own mental ledger. The author is playing an incredibly long game, but they’ve left some massive, loaded shotguns sitting on the mantle. 

Here is everything that felt unresolved, confusing, or like a Chekhov’s gun waiting to go off in Chapter 9:

### 1. The Cracked Waster (The Literal Gun Left on the Rack)
This is the most immediate, physical "gun" that should have fired. Lucan Drusane tries to sabotage Kael by rigging a wooden practice sword with a cracked core hidden under tight cord whipping [1]. Kael’s hyper-sensitive hands (trained by his father’s obsessive wood-balancing [1, 2]) detect the crack instantly. 

Instead of exposing Lucan or throwing the broken sword away, Kael spends the spar fighting left-handed to protect it, wins anyway, and then **puts the cracked waster back on the rack "crack-up, so the next boy's hands would have a fair chance of finding it"** [1]. 

*   **Why it's unresolved:** Who took that waster next? Did Lucan end up picking up his own rigged weapon in a later class? Did it shatter in the hands of some innocent kid like Oss? Leaving a structurally compromised weapon in an elite martial training yard is a massive safety hazard, and the fact that we transitioned to the slate incident [3] without seeing the fallout of that cracked wood feels like a major thread left hanging.

### 2. The Sums Slate (The Delayed Retaliation)
Lucan Drusane's escalation is getting incredibly ugly. Wiping and fouling Kael's entire term's worth of math sums—replacing them with "a smeared obscenity"—is a devastating academic blow [3]. Because Kael has no witnesses and knows the Temple masters will never take a sponsored "charity case's" word over a lord's son, Kael silently accepts the ruined mark, which drags his standing down [3].

*   **What felt unresolved:** We are taught from Chapter 3 that Kael is a master of leverage—he knows how to let an opponent’s own weight and momentum destroy them [4, 5]. Yet, he does *absolutely nothing* to retaliate here. He just files this academic sabotage into **"the drawer with no name on it yet, getting heavy"** [6]. The psychological tension is almost unbearable. The author is stretching the bowstring back as far as it will go, but we are desperately waiting for Kael to let the arrow fly. 

### 3. Elarine Sedrane's Five Words (The Ultimate Brain-Teaser)
After Kael completely derails Master Priscian's lecture on the rigid "tidy fences" of the elements by asking **"whose is the mist?"** [7, 8], Elarine Sedrane is the only cohort who goes entirely still [9]. Kael watches her from the door as she writes **exactly five words** on her slate, then immediately covers them with her hand "the way you put your hand over a coin on a table" [10].

*   **What it means/unresolved:** What did she write?! Did she write the name of a forbidden, mixed magical art? Did she write a realization about Kael's unorthodox style? Or did she write a warning? The mystery of those five hidden words is a massive, tantalizing cliffhanger that I need answered immediately.

### 4. The Aborted Yard Trial (The Missing Scene)
At the very end of the chapter, after Kael successfully makes his mother’s candle lean a hair’s width at the supper table using her ultra-efficient, whisper-quiet "small spending" method [11, 12], he formulates a brilliant scientific experiment. He decides to go into the Temple yard the next morning and test his mother's "long feeling first" and "small ask" methods **on the Temple's own rigid tallow candles** to see how the magic reacts under a controlled variable [13]. He falls asleep mapping out this trial [13, 14].

*   **Why it felt unresolved:** The chapter ends right there! We are completely denied the payoff of his experiment. Does the Temple's candle obey his quiet "carrying" magic? Does Master Ruthen notice a prickle in the air? The author builds the entire mechanical setup for an incredible magical breakthrough and then cuts to black.

### 5. A Point of Confusion: The "Bastard" Rumor
During the candle trial, when Kael's flame doesn't physically move, some of the noble students sneer: *"What did the mountain expect... Somebody's blood in that line somewhere. Someone's bastard, then"* [15]. 

*   **Why this is confusing at first:** Kael entered the school under the false story of being a simple herder's boy [16, 17]. How did they jump so quickly from "herder" to "high-born bastard"? 
*   **The Resolution:** When you look closely at the lore, it actually makes perfect sense. The Temple teaches that **"the art runs in the blood, and the blood runs in the houses"** [18]. Since Kael has no noble house [17, 19] but was sponsored by the First Master [17], the cohorts' rigid doctrine literally *forces* them to assume he must be the illegitimate, unregistered child of some noble lord, because their world-view cannot grasp a common mountain boy possessing innate magical sensitivity [15].

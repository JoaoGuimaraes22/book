# Chapter Fifty — The Reward

On the second evening a man came up the length of the walk with a sack on his shoulder and put it down on the table.

There was iron in it. Two pairs, and a short chain for each. They had been made that week: the file marks were still bright along the edges, and the sack smelled of a forge.

Kael picked a pair up. It weighed about what the other had weighed.

"There is a pin," the man said. "It pulls with two fingers. It does not pull with one, and it will not come out by itself. You do not touch it until somebody tells you to."

"Who tells me."

"You will not have to ask."

Valeria had not picked hers up.

"There is no black in them," she said.

"No."

"His have black in them."

"So the boy told us." The man pushed the second pair across the boards toward her with the back of his hand. "There is none of that in this city we could ask for without saying why we wanted it. And we would not put it on you if there were. If it comes apart in that room you two are the only ones getting out of it, and you will not do that in iron that works."

Valeria picked the pair up, turned it over once, and put it down again.

"Very well," she said.

---

The woman with the basket did the weapons.

She had them out on a cloth on the ground, five of them, and went round him with them one at a time.

"Right boot. That is for the one who will have your right arm, and he will have it high, up by the shoulder, so his hand comes down past your hip and not your knee." She worked it down inside the boot along the seam and pressed the leather flat over it with her thumb. "Walk."

He walked twenty strides down the boards and came back.

"Again. Slower. You have been on a road and you have not eaten."

He did it again, slower.

The second went in the other boot. The third lay along the inside of his left forearm under the sleeve, handle at the wrist and point up toward the elbow, tied there with two turns of thread that would break if a man pulled on it. The fourth sat flat in the small of his back under the belt with the coat down over it.

The fifth she put inside the front of the coat, high, under the arm.

"That one is mine. I will be on your left and I will be talking to somebody."

She stood back and looked at him from about four strides, then walked round him once.

"Turn round. Now sit down on the boards and get up again."

He sat down and got up again.

"Once more without using your hands, because your hands are going to be in front of you."

He did it without using his hands.

"Good," she said, and then, to Valeria: "And you."

"No."

"You are certain."

"Yes."

The woman looked at her for a moment, folded the cloth, and did not ask her again.

---

"Say it," said the man at the table.

He had the sheet in front of him and had been writing on it since before the iron came up the walk. He did not look up for any of it.

The one who was going to do the talking said it, in the Koine.

"We have two of the three he is looking for. The third got away from us west of here five days ago, and we will take the work of bringing her in."

Then he looked at Kael.

"Take the five days out," Kael said. "And the west."

"It is true."

"It does not matter that it is true. He is going to ask you where, and what day, and how many of you there were, and how a girl got past all of them. Every one of those wants an answer, and the answers have to agree with each other for as long as he keeps asking." He had his sleeve back and was looking at the thread on his forearm. "You do not tell him how you got us. You tell him what you want."

"So what is it I say."

"We have two of them. We want the price on these two, and we want the work of getting the third."

"And nothing after that."

"Nothing after that. If he asks, you answer him in four words and you look like a man who would rather be sitting down. He does not have to like you. He has to want the thing you are standing next to, and he has wanted it for two months."

The man said it again without the five days and the west.

The man at the table turned his sheet over and went on writing.

---

They did the iron last. Out past the posts the river had gone the colour the sky was, and there was nothing else left to do with the day.

"Wrists."

Kael put his hands out. Valeria took the pair off the table before the man got to it.

"I will do his."

The man gave her the pin and went and stood by the door.

She opened the band, put it round his right wrist and closed it. It sat down on the ridge of skin that had not gone and was not going to go. She pushed the pin home with her thumb.

"Too tight."

"No." He turned the wrist over and looked at it. "I hate these things."

"Yes."

"If you have any ideas besides this I'd love to hear them."

Her thumb stopped on the pin for about half a second.

"I do not," she said.

She started the left one, working it over the back of his hand without once touching the back of his hand, which took her a while.

"His were heavier," he said.

"They were the same."

"They were heavier."

"Very well. They were heavier."

Then she held her own arms out.

He had one hand that was any use, so he was slow. She stood and let him be slow, and nobody in that building looked at either of them while it was going on.

"Tighter," she said.

"It does not need to be tighter."

"It needs to look like it was done by somebody who did not care."

He did it again, tighter.

"Better."

When it was done the chain hung between his wrists, swung, and touched itself twice.

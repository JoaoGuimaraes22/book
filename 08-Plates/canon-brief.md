# Canon brief — paste at the top of any prompting conversation

The prompts are written where `00`, `02` and `03` cannot be seen. Every canon error of s33 came from that one blind spot and none from craft. This is the fix.

**This file is a SUMMARY, not canon.** `00`, `02` and `03` govern; if this disagrees with a sheet, the sheet wins and this file is the bug. Re-read it against them whenever appearance canon moves.

---

## Cross-cutting — true of every image

- **The world has exactly two body-marks of magic: RED EYES** (the Kynemir bloodline) **and WHITE HAIR** (drain — the mark of having gone too far down). **White hair is DATA, never styling.** It says this person went to the bottom of their reserve and nearly died, and it never reverses. Do not whiten a head for drama, age or contrast.
- **Aeliana's silver is a VESSANTINE HOUSE TRAIT and is NOT the drain-mark.** The drain-mark's register is **pure quartz white** — cold and hard. Her silver is **warm and bright.** Keep them visibly distinct.
- **Colour lives in the eyes.** Everything else muted and earthy. Saturated eyes win on saturation; **Kael's grey cannot and wins on value instead** — the lightest point in the face.
- **Two visual registers.** The **island** keeps its own material culture; the **capital and imperial houses** are Latin-Byzantine. A character reads by where they are and what they are doing, not by their house.
- **NEVER DESIGN "THE COAT."** It is the book's central metaphor for Kael's concealment — Ch. 13 is titled for it — not a garment to style. Literalising it flattens the image.
- The empire's honourable arms are **longsword, rapier, spear**, bought from paid fencing-masters and carried as posture.
- **Book One ages: Kael 13 · Valeria 13 · Aeliana 14.** She reads one year ABOVE the other two, never below.

## KAEL

- **A handsome boy, and filthy with it. He is NOT plain** — and *plain* does not render anyway. Village dirt, weather, hair cut by somebody who was not being careful.
- **NOT the ornate royal register.** That is Aeliana's and is a different order of thing.
- Dark hair, his mother's. Lean, hard, visibly fit; **strong without bulk**, the strength sitting in the forearms and the hands.
- **What makes him invisible is STATUS and STILLNESS, not his face** — no house, no name, working cloth, a body trained not to move.
- **EYES PALE GREY — and UNSPENT ON THE PAGE.** An anchor or portrait may show them. **No published plate may show his eyes until a chapter spends the grey.** Frame them out.
- **Marks:** blade scar on the **LEFT forearm** (Ch. 6, echoes his father's tally-marks) · long shallow scar on the **outside of the LEFT arm above the elbow** (Ch. 23) · burn on the **LEFT forearm, wrist to elbow** (Ch. 24).
- **He is armed.** Two short blades carried **across his back in a cloth wrap**, hilts covered, reading as an anonymous bundle rather than weapons. A knife carried **off the belt, inside the clothing**, not visible. They came out of a shed and answer to no school and no seller — plain, unmatched, one heavier than the other, grips wound by an amateur.
- **Clothing: village working cloth** — undyed, coarse, frayed, worn. **Never well-made kit "chosen to go unnoticed"** — he owns nothing worth looking at, and that phrasing literalises the coat.

## VALERIA VEL

- Lean, strong, athletic — **a working build that reads as function even at rest.**
- **Long black hair worn loose, well past the shoulders, no ornament.**
- **EMERALD GREEN eyes** — the colour is spent at **reading distance only**, so it lands in close framing.
- **Her stillness WARNS — nothing like Kael's.** In a close-up it lives entirely in the gaze: level, flat, appraising. *Unimpressed and faintly tired. Not vulnerable, not wistful, not searching.*
- Knuckles older than her face; guard-lineage calluses; old pale scar lines on both forearms with a few fresher.
- **TWIN BLADES, BOTH HANDS FULL.** This is her defining trait and the empire's one-handed convention is precisely what she defies. **Never one sword.** A matched pair, plain, well-made, well-maintained.
- **The capital's grey** — disciplined, uniform-adjacent, austere. Her Line does not decorate; the plainness IS the station. (A bronze collar insignia was adopted s33 as canon-by-precedent.)
- She must stay **distinct from Kael at thumbnail size**: hers long, his short; her blades in hand, his bundled on his back; her provenance eleven generations of service, his a shed.

## AELIANA CASSIVARA VESSANTINE

- **Unmatched, radiant, royal beauty** — the most beautiful person in the book, **never in competition with anyone for it.** The plain cast exists to frame her.
- **Not tall.** Slight, sheltered, soft — **a body that has never worked.** No muscle, no weathering.
- **LONG SILVER-WHITE hair — Vessantine house trait, NOT a drain-mark.** Colourless silver, **never blonde, no yellow or gold.** The lightest mass in the picture.
- **PALE BLUE eyes.**
- **Hair LOOSE by default. UP off the neck at the tournament** (Ch. 22).
- **NO ARMOUR IN BOOK ONE.** She is a symbol, not a fighter. Court dress only.
- **The Ch. 22 gown:** a blue so dark it reads black until the light moves on it; **gold at the shoulder AND the hem, in a quantity with no function except to be a quantity** — the excess itself is the point.
- **The Ch. 24 audience:** she is **SEATED in a chair**, in **a lighter dress with less of it** — not the arrival gown — and **her hair is down.**
- **HER HANDS DO NOTHING.** Unoccupied, holding nothing, at rest. Kael looks for what her hands do and gets nothing back; it is the detail that defeats him.
- **Her beauty may never arrive as a verdict.** A portrait PLATE of her is illegal. It arrives two ways only: **THE RADIUS** (what a room does in her presence) or **KAEL'S INSTRUMENTS FAILING.** Anchors and portraits are exempt — they are references, not pages.

## The rule that outranks everything here

**THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE.** An image may depict only what its own chapter has already put in front of the reader. An image cannot decline to answer — so where the page is deliberately silent, the plate answers with **framing** (distance, angle, occlusion), never with invention. If a moment cannot be composed without spending something unspent, the moment is wrong.

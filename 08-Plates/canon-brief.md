CHARACTER APPEARANCE — the facts that hold in every picture of this set. Colouring, build, bone, bearing and permanent marks only. Clothing, arms, hair styling, dirt and damage are given per picture, because they change from scene to scene.

EVERY IMAGE
Two body-marks of magic exist in this world: red eyes and white hair. White hair is a mark and never styling — a head is not whitened for drama, age or contrast. Aeliana's silver is a family trait, warm and bright; the drain-mark white is pure quartz white, cold and hard. The two are distinct.
Colour lives in the eyes; everything else is muted and earthy. Kael's grey wins on value, not saturation.
Three visual registers: the island keeps its own material culture; the capital and the imperial houses are Latin-Byzantine; the south is loose pale cloth wound and hung on men and women alike, heads covered, brown faces and dark eyes, walls the colour of the ground with the ground's own grain in them.
The empire's honourable arms are the longsword, the rapier and the spear, carried as posture.
Ages are relative and the age in a picture is the chapter's. Aeliana is one year above Kael, Valeria, Aurelian and Elarine, who are of an age; the parents are the same age as each other; Severin is old. The reference images are cut at fourteen; where a prompt gives a different age, the prompt's age is the one wanted.

KAEL
Lean, hard, strong without bulk; the strength sits in the forearms and the hands. Narrow through the shoulders — adolescent, not grown.
Dark hair, his mother's, worn short and cut by somebody who was not being careful.
Eyes pale grey, near-white, the lightest value in the face.
Handsome, with faintly asymmetrical features — one brow set slightly higher, the nose a fraction off centre. He is invisible by status and stillness, not by his face.
Sun-weathered and wind-chapped from work at altitude, with freckles across the nose under the weather.
Marks, each from the chapter that gives it: burn speckles from the wrist to the second knuckle (Ch. 13); a blade scar on the left forearm (Ch. 6); a long shallow scar on the outside of the left arm above the elbow (Ch. 23); a burn on the left forearm from wrist to elbow (Ch. 26), lying over the blade scar.

VALERIA VEL
Lean, athletic; a working build that reads as function even at rest.
Long black hair, well past the shoulders, no ornament.
Emerald green eyes.
Her stillness warns. Gaze level, flat, appraising — unimpressed and faintly tired. Not vulnerable, not wistful, not searching.
Knuckles older than her face; guard-lineage calluses; old pale scar lines on both forearms, and four newer.
Twin blades, one in each hand, are what she is.
From Ch. 36 the LEFT side of her face is burned, healed to a shine by Ch. 37 and mostly healed after it: a smooth shine on the skin, not a wound and not raw.

AELIANA CASSIVARA VESSANTINE
Not tall; slight, sheltered, soft — a body that has never worked.
Long silver-white hair, colourless, never blonde, no yellow or gold in it — the lightest mass in the picture, and nothing else in the world is that colour.
Pale blue eyes.
Pale untouched skin, freckles across the nose and cheeks. Face slightly rounded, small rounded chin, short philtrum, slender neck.
The most beautiful person in the book, and in competition with no one.

AURELIAN CORVANE
Tall for the cohort and built a year ahead of it — broad, heavy through the shoulder, high carriage, the face still soft. A body ahead of its face.
Dark-gold hair. Amber, gold-brown eyes — the one warm saturated point, with the skin around the eye kept cool or the face eats it. His ground stays cool; warm parchment is Aeliana's alone.
Clear skin over good bone, with no weathering worked into it — the station is in the skin, whatever is on top of it.
Open, easy, pleasant — unspent strength carried high, a boy never once put on the ground. Never sneering, smug or cruel.
From Ch. 26 one arm is not all right and he pretends about it. Which arm is not established.

ELARINE SEDRANE
Small, neat, finely built; nothing on her that does not work. Narrow shoulders, a small head, a slender neck.
Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral black with no blue in it, bound severe. The binding is her silhouette: she reads scholar where Valeria reads uniform.
Eyes very dark brown, near black.
Pale indoor skin, clear and even, no high colour in it.
Assessment in progress: attention fully engaged, giving nothing back, no verdict reached. Not cold, haughty, bored or tired — Valeria owns tired.
She carries a sword, in the doctrine grip: right hand full, left hand open.
Spots of colour high on the cheeks are a state she has at Ch. 13 and Ch. 17, not a feature.

VASK — the father
Broad through the shoulders and no further; fills a doorway by carriage; moves exactly as much as is required.
Iron-dark hair of medium length, greying early at the temples as diffuse scatter — never a streak, seam, blaze or band, which is the drain-mark's shape.
Iron-dark eyes, and they are empty: level, giving nothing — not hostile, cold, sad, kind or tired. Both parents are dark-eyed; Kael's grey is no one's.
A face like used tools, the most weathered in the book. Hands permanently half-curled, as if around something set down.
Scars, from Ch. 3: a long seam under the left shoulder; a round through-wound with its brother on the front; tally-marks on the forearms, all one length, all one slant.

NERIS — the mother
Passes as village until she is still; the stillness is the portrait. Fine bones under worked hands; weather and sun on her, but not the coarsening.
Dark brown hair, long — warm rather than cool, plainly brown and not black, no blue in it. Dark eyes.
Three quartz-white marks in her hair, permanent — two from Ch. 32, the third at the front of the hair from Ch. 33. Cold and hard, and distinct in kind from Aeliana's silver.
Not warm, not maternal, not soft.
Her hands are where the book keeps her: any full figure or scene puts them in frame and occupied.

SEVERIN — the First Master
Old, worn to what mattered; not frail, not kindly-grandfatherly. Tall, gone lean, still broad across the shoulder. The wrists are the tell — too thick for a man whose work was paper. The scholar's stoop is borrowed, over an enforcer's frame; both visible at once.
Hair fully grey, the whole head, a true neutral grey — not quartz white, not warm silver — and even everywhere: no seam, streak or patch.
Eyes dark, near black.
Courteous, unhurried, attentive; the face gives out nothing it has not priced. Not warm, not kindly, not stern.

WREN — the brother
A newborn; his mother's dark hair. From crown to heel about the length of a grown man's forearm.
His eyes are red.
A narrow strip of pale linen is bound level across his eyes — clear of the nose and the mouth, tucked behind the ear, neatly done and not tight — and has been on him since the birth. At one moment in Ch. 33 it rides up and one eye shows.

**Recovered from the session "AI-generated character designs" (24 Aug), which is where the scene system was built.** The system files kept the lessons and discarded the specimens; this sheet is the specimens.

**PROVENANCE.** Every block below is transcribed verbatim from that chat, not reassembled from the current system. Where a prompt has more than one version, the version that produced the uploaded image is marked `AS RUN` and the others are kept so nobody re-derives them.

**THIS SHEET IS THE SINGLE HOME FOR THESE PROMPTS (s58).** Copies were generated into `prompts/scene-tests/` until then; they are gone, because several entries here carry a variant beside the adopted one and that pairing is the whole value of the sheet. The plate template on `prompt-system.md` is cut from these.

**NOTE ON HEADINGS.** These use `### SCENE-VERBATIM: <slug>` to mirror `plates/`' `### PLATE-VERBATIM:`. The scene system has no such convention, which is exactly why these went missing.

**Six attempts. This is the version whose corrections the scene system canonised** — the count attached per hand rather than per image, the blade sized by comparison, the low sword given a contact point instead of a trajectory.

Composition: two figures mid-fight, three-quarter length, facing

Bodies overlapping at the point of contact.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
His right hand holds one short dagger and nothing else.
His left hand is empty and open.
Her right hand holds one longsword and nothing else.
Her left hand holds one longsword and nothing else.
Four hands, three blades, nothing else in any hand. Both her
scabbards are empty. No other blade, blade tip or hilt appears
anywhere in the frame.

The dagger is short — blade no longer than his forearm, less than
half the length of either of her swords.

THE ACTION — Kael has closed inside her reach. He is low, weight

His right dagger is crossed against her right-hand longsword, blade

His left hand is empty and open, held low and out for balance.
Her left-hand longsword is extended low, the edge inches from his

**RECOVERY GAP:** the closing paragraph — the preserve line and Valeria's costume block — was not captured in retrieval. It must be restored before any re-run. The run that stripped costume description put **Aeliana's gold hem on Valeria's body and turned the uniform coat into a long skirt**, which is the failure the costume-ownership rule exists to stop.

**AND THE ADOPTED IMAGE IS NOT THIS PROMPT UNMODIFIED.** It is chest-up rather than three-quarter length, and the blade in his right hand is the heavy short blade, not the dagger — so at minimum the crop was pulled in and the blade-swap edit was applied on top. The edit prompt:

```
Edit this illustration, keeping everything else exactly as it is.

Keep: the composition, both poses, the setting and flagstones, both
characters' faces, hair, colouring and clothing, her two longswords,
his empty open left hand, and the loose scratchy ink linework.

Change only the blade in his right hand. Replace the slim dagger
with a heavy short blade — broad-bladed, thick-spined, brutal
working steel, roughly the length of his forearm and hand together.
Plain crossguard, no ornament. Keep it noticeably shorter than her
longswords but far more substantial than a dagger. Keep it in the
same position, crossed against her blade at the same bind point.
```

**The blade description in that edit is more specific than the one the system files preserved, and it is the one that ran:** *heavy, broad-bladed, brutal working steel, roughly the length of his forearm and hand together, thick spine, plain crossguard, no ornament.* The doc has only *roughly the length of his forearm, noticeably shorter than her longswords.*

## What this recovery turned up that the system files do not say

**1. FULL-BODY REFERENCES BEAT PORTRAITS FOR TWO-FIGURE SCENES, and the reason is stated.** Asked directly during the kneel, the answer was full body for both: portraits carry the face but nothing about proportions, build or how the clothing sits, which is most of what a two-figure scene needs. **The tradeoff is named too** — a full-body reference has a smaller face, so identity holds slightly less firmly. It is worth it because *a wrong costume is more visible than a slightly-off face.* The scene system asserts the rule at the top of the file without the reasoning or the cost.

**2. THE ABSENCE OF WEAPONS MUST BE STATED STRUCTURALLY, NOT AS MOOD.** With armed sheets attached, the model keeps reaching for confrontation. The fix is the hand-inventory trick pointed at nothing: *no weapons visible anywhere in the frame, both hands empty and below the crop.* This is not in either system file, and it is the mechanism behind the `Nothing is happening` finding rather than a separate one.

**3. THE STRIPPED-PROMPT EXPERIMENT IS THE ORIGIN OF THE COSTUME RULE.** A deliberate run removed everything the references already carry. Identity survived — her face was right — and **costume did not**, because two characters wear dark garments and the model blended them. That experiment is why `Identity lines *are* redundant when a reference is attached. Costume lines are not.` The system file states the conclusion without the test.

**4. THE THREE-BLADES-TOTAL FORMULATION FAILED TWICE BEFORE THE PER-HAND ONE WORKED.** Both failures are worth keeping: a blade tip sprouting from behind Kael's head with no owner, and a second short blade coming out of the same fist as Valeria's longsword. The doc records that `"Three blades total" failed repeatedly` — these are the two specimens.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the yard of a village smith on a cold bright afternoon, at
the top end of a narrow fold — hard swept dirt, a water trough, a
woodpile, the open door of a forge shed at one side, a house door at
the other, and a low dry-stone wall along the downhill side. Past the
wall the fold goes away down and the roofs of the rest of the village
show below, small, strung along the water. Flat winter daylight from
one side.

Composition: 3:4. A wide view of the whole yard from near the shed.
In the middle of it, EIGHT OR NINE VILLAGERS stand about in twos and
threes, all of them talking at once — a heavy smith, three men up
from the houses below with their coats still on, an old man with a
stick and a very straight back, a woman with her head back laughing.
They are the crowd of the picture: drawn at middle distance, faces
loose and mostly turned away or turned to each other, nothing
resolved on any of them.

THREE PEOPLE IN THIS YARD ARE NOT IN THAT CONVERSATION, and they are
placed apart from it and from each other, one at each edge.

KAEL is nearest the camera, at one side, standing with his back flat
against the wall of the shed and his bound left arm across his chest,
not looking at anybody's face. His is the only fully drawn face in
the picture: young, thin, hollowed out, doing nothing at all, the
eyes down and away from the crowd. The left arm is bound from the
knuckles to above the elbow in clean linen with the thumb splinted
between two flat pieces of wood.

VALERIA stands at a gap in the low wall on the far side, in profile,
her back to the whole yard, looking out and down the track. Long
black hair, dark clothing, hands loose at her sides.

THE PRINCESS stands in the house doorway at the other edge, just
out of it, small, with a plain cloth tied over her hair so that none
of it shows and a coarse village dress far too big for her, watching
the crowd.

IT IS RELIEF WITH NOWHERE TO PUT ITSELF. Everybody in the middle is
loud and nobody is saying anything: too many hands moving, one man
repeating himself to another, the laugh a little too high and a beat
too long. Nothing threatening is happening and nobody is looking at
the three.

Kael and the near wall of the shed carry the finished drawing; the
crowd, the woodpile, the far wall and the roofs below stay at quiet
middle values with soft wash-bled edges. No hard edge anywhere in the
crowd.

Do not blend the three faces with each other or with the villagers,
and do not mix their clothing.

Preserve each face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the
reference images. His eyes pale grey, Valeria's green, the princess's
pale blue and her hair entirely covered.

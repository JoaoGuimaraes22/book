Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a bare shelf of rock high on a mountainside on a cold clean
morning, as loose wash suggestion — bare pale stone, thin frozen
grass, the ground falling away steeply beyond, and far below them the
sea, flat and open, running to a high horizon. Soft cold daylight
coming up off the water, no sun disc and no cast shadow.

Composition: 4:3. The camera is close and level with them, a few paces
off, side-on. They are sitting on the rock turned toward each other,
the woman on the left and the boy on the right, with the drop and the
sea behind them. The bottom edge of the frame crosses just under her
held-out hand, so neither of them is in the picture below that line.

HER RIGHT HAND IS HELD OUT BETWEEN THEM at the height of the boy's
eyes, palm up, fingers open and relaxed, and it is the centre of the
picture. It is an ordinary open hand with nothing in it and nothing
above it or around it, and that is the point of the image: the hand is
doing something that cannot be seen. Her left hand rests on her own
knee.

THE BOY HAS JUST FOUND IT. He sits straight with both hands quiet on
his knees, holding a stillness he has been told to hold. His head is
bent a little toward her palm; his eyes are wide and fixed on it, and
his brows have drawn slightly together — the face of somebody getting
a report from a sense he did not know he had, a second before he
understands it. Not frightened and not delighted.

SHE IS WATCHING HIS FACE AND NOT HER OWN HAND. Her eyes are on him,
her mouth closed, the whole of her attention on what his face is about
to do: somebody who has set a thing running and is waiting for the
result. Their lines of sight do not meet, and neither of them is
looking at the viewer.

THE WOMAN: fully grown, fine bones under skin with weather and sun on
it but not coarsened. Long dark brown hair falling loose past the
shoulders, warm rather than cool, plainly brown and not black. Plain
coarse village working cloth in dull grey-brown and undyed oatmeal,
worn soft and much mended, with a heavy shawl of the same undyed wool
round her shoulders. No ornament of any kind.

THE BOY: nine years old, small and lean, in coarse undyed mended wool
in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much too large for him. Dark hair, cut
short and uneven at home by somebody who was not being careful.
Wind-chapped, with cool grey dust worked into the skin. Eyes pale
grey, near white.

Colour: the sea is a cold grey-green and it is the one colour in the
picture that does not come out of the ground; the rock is pale
grey-brown, the frozen grass dull straw, their cloth undyed, and the
warmth in the frame is in their two faces and her open hand.

His face is the most finished thing in the picture and the sharpest
step from light to dark happens inside his eye. Her open hand is drawn
clean and whole, every finger readable. The rock, the sea, the sky and
the clothing stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

They are the only two people on the mountain.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces, hair,
colouring. Do not blend their features. The boy is nine here.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a bare shelf of rock high on a mountainside on a cold clean
morning, as loose wash suggestion — bare pale stone under them, thin
frozen grass, the ground falling away steeply beyond, and far below
and behind them the sea, flat and open, running to a high horizon.
Soft cold daylight coming up off the water, no sun disc and no cast
shadow anywhere.

Composition: 4:3. The camera is close and level with them, a few paces
off, side-on. THE TWO ARE SITTING FACING EACH OTHER on the rock, both
cut by the bottom edge of the frame at about the waist, the woman on
the left and the boy on the right, near enough that a hand held out
from either of them reaches the space between. Behind and below them
the drop and then the sea, held flat and pale.

HER RIGHT HAND IS HELD OUT BETWEEN THEM AT ABOUT THE HEIGHT OF HER OWN
CHEST, palm up, fingers open and relaxed, and it is empty. It is the
centre of the picture and it is the only thing in the frame that is
being looked at. Her left hand rests on her knee. Nothing is in either
hand.

HER HAND IS DOING NOTHING THAT CAN BE SEEN, AND THAT IS THE POINT OF
THE PICTURE. It is an open empty hand and nothing whatever is
happening above it or around it.

THE BOY IS THE ONE WHO IS CHANGING. He sits absolutely still, his back
straight, both hands quiet on his own knees, holding a stillness he has
been told to hold. His head is bent a little toward her palm and his
eyes are locked on it and on nothing else. His lips have come apart
and his brows have drawn slightly together: the face of somebody
receiving a report from a sense he did not know he had, a second
before he understands it. Not frightened, and not delighted — arrested.

SHE IS WATCHING HIS FACE, NOT HER OWN HAND. Her eyes are on him, her
mouth closed, the whole of her attention on what his face is about to
do: the look of somebody who has set a thing running and is waiting for
the result. Their lines of sight do not meet — his go to her palm, hers
to his face — and neither of them is looking at the viewer.

THE WOMAN: fully grown, fine bones under skin with weather and sun on
it but not coarsened. Long dark brown hair falling loose past the
shoulders, warm rather than cool, plainly brown and not black. Plain
coarse village working cloth, an overdress over a plain undershirt in
dull grey-brown and undyed oatmeal, worn soft and much mended, with a
heavy shawl of the same undyed wool round her shoulders. No ornament
of any kind.

THE BOY: nine years old, small and lean. Dark hair, cut short and
uneven at home by somebody who was not being careful. Wind-chapped,
cool grey dust worked into the skin. Coarse undyed mended wool in dull
oatmeal and dead olive, thin and too large for him. Eyes pale grey,
near white — the lightest value in the picture.

Colour: the sea is a cold grey-green and it is the one colour in the
picture that does not come out of the ground; the rock is pale
grey-brown, the frozen grass dull straw, and their cloth undyed. Their
skin is the one warm thing in the frame.

His face is the most finished thing in the picture and the sharpest
step from light to dark happens inside his eye, at the edge of the
iris. Her open palm is the second: drawn clean and whole, every finger
readable. The rock, the sea, the sky and both sets of clothing are held
at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

Nothing is happening. Nobody else is on the mountain, there is no third
figure anywhere in the frame, and nothing is approaching them.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces, hair,
colouring. Do not blend their features. The boy is nine here.

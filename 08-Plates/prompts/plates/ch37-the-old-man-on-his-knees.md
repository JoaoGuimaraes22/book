Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a flat open beach of wet sand at the edge of a shallow sea,
as loose wash suggestion. Behind him the sand runs up the picture to a
low brown shore, and beyond that a haze that goes back and does not
stop. The light is a high white southern glare out of a bleached sky,
coming down and up again off the wet sand, the shadows short and weak
and pale everywhere in the picture.

Composition: the old man kneels in the near ground, large in the
frame, turned three-quarters toward the viewer, his head bent down
over what he is holding. The viewer is out on the water side and low:
the horizon sits level with his shoulders. The bottom edge of the
picture runs across the wet sand just in front of his planted right
hand, so that both his arms and the child are fully in frame and his
lower legs and feet are out of it behind him.

HIS RIGHT HAND IS FLAT AND OPEN ON THE WET SAND, palm down, all five
fingers spread and every one of them touching the ground, resting
there and carrying no weight. The hand is wider in the frame than the
child's whole head. A thin film of water lies on the sand around it
throwing the bleached sky back up, and the hand sits dark on it.

IN HIS LEFT ARM, HELD AGAINST THE FRONT OF HIS COAT, IS A NEWBORN
CHILD wrapped in a piece of coarse pale cloth, the cloth drawn up over
the crown of his head. From the top of his head to his heel he is
about the length of the old man's forearm. A narrow strip of pale
linen lies level across his eyes, clear of the nose and clear of the
mouth, tucked away behind the ear and neatly done; no eyes are visible
on the child anywhere in the picture. He is asleep, his mouth a little
open, one fist shut against the cloth.

His eyes are down on the child's face, the brows level and unmoved,
the mouth closed inside the beard. He is not praying and he is not
looking at the viewer.

FURTHER UP THE BEACH, BETWEEN HIM AND THE SHORE, FOUR FIGURES STAND
WITH THEIR BACKS TO THE VIEWER, spread in a line, small — each about a
hand tall in the frame — and not resolved: no faces, no features and
no costume detail, only shapes in loose wash, arms down, a blade in
one hand each. Above and beyond them on the brown, smaller again, six
figures come down toward the water with upright spears. Nothing has
happened yet and nobody is touching anybody. There is nothing in the
old man's hands but the child, and no blade anywhere near him.

A month at sea is on him and it is all on the surface: the skin
scoured by wind and sun and salt, and the frame under it sound — the
strength still there, nothing taken out of him, not frail and not
diminished. The hair and beard are fully and evenly grey, a true
neutral grey, and the salt has dried pale through them as scattered
light strands, never as a streak, seam or patch. He wears a plain
heavy coat, well made and sound, open at the throat, soaked black with
water up to the shoulder and salt-stained pale where it has dried, in
cold grey-brown and deep charcoal. No robes, no chain and no ornament
of any kind.

Colour: the sand, the shore and the haze in dust-brown, dull ochre and
pale bone, the sky bleached almost to bare paper, the child's cloth
pale bone and lighter than the sand around it. The sharpest step from
light to dark in the whole picture happens at the edge of his hand
against the wet sand; his face is fully drawn and held at closer,
quieter values with soft wash-bled edges, and the beach, the figures
and the shore stay quiet and soft-edged behind him.

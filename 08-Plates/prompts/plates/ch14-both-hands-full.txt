Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the edge of an open flagstone training yard on a bright
spring afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a stone wall directly
behind her in shade, the upright posts of a weapon rack close behind
her shoulder, and the open yard beyond her to her left running away
pale and bright. Soft open daylight coming across her from the left
out of that bright yard, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: waist-up, one girl standing at the racks, turned three
quarters toward the viewer, filling the frame. The rack of plain
wooden practice swords stands upright behind her shoulder. Behind her
and much further off, at the edges of the frame, the lines of children
stand three deep as loose dark shapes.

THE TWO REFERENCE IMAGES ARE THE SAME PERSON AND THERE IS ONE GIRL IN
THIS PICTURE.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her right hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword and nothing else. Her left hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword and nothing else. Both are carried low and loose at her
sides with the points down, not raised, not crossed and not touching
each other. Two hands, two swords, and the only other wooden swords in
the picture are the ones standing in the rack behind her.

BOTH HER HANDS BEING FULL IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE, AND THE POSE
SHOWS IT: both arms hang clear of her body, so that each hand and each
sword is separately visible against the ground behind it and neither
is lost against her clothing.

WHERE SHE IS LOOKING: out across the yard, level, past the left edge
of the frame, at nothing in particular. Not at the viewer and not at
the swords in her hands.

SHE IS BORED AND IT IS NOT A POSE. Even and unhurried, the mouth
closed, the brows unmoved, the eyes level and unimpressed, faintly
tired — bored by something she has seen many times already. NOT
sneering, NOT hostile, NOT amused, NOT proud, and not blank. She is
completely still, and the stillness is not restful.

SHE IS TWELVE: the eyes sit a little below the vertical midpoint of
the head, nearer that midpoint than a young child's and still below
it, and the jaw has not come in.

Black hair well past the shoulders, worn loose, with no ornament, no
band and nothing tied in it. Eyes a clear hard green, deep and cold,
the one saturated colour in the picture. Plain grey cloth, close,
exactly made and austere, with no badge, no house colour and no
decoration anywhere on it. A lean working build with nothing spare on
it anywhere, the shoulders and the forearms plainly worked, and
knuckles older than the face.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: her skin is the only
warm colour anywhere, and the wall, the rack, the wooden swords, the
flagstones and all the clothing in the picture are cool grey and
grey-blue with no brown in them.

HER FACE AND HER TWO OCCUPIED HANDS ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN
THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The rack,
the wall and the standing children stay at quiet middle values with
soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there competes for finish.
The bright open yard to her left is reserved as bare unpainted paper.
The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and does not
blur into the wash.

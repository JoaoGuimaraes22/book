Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a hollow behind a bank in a close wood, where the roots of a
live tree have pulled up out of the earth and made a low shelter of
themselves. Needle floor, raw earth, roots overhead and behind. Loose
wash suggestion, the wood beyond going dark. Thin cold daylight
coming in low from one side only.

Composition: 3:4. Close, two figures, chest-up, the two heads near
each other and near the same height. THE PRINCESS is set back in the
hollow with the roots behind her, facing out. KAEL is in front of her
and lower, down on his heels, turned three-quarters away from the
camera with his face in profile toward her. Their four hands are
together low in the frame between them. Nobody else is in the
picture.

HE IS PUTTING A KNIFE INTO HER HANDS, HANDLE FIRST. He holds the
blade flat along his own palm, pointed down and away from both of
them, and the plain wooden handle is going into her two bound hands.
Nothing is being threatened and nothing is raised.

SHE IS TAKING IT AND SHE IS LOOKING AT HIM, NOT AT IT. Her eyes are
up on his face, level and steady, the head still. Her hands close on
the handle together, because they must: a plain closed ring of dark
metal on each wrist with no more chain between them than the width of
her own hand. Silver-white hair, unwashed and heavy, hanging in ropes.
A man's coat far too big for her, dull dark wool, the shoulders off
her. At each corner of her mouth a split, dark and dry.

HE IS GREY AND SHE HAS JUST SEEN IT. His face in profile, the jaw
set, the mouth closed, sweat at the hairline, and no colour in him.
His left arm is folded away inside his coat and out of sight. Undyed
coarse working wool, much mended, dark red gone brown dried down one
side of his face.

Her face carries the most finished drawing in the picture and his
profile the next; the roots, the earth and the wood stay at quiet
middle values with soft wash-bled edges. The sharpest step from light
to dark is at the four hands and the handle between them.

Do not blend their features and do not mix their clothing: the coat
belongs only to her, the mended wool only to him.

Preserve each face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the
reference images. His eyes pale grey, hers pale blue.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: a narrow fold in high country in the middle of the night,
with a village of about forty low stone houses strung out along the
water down the length of it. Cold, clear, no moon to speak of. The
sides of the fold go up dark on both sides and the country above
them goes on into nothing.

Composition: 3:4. A high vantage from up the fold, looking down its
length. The frame stacks away downward: nearest, bare stone and
thorn; below that the near end of the village and the yard of the
smith; then the whole village going away down the water in two
uneven lines, small and complete, the water a pale thread running
between them; then the fold narrowing into dark, and at the top of
the frame the sky. Nobody is in the picture.

EVERY HOUSE IS SHUT AND DARK AND ONE DOOR IS NOT. Not a light in any
window, any doorway or any yard in the whole length of the village —
no lamp, no candle, no fire, nothing lit anywhere — except the open
front of the smith's forge at the near end, which stands open and
has a low red fire in it, and lays one small patch of light out onto
the dirt of its own yard and no further.

THE LIT DOOR IS SMALL AND FAR. It sits well down in the frame and it
is the only warm thing in the picture; everything else in the fold is
cold grey and near-black. It does not light the village, it does not
light the fold, and nothing around it is warmed by it. The forty dark
houses are drawn small and exact, the roofs and the shut doors
readable, and they carry the ink; the stone, the water and the sides
of the fold stay at quiet middle values with open granulation and
soft wash-bled edges.

Lighting: cold night, the whole picture in low values with the sky
the lightest thing in it and carrying bare unpainted paper. One small
far warm source, out at the near end, and no glow, no haze and no
spill anywhere beyond its own yard.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the corner of a path outside a brick court at night, as
loose wash suggestion — a brick wall implied close behind them, a
dark path going up out of the frame, and black night sky above. One
lamp stands out of frame behind them in the court: its light is low
and warm on one side of each face and everything away from it is
dark.

Composition: chest-up, two figures, close and near the viewer,
standing a pace apart and turned three-quarters toward each other,
having stopped on their way apart. She has turned her head back over
her shoulder to him. A case on a strap rides her shoulder and her
hand is on the strap.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. This eye contact is the
subject of the image and neither of them is looking at the viewer.

HER FACE IS THE SUBJECT AND THE SMILE IS ALMOST NOTHING: the corners
of the mouth have lifted very slightly and nothing else in her face
has moved with them — the eyes are unchanged and do not crease, the
cheeks do not lift, the brows do not move, the head is still. It is a
small closed-mouth smile on an otherwise level face, and it is
already going. Not a grin, no teeth, not laughing, not fond, not sad.

His face is doing nothing at all and he is simply looking at her:
mouth closed, brows still, the attention complete.

Her black hair is loose, well past the shoulders, no ornament. Her
clothing as in her full-figure reference: the charcoal sleeveless
coat over the grey-blue underlayer, the high collar, the steel
vambraces. He wears undyed coarse working wool, much mended, in dull
oatmeal and dead olive, with no metal anywhere on him; his left arm
is bound above the elbow in four turns of plain cloth.

Their two faces are the most finished things in the picture and carry
the sharpest step from light to dark; the wall, the path and the
night stay at quiet dark values with soft edges. Nobody else is
anywhere in the picture.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring; the two images of the girl are the same girl and
there are two figures in the picture. Do not blend their features. Do
not mix their clothing: the steel and the charcoal coat belong only
to her, the mended undyed wool only to him. Her eyes green, his pale
grey.

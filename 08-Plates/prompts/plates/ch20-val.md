Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: an old open flagstone court above the sea at the very end of
a winter afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — worn flagstones, a low
broken wall at the edge, and beyond it open water going dark. The
last flat grey light is off the water and the sky is nearly out.
Along the far waterline, small warm lamps have come up one at a time;
they are the only warm thing in the picture and they light nothing —
the two figures are lit by the last of the sky.

Composition: the viewer is at one side of them, level and close, so
both faces are seen in profile. Two figures sit side by side on the
cold flagstones with their backs to the low wall, the boy nearer the
viewer, the girl beyond him, both facing out to the water. She sits
with her forearms across her knees. His near hand lies open and slack
on his knee, not closed on anything. The bottom edge crosses them at
the shin. They are twelve years old. Nobody else is anywhere in the
picture.

NEITHER OF THEM IS LOOKING AT THE OTHER AND NEITHER IS LOOKING AT THE
VIEWER. Both are looking straight out at the water, level, and the
sightlines run out of the frame on the same side. This is the subject
of the image: two people side by side and no eye contact anywhere in
it.

He has just said one word and it has not been answered: his mouth
closed, his face still, nothing asking and nothing withdrawn.

Her face gives nothing away — level, the mouth closed, the brows
unmoved, tired around the eyes, entirely unhurried. Not sad, not
softened. Her black hair is loose, well past the shoulders, no
ornament. Her clothing as in her full-figure reference: the charcoal
sleeveless coat over the grey-blue underlayer, the high collar, the
steel vambraces.

He wears undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dull oatmeal and
dead olive, no ornament and no metal on him anywhere.

Their two profiles carry the most finished drawing in the picture;
the flagstones, the wall and the water stay at quiet middle values
with soft edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring; the two images of the girl are the same girl and
there are two figures in the picture. Do not blend their features. Do
not mix their clothing: the steel and the charcoal coat belong only
to her, the mended undyed wool only to him. Her eyes green, his pale
grey.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: a walled stone training yard at a temple on a winter
morning, as loose wash suggestion — flat flagstones with frost on
them, a long wall behind with wooden weapon racks against it, the far
corners going into cool wash. Flat cold overcast daylight, no sun and
no shadows with edges.

Composition: the boy and the stone together in the middle of the
frame, cropped just below his knees, seen from the side and a little
below him.

SCALE, AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE: a drum of grey stone lying
on its side, longer than the boy is tall, its top edge level with his
shoulder. It is one solid piece, plain and unmarked, with no axle, no
frame, no handles and no rope on it.

The drum is tipped up a few degrees on the near end and is held there
by ONE IRON PRY-BAR, its tip under the stone's edge and a small block
of wood set under the bar as a fulcrum. WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: both
the boy's hands are on the free end of the bar, one above the other,
pushing it down and across. Nothing else is in either hand and there
is no rope, no lever and no second bar anywhere in the picture.

HE IS NOT STRAINING. Weight settled, shoulders down, arms not locked,
back straight, breathing easily — the ease of somebody doing a chore
he has done a hundred times. NOT heaving, NOT braced, NOT triumphant,
NOT proud, NOT grimacing, no teeth showing, no effort anywhere in the
face or the body. His attention is on the point where the bar meets
the stone.

Behind them, at a distance along the wall, a line of children of
about his own age, standing and watching, silent and completely
still. THREE OF THEM, NEARER, ARE BENT OVER WITH THEIR HANDS ON THEIR
KNEES, still getting their breath back. No face in the line or among
the three is drawn — they are dark summary shapes, loosely washed,
with soft edges and no features. There is no adult in the picture.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the boy's skin, the
pale wood of the fulcrum block and the worn wooden grip of the bar
are the only warm colour anywhere. The stone, the flagstones, the
frost, the wall and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue with
no brown in them. The frost on the flagstones is reserved as bare
unpainted paper.

His face and his two hands on the bar are the most finished things in
the picture and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The drum
is a large quiet grey mass with no detail and no hard edge on it, and
the watching children are quieter still. The ink linework stays the
strongest element throughout and does not blur into the wash.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless — no more colour in
the iris than in the whites around it. Dark hair cut short and
uneven. Plain undyed working cloth, coarse and mended and a little
too large for him, the ink carrying the cloth at the seams and the
mended places. He wears no ornament and no metal.

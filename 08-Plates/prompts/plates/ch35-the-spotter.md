Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the side of a small dark wooden boat at night, as loose wash
suggestion — a wet plank strake close in front of him, rope and tarred
timber, the sea beyond it black. A wall of white steam stands off the
water further out at the left. No land is drawn.

Composition: one boy kneeling up at the side of the boat, close to the
viewer, turned three-quarters away toward the left of the frame with his
head turned further that way so that his face is in profile-to-
three-quarter view and fully visible. The bottom edge of the picture
crosses him at the lower chest. He and the child are the only figures in
the picture.

A NEWBORN CHILD IS UP ON HIS LEFT SHOULDER, very small, wrapped in coarse
pale cloth, with his face turned in against the boy's neck. A NARROW
STRIP OF PALE LINEN IS BOUND ACROSS THE CHILD'S EYES, level, clear of the
nose and clear of the mouth. No eye is drawn on the child anywhere. The
child's mouth is wide open and square and he is screaming, and both his
fists are shut and drawn up in the cloth.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: the boy's left hand is flat and spread on the small
of the child's back, holding him against the shoulder. His right forearm
is laid along the top of the strake, and that hand is open and empty and
gripping nothing. Two hands, one child, nothing else in either hand, and
no blade is drawn anywhere in the picture.

HIS MOUTH IS WIDE OPEN ON A SHOUTED WORD: the jaw dropped, the brows
drawn hard together, the lids narrowed against the heat, the eyes fixed
far off to the left. He is not looking at the child, not at the viewer,
and his head is not turned toward the viewer. Nothing in his face is
crying.

THE LIGHT COMES FROM SOMETHING BURNING OFF THE LEFT SIDE OF THE PICTURE
AND OUT OF THE FRAME. It is hard, warm and orange, and it lights the near
side of his face, his hand on the child's back, the child's cloth and the
top edge of the wet strake, and it leaves the far side of his face and the
whole of the rest of the boat dark. There is no fire drawn in the frame
and no other light in it.

His hair, his face and his clothing are marked with soot and dried dark
red, old and brown on the forearms. He wears undyed coarse working wool,
much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, scorched down the front, with
no metal anywhere on him.

His face and the child's fist carry the sharpest steps from light to dark;
the strake, the rope, the steam and the sea stay at quiet middle values
with soft edges.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a hot crowded room raised in timber, at night, as loose wash
suggestion — walls hung with cloth, lamps along both walls, and four
open braziers of burning coals standing in the room. A tall screen at
the back with gold beaten flat and nailed along its edge. The light
is warm and comes from many small sources at once; the corners are
dark.

Composition: waist-up, two figures at the two ends of the frame with
a long table between them running away from the viewer. The
dark-haired boy stands at the near end, turned so that the lamplight
falls along one side of his face, his body squared and his hands
loose at his sides. The silver-haired girl sits beyond the far end of
the table in a high carved chair, facing him, her body still. Behind
her chair two women stand as plain shapes with their faces undrawn.
Nobody in the picture is eating and no plate, cup or dish is in
anybody's hands.

THE TABLE IS COVERED END TO END AND NOBODY HAS TOUCHED IT: whole fish
laid in a row with their eyes in, three cuts of roast meat, small
birds arranged in a pattern, piled fruit with some of it going soft,
loaves in a fancy shape, cut flowers lying wilting along the middle,
and glass everywhere. In the middle of it a wide silver bowl packed
with ice around a jug, sweating into the cloth beneath it.

SHE IS LOOKING HIM OVER THE WAY A PERSON LOOKS AT A HORSE OR A PIECE
OF WORK — from the top, then across, then back again, and going on
past the point at which a person stops. Her face is level and
unhurried and gives nothing away; the mouth closed, the brows
untroubled. Not smiling, not cruel, not curious, not kind. Her hands
rest in her lap and hold nothing.

HIS FACE IS TURNED INTO THE LAMPLIGHT AND IT GIVES HER NOTHING. The
eyes level and open, the mouth closed, the jaw easy, the whole face
plain and unarranged. Not proud, not ashamed, not defiant, not
frightened.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER, down the length of the
table. This eye contact is the subject of the image, and neither of
them is looking at the viewer.

Her hair is silver-white, colourless, with no yellow or gold in it,
and it is down and loose. Her gown is a light one, close and plain
and finely made, in pale grey-blue with a little gold at the collar
and nothing else on it — much less on it than a court gown, and no
jewellery.

He wears undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive,
whole and patched and closed at the throat, with no ornament, badge,
colour or metal anywhere on him. His left arm is bound above the
elbow in four turns of plain cloth, stained dark brown where it has
dried. Below the binding, on the same forearm, an older healed mark
runs up out of the cuff toward the elbow, pale against the skin.

Their two faces carry the sharpest step from light to dark and are
the most finished things in the picture. The table, the food, the
gold, the braziers and the standing women all stay at warm middle
values with soft edges and no sharp detail, and nothing on the table
competes with the two faces for finish.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring; the two images of the girl are the same girl. Do not
blend their features. Do not mix their clothing: the fine pale gown
and the gold belong only to her, the mended working wool only to him.
Her eyes pale blue, his pale grey.

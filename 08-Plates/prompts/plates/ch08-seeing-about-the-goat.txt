Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

No reference image.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at night,
wet after rain, as loose wash suggestion — a fenced pen, a woodpile,
the mountain behind lost in deep cool wash.

Composition: the man standing full length in the yard with his BACK
TO THE VIEWER, facing away toward a goat in the pen. He is between
the viewer and the animal. No part of his face is visible.

Behind him and to one side, an open doorway stands empty — a plain
plank door open on a lit room, with NOBODY IN IT and nobody in the
doorway. The lamplight from it lies across the wet ground in a long
bright rectangle, and his own shadow is cut into it, long and black,
reaching away from the door toward him.

THE LIGHT ON THE GROUND IS THE BRIGHTEST THING IN THE PICTURE and
carries the sharpest step from light to dark, wet ground taking the
light in broken reflections. The man is a dark shape against it. The
yard, the pen, the woodpile and the sky are held dark and loose.

He is doing nothing. Standing square and still with his arms at his
sides, both hands empty, not leaning on the rail, not reaching for
the animal, not working. ONE GOAT, side-on in the pen, entirely
indifferent — not looking at him, not curious, not alarmed.

Broad across the top of the back and nothing wasted below it. Coarse
undyed mended working cloth, the ink carrying the cloth at the seams
and the mended places.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his hair and colouring
from it only. His face is not visible in this picture.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at night
in autumn, as loose wash suggestion — the house wall behind him with
one open door in it, a fenced pen, the mountain beyond lost in deep
cool wash. Wet ground. No snow.

Composition: the man full length, standing still in the middle of the
dark yard with his back to the house and to the viewer, a few steps
short of the pen. THE GOAT in the pen in front of him, side-on,
paying him no attention at all. The open door behind him and to one
side, small in the frame. All grounded on the same ground plane.

HIS BACK IS TO THE VIEWER AND HIS FACE IS NOT IN THE PICTURE. The
back of his head and his shoulders are what the viewer sees. He is
not turned, not in profile, not glancing back. NO FEATURE OF HIS FACE
IS DRAWN. This is deliberate and it is the subject of the image.

HE IS DOING NOTHING. Standing, weight even, arms down at his sides,
both hands empty and open, not reaching for the goat, not working,
not carrying anything. Nothing is in either hand and there is no
tool, no bucket, no rope and no lamp anywhere in the frame. A man who
came out for a reason he has not given and is not doing it.

THE LIGHT COMES FROM THE OPEN DOOR AND IT MUST FALL ON THINGS. Warm
lamplight from inside the house. On the wet ground it lies as A LONG
BRIGHT RECTANGLE WITH HARD STRAIGHT EDGES, stretching out across the
yard toward the pen, and THE MAN'S OWN SHADOW IS CUT INTO IT as a
long dark shape running away from the door. His back and the tops of
his shoulders carry a warm rim from it. Everything the rectangle does
not reach is cold and dark. The rectangle of light on the ground and
its warm edge along his shoulders are the brightest things in the
picture and the only warm colour in it.

THE DOORWAY IS EMPTY. No person stands in it, no figure, no
silhouette, nobody watching from the house. The doorway is a bright
empty shape.

THE GOAT NEEDS NOTHING. Ordinary and shaggy, standing at ease with
its head down, eating or simply standing, entirely uninterested in
the man. It is not frightened, not curious, not approaching him. It
does not require attention and is not receiving any.


THE MAN: a grown man of a mountain village. Broad through the
shoulders and no further — heavy across the top of the back and the
neck, nothing wasted below it, an economy rather than a bulk.
Iron-dark hair of medium length, pushed back off the forehead.
Coarse undyed working cloth, heavy weave, much mended, in dull
grey-brown and dead olive. No ornament, no buckle, no badge.

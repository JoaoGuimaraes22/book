Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his face, hair and
colouring from it only.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house in
winter, as loose wash suggestion — a low roof-line behind, a
woodpile, a fenced pen to one side, snow on the ground and on the
roof, the mountain beyond going up into pale wash. Bare unpainted
paper carries the snow.

Composition: full length, all three subjects in one frame, grounded
on the same ground plane. THE MAN at the left at a chopping block,
mid-stroke. THE BOY small, in the middle distance at the centre,
walking away from the viewer and away from his father, toward the
pen. THE GOAT in the pen at the right, behind the fence. The three
of them read left to right as man, boy, goat.

THE BOY IS SEEN FROM DIRECTLY BEHIND AND HIS FACE IS NOT IN THE
PICTURE — he is walking away, and the back of his head, his back and
his heels are what the viewer sees. Do not turn him. Do not show his
face in profile. No feature of his face is drawn at all.

THE BOY IS FOUR YEARS OLD AND MUST READ AS FOUR. Very small — the
top of his head no higher than the man's hip. The head large in
proportion to the body, the shoulders narrow and soft, the arms and
legs short with no length in them, the walk slightly wide and
unsteady the way a small child walks in snow. He is a toddler, not a
boy of seven or eight. Dark hair, fine and soft, cut short and
uneven at home.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
The man's two hands are together on the haft of one axe, raised
above and behind his head at the top of the stroke. One axe, held in
two hands, nothing else in either hand. The boy's hands are empty
and down at his sides. No tool, no stick, no rope, no bucket
anywhere else in the frame.

THE MAN: a grown man of a mountain village. Broad through the
shoulders and no further — heavy across the top of the back and the
neck, nothing wasted below it, an economy rather than a bulk. Iron-
dark hair of medium length, pushed back off the forehead. Weathered
hard. He is looking down at the block and the wood, not at the boy,
his face level and giving nothing, mouth closed, entirely unhurried.
He has already answered and gone back to work.

ONE GOAT, standing in the pen, ordinary and shaggy, side-on. THE
GOAT IS NOT LOOKING AT THE BOY — its head is turned away toward
something else entirely, wholly indifferent. This indifference is
the joke of the picture. Do not make the goat curious, friendly,
alarmed or attentive.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is in danger, nobody is hurrying,
nobody is calling out. It is an ordinary cold morning and each of
the three is doing his own thing.

Do not mix their cloth: the man wears coarse undyed working cloth,
heavy weave, plain collar closed to the throat, much mended, in dull
grey-brown and dead olive; the boy wears plain undyed wool, coarse
and much mended and a little too big for him, in dull oatmeal. No
ornament on either of them, no buckle, no badge.

Light: flat, cold, even winter daylight from an overcast sky. No
warm source anywhere in the picture, no fire, no lamp, no sun.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No woman, nobody watching,
no other animals.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the one room of a small mountain house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — the edge of a heavy hand-made table, rough boards
behind going into deep shadow. One oil lamp stands on the table just
out of the frame's edge and is the only light in the picture.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is close and low, level with the table
top, a hand's reach away. In the lower half of the frame his own left
hand is held out over the boards, cupped, with a little water standing
in the palm. In the upper half, above and behind it, his face, looking
down at his own hand. Nothing else of him is in the picture but the
one hand, the forearm, and the head and shoulders above it.

A GHOST OF MIST IS STANDING OFF THE PALM AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
PICTURE. It is one breath's worth: a small soft white cloud no wider
than the hand, standing just clear of the water and already thinning
at its top, with its edges going away into nothing. It is reserved as
bare unpainted paper, and it is the lightest thing in the frame. It
does not touch his face and it does not cover his hand: the whole hand
and every finger stay drawn and visible underneath it.

Nothing else is coming off the hand. There is no flame anywhere in the
picture, nothing bright in the water, and nothing in the air except
that one small standing cloud.

HIS FACE IS DOING ONE THING: looking down at his own hand and reading
it. The eyes are wide and steady and on the palm, the brows drawn very
slightly together, the mouth closed. This is a boy checking an answer,
not a boy astonished — the attention is entirely on the hand and none
of it is on himself.

THE BOY: eleven years old, lean, small in the shoulder. Eyes pale
grey, near white. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home by somebody
who was not being careful. Wind-chapped, weathered, with the cool grey
dust of a working day still on the skin. A plain undyed tunic in dull
oatmeal, coarse and much mended, the sleeve pushed back off the
forearm that is held out.

Lighting: the lamp is warm and low and stands off to one side just out
of frame. It lights the near side of his face, the underside of the
jaw, and the held-out hand and forearm, and leaves the far side of the
face and everything behind him to go into deep cool shadow. The lamp
itself, its flame and its body, are not in the picture.

Colour: the lamplight is a deep warm orange and it is the only
saturated colour in the frame; his skin is warm under it; the boards,
the table and the shadowed side of the picture are cool grey-brown.

The mist and his eyes carry the picture between them: the mist is the
lightest value and his eye holds the sharpest step from light to dark,
inside it, at the edge of the iris. The table, the boards and his
clothing stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges, and
his hair is drawn as fine ink strands with bare paper left through the
mass rather than as a solid black.

He is alone in the room. There is no other figure and no part of one
anywhere in the frame.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Setting: a long dining hall in a stone house at dusk, as loose wash
suggestion — a bare table running away down the middle of the room with
the chairs pushed back from it, standing figures along both sides of it,
and a shuttered window in the near wall at the left. Warm lamplight
fills the room from a lamp on a side table, low and orange, and it is
the only light inside.

Composition: the bottom edge of the picture crosses the near man at the
waist. He stands at the left, close to the viewer, seen from behind and
slightly to one side, his back to the room and his face entirely out of
sight. The window is in front of him. Past him the room runs away to the
right and back, and the standing figures are further off and smaller.

HIS RIGHT HAND IS ON THE EDGE OF THE WOODEN SHUTTER at the height of his
own shoulder, drawing it across the window. The leaf is most of the way
closed and a hand's width of the opening is still uncovered. His left
hand hangs at his side. Nothing else is in either hand.

WHAT IS IN THE GAP: the last of a cold grey daylight, a dull sky with
smoke standing in it, and nothing else drawn or resolved out there at
all. The light coming through that gap is thin, cold and blue against
the orange of the room, and it falls in one narrow band across the
floorboards and stops.

The room behind him: eighteen other people, standing, drawn as a wash of
dark shapes at quiet middle values with soft edges. One chair at the far
end of the table has somebody seated in it and that figure is not
resolved either. Nobody is turned toward the man at the window.

NO EYES ARE VISIBLE ANYWHERE IN THE PICTURE and no face is drawn on
anybody. Do not turn any head toward the viewer.

The man is broad and heavy through the shoulder, in good plain dark
clothing well cut, with no ornament, no badge and no chain. Nothing
about him is hurrying.

The hand on the shutter and the narrow band of cold light on the floor
carry the sharpest steps from light to dark in the picture; the table,
the chairs, the standing figures and the far end of the room stay soft
and quiet.

Image 1: elarine-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a walled stone training yard in falling rain, as loose wash
suggestion — wet flagstones, a low stone wall behind her going into
cool shadow, the far corners lost in wash. Flat grey rain-light, and
the only other light in the picture is the flame below.

Composition: chest-up, the girl turned three-quarters toward the
viewer with her open left hand raised to her own shoulder height, so
that her hand and her face stand side by side in the frame at the
same height.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her left hand is open, palm up, fingers
straight, holding nothing. Her right hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword and nothing else, carried low and crossing the bottom
edge of the frame. Nothing else is in either hand.

THE FLAME STANDS ON THE OPEN PALM AND IT IS BURNING ON NOTHING. One
body of flame stands upright off the flat of her palm, as tall as
the span from her thumb to her little finger, with a clear gap of
air between the flame and her skin. NO WICK, no candle, no lamp, no
torch, no wood and no coal — there is nothing there to burn and
nothing in her hand. It stands straight and holds its size: not
guttering, not streaming sideways, not spreading.

RAIN FALLS PAST THE FLAME AND DOES NOT TOUCH IT. Fine rain crosses
the whole frame in one direction as flicked spatter and dry-brush
drag, falling past the flame and through its light, and the flame is
unchanged by it. No steam and no smoke anywhere in the picture.

THE FLAME IS THE ONLY WARM LIGHT AND IT REACHES ONLY HER: the palm,
the inside of the raised forearm, and the near side of the face and
throat, lit from the side at her own height and not from below.
Everything else stays cold — the rain, the flagstones, the wall, the
wooden sword and all her clothing in cool grey and grey-blue with no
brown in them. The wall behind her takes none of the warm light.

HER FACE IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Attention fully engaged and
giving nothing back, mouth closed and still, brows unmoved, the
breathing easy — a girl who has not hurried once and does not intend
to. NOT fierce, NOT angry, NOT triumphant, NOT strained, NOT
effortful — and equally NOT cold, NOT haughty, NOT bored, NOT tired.
Nothing has been decided in the face.

SHE IS ELEVEN AND SMALL FOR IT: the eyes sit a little below the
vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still below it; the jaw has not come in, the cheeks are
still full under the bone, the neck slender, the shoulders narrow.
Finely built, with nothing spare on her anywhere.

Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral black with no blue in it, drawn back
severely and bound tight at the nape, wet with the rain, nothing
loose anywhere. Pale indoor skin, and the only warm colour on it is
the flame's. Eyes very dark, near black. Plain dark close-fitting
clothing of very good quality, exactly made and buttoned high at the
throat. No ornament, no metal, no badge and no white anywhere on
her.

THE FLAME IS THE BRIGHTEST THING IN THE PICTURE AND HER FACE IS THE
MOST FINISHED. The flame is soft-edged and loose with no hard edge
and no detail in it, and the sharpest step from light to dark
happens along the lit boundary of her face. The yard, the wall and
the flagstones stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no
detail, and nothing back there competes for finish. The falling rain
and the wet gleam on the flagstones are reserved as bare unpainted
paper. The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and
does not blur into the wash.

THERE IS NO OTHER PERSON IN THE PICTURE — no opponent, nobody
watching at the edge of the yard, and nobody in the background.

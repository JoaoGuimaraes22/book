Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of Kael.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the foot of a dry-stone terrace on a hillside in the middle
of the afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a length of the wall
fallen in, the earth bank behind it undercut into a low lip, dry
grass and dead bramble around it, and stubble ground above going out
of frame. Flat daylight from one side, and the inside of the hollow
going to deep shadow.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is outside the hollow, low, at the
height of the fallen wall. KAEL is nearest, on the left, crouched
side-on to the camera with a long fall of dead bramble held in both
hands and half drawn across the mouth of the hollow. Inside it, under
the lip, A WOMAN sits well back in the shadow with a small child on
one arm, its face turned into her neck, and a boy of about ten
packed in beside her with a cloth bundle set upright in front of the
smallest one. The bottom edge crosses the fallen stones and Kael at
mid-thigh, so that no feet and no ground below the wall are in the
frame at all. Nobody else is in the picture.

SHE HAS ASKED HIM SOMETHING AND HE IS ANSWERING IT. She is looking
out of the dark at him over the child's head, her face level and
unconvinced and holding still; his face is turned to her over his
shoulder, the mouth just closing on a short word, the eyes steady on
her, the whole of him still working at the bramble while he answers.
Neither is looking at the viewer, and this look between them is the
subject of the image.

The bramble reads as a thing that fell rather than a thing that was
placed: dead, grey-brown, tangled, lying at the angle a fall lies at.

KAEL — thirteen, lean, moving quickly and quietly. Undyed coarse
working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much mended, no
ornament, badge, colour or metal on him anywhere; two short blades in
a wrap across his back and nothing in either hand but the bramble.
There is dried red to the elbow on both forearms, dark and gone
brown, and none of it is his; his face and his hands are otherwise
clean and nothing on him is bleeding.

The woman is a village woman in worn brown, a shawl over her hair,
one bundle and nothing else with her. The children's faces are not
resolved: the small one's is turned away into her neck and the boy's
is in the shadow behind her shoulder.

The two lit faces carry the sharpest step from light to dark and are
the most finished things in the picture; the wall, the bank, the
bramble and the children stay at quiet middle values with soft
wash-bled edges.

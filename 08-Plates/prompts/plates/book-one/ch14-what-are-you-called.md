Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a stone training yard at a school by the sea, in the last of
a spring afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — flagstones underfoot
with a measure scored on them in chalk, a sand barrel at one corner,
a colonnade implied behind, and beyond it a crowd breaking up into
loose grey and brown wash with no faces in it. The sun is low behind
the far colonnade, level and warm, and the barrel throws a shadow a
stride long.

Composition: 4:3. Waist-up. Both are grounded on the same flagstones
and the scored line of the measure passes between them along the
bottom of the frame. The girl stands on the right, square to the
camera and turned a little toward the boy; the boy stands on the
left, three-quarters on, facing her; there is one stride between
them. At the extreme left edge a woman in a plain apron is cut off at
the shoulder by the frame, with no face in the picture, both her
hands on the girl's left forearm winding a linen strip round it.

THE GIRL HAS JUST ASKED HIM WHAT HE IS CALLED AND SHE IS WAITING FOR
THE ANSWER. She waits the way stone waits: perfectly still, weight
even, chin level, mouth closed, brows unmoved — and the flat bored
look she has worn since she came off the ships is gone out of her
eyes and has been replaced by attention, fully awake and entirely on
him. She is not angry.

HER LEFT ARM IS CUT AND SHE IS NOT LOOKING AT IT. A slash runs across
the outside of the left forearm a hand above the wrist, its edges
clean; the cloth of her sleeve is cut open where it is; there is red
along it and a little down the back of that hand, dark and drying,
and red gone into the linen the woman is winding. Her face is
forbidden the arm entirely — not pained, not braced, not attending to
it.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
His right hand is empty, open and hanging at his side.
His left hand is empty and open.
Her right hand holds both of her wooden practice swords together,
gripped in the one fist with the points down to the flagstones.
Her left arm is being wound with linen and her left hand is empty.
One wooden practice sword lies on the stones at the boy's feet where
it was struck out of his hand. No other blade, blade tip or hilt
appears anywhere in the frame.

THE BOY GIVES NOTHING. His face level and plain, his mouth just
closing on one short word, his eyes steady on hers. His right arm
hangs straight down at his side as dead weight, the hand open and
slack, and the shoulder on that side sits lower than the other.
Coarse undyed working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead
olive, no ornament and no metal on him.

Her clothing as in her full-figure reference: the fitted sleeveless
coat in deep charcoal over a close-fitting grey-blue long-sleeved
underlayer, the high collar with its small bronze insignia. There is
nothing on either of her forearms but cloth and the linen strip: no
vambraces and no steel on her arms at all.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER across the one stride
between them, the lines of sight meeting. This eye contact is the
subject of the image. Neither of them is looking at the viewer.

Colour: the low sun warm on the flagstones and on the near side of
both faces; the coat charcoal, the underlayer grey-blue, the boy's
wool dull oatmeal; the red on her forearm and the green of her eyes
are the only saturated colours in the frame.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age; her two images are the same girl and
there is one girl in this picture. Do not blend their features. Do
not mix their clothing: the charcoal coat, the grey-blue underlayer
and the bronze insignia belong only to her, and the boy wears no
metal and nothing dyed.

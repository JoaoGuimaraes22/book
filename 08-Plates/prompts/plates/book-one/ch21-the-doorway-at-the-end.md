Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the one lived-in room of a small stone mountain house late
at night, as loose wash suggestion — a plain heavy table, rough
plastered wall behind it, and an inner doorway standing open on a
room that is completely dark. Two small oil lamps stand on the table
and are the only light in the picture.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is at the side of the table, close and
level with it. KAEL is nearest, on the left, seated at the table
three-quarters toward the camera, a sheet of paper in front of him
and a pencil still down on it, his head just come up off the page.
NERIS is seated beyond him, side-on to the table, leaning forward off
the back of her chair with her near arm stretched along the tabletop
toward a cup. The tabletop and the things on it — the lamps, the
papers, the cup — cross in front of her, so that nothing of her below
the collar is in the frame except that one reaching arm and the
shoulder it comes off. Behind and beyond them both, deeper in the
picture, VASK stands in the open inner doorway with one shoulder set
against the frame, his hands empty and down, the dark room behind
him. The bottom edge crosses the near edge of the table, so that no
laps, no knees, no chairs and no floor are in the frame at all.

TWO LINES OF SIGHT, ONE BEHIND THE OTHER, AND THAT IS THE SUBJECT OF
THE IMAGE. The man in the doorway is watching the woman reach.
The boy at the table has looked up from his page and is watching the
man. Nobody is looking at the viewer, and the woman is looking only
at the cup.

VASK — settled, his weight all on the one shoulder against the frame,
completely still, the eyes level and steady on her, the mouth closed
and the jaw easy. He is not going to her. Plain village working cloth
in undyed oatmeal, worn soft, no ornament and no metal on him.

NERIS — working, and asking nobody for anything: the arm out at full
stretch along the boards, the fingers open and nearly at the cup, the
shoulder come forward and the weight off the chair back, her face in
profile and level, the mouth closed. Not appealing to anybody and not
in difficulty. Long dark brown hair loose. Plain village working
cloth in undyed oatmeal.

KAEL — the pencil left where it was, the head lifted, the eyes gone
past his mother to the doorway, the mouth closed, the brows a
fraction down. He has just seen something and has not yet decided
what it is. Undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead
olive, much mended, no metal on him anywhere.

Lighting: the two lamps on the table are warm and low and do all the
work — up onto the boy's face and along the woman's cheek and
forearm, out along the tabletop, and reaching the man in the doorway
only just, so that he stands at the edge of the light with the black
of the far room behind him.

Bare unpainted paper is reserved by name in the lit edge of each
face, on the tabletop and along the rim of the cup, and the ink
linework stays the strongest element in the picture and is not
softened anywhere by the warm light.

The three faces carry the most finished drawing in the picture; the
table, the wall, the doorway and the far room stay at quiet middle
values with soft wash-bled edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. The man's
eyes and the woman's are dark; the boy's are pale near-white grey.

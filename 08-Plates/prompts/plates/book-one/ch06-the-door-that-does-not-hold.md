Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine ink
linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

No reference image.

Setting: the edge of a small fishing village on a cold clear midwinter
night, seen from the dark at the corner of a boat-shed. In the near
half of the picture, one low stone-and-turf house standing in its own
dark, its wall running across the frame, unlit, with no lamp anywhere
on it or in it. Beyond it and below, along the waterline, the rest of
the village is burning with lamps end to end, and every one of them is
doubled and broken in the black water beneath.

Composition: 4:3. The camera is low and off to one side in the dark,
a few paces from the house, looking along its wall. THE OPEN DOOR is
the near subject and sits left of centre: a heavy plank door standing
open, hanging a little askew, with a hand's width of gap between its
edge and the frame it is knocking against. The opening behind it is
solid black. Past the corner of the house the ground falls away, and
the far shore with its lamps runs in a bright band across the middle
of the frame, small and distant. Above that, black sky and a few cold
stars as tiny reserved flecks.

THE DOORWAY IS EMPTY. There is nothing on the step, nothing in the
opening and nothing on the ground in front of it: only the dark
standing inside the house and the dark lying in front of it.

THE DOOR IS THE ONLY THING IN THE PICTURE THAT IS NOT WHERE IT SHOULD
BE. Every other line in the frame is a line that is doing its work —
the wall standing, the roof lying, the lamps burning in their row —
and this one thing hangs off its own frame, open on a winter night,
with nobody closing it.

Nobody is in the picture. No figure stands in the doorway, none at any
window, none on the ground between the house and the water, and none
at the frame's edges.

Lighting: there is no light on the near house at all. Every light in
the picture is far off and warm: the lamps of the shore and their
broken doubles in the water, which are the lightest and the only
saturated colour in the frame — a deep warm orange reserved as bare
paper with the tint carried only at their edges. The near house, its
wall, its door and the ground around it are cold blue-black, drawn in
ink and thin dark washes, and the black of the open doorway is the
darkest value in the picture.

The door and its frame are the most finished things in the image and
the sharpest step from light to dark happens along the door's near
edge. The lamps stay small; the shore and the water are held loose,
with soft wash-bled edges and nothing rendered in them.

Nothing is happening. Nothing is approaching, nothing is moving in the
frame but the door, and it has only moved the width of the wind.

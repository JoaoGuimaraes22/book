Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy, for the medium and for his colouring. He is small in this picture, seen from behind, and his face is not drawn.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: high terraced farm ground on a headland in the middle of the
day, looking inland and east. Dry-stone terrace walls, olive ground,
scrub and goat ground above it, and four miles of open country beyond
that rising into haze. The daylight is flat and cold and there is
smoke in the air.

Composition: 3:4. The viewer stands a little below and behind him,
and the frame stacks away upward. Nearest, at the bottom, the top of
a dry-stone terrace wall, and A BOY standing on it with his back to
the camera, small, his arms down and his hands hanging open, doing
nothing. Above him, the terraces going up in bands to a broken olive
line. Above that, four miles of open field ground with eleven
separate columns of smoke standing up off it, thin and straight in
the still air — some low down where the ground is wet, some strung
out in a line along the run of a track. Small groups of men, two and
three and four at a time, are going along the field walls between
them. At the top of the frame, above all of it, a mountain standing
in haze with its summit lost in cloud. Along the right-hand edge,
beyond the low ground, a strip of open water with boats standing in
toward the shore.

WHAT HE IS LOOKING AT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE, and it is a
distance with people working across it. Nothing is happening in the
foreground: he is still, unhurried, not braced and not moving
anywhere, and only his stillness is drawn.

No face and no feature of a face appears anywhere in the picture:
the boy is seen from behind, and the men on the ground are small
enough that they are shapes and nothing more. Nothing violent is in
the frame.

He wears undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive,
much mended, with a wrapped bundle and two short blades across his
back on a cord.

The eleven columns of smoke and the boy on the wall carry the hardest
edges in the picture; the terraces, the field ground, the water and
the mountain all stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled
edges, and the haze takes everything above the olive line.

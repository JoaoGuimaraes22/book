Image 1: portrait reference of the boy. Take his face, hair,
colouring and apparent age from it. THE REFERENCE SHOWS HIM ALERT AND
ASKING. THIS PICTURE IS THE OPPOSITE, AND THE EXPRESSION DESCRIBED
BELOW OVERRIDES THE REFERENCE COMPLETELY.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the sleeping loft of a small stone mountain house at night,
as loose wash suggestion — rough boards beneath him, a low roof beam
above going into deep shadow, coarse undyed wool bunched under his
head. No detail anywhere except the face.

Composition: a CLOSE-UP of the boy's face filling most of the frame.
He lies on his back on the boards, seen from above and slightly to
one side, his head turned a little away. His eyes are open and
directed up past the viewer at a roof he is not looking at. He is not
looking at the viewer. Nothing below his collar is in frame.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS FAINT AND WARM AND ENTERS FROM THE LOWER EDGE OF
THE FRAME — a banked fire in the room below, reaching him over the
edge of the loft floor. It catches the underside of the jaw, the near
cheekbone and the lower lip, and nothing else. The roof, the beam and
the far side of his face go into deep cool shadow. No lamp, no
candle, no flame is visible in the picture.

HIS EYES ARE OPEN, CLEAR AND DRY: near-white neutral grey, cool and
colourless, large in a young face, a distinct dark limbal ring and a
small dark pupil. THEY ARE THE LIGHTEST VALUE IN THE PICTURE, and the
sharpest step from light to dark in the whole image happens inside
the eye itself, at the edge of the iris. The hair is dark but never a
solid mass — fine ink strands and open transparent washes with bare
paper left through it, soft wash-bled edges, no hard-edged black
anywhere in it, nothing competing with the eye for finish.

THE FACE HAS JUST FINISHED WORKING SOMETHING OUT AND HAS THE ANSWER.
Completely still, mouth closed and relaxed, brows unmoved, nothing
moving in it anywhere. He is NOT crying: no tears, no wet on the
face, the eyes not red and not swollen. NOT sad, NOT grieving, NOT
frightened, NOT angry, NOT sleepy, NOT peaceful. DO NOT DRAW A SAD
CHILD LYING AWAKE. Draw a child who has finished a calculation.

Nine years old: the face still soft and unfinished under the wear,
the cheek full, no length in the jaw yet. Wind-chapped, high colour
across the cheeks and the nose, cool grey dust in the skin — the
grime staying cool grey, never warm brown. A child's skin under it,
nothing worked deep. No scars and no marks anywhere on him.

Nobody else is in the picture — no second figure, no hand, no
shoulder at the frame's edge.

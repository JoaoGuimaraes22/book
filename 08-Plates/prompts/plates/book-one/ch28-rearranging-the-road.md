Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a village lane wide enough for a cart, as loose wash
suggestion — rough stone house fronts close on both sides, low roofs
above them, the far end of the lane going up into smoke and fog. A low
stone well-head at one side. Flat cold fogged daylight, no sun and no
hard-edged shadow anywhere.

Composition: the girl standing in the near third of the frame at the
left, side-on to the viewer and close to it, the bottom edge of the
picture crossing her at mid-thigh. The lane runs away from her to the
right and a little up, and its whole depth is visible past her. She is
the only figure drawn anywhere in the picture.

SHE IS COMPLETELY STILL: both feet flat and set apart on the road, the
weight even between them, both arms straight down at her sides, the
shoulders level. Nothing about her is raised, drawn back, reaching or
in motion. Nothing is coming off her hands, and there is no light, no
glow and no spark anywhere on her or near her.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: her right hand holds one plain straight short
sword and nothing else, hanging straight down at her side with the
point toward the road. Her left hand holds one plain straight short
sword and nothing else, in the same way. A matched pair, undecorated
and well kept. Two hands, two blades, nothing else in either hand, and
nothing raised.

WHAT THE ROAD HAS DONE, EACH IN ITS OWN PLACE: one broken mass of
roadbed stands upright out of the lane just past her and a little
behind, thicker through than a man's chest and going up to the height
of the house roofs. A second stands a few strides further up the lane.
A third further still and smaller with the distance. Three of them,
standing in a line up the middle of the lane and away from her, and
nothing else is standing upright in the lane.

Each is torn upward out of the road and made of what the road is made
of — packed brown earth, old grey paving stone, gravel and roots —
crooked, irregular and broken off at the top, not flat. The ground at
the foot of each is opened and thrown outward in a ring, and dirt and
small stones are still coming off them and hanging in the air. They are
not smooth, not carved, not built: not architecture, not pillars, not
masonry.

HER FACE IS UNHURRIED AND ENTIRELY PROFESSIONAL: the chin level, the
lids fully raised, the mouth closed and even, the brows unmoved, the
jaw easy. Nothing in it is straining and nothing in it is angry. Her
eyes are on the far end of the lane, past all three, and she is not
looking at the viewer and not looking at the nearest of them.

Red over both hands and up both arms to the elbow, dark and drying. A
short slash across the line of her jaw. Her black hair is BOUND —
gathered close against the head and tied back at the nape, with nothing
falling loose past her shoulders.

Her face is the most finished thing in the picture and carries the
sharpest step from light to dark; the house fronts, the smoke and the
far end of the lane stay at quiet middle values with soft edges, and
the three standing masses are drawn with hard broken edges against
them.

The only saturated colour in the picture is the green of her eyes and
the red on her arms — everything else is cold grey, brown earth, wet
stone and smoke.

Preserve her exactly as shown in the reference images — face, hair,
colouring, apparent age and clothing; the two images are the same girl
and there is one figure in the picture. Her clothing as in her
full-figure reference: the charcoal sleeveless coat over the grey-blue
underlayer, the high collar, the steel vambraces.

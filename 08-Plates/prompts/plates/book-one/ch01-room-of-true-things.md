Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

No reference image.

Subject: the single room of a small, poor, very well-kept mountain
house at night, seen from ABOVE — from the height of a sleeping loft,
looking down over the edge of the loft floor, so a roof beam and the
near edge of rough boards frame the top of the picture. Interior
only, the whole room in one view.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS A SMALL FIRE DOWN IN THE FLOOR: a square stone-
lined hole in the middle of the room with a low contained fire in it,
banked, not a blaze, no flames reaching. It throws warm light up the
walls and up the underside of the table, the beams and the tools, and
leaves the corners and the roof in deep cool shadow.

In the room, readable at a glance: a heavy hand-made table; two low
beds against one wall with folded coarse wool on them; a closed
wooden chest; one heavy plank door, shut, with a wooden bar down
across it in iron brackets. And ONE WHOLE WALL OF TOOLS — a dozen or
more hand tools on pegs in deliberate order, each with its own place,
evenly spaced, every one hanging straight and level and not one
crooked. After the fire it is the most important thing in the frame.

NOBODY IS IN THE ROOM: no figures, no hands, no silhouettes, nobody
in the beds, nobody at the door.

Colour: muted, earthy, desaturated — dull grey-brown timber, dead
olive wool, cold grey stone, and the single warm orange source down
in the floor. There is no skin in this picture; the warmth belongs to
the firelight and falls on timber, stone and wool.

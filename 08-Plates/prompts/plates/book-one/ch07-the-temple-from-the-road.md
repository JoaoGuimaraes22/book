Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the shoulder of a coast road high on a headland, early on an
autumn morning, as loose wash suggestion — thin dry grass and bare
pale rock underfoot, the road running down and away to the right, the
sea flat and pale beyond it and far below, the sky a flat pale wash
above.

Composition: 4:3. Real depth, stacked from the bottom of the frame to
the top, with the viewer standing on the road a few steps behind the
boy and a little above him. Nearest and lowest, in the bottom left of
the frame, THE BOY, on the road with his back to the viewer, about a
quarter of the picture's height. Beyond him the ground falls away.
Across the middle band of the frame, below the road and running the
whole width of the picture, THE WALLED COMPOUND on its headland. Above
it the tower, and above that a strip of pale sky.

THE COMPOUND, BY CONSTRUCTION: a large walled settlement built out
onto a rock headland over the sea, in the same pale stone as the rock
it stands on, so that the outer walls read as a continuation of the
headland rather than as something set down on top of it. Long
unbroken curtain walls following the line of the rock, with one gate
in the near side. Inside the walls, open paved courts; among and
between the courts, low buildings with shallow tiled roofs. One square
tower stands above all of it and is the tallest thing in the frame.
Thin smoke goes up straight from the kitchen roofs. Small plain
banners of dull undyed cloth stand along the wall-tops, lifting a
little. It is a walled school and not a shrine: no free-standing
columns, no pediment, no dome, nothing of a classical temple front
anywhere in it.

IT IS FULL, AND THAT IS THE SUBJECT. In the largest court, inside the
gate, small dark figures are crossing between the buildings — dozens
of them, children, spread right across the court in twos and threes
and small clusters, all of them walking. They are marks and not
portraits, and no face is drawn on anybody in the court.

SCALE — the distance is the picture: the boy is taller in the frame
than the gate in the wall below him, and each figure crossing the
court is no taller than his hand is long.

THE BOY: ten years old, small and narrow, standing square on both
feet. Seen from directly behind — the back of his head, his back and
his heels — with no feature of his face anywhere in the picture. Do
not turn his head toward the viewer. Dark hair, cut short and uneven.
Coarse undyed mended wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, a little too
large for him. A plain cloth bag hangs on a strap over his right
shoulder, and his right hand has come up and closed on the strap. His
left hand hangs empty. He carries nothing else.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is coming up the road, nobody below has
seen him, and there is no other figure anywhere on the road.

Lighting: low early morning sun from the left, out of frame, and it is
the only light. It lands square on the face of the tower and along the
tops of the seaward walls, which take it warm and are the lightest
values in the picture, reserved as bare unpainted paper. The courts
inside the walls, the near ground and the boy are all in cool shade;
the sea and the sky stay flat and pale with no hard edge anywhere in
them.

The sharpest step from light to dark in the whole picture happens at
the edge of the lit face of the tower, against the wall in shadow
beneath it. Nothing in the near ground or on the boy takes a harder
edge than that.

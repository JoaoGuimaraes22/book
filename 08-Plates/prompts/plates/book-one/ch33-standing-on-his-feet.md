Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights. Plus dry-brush drag and flicked spatter
for the movement.

Setting: the trodden yard of a small stone house on a mountain at
night, as loose wash suggestion — the wall of the house close behind
her with a low doorway standing open in it, and a line of dry-stone
pens burning further off along the slope.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is at the side of the two of them,
close, at the height of the girl's head and looking slightly up, so
that the underside of the man's jaw is visible and the tops of his
shoulders are not. VALERIA is on the left, front-on to him and
driven the whole way in against him. THE MAN is on the right, two
heads taller, front-on to her, standing between her and the burning
pens. THE TWO OF THEM ARE ONE MASS IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FRAME,
overlapping from the hip to the shoulder: there is no gap of
background between their bodies anywhere below the chin. The bottom
edge crosses her at the hip and him at mid-thigh, so that the ground
and anything lying on it are out of the frame. Nobody else is in the
picture.

HER BLADE IS IN HIM. One longsword is in his side, low, under the
ribs on the left, going in at a slight upward angle, and about four
inches of it is not showing. Her fist is against his clothing where
it enters. There is red on his clothing around it, dark and wet.
Nothing else is bleeding.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
Her right hand holds one longsword and nothing else, and the point
of that blade is the one in his side.
Her left hand holds one longsword and nothing else, held low and
back at her own hip, the point down and behind her, the whole blade
clear of both bodies and touching nothing.
His left hand is shut in her hair at the back of her head, closed.
His right hand is open and coming toward her right wrist and has not
reached it.
Four hands, two blades, nothing else in any hand. No other blade,
blade tip or hilt appears anywhere in the frame.

Each blade is a longsword with the blade about as long as she is
from her shoulder to her knee, a straight cross guard, no ornament.

VALERIA — the weight all forward and up into him, the near shoulder
driven into his body, the head back so the face is up into his. The
eyes on his face, the mouth closed, the jaw hard. Not triumphant,
not frightened. Her hair pulled hard at the back by the hand shut in
it, the rest of it out and loose with the movement. Dark near-black
clothing, well made, filthy and torn at one shoulder — good cloth
with a bad night on it, not ragged. There is a great deal of dried
red on her forearms and down one side of her, dark and gone brown,
and none of that is hers. There is no mark of any kind on her face.

THE MAN — tall, thin, in plain dark clothing. He is over her: the
chin coming down toward her and the shoulders coming forward. NO
FEATURES ARE DRAWN ON HIS FACE AT ALL.

Lighting: the burning pens beyond his shoulder are the source that
does the work — a hard warm edge down his whole outline, and her
face and the front of her lit from past him. The open doorway behind
her puts a second warm edge along her hair and her near shoulder.
Everything else in the yard goes to a deep dark.

Her face carries the only finished drawing in the picture; the man
apart from the point where the blade enters him, the doorway, the
pens and the yard have no hard edge anywhere in them, soft and
wash-bled throughout. The sharpest step from light to dark in the
picture happens at her face.

Preserve her face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown
in her reference images.

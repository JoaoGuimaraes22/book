Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the flat ground of a mountain smallholding before sunrise on
a cold spring morning, as loose wash suggestion — frozen ground under
frost, the yard falling away into cool wash behind him, the far side
of it lost. Cold flat half-light before sunrise, no sun and no shadow
with an edge anywhere, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: chest-up and close. The boy is down on the frozen
ground, sitting back on one hip, filling the frame, his shoulders
squared and his head up. A plain wooden practice sword lies on the
frost across the bottom corner of the frame where it fell, held by
nobody.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: his right forearm is held up hard against
his own chest, the back of the hand and the wrist turned out toward
the viewer, the fingers half-curled and slack. His left hand is
closed around that forearm just below the elbow, holding it in.
Nothing is held in either hand. Both hands are a child's, small and
short in the fingers.

THE MARKS ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE AND THEY SIT DIRECTLY BELOW
HIS FACE. Across the back of the right hand and up the wrist runs a
scatter of small round marks, pale pink and white, flat to the skin
and healed — thirty or more of them, none larger than a grain of
barley, spread from the knuckles to a hand's width up the forearm.
Each one is drawn with the ink as a small ring, not left to the grain
of the paper. One more of them, at the wrist, is new: red where the
others are pale, and no bigger than they are.

HIS FACE IS HURT AND STILL WORKING. The jaw set hard, the mouth
closed, the breath held, the eyes open and wet with the lower lashes
stuck together — and the attention already back on, level, pricing
what it has just cost him. NOT weeping, NOT sobbing, NOT crying out,
NOT frightened, and not blank.

WHERE HE IS LOOKING: up and to his left, past the top corner of the
frame, at a man who is standing over him and is not in the picture.
Not at the viewer.

THE MEASUREMENT IS THE HAND AGAINST THE FACE: his right hand, held up
below his own chin, is plainly shorter from the heel of the palm to
the fingertips than his face is from the chin to the hairline. The
eyes sit a little below the vertical midpoint of the head, nearer
that midpoint than a young child's and still below it, the jaw not
come in.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest value
in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home.

The cloth stops above the elbow: no cuff, no sleeve edge and no cloth
of any kind lies on the hand, the wrist or the forearm anywhere in
the frame. Plain undyed working wool everywhere else, coarse and much
mended, the collar loose at the throat — the ink carries the cloth in
dense scratchy linework at the seams, the patches and the mended
places.

His breath stands in the cold air in front of his mouth. The frost is
broken and smeared under him where he went down, and stands unbroken
everywhere else.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: his skin is the only
warm colour anywhere, the marks on it are pinker than the skin around
them, and the new one is the only red in the picture. The frost, the
ground, the wooden sword, the yard and all his clothing are cool grey
and grey-blue with no brown in them.

HIS FACE AND THE BACK OF HIS RIGHT HAND ARE THE TWO MOST FINISHED
THINGS IN THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark.
The frost is the brightest thing in it and is reserved as bare
unpainted paper along with his breath. The wooden sword, the ground
and the yard behind stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and
no detail, and nothing back there competes for finish. The ink
linework stays the strongest element throughout and does not blur
into the wash.

There is no other person in the picture: no man standing over him,
and nobody at the edge of the yard.

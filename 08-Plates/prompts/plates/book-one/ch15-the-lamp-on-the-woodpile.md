Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the bare yard of a mountain house in the middle of the
night, as loose wash suggestion — hard bare ground with frost lying
on it, a low drystone wall at one side, and a stacked woodpile at the
left. An oil lamp stands on top of the woodpile and is the only light
in the picture. Past the edge of the yard the mountain and the sea
below it are solid black, with no horizon and no sky drawn anywhere.

Composition: 4:3. The camera stands at the yard's edge at chest
height, far enough back that both figures are in frame from the knee
up with bare ground between them, and both are grounded on the same
ground. The man is on the left, nearer the lamp, three-quarters on,
his blade held down at his side. The boy is on the right, further
from the lamp, turned to him, just come up onto his feet with his
blade brought back up in front of him.

NOTHING IS HAPPENING IN THIS SECOND. They are between exchanges. The
boy is breathing hard and the man is not.

THE MAN IS NOT HURRYING AND HAS NOT HURRIED ALL NIGHT: standing at
his own pace, the weight settled and even, the blade down and the
wrist loose, the free hand open at his side, his mouth just opening
on one short word. The eyes level and empty, on the boy — not hard,
not angry, not pleased.

THE BOY IS WORKING AND HE IS PROUD OF IT. Mouth closed, the breath
going through the nose, the brows drawn, the eyes fixed on the man's
blade hand. Dark hair stuck wet to the forehead. Nothing in his face
is frightened and nothing in it is asking for anything.

HIS FACE AND HIS HANDS ARE UNMARKED. Every mark he has is under the
shirt: nothing on the face, nothing on the neck, nothing below the
wrist on either hand.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
The man's right hand holds one blunted practice blade, point to the
ground. His left hand is empty and open.
The boy's right hand holds one blunted practice blade, brought up in
front of him. His left hand is empty and open at his shoulder.
Four hands, two blades, nothing else in any hand and no scabbard
anywhere in the frame.

The two blades are a matched pair and plain as spoons: straight
double-edged bars of steel about the length of a man's arm, with no
edge ground on either side and the point rounded off, a plain
straight crossguard, a plain leather grip, and no ornament on either
of them.

Both wear coarse undyed working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal
and dead olive, the sleeves pushed back off the forearms; no ornament
and no metal on either of them but the two blades.

Lighting: the lamp on the woodpile is warm and low and off to the
left; it lights the near side of both faces, the forearms and the
upper surfaces of the blades, and it dies out completely a few feet
past the boy, and the frost on the ground picks it up in a scatter of
small hard points. Everything the lamp does not reach is black.

Colour: the lamplight is a deep warm orange and it is the only warm
thing in the picture; the ground, the wall and the wool go cool
grey-brown where the light falls off; the boy's eyes are pale grey
and near white and are the lightest thing in his face.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. The man is the older
and much heavier of the two, and the boy is narrow through the
shoulders.

Image 1: neris.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired woman. Her
face is not visible in this picture.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a mountainside at night, seen from a bare shelf of rock
looking down into a wide shallow bowl of ground about three hundred
strides across — stone, burnt stubble, dead thorn, an old terrace
wall running along under the shelf. Low cloud sits over everything,
lit dull orange from beneath.

Composition: the woman stands at the front edge of the shelf in the
lower third of the frame, near the viewer, seen from directly behind
and a little above, small in the frame and dark. The bottom edge of
the picture crosses her at the hip. Her head is not turned and her
face is not visible. She is the only person in the picture.

HER TWO ARMS ARE OUT IN FRONT OF HER AT SHOULDER HEIGHT, level and
straight, the palms turned down. Nothing is in either hand. NOTHING
IS COMING OFF HER HANDS: no light, no glow, no spark, and nothing
drawn in the air between her and the bowl.

WHAT IS IN THE BOWL BELOW HER, EACH IN ITS OWN PLACE: a wall of fire
standing right across the bowl from one side of the frame to the
other, unbroken, low and long, about a man and a half high. In front
of it, a bare strip of ground eighty strides long where everything
has been taken off down to the stone, its near edge as clean and
straight as a scythe cut. And on the terrace just below the shelf,
three old olive trees already split open from the top down along the
grain, the trunks standing as ragged upright shells, and the long
pale strips off them out in the air clear of the trunks, low and
level and pointing away from the viewer, straight and mid-flight.

SIX MORE OLIVE TREES STAND FURTHER ALONG THE SAME TERRACE, WHOLE AND
UNSPLIT. Nine trees in the frame and no other tree anywhere in it.

No figures are drawn anywhere in the bowl and no body is lying in it.
The bottom of it beyond the fire is smoke, glare and darkness with
nothing resolved.

THE FIRE BELOW IS THE ONLY LIGHT IN THE PICTURE. It comes up from
underneath: it puts a hard orange rim along the woman's shoulders and
the outside of both arms, lights the underside of the smoke, and
leaves the shelf, the stone and the whole of her front dark.

TWO PIECES OF HER LONG DARK HAIR, AND ONLY TWO, ARE WHITE FROM THE
ROOT — a hard cold white with no warmth and no yellow in it — and
they take the light from below and are the palest thing on her. Every
other hair on her head is dark brown, with no grey, no silver and no
third white piece anywhere on her.

The white pieces in her hair and the rim on her shoulders carry the
sharpest step from light to dark near the viewer; the wall of fire is
the brightest thing in the frame but soft-edged and far.

She wears a heavy coarse village coat of undyed dark wool, worn and
mended. Preserve her build, hair colour and apparent age as shown in
the reference image.

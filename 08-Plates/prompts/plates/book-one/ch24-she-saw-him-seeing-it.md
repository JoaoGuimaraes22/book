Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a small brick court at night, high brick walls on every side
and a square of black sky above them, as loose wash suggestion. Two
small oil lamps stand on the brick rim of a cistern. They are the
only light in the picture and everything a few feet from them goes
dark.

Composition: 3:4. Four young people sit on the brick rim around a
cloth spread with the end of a supper — bread, a cold fowl, a wedge
of hard cheese, an earthenware jar. KAEL sits nearest the camera on
the left, three-quarters toward it, a plain jar just down from his
mouth and held in both hands at chest height. VALERIA sits across the
cloth facing him, a knife in her hand resting in the cheese. At the
two edges of the frame, cut by it, two more seated figures on the
same rim, heads back and mouths open in the middle of laughing, drawn
as dark summary shapes with their faces not resolved. The bottom edge
crosses the cloth and both near figures at the waist, so that no
legs, no feet and no ground are in the frame at all.

TWO FACES ARE HELD STILL IN THE MIDDLE OF OTHER PEOPLE'S LAUGHTER.
Neither of them has joined it.

KAEL — the jar down from his mouth and stopped there, his mouth
closed, his shoulders a fraction high and tight, caught in the middle
of finding something to do with his hands. Eyes pale grey. He wears
undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much
mended, no ornament and no metal on him anywhere.

VALERIA — sitting square and easy with the knife where it was, her
face level, the mouth closed and the brows unmoved, the attention
complete: somebody who has watched a thing happen from outside it and
understood the shape of it exactly. Eyes green. Black hair loose,
well past the shoulders. Her clothing as in her full-figure
reference: the charcoal sleeveless coat over the grey-blue
underlayer, the high collar, the steel vambraces.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER across the cloth, and each
of them can see that the other is not laughing either. This eye
contact is the subject of the image, and neither is looking at the
viewer.

The two lamps put warm light up onto both their faces and leave the
brick, the walls and the two laughing figures in low warm dark. Their
two faces are the most finished things in the picture and carry the
sharpest step from light to dark. Bare unpainted paper is reserved by
name along the lit edge of each face, on the jar and along the brick
rim, and the ink linework stays the strongest element in the picture
and is not softened anywhere by the warm light.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age; the two images of the girl are the
same girl and there are four figures in the picture. Do not blend
their features. Do not mix their clothing: the steel and the charcoal
coat belong only to her, the mended undyed wool only to him.

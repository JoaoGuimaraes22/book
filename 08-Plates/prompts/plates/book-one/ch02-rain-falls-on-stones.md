Image 1: kael-4.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the corner of a fenced pen on a high mountain smallholding at
the end of summer, as loose wash suggestion. A weathered grey fence
post stands at his back and runs up out of the top of the frame;
behind him thin dry grass gone straw-gold and bare pale rock, and past
that the ground falling away into flat pale wash. A white-sky day:
flat overcast, no sun disc and no cast shadow anywhere, everything
held at close middle values.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is close, out in front of him and a
little to one side, at about the height of his own head. The bottom
edge of the picture crosses just below his hands, so the ground he is
sitting on is in the frame at the very bottom and his feet are not.

He is folded down at the foot of the post with his knees drawn up
against his chest and both hands quiet and still on his shins, his
back against the timber. He has arranged himself into a shape and
stopped — not crouched ready to move, not hiding behind anything,
nothing braced anywhere in him.

HIS HEAD IS LOWERED AND HIS EYES ARE RAISED UNDER THE BROW, looking
out past the viewer's shoulder and slightly down, at something a long
way off. He is not looking at the viewer.

HIS FACE IS STILL AND IT IS WET, AND THAT CONTRADICTION IS THE SUBJECT
OF THE PICTURE. Two clear wet tracks run down from the outer corner of
each eye and across the cheek, and the lower lashes are wet and
clumped. The eyes are wide open, steady and unblinking, and they are
the one working part of the face. The mouth is closed and level, the
brows are unmoved, and nothing in the face is crumpled. The shoulders
are down and even and there is no sob anywhere in the body: the water
is the only thing on him that is moving.

THE BOY: five years old, small, the head large for the body. Eyes pale
grey, near white — the lightest value in the picture, and the sharpest
step from light to dark in it happens inside the eye, at the edge of
the iris. Dark hair, fine and soft, cut short and uneven at home by
somebody who was not being careful. Sun and wind worked into the skin,
with cool grey dust on it and in the creases of the neck. Coarse
undyed mended wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, thin and a little
too large for him, the sleeves swallowing his wrists.

Colour: the summer grass is dry straw-gold, and there is one patch of
mustard-yellow lichen on the weathered grey of the post above his
head. The post, the grass and the rock are held at quiet middle values
with soft wash-bled edges; his skin is the one warm thing in the
picture and his face is the only fully resolved thing in it.

Nobody is coming. Nothing is approaching him and nothing is being done
to him, and there is no other figure anywhere in the frame and no part
of one at any edge.

Preserve his face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: one long straight street of a walled district in the middle
of a bright morning, as loose wash suggestion — plastered house
fronts in warm ochre and dull terracotta going up both sides, a
stone gutter running down one side of it, and the street bending out
of sight at the far end. A bay tree in a terracotta pot stands
outside a door on the near side and is the one strong green in the
picture. Behind her at the bottom of the street, a heavy timber gate
stands shut with a squared oak beam bedded in iron brackets across
both leaves, and six men stand on the inside of it with their hands
on the timber, seen from behind. Further up the street, past her, a
woman carries a basin across the mouth of a courtyard, and where the
street bends two children sit on a step with a servant standing over
them, telling them something and not hurrying. Full daylight from
one side, and no lamp, torch or fire anywhere in the picture.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is in the street with her, at her own
height, a little off the line of it so that the street recedes past
her to the bend. VALERIA stands in the near half of the frame on the
left, three-quarters toward the camera, square on both feet, her
head turned up the street away from the gate. The barred gate and
the six men are behind her and below her in the picture, small with
distance. The bottom edge crosses her at the waist, so that her
hands, her hips, her legs and the ground immediately under her are
out of the frame; the street floor beyond her stays in and runs back
to the bend.

Nothing is happening. She has just been let go of and has stopped
where she stands, and the street is going on around her: a basin
carried across a doorway, two children being told something on a
step, a gate shut and held. She is the only person in the picture
who is not doing anything.

VALERIA — completely still, the weight even on both feet, the
shoulders down, both arms hanging. Nothing in her is braced and
nothing in her is slack. The face level, the eyes going up the
street and taking it in one piece, the mouth closed, the jaw set and
easy. The body is at rest and the attention is not. Not angry on the
face, not grieving, not searching.

Long black hair worn loose well past the shoulders, no ornament.
Dark near-black clothing, plain and well made, dusty and marked from
a long road, whole and not torn. Twin blades in their scabbards at
her hips, below the bottom edge and out of the frame; nothing is in
either hand. On both forearms, from the wrist to the elbow, there is
dried red gone dark and brown, and none of it is hers; nothing on
her is bleeding and there is no mark on her face.

Her eyes go up the street toward the bend. She is not looking at the
gate behind her and she is not looking at the viewer. No other face
in the picture is resolved: the six men are backs and hands, and the
woman with the basin, the servant and the two children are small and
loose at the far end of the street.

Her face carries the most finished drawing in the picture and the
sharpest step from light to dark; the walls, the gutter, the gate,
the bay tree and the people at the far end stay at quiet middle
values with soft wash-bled edges.

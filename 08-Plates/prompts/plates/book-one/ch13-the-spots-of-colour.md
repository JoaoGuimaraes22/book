Image 1: elarine-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the long hall of a temple at midday in autumn, as loose wash
suggestion — high windows down one wall, long plank tables and
benches running away behind her into quiet wash, the far end of it
going dark. Flat cold daylight coming across the hall from the
windows on her left, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: chest-up and close. One girl sits at a plank table in
the near foreground, nearest the viewer and much the largest figure
in the frame, seen in three-quarter view, bolt upright, her shoulders
square and level. The near edge of the tabletop crosses the bottom of
the frame and one plain wooden cup stands on it beside her hand.
Behind her and much further off, at the middle tables, two children
turned away up the hall, seen from behind, their heads back and their
mouths open; and beyond them, small and at the far end of the hall,
one child standing up on a bench with both arms out.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her right hand rests on the boards beside
the cup, loosely closed and still. Her left hand is in her lap and
out of frame. Nothing is held in either hand.

WHERE EVERY FACE IS LOOKING: hers is the only face in the picture,
and she is looking straight ahead, level, down the length of the
table at nothing, and not at the viewer.

THE COLOUR ON HER CHEEKS IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. High on each
cheekbone, under the eye, one patch of warm pink with a soft edge,
about the size of her own thumbprint. The rest of her face and her
throat stay pale and cool with no other colour in them.

HER FACE GIVES NOTHING BACK. Completely still, mouth closed and
level, the brows unmoved, the eyes steady, the shoulders not moved at
all. NOT embarrassed, NOT ashamed, NOT angry — and equally NOT cold,
NOT haughty, NOT bored.

SHE IS TWELVE AND SMALL FOR IT, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS THE CUP: the
eyes sit a little below the vertical midpoint of the head, nearer
that midpoint than a young child's and still below it. Her hand on
the boards is a child's, and the wooden cup standing beside it is as
tall as her hand is long.

Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral black with no blue in it, drawn back
severely and bound tight at the nape, nothing loose anywhere. Pale
indoor skin. Plain dark close-fitting clothing of very good quality,
exactly made and buttoned high at the throat. No ornament, no metal
and no badge anywhere on her.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the two patches of
colour on her cheekbones are the only warm colour in the whole
picture. Her skin is pale and cool, and the boards, the cup, the
stone, the benches, the air and all the clothing in the hall are cool
grey and grey-blue with no brown in them.

HER FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING IN THE PICTURE and carries the
sharpest step from light to dark, along the lit edge of it where the
window light comes across. The two children behind her are looser and
softer-edged with no detail drawn in them, the standing child beyond
them is a loose dark shape with no features at all, and the
rest of the hall is summary shapes at the edges with nothing drawn in
them. The daylight from the high windows and the light lying along
the scrubbed boards are reserved as bare unpainted paper. The ink
linework stays the strongest element throughout and does not blur
into the wash.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: an old open flagstone court at the seaward edge of a temple,
early on an autumn morning, as loose wash suggestion — broken
colonnade on three sides, gorse and low scrub crowding the fourth,
the sea beyond going into pale wash. Low early sunlight coming across
the court from behind the scrub, and it is the only light in the
picture.

Composition: seen from behind the girl and slightly above her
shoulder. She stands in the near foreground with her back squarely to
the viewer, cropped at the waist by the bottom edge, filling the right
of the frame; the back of her head and her shoulders are in frame and
no part of her face is. The flagstones run away unbroken from her feet
to the far edge of the court, and at that far edge, small in the
frame, a boy stands upright at the top of the scrub. Two plain wooden
practice swords lean against the base of a column at the left, held
by nobody.

THE DISTANCE BETWEEN THEM IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE, AND IT IS
STATED AS A MEASUREMENT: the whole standing boy, head to foot, is
about as tall in the frame as the back of the girl's head is wide. If
he is bigger than that, the distance is wrong.

THE BOY HAS JUST STOOD UP AND HIS HANDS ARE WHERE SHE CAN SEE THEM:
both arms out from his body and lifted a little, palms open and
forward, holding nothing. He stands square to her, not moving.

THE GIRL HAS NOT TURNED AROUND. Her head is level and faces away
across the court, her shoulders square and still, her weight even.
Nothing about her is turned toward him.

Her black hair is well past the shoulders, worn loose, no ornament.
She wears plain grey cloth, close, exactly made and austere, with no
badge and no decoration. The boy wears plain undyed working wool,
coarse and much mended. The grey belongs only to the girl and the
mended wool only to the boy.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the low sun on the
tops of the flagstones and on the boy's outline is the only warm
colour, and the colonnade, the scrub, the sea, the wooden swords and
all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue with no brown in them.

THE BACK OF THE GIRL AND THE SMALL STANDING BOY ARE THE TWO FINISHED
THINGS IN THE PICTURE, and the sharpest step from light to dark
happens along her shoulder. The colonnade, the scrub and the sea stay
at quiet middle values with soft edges and no detail. The lit tops of
the flagstones are reserved as bare unpainted paper. The ink linework
stays the strongest element and does not blur into the wash.

There is nobody else in the picture: no third person anywhere in the
court, in the colonnade or at the edge of the scrub.

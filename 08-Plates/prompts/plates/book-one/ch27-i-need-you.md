Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the floor of a great stone arena in fog. Heavy new timber
close behind the boy — the boarded side of an enclosure — and beyond
them the bowl of the building, packed and moving, with one curve of
seating broken and burning a long way off and grey smoke going up
into the fog. Flat cold fogged daylight on the two of them, no sun and
no hard-edged shadow.

Composition: two figures, very close together and near the viewer,
the bottom edge of the picture crossing both at the lower chest. His
back and one shoulder are against the timber. She stands squared in
front of him with her face a hand's width from his, both of them in
three-quarter view and turned toward each other. Nobody else is drawn
near them.

HER LEFT FIST IS CLOSED ON THE CLOTH AT HIS SHOULDER, holding him
against the boards. There is a plain short blade still in that same
hand and another in her right hand, both held clear and pointing away
from him, and neither blade is near either of their bodies. Nothing
is raised and nothing is being swung.

HIS HEAD IS STILL TURNED A LITTLE TO ONE SIDE and he is coming back
into himself: the eyes just arrived on her and focused, wide open with
the pale grey clear, the mouth slightly open, the brows level. He is
not resisting her and not braced.

HER FACE IS ENTIRELY PROFESSIONAL AND ALREADY PAST IT: the chin level,
the lids fully raised, the eyes steady on his, the brows unmoved, the
mouth closed and even. Nothing in it is angry and nothing in it is
pleading. Not fierce, not tender, not desperate, not wistful.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER, and neither is looking at
the viewer.

Her black hair is BOUND — gathered close against the head and tied
back at the nape, with nothing falling loose past her shoulders. Her
clothing as in her full-figure reference: the charcoal sleeveless coat
over the grey-blue underlayer, the high collar, the steel vambraces.
There is red down one of her bare forearms, dark and drying, and a
reddened burn on the side of her neck. He wears undyed coarse working
wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, torn at one
shoulder, with no metal anywhere on him.

Their two faces are the most finished things in the picture and carry
the sharpest step from light to dark; the timber, the burning curve,
the smoke and the crowd stay at quiet middle values with soft edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age; the two images of the girl are the same
girl and there are two figures in the picture. Do not blend their
features. Do not mix their clothing: the steel and the charcoal coat
belong only to her, the mended undyed wool only to him. Her eyes
green, his pale grey.

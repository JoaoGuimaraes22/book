Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of Kael.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the mouth of a competitors' gate under the banked seating of
a great open arena, on a bright cold winter morning. Cut stone and
new timber, the tunnel behind him going dark. Beyond the opening,
raked white sand and rank on rank of packed seating under an open
sky, drawn as a dense wash of small shapes with no faces anywhere in
it.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is inside the gate mouth with him, close
and a little to one side, so that he stands large in the near dark
and the bright opening rises beyond his shoulder and fills the upper
corner of the frame. KAEL is in the mouth of the gate, three-quarters
toward the camera, facing out. The bottom edge of the picture crosses
him just below the belt, so that no legs, no feet, no floor and no
other person's body are in the frame at all.

HIS HAND HAS GONE TO THE KNIFE AND HIS FACE HAS NOT CAUGHT UP WITH
IT. His right hand is shut round the hilt of a plain knife at his
belt, the whole fist closed on it and the arm tight in against the
body, and nothing in his face has anything to do with that hand. His
left hand hangs open and empty at his side.

His face: the eyes wide open and level, out and up at the packed
seating beyond the opening, the mouth closed, the jaw set — working,
and not frightened.

He wears undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive,
much mended, with no ornament, badge, colour or metal on him
anywhere. A wrapped bundle rides across his back on a cord, closed,
with nothing of what is in it showing.

Lighting: everything in the picture is lit through the opening —
cold white winter daylight off the sand, full on his face and along
one shoulder, with the stone around and behind him going to deep
shadow.

His face carries the sharpest step from light to dark and is the most
finished thing in the picture; the crowd, the seating and the stone
stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

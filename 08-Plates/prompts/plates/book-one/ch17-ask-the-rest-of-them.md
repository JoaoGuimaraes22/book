Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the steps of an old disused sea court in the morning, as
loose wash suggestion — three shallow stone steps running across the
picture, a broken colonnade of plain columns implied behind and above
them, and past that open grey water with no far shore in it. Soft
daylight from one side under an overcast, no hard shadows anywhere.

Composition: 4:3. The camera is low, at about the height of the
second step, and close to both of them. The girl sits on the steps at
the right, in frame from the knee up, her forearms across her knees
and her hands hanging loose off them, her head hanging forward. The
boy is crouched on the balls of his feet on the step below her at the
left, one forearm across his knee and the other hand flat on the
stone, his face turned up to hers. Both are grounded on the same
steps.

SHE HAS SPENT EVERYTHING SHE HAS AND SHE HAS COME BACK TO ANSWER THE
NEXT QUESTION. This is the state after, not the spending: the head
down, the shoulders dropped, the back not straight for once, the skin
grey and wet at the hairline, the mouth a little open with the breath
going through it. She is not weeping and she is not finished. She is
answering.

HE IS ASKING THE NEXT ONE. His face up at her, his mouth open
mid-word, the brows drawn, the eyes entirely on her face. Nothing in
him is worried; the whole of him is occupied by the question. Not
tender, not alarmed.

His eyes are on her face and her eyes are down on the stone between
her own boots, and neither of them is looking at the viewer.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: four hands and nothing in any of them. Both her
blades lie sheathed on the step beside her, out of both hands, and no
other blade appears anywhere in the frame.

Her clothing as in her full-figure reference: the fitted sleeveless
coat in deep charcoal over the close-fitting grey-blue long-sleeved
underlayer, the high collar, the plain steel vambraces on both
forearms. The boy wears coarse undyed working wool, much mended, in
dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no ornament, no metal and no white
anywhere on him.

Colour: cool grey stone and cool grey water throughout; the coat
charcoal, the underlayer grey-blue, the vambraces dull steel; the
only warmth in the picture is in the two faces.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age; her two images are the same girl and
there is one girl in this picture. Do not blend their features. Do
not mix their clothing: the charcoal coat and the steel belong only
to her and the boy wears no metal.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a disused cistern court in the cold term, as loose wash
suggestion — three walls of old brick close around it, warm dark
grey-brown with moss in the joints, and a wet brick floor with
standing water lying in the hollows of it. A plank bench against the
far wall. Soft grey daylight from the open side.

Composition: 4:3. The camera stands at the open side of the court at
head height. The floor runs away from the bottom edge and all three
figures are grounded on it. On the left, alone, the fair-haired boy
stands three-quarters on to the camera and turned toward the bench,
in frame from the knee up, his feet together and both hands empty at
his sides. On the right, on the bench, the small girl sits with her
back straight, and the dark-haired boy sits at the far end of the
same bench. Between the standing boy and the bench there is a clear
stretch of wet brick, four strides of it, with nothing in it.

THE DISTANCE ACROSS THAT WET BRICK IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. He
has stopped exactly there — close enough to speak low, far enough to
be refused — and he has not come one step further.

THE FAIR-HAIRED BOY IS PAYING FOR SOMETHING AT FULL RATE, IN THE
OPEN, IN FRONT OF A WITNESS. His mouth is closed on the end of a
short sentence, the chin level, the eyes steady on the bench, the
shoulders held. Not sullen, not sneering, not smug, and not humble.
Good dark wool, well cut, in deep umber and warm charcoal, an open
collar over a clean white undershirt, no ornament and no metal.

THE SMALL GIRL IS PRICING HIM. One eyebrow up a fraction, the mouth
closed, her attention entirely on him and giving nothing back. On her
open left palm, held level at her waist, a small coin of flame stands
upright and perfectly still — no wick, nothing under it, the size of
a coin and no larger. Plain close-fitting dark clothing of very good
quality, a high buttoned collar to the throat, ink-black and dark
olive; her hair drawn back severely and bound tight at the nape,
nothing loose; no ornament of any kind.

THE DARK-HAIRED BOY SAYS NOTHING. His face level, his mouth closed,
his hands on his knees, his eyes on the standing boy. Coarse undyed
working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, no
ornament, no metal and no white anywhere on him.

Both seated figures are looking at the standing boy and he is looking
at them; nobody in the picture is looking at the viewer.

Colour: the brick warm grey-brown, the standing water holding a pale
grey sky, the umber wool the one warm note in any cloth; the coin of
flame is the smallest bright thing in the frame and it does not light
anybody's face.

Preserve all three exactly as shown in their reference images —
faces, hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. Do
not mix their clothing: the umber wool and the white collar belong
only to the fair-haired boy, and neither of the other two wears white
or any ornament.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: portrait reference of the old man.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the end of a footpath high on a bare mountainside at first
light, as loose wash suggestion — thin frozen grass, bare rock, the
sea far below in flat pale wash, the sky bare unpainted paper. Flat
cold dawn light coming up off the sea, no sun disc and no warm colour
anywhere.

A natural boulder stands beside them, about the height of the man:
irregular in outline, weathered all over, broader at the base than at
the top, with no straight edges and no flat faces. A rough fracture
runs down through it, wide and uneven, opened by frost. It is not a
monument, not a menhir and nothing about it is shaped or placed by
hands.

Composition: the two of them facing each other on the path, cropped
at the knee, the boulder behind and between them. The top of the
boy's head reaches the man's chest.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS
THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE.

THE BOY HAS JUST BEEN ASKED SOMETHING HE CANNOT ANSWER. His mouth is
open on a word that is not coming, his eyes still steady on the man's
— caught mid-sentence with nothing behind it. NOT ashamed, NOT
confused, NOT distressed, NOT frightened, NOT sullen; his chin stays
up and his eyes do not drop. A stop, not a feeling.

The man waits. Unhurried, courteous, giving nothing away, mouth
closed, brows level — a face that has priced this and is letting it
sit. NOT kind, NOT stern, NOT amused, NOT expectant.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: the man's right hand rests on the head of a
plain walking stick whose tip is on the ground beside him, carrying
none of his weight — he stands square and even on both feet. His left
hand is empty at his side. The boy's right hand is on the strap of a
cloth bag over his shoulder; his left hangs empty. Nothing else is
carried and there is no other object in the picture.

The boy's face is the most finished thing in the picture and carries
the sharpest step from light to dark. The man, the boulder, the path
and all the clothing are held at quiet middle values with no hard
edges.

THE BOY: ten years old, eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and
colourless — no more colour in the iris than in the whites around it.
Dark hair cut short and uneven. Wind-chapped, high colour across the
cheeks and nose, cool grey dust on the skin. Coarse undyed mended
wool, a little too large for him.

THE MAN: tall, lean and still broad across the shoulder, standing
with a scholar's stoop — head carried forward of the shoulders, the
upper back rounded. Hair fully grey, the whole head, an even neutral
grey, with no streak, seam or patch anywhere in it. Old and worn down
to structure, deep lines from nose to mouth and at the outer corners
of the eyes, skin dry and drawn tight over the bone, and no
frailty anywhere in him. HIS GARMENT, BY CONSTRUCTION: one long
straight dark overgarment falling to mid-calf, close at the shoulder
and loose below, closed edge to edge down the centre front, with no
lapel, no turned collar, no placket and no pockets. A standing collar
at the base of the throat with no shirt collar showing. Plain sleeves
ending square at a bare wrist, and the wrists are thick and heavy.
Nothing nineteenth-century: not a frock coat, not a greatcoat, not a
suit, and no waistcoat under it.

The ink carries the cloth on both of them, in dense scratchy linework
at the seams and the mended places.

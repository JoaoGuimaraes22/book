Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the open stone court of a temple on a grey autumn morning,
as loose wash suggestion — wet-looking flagstones, a pale stone wall
behind, the far side of the court going into quiet wash. Flat
overcast daylight, no sun and no shadows with edges, and it is the
only light in the picture.

Composition: close on the boy's head, shoulders and one hand, seen
from the side, his head tipped down toward his own hand. The near
edge of a low table crosses the bottom of the frame, and on it a
shallow round iron dish about two hands across, seen from slightly
above so its far rim shows, with a plain round copper bar the length
of a man's forearm lying on a folded cloth beside it.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: his right hand is open, fingers together
and a little spread, held a hand's width above the copper bar and
touching nothing. His left hand is down at his side and out of
frame. Nothing is held in either hand.

THE STITCH OF LIGHT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE AND IT IS DRAWN AS
BARE UNPAINTED PAPER. One single straight thread of white runs from
his fingertips to the copper bar, a hand's width long, no thicker
than a drawn line, unbroken from end to end — reserved paper with
the ink drawing stopping at its edges, not white paint. NO FORK, no
branch, no zigzag, no second strand, no scattered sparks, and no
halo or bloom of light around it. Nothing in the picture is lit by
it.

HIS ATTENTION IS ENTIRELY ON HIS OWN HAND. Eyes down, the lids
lowered, the head tipped to it. Still, mouth closed, the face not
yet caught up with what has happened — a boy recognising something
he turns out to have known for years. NOT delighted, NOT triumphant,
NOT proud, NOT pleased — and equally NOT frightened, NOT startled,
NOT alarmed, and not blank. There is nobody in the frame he could
be looking at.

HE IS ELEVEN AND THE FACE CARRIES IT: the eyes sit a little below
the vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still below it; the jaw has not come in, the cheeks are
still full under the bone, the neck narrow. His hand is a child's,
small and short-fingered, and the copper bar is longer than his
forearm.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest
value in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home. Plain
undyed working wool, coarse and much mended, the collar loose at the
throat — the ink carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the
seams, the patches and the mended places. No ornament and no metal.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: his skin and the
copper bar carry the only warm colour, and the iron, the flagstones,
the wall, the cloth and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue
with no brown in them. His face and his open hand are the most
finished things in the picture and carry the sharpest step from
light to dark. The court and the rest of the table stay at quiet
middle values with soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there
competes for finish. The ink linework stays the strongest element
throughout and does not blur into the wash.

NO OTHER FACE IS DRAWN. The other children stand behind him as dark
summary shapes at the edges of the frame, loosely washed, with no
features, and there is no adult anywhere in the picture.

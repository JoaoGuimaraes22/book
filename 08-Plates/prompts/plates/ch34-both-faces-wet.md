Image 2: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights. Plus dry-brush drag for the movement.

Setting: open goat ground high on a mountain at night, as loose wash
suggestion — dark stony ground going away behind them and a low
dry-stone terrace wall running off into the dark, everything past
ten feet lost. The underside of a low cloud overhead is orange from
one side of the frame to the other and is the only light in the
picture.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is alongside them and level with their
faces, close, going with them. NERIS is nearest and half a pace
ahead, on the right, three-quarters away from the camera with her
head turned back and down toward the boy. KAEL is behind and below
her, on the left, three-quarters toward the camera with his head
turned up and across to her. Both are mid-stride with the weight
forward, the hair and the loose cloth carried back off them and both
mouths open on breath. The bottom edge crosses both of them at the
waist.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. Her eyes down to him,
his eyes up to her, the lines of sight meeting across the half pace
between them. This eye contact is the subject of the image.

NERIS — one forearm laid flat across the front of her coat, holding
it closed over something that is not visible; the other arm back and
open. Her face is wet all the way through, the water down both sides
of the nose and along the jaw and going into the collar, and her
mouth is doing nothing at all. Long dark brown hair loose and
carried back, dark brown throughout, and in it two quartz-white
marks near each other, each about the width of a finger, running
from the root down the length of one piece — marks, not grey and not
age. Plain village working cloth in undyed oatmeal under a heavy
dark brown coat closed to the throat.

KAEL — both hands closed on the grip of a sword far too big for him,
held down and across in front of his body with the blade going out
through the bottom edge of the frame. His face is wet all the way
through as well, the water on the jaw and not wiped; the mouth open
on breath, the brows flat, the eyes up and steady on his mother.
Undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much
mended, and it is more red than cloth now, dark and gone brown, and
none of it is his.

The two faces carry the only finished drawing in the picture and the
sharpest step from light to dark; the ground, the terrace wall, the
coat and the sword have no hard edge anywhere in them.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. The
heavy dark coat belongs only to the woman. Her eyes are dark; his
are pale near-white grey and are the lightest value in his face.

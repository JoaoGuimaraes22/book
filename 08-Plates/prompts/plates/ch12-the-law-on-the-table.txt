Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house at night, as loose wash
suggestion — a plank wall behind, the corners going into deep cool
shadow. One small oil lamp stands on the table and is the only light
in the picture.

Composition: waist-up, three people at a plank table, the tabletop
crossing the lower third of the frame. The boy sits at the near left,
seen from the side so his face is in three-quarter view. The man and
the woman sit opposite him, facing him and the viewer. The lamp
stands at the far end of the table.

FOUR HANDS ON THE BOARDS, AND THEY ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. The
woman's two hands lie flat on the table, palms down, fingers
together. Directly opposite them, a hand's width away across the same
boards, the boy's two hands lie flat in exactly the same way, palms
down, fingers together. The man's two hands rest together on the table beyond them and are
still. Nothing is held by anybody and there is nothing on the table
but the lamp.

WHERE EVERY FACE IS LOOKING: the boy is looking at the man, the man
is looking back at the boy, and the woman is looking at the boy. None
of them is looking at the viewer. Three closed mouths, and nobody is
moving.

THE BOY has just been refused something and has not accepted it: the
face level, the eyes steady on the man, the jaw set but easy. NOT
angry, NOT pleading, NOT tearful, and not blank. Eyes near-white
neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest value in his face.
Dark hair cut short and uneven.

HE IS ELEVEN, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS THE HANDS: the eyes sit a little
below the vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a
young child's and still below it, the jaw not come in and the cheeks
still full under the bone — and his flat hand on the boards is half
the width and half the length of the woman's flat hand directly
across from it.

THE MAN gives nothing: level, unhurried, the brows unmoved, the eyes
empty — not angry, not cold, not kind, not sad. Iron-dark hair with
grey scattered through it at the temples, salt through the dark, no
streak and no band of white anywhere. A face weathered far past its
years, broad across the top of the back and no further.

THE WOMAN is completely still, and the stillness is the whole of her:
fine bones under worked hands, weather on her but no coarseness, the
face handsome and unremarkable at once. Not warm, not maternal, not
soft, and not stern. Dark hair, dark eyes.

All three wear coarse undyed village cloth, much mended and closed at
the throat — the ink carries the cloth at the seams and the mended
places on all three.

THE LAMP IS THE ONLY WARM THING AND ITS LIGHT FALLS FIRST ON THE
HANDS: the boards and the four hands take the warm light, the faces
take less of it, and the walls, the corners and all the clothing stay
cool grey and grey-blue. The lamp's flame and the lit edge of the
tabletop are reserved as bare unpainted paper.

THE FOUR HANDS AND THE THREE FACES ARE THE FINISHED THINGS IN THIS
PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark; the room
behind them stays at quiet middle values with soft edges and no
detail. The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and
does not blur into the wash.

Preserve all three exactly as shown in their reference images —
faces, hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features.

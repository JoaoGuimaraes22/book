Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the old man.

Setting: a steep mountain path coming up to a smallholding yard,
midmorning, as loose wash suggestion — thin grass, bare rock, the
sea far below in pale flat wash, the sky bare unpainted paper.

Composition: the old man full length on the path, coming up toward
the viewer, most of the frame. THE VIEWER IS LOW, at the eye height
of a boy of nine, so the man is seen slightly from below.

HIS POSTURE AND HIS BODY DISAGREE, AND BOTH MUST SURVIVE. He walks
with a scholar's stoop — head carried forward, shoulders rounded —
and it sits on him like borrowed clothing rather than belonging to
him. Underneath it he is tall, lean and still broad across the
shoulder, and HIS WRISTS COMING OUT OF THE SLEEVES ARE THICK AND
HEAVY, too thick for a man whose work is paper. The wrists are the
most precisely drawn thing in the picture. Do not resolve him into
a frail old scholar.

Hair fully grey, the whole head, a true even neutral grey — not
warm silver, not white. No streak, no seam, no patch, nothing
gathered anywhere in it.

Old and worn down to structure: deep lines from nose to mouth,
across the forehead and at the outer corners of the eyes, skin dry
and drawn tight over the bone. No hollow cheeks, no sunken temples,
no thin neck, no slackness, no frailty. Face courteous and
unhurried, giving nothing away — not warm, not kindly, not stern.

A plain dark coat, well cut and well kept, no ornament of any kind:
too plain for a gentleman and too well made for a villager. One
wrapped bundle carried on a strap over the shoulder, his left hand
resting on the strap. His right hand is empty and open at his side.
Nothing else is carried. THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH in dense
scratchy linework at the seams and the strap.

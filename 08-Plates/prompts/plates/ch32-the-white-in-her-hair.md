Image 2: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the inside of a small one-room stone house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — rough boards under them, a sheepskin fleece, the edge
of a hearth close by at the left with a big fire standing up in it. The
fire is the only strong light and it is low and close and warm; the room
above them goes dark quickly and the far side of it is lost.

Composition: two heads, very close together and near the viewer. The boy
is on his back on the fleece at the bottom of the frame, seen from above
and slightly to one side, his face turned up and filling the lower part
of the picture. The woman is above him, leaning down over him, her face
in three-quarter view and fully visible in the upper part. THE BOTTOM
EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER JUST ABOVE THE COLLARBONE: nothing of
her below the collar is in the frame.

WHAT IS IN THE FRAME, EACH IN ITS OWN PLACE: her two arms come down into
the picture from above, and both her hands are flat on the boy's chest
at the bottom edge, the fingers spread, the wrists loose. A man's two
hands come into the frame from the top corner behind the boy's head and
rest on the boy's two shoulders, holding him steady and not gripping;
the man himself is not otherwise in the picture. The boy's own hands are
not in the frame. Nothing is in any hand.

BETWEEN THE TWO FACES, ONE PIECE OF HER HAIR HANGS DOWN out of the rest,
come loose, thick as a finger, falling past the boy's cheek and hanging
clear in the air. THAT ONE PIECE IS WHITE FROM THE ROOT TO THE END — a
hard cold white with no warmth and no yellow in it, the white of a split
stone. EVERY OTHER HAIR ON HER HEAD IS DARK BROWN AND UNCHANGED: no
grey, no silver and no second white piece anywhere on her.

HER FACE IS WORKING: the mouth a little open with the breath going
through the teeth, the jaw set, sweat standing along the hairline and at
the temple, the lids lowered and the eyes down on her own hands. She is
not looking at the boy's face and she is not looking at the viewer.

HIS EYES ARE ON THE HANGING PIECE OF HAIR, not on her face and not on
the viewer: the lids fully raised, the pale grey clear, the head
slightly turned toward it. His mouth is open on one short word.

He is stripped to the waist on the fleece with grey dust and dried dark
red on him, older and browner on the forearms. Her sleeves are pushed up
to the elbow and her forearms are bare and worked.

The white piece of hair carries the sharpest step from light to dark in
the picture; both faces are finished but held at closer values, and the
fire, the boards and the room stay soft and quiet. The firelight is
orange on the near sides of both faces and on her forearms, and the
white hair takes none of it.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces, hair
colour, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. Her eyes
are dark; his are pale grey.

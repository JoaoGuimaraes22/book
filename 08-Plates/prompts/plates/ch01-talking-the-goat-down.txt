Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his face, hair and
colouring from it only.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house in a
storm, as loose wash suggestion — a fenced pen, a low roof-line
implied behind, the mountain beyond lost in grey wash. Rain coming
across at a steep angle: dry-brush drag and flicked spatter through
the whole background. Everything soaked and dark with wet.

Composition: three figures close together and touching, cropped at
about the knee, all grounded on the same ground plane. THE MAN down
on one knee in the mud at the goat's level, turned in toward the
animal. THE GOAT standing in front of him, side-on, its head near
his. THE BOY standing at the man's side, pressed in against his
shoulder, very small. The three of them form one close group filling
the middle of the frame, with the storm loose and open around them.

THE MAN IS DOWN AT THE ANIMAL'S HEIGHT, AND THAT IS THE SUBJECT OF
THE PICTURE. A large man folded down small, on one knee, close.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
His right hand is flat and still on the goat's neck, resting there
with the fingers spread, not gripping and not scratching. His left
hand is down, resting on his own raised knee, open. Two hands, no
object in either. The boy's near hand is holding a fold of the man's
sleeve. THERE IS NO SLATE, no tool, no rope, no stick and no bucket
anywhere in this picture.

THE MAN IS TALKING TO THE GOAT: mouth open mid-word, his face turned
to the animal, not to the boy and not to the viewer. HIS EXPRESSION
DOES NOT CHANGE FOR ANY OF IT — level, unhurried, giving nothing
away, exactly as unreadable as a man discussing weather. NOT
smiling, NOT tender, NOT soft, NOT gentle-faced, NOT sad. The
kindness in this picture is in where his body is and what his hand
is doing, never in his face.

THE GOAT HAS STOPPED BEING FRIGHTENED. Ordinary and shaggy, soaked
through, standing still with its head lowered slightly toward the
man, ears loose, no white showing in the eye. It was upset a moment
ago and it is not upset now. This is the only evidence in the frame
that anything kind is happening.

THE BOY IS FOUR YEARS OLD AND MUST READ AS FOUR. Very small — his
head no higher than the kneeling man's shoulder. The head large in
proportion to the body, shoulders narrow and soft, arms and legs
short with no length in them. He is a toddler, not a boy of seven or
eight. Dark hair, fine and soft, cut short and uneven at home,
plastered flat with rain. HIS FACE IS TURNED AWAY FROM THE VIEWER
AND DOWN TOWARD THE GOAT — seen from behind and to one side, the
back and side of his head only. NO EYE, NO BROW AND NO FEATURE OF
HIS FACE IS DRAWN. Do not turn him toward the viewer.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is in danger, nobody is hurrying,
nobody is calling out, nothing is falling. It is bad weather and an
ordinary chore.

Do not mix their cloth: the man wears coarse undyed working cloth,
heavy weave, a plain collar closed to the throat, much mended, in
dull grey-brown and dead olive, soaked dark; the boy wears plain
undyed wool, coarse and much mended and a little too big for him, in
dull oatmeal, soaked dark. No ornament on either of them, no buckle,
no badge.

Light: flat, cold, even storm light from a low grey sky. No warm
source anywhere — no fire, no lamp, no sun. The only warm value in
the picture is the skin of the man's face and hand.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No woman, nobody watching,
no other animals.

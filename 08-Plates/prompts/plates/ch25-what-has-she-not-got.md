Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: bare open ground outside a mountain house before sunrise, as
loose wash suggestion — a stone step underfoot, a bare slope going up
behind them in pale cool grey, and a track running away downhill past
them into shadow. Cold flat pre-dawn light, no sun anywhere in the
picture and no hard-edged shadow.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is close and a little below them, side
on, with the house and its doorway out of the frame entirely. VASK
stands on the left, square to the boy, filling most of the height of
the picture; KAEL faces him, a head and a half shorter and narrow
beside him. The bottom edge crosses both of them at the lower ribs,
so that no legs, no feet and no ground beyond the step are in the
frame at all. Nobody else is anywhere in the picture.

HE HAS JUST LET GO OF THE ARM. The man's hands have come away and
hang where they hang, half curled, as if round something set down.
The boy's left arm is at his side, bound above the elbow in four
turns of plain cloth, the last turn newly pulled tight and its end
tucked, stained dark brown lower down. Neither of them is touching
the other now.

VASK — he has asked a question, the answer has come back, and he is
taking it: standing exactly still, weight even, shoulders level, the
eyes steady on the boy's face and giving nothing back, the mouth
closed and the jaw easy. Not warm. Plain village working cloth in
undyed oatmeal, worn soft, the sleeves down, no ornament and no metal
anywhere on him.

KAEL — he has just answered and is waiting to be told whether it was
right: the eyes up and level on the man, the mouth closed, the chin a
fraction up, the whole attention on him. Undyed coarse working wool
in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much mended, nothing on him anywhere.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER and neither is looking at
the viewer. This eye contact is the subject of the image.

Their two faces carry the most finished drawing and the sharpest step
from light to dark in the picture; the slope, the track and the step
stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. The man's
eyes are iron-dark; the boy's are pale near-white grey.

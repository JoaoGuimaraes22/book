Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the long hall of a temple at midday in winter, as loose wash
suggestion — a stone wall and one high window behind, long plank
tables and benches running away into quiet wash, lamp-smoke in the
upper air. Flat cold daylight from the high window, and it is the
only light in the picture.

Composition: waist-up. The boy sits at the very end of a bench in the
near foreground, closest to the viewer and the largest figure in the
frame, turned three-quarters away from the others. Behind him and
further off, at the middle tables, three other children: one standing
with a serving tray and his mouth open mid-word, and two seated
and turned right round toward him.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: the boy's two hands rest on the table in
front of him, the right one holding a torn piece of bread. The
standing child's left hand holds the tray against his hip and his
right hand is open in the air in front of him. Nothing else is held
by anybody.

WHERE EVERY FACE IS LOOKING. The
two seated children are turned right round and are looking at the boy
with the tray. He is looking at them. The boy in
the foreground is looking down at the bread in his own hands and not
at the viewer.

NOTHING IS SHOWING IN HIS FACE, AND THAT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
IMAGE. Still, mouth closed, the eyes lowered
and easy, the brows unmoved, both hands quiet on the table. NOT
shocked, NOT grieving, NOT frightened — and equally NOT amused, NOT
curious, and not blank. He is eating.

HE IS ELEVEN AND TWO THINGS CARRY IT: the eyes sit a little below the
vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still clearly below it, the jaw not come in and the
cheeks still full under the bone. AND THE MEASUREMENT: his hand on
the table is a child's and is plainly smaller and shorter-fingered
than the standing boy's hand on the tray, and seated at a grown
man's table the top of the boards comes to the height of his lower
ribs.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest value
in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home. Plain undyed
working wool, coarse and much mended, the collar loose at the throat
— the ink carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the seams,
the patches and the mended places.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: skin and the bread
carry the only warm colour, and the stone, the tables, the benches,
the air and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue with no
brown in them. The light from the high window and the cut face of the
bread are reserved as bare unpainted paper.

HIS FACE AND HIS TWO HANDS ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN THE
PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The three
children behind him are looser and softer-edged, and the rest of the
hall is dark summary shapes at the edges with no features drawn at
all. The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and does
not blur into the wash.

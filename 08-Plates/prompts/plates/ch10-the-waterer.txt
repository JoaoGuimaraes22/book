Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the man.

Setting: a mountain kitchen garden on a summer afternoon, as loose
wash suggestion — a dug bed with three low rows of young bean plants
running away behind, a drystone wall and thin grass beyond it, the far
edge falling into quiet wash. High side sunlight, hard and clear, and
it is the only light.

Composition: close on the man and the machine together, the machine
filling the lower third of the frame and the man crouched over it,
cropped at mid-thigh. He is seen from the side, his face above his own
hand.

THE MACHINE, BY CONSTRUCTION: a hollowed half-log a little longer than
his forearm, hung crosswise on one iron pin driven through it nearer
one end than the other so that it swings like a balance, its long end
tipped down toward the rows. At the short end a rounded stone the size
of two fists is lashed on with cord. Behind it a split log laid open
as a channel brings a thin fall of water down into the trough from
higher ground. It is rough farm carpentry, cut and pegged by hand,
with no wheel, no frame, no crank and no metal on it but the one pin.

THE TROUGH IS STOPPED MID-POUR AND ONE FINGER IS STOPPING IT. His
right forefinger rests on the trough's high edge and holds it still,
and nothing else touches it. The water from the channel is still
running into it, and the last of the pour runs off the low lip in a
thin thread into the cut between two rows. His left hand is flat on
the ground taking his weight. Nothing is held in either hand.

HIS HAND AND THE PIN ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN THE PICTURE and
carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The rows, the wall and the
ground behind stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no
detail in them, and nothing back there competes for finish. The sunlit
top edge of the trough and the falling water are reserved as bare
unpainted paper. The ink linework stays the strongest element
throughout and does not blur into the wash.

THE PICTURE IS WARM AND THE COOL IN IT IS NAMED: the timber of the
trough and the channel, the dry ground and his skin carry the warmth;
the water, the lashed stone and the shadow under the bed are cool grey
and grey-blue with no brown in them.

HIS FACE GIVES NOTHING. His attention is entirely on the pin's seat
under his finger, mouth closed, brows level and not lowered. NOT
pleased, NOT proud, NOT approving, NOT fond, NOT amused — and equally
NOT disapproving, NOT critical, NOT stern, NOT angry and NOT tired.
Level, and giving nothing back.

Weathered far past his years, a face like used tools. Broad across the
top of the back and no further, nothing wasted below it, the crouch
settled and easy with no strain in it. Iron-dark hair going
grey at the temples as a diffuse scatter through the dark, salt
through it, with NO streak, NO seam, NO band and NO patch of white
anywhere in it. Iron-dark eyes. Village working cloth, coarse and
mended, closed at the throat, the sleeves down to the wrist — the ink
carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the seams and the
mended places. He wears no ornament and no metal.

THERE IS NO OTHER PERSON IN THE PICTURE: no boy, no woman, nobody at
the edge of the plot and nobody in the background.

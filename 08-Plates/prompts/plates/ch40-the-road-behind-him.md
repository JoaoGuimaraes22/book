Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: a road on a long high back of open country, late in the
afternoon. Brown ground with grey-green scrub low in the hollows,
stone breaking through at the edges, thorn standing in a dry cut off
to one side. The road is dust over stone and it runs straight and
rises.

Composition: 3:4. The viewer stands on the road itself, low, at the
back of everything, and the frame stacks away upward. Nearest, the
bottom third: empty road, dust and stone, long shadows running down
it toward the viewer. Above that, THE RIDER, seen from behind and a
little to the left, dark against the pale road, turned round in his
saddle and looking back down it. Above him, small and strung out
along the rising road, the rest of the column — riders in ones and
twos, with a long gap of empty road between him and the last of
them. Above them, smaller again, a black box on four wheels behind
four mules, a barred slot in its door and two men on the seat. At
the top, the road going over the rise, and pale sky.

HE IS LOOKING AT AN EMPTY ROAD, and that is the subject of the
image. There is nothing whatever on the road behind him. The picture
holds him at the top of the turn: the body twisted round in the
saddle, the near shoulder come round, the head still and held there.

THE MAN IS A SHAPE. Not tall, square through the body — a man who
was strong once and has kept the shape of it — the head covered in
the manner of the south, the cloth drawn over and across, and the
face beneath it one unresolved dark mass with no features on him
anywhere. His clothing is the loose pale wound cloth of the south,
dust-coloured and no longer clean, hanging slacker on him than it
was cut to, with one narrow band of imperial blue at the hem. His
left hand holds the reins low at the horse's neck. His right hand is
flat on the back of the saddle behind him, taking his weight as he
turns.

The horse walks on under him, unhurried, head low, dark bay and
dusty.

Lighting: low afternoon sun from the right, warm on the road and on
the dust standing in the air, the shadows long and cool.

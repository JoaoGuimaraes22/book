Image 2: severin.png — half-figure reference of the old man.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the stern of a small open boat lying still on flat water in
the middle of the afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — worn
planking, a low gunwale, the stern sheets and the tiller, and pale
flat sea going out behind them to a horizon with nothing on it.
Three of the strakes on the near side are burned through in patches
and one has a fist-sized hole with a coat stuffed into it; the cut
rope ends lying along the top of the side all end in char. Soft
daylight from one side, and no fire, lamp or flame anywhere in the
picture.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is in the boat with them, low and
close, level with the two heads. SEVERIN sits in the stern on the
right, three-quarters toward the camera, the tiller under his near
forearm. KAEL sits on the left and lower in the frame, leaning back
against the side of the boat, his head turned up to the old man, one
hand flat on the top edge of the gunwale beside him with the fingers
spread. The bottom edge crosses the boy at the bottom of the ribs
and the old man at the waist, so that no laps, no knees, no legs and
no bottom boards are in the frame at all. Nobody else is in the
picture.

The old man has just named the piece of wood under the boy's hand
and the boy is saying it back to him. The boy's hand has not come
off it.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. The old man's eyes down
on the boy, the boy's eyes up on him, the lines of sight meeting.
This eye contact is the subject of the image. Neither is looking at
the viewer.

SEVERIN — settled and unhurried, the near forearm laid along the
tiller, both hands loose and open and not entirely steady. His mouth
is closed with one corner moved very slightly and nowhere else; the
eyes are steady and attentive on the boy; the brows level. Not
smiling, not stern, not kindly. A very bad day and night are on him
and they are all on the surface — the skin grey and dry, the
shoulders down — and the frame under it is sound: still broad across
the shoulder, the strength still in him, not frail and not
diminished. Hair fully grey, the whole head, a true neutral grey and
even everywhere. A heavy coat somebody else gave him, too big
through the shoulders and short at the wrist, dull umber, no
ornament on it.

KAEL — sitting back against the side, one hand flat on the top edge
of the gunwale with the fingers spread, the other arm down and easy.
The mouth open on one short word, the eyes up and steady on the old
man, the brows a fraction up. Undyed coarse working wool in dull
oatmeal and dead olive, much mended, no ornament, badge, colour or
metal on him anywhere; washed out, stiff with salt and dirty, and
nothing red left anywhere on it. A healed burn from wrist to elbow
on the outside of the left forearm. Nothing bleeding.

The two faces carry the most finished drawing in the picture and the
sharpest step from light to dark; the tiller, the burnt strakes, the
boat and the water stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled
edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. The old
man's eyes are dark, near black; the boy's are pale near-white grey
and are the lightest value in his face.

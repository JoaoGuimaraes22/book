Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of Kael.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the inside of a small village forge very late at night, with
the work finished. An anvil on a squat wooden block in the middle of
the floor, scale and dust on the floor around it, tools on the wall,
a leather bucket standing beside the block with iron in it. The
hearth is off to one side and mostly out of frame, burnt down to a
low banked red that gives almost nothing.

Composition: 3:4. Close, one figure, chest-up and a little more.
KAEL sits on the edge of the anvil block, turned three-quarters
toward the camera, leaning slightly in. His right arm is out and his
hand is on the face of the anvil in front of him. Nobody else is in
the picture and no part of anybody else is in it.

TWO FINGERS ARE FLAT ON THE ANVIL AND THAT IS THE WHOLE ACTION. The
index and middle finger of his right hand rest flat on the top face
of the iron, the rest of the hand relaxed, no weight going through
it, no grip. It is a small, exact, unremarkable gesture and he is not
performing it.

NOTHING IS GLOWING. There is no light, no spark, no glow, no arc and
nothing rising off the iron or off his hand anywhere in the picture.
The iron is cold and ordinary and looks it.

HIS FACE IS DOING ALMOST NOTHING AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME. Head
slightly down toward his own hand, the eyes open and unfocused and
not on anything, the mouth closed and loose, the jaw let go. Not
triumph, not relief, not grief, not effort — a face that has stopped
holding itself, which no picture of him has shown.

His left arm rests across his lap, bound from the knuckles to above
the elbow in clean linen with the thumb splinted between two flat
pieces of wood. Both wrists are bare: the skin where the iron was is
soft and paler than the rest of him and unmarked by any ring. Undyed
coarse working wool, much mended.

The two fingers where they meet the iron carry the sharpest step from
light to dark in the frame and the most finished ink drawing in it;
his face comes next; the anvil, the block, the bucket and the whole
of the dark forge behind him stay at quiet low values with soft
wash-bled edges and nothing resolved in them.

Lighting: one low banked red fire off to one side and out of frame,
weak and near the floor, and nothing else. Most of the picture is
dark.

Preserve his face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the
reference image. Pale grey eyes, the lightest value in his face.

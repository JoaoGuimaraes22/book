Image 1: borren.png — portrait reference of the one-eyed man.
Image 2: tirzah.png — portrait reference of the woman with the basket.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a camp on open ground under trees at night. Loose wash
suggestion — the dark, and a wagon wheel and roped canvas behind and
to one side, unresolved. A fire burns on the open ground and not in
a pit, with rough stones set all the way round it in a ring, low
across the bottom of the frame between the two figures.

Composition: 3:4. Two figures at different depths across the fire.
THE ONE-EYED MAN is nearest, chest-up, at the left of the frame,
sitting on the ground with his body angled away from the viewer and
his head turned across the frame to the right, looking into the
depth of the picture. THE WOMAN is beyond the fire at the right,
smaller and further back, sitting, turned three-quarters away from
him and toward somebody outside the frame.

Nothing is happening. It is late and the talk has been going a long
time.

THE OLD MAN IS LOOKING AT HER AND HE IS NOT GOING TO SAY ANYTHING.
The chin is a little down so the one eye comes out from under the
brow; the brow is level, the beard keeps his mouth, and the whole of
the expression is in that brow and that eye. He is turned so that
the patch over his right eye is toward the viewer and his uncovered
left eye is pointed across the fire at her. Nothing in the face is
moving. His right hand holds a plain cup, low and forgotten, resting
against his own knee; his left hand is flat on the ground.

THE WOMAN IS IN THE MIDDLE OF A SENTENCE AND SHE IS LAUGHING. Both
her hands are up in front of her, open, mid-gesture, describing
something. Her head is up, her mouth is open, and the laugh is in
the cheeks and the creased eyes as much as in the mouth. Her eyes
are on the person outside the frame.

She wears coarse undyed cloth with her head covered, nothing dyed on
her anywhere and no ornament on her. He wears a plain dark coat,
worn, with nothing on it.

Lighting: the fire is the only light in the picture and it is below
both of them and between them. It comes up from underneath onto the
old man's face, his brow and his cup, and across the open ground
onto her face and her two raised hands, which take more of it than
anything else in the picture. The trees, the wagon and the ground
beyond the stones go into black.

The old man's face carries the finished drawing and the sharpest
step from light to dark sits at the wet edge of his one eye; the
woman is the brightest thing in the picture after the fire itself;
the stones, the wagon and the ground stay at quiet low values.

Preserve each face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as
shown in their own reference image, and keep the leather patch over
the same eye. Do not blend their features.

Her head is covered: a coarse undyed cloth wound over her head and
once round the throat and knotted at one side, with no hair showing
anywhere.

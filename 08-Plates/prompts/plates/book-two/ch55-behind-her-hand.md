Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the inside of a small low tent on a summer night, with the
door of it open. Loose wash suggestion — canvas close overhead and
behind, and through the open doorway the night sky and, a long way
off and very low, the last warmth of a fire in the camp. Nothing
resolved anywhere.

Composition: 3:4. Chest-up, two figures lying on the ground close
together and side by side, the camera low and at their own level.
THE PRINCESS is nearer, on her side, turned toward the boy. KAEL is
beyond her, on his back with his head turned toward her. Past both
of them, in the open doorway, a third figure lies across the opening
as a dark shape against the sky, with no features drawn on her.

Age: the girl fifteen, the boy fourteen. Eyes at the vertical
midpoint of the head, jaws softly defined and not sharp, adolescent
and not grown.

Lighting: the open doorway is the only light in the picture. Cool
light off the night sky comes in through it and lands along the
tops of their faces, their shoulders and the hand at her mouth; the
canvas, the ground and everything above them go into dark. One small
low warmth off the far fire touches the doorway alone and reaches
neither face.

SHE IS LAUGHING PROPERLY AND HER HAND IS OVER HER MOUTH. The whole
laugh is in the eyes and the cheeks: the cheeks up hard and pushing
the lower lids up from beneath so that both eyes are creased nearly
shut, the skin folded deep at their outer corners, the eyes wet and
bright in what light there is. The brow has not moved and the
forehead is smooth. Her right hand is flat over her mouth, the
fingers together, holding it in and not hiding it. Her left arm is
under her head.

Her long silver-white hair is loose and uncovered, spilling on the
ground beneath her and over her shoulder — colourless, no yellow and
no gold in it, rendered mostly as bare paper and the palest cool
greys, and the lightest thing in the picture by a long way.

KAEL IS WATCHING HER DO IT. His head is turned to her on the ground,
his eyes on her face. The mouth closed and the corner of it gone
soft — not smiling, just not braced. Dark uneven hair, and his eyes
pale grey.

A coarse blanket is pushed down and rucked at her waist. She wears
plain undyed working cloth; he wears a plain dark tunic. Neither of
them has anything on their head.

Their two faces carry the finished drawing and the sharpest step from
light to dark sits at the lit edge of each; the doorway, the shape
lying across it and the canvas stay at quiet low values with no hard
edge in them. The picture falls away to bare unpainted paper at its
edges rather than being painted out to the border.

Preserve each face, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in
the reference images. Her eyes pale blue, his pale grey. Do not blend
their features. The silver-white hair belongs only to her.

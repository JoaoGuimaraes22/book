Image 1: crew-leader.png — portrait reference of the man at the table.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a small room off the end of a rope-walk, with a window in
the wall behind him and weighted piles of paper on the table below
it, both drawn as loose wash with nothing resolved in them. Daylight
from the window.

Composition: 3:4. A close-up of his face. Head and shoulders only,
the head large in the frame, turned three-quarters and tipped back a
little. Nobody else is in the picture.

HIS WHOLE FACE IS OPEN AND IT HAS CAUGHT HIM BY SURPRISE. The chin is
up and the head has gone back an inch. The cheek is lifted hard and
has pushed the lower lid up so that the one eye is creased almost
shut, with the skin at its outer corner folded into three deep lines.
The brow is up and the forehead is creased right across. The mouth is
open inside the beard, and the beard and the moustache are pushed up
and out of their line by it.

THE LEATHER PATCH IS THE ONE STILL THING IN THE FACE. It sits exactly
as it sits at rest, flat and dark and unmoved, while everything
around it has gone.

Nothing about it is loud. The shoulders are barely up and the head
has not gone far. It is a short thing near its top and there is
already less of it than there was.

A plain dark coat over a plain collar, good cloth, no ornament on it
anywhere.

His face carries the whole of the finished drawing, and the sharpest
step from light to dark sits at the creased corner of the eye; the
window, the paper and the wall stay at quiet middle values with soft
wash-bled edges and no hard edge in them.

Preserve his face, hair, beard, colouring and apparent age exactly as
shown in the reference image, and keep the leather patch over the
same eye.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a small room off the end of a rope-walk by a river, with a
beaten earth floor and one window in the far wall.
A plain table under the window carrying three weighted piles of
paper, a stick of wax and a dish. Loose wash suggestion, no detail
anywhere. Daylight from the window, and it is the only light.

Composition: 3:4. Two figures, three-quarter length, well apart, with
a standing column of earth between them. VALERIA stands on the near
side, turned toward the far wall, having just stepped back. THE MAN
sits at the table beyond, side on, his pen down.

BETWEEN THEM STANDS A COLUMN OF THE FLOOR. It is the same beaten
earth as the ground it came out of, about as thick through as a man's
body, standing free and upright between the table and the window, and
it stops level with Valeria's shoulder. Its top is rough and broken
where it stopped rather than cut flat. For about three feet round its
foot the floor has gone down by roughly the width of a thumb, a
shallow saucer pressed into the beaten earth.

NOTHING IS HAPPENING NOW. There is no glow and nothing lifted into
the air anywhere in the picture, and no weapon anywhere in it either.

VALERIA HAS STOOD UP AND IS WAITING. Weight even, both hands loose at
her sides, one knee still dusty from the floor. The face level, the
eyes steady on the man and not on what she has made, the jaw set but
easy. Long black hair, well past the shoulders. Plain dark road
clothing, coarse and travel-worn, the sleeves pulled down over both
wrists.

THE MAN IS LOOKING AT THE COLUMN AND NOT AT HER. Sitting back from
the table, one hand flat on the paper, the head turned up and across
to the top of it. Mouth closed, brows unmoved. A plain dark coat.

The column and Valeria's face carry the finished drawing; the man
comes next; the table, the paper, the window and the walls stay at
quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges and no hard edge in
them.

Do not blend their features. Do not mix their clothing.

Preserve each face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown
in the reference images. Her eyes green, his light grey-brown.

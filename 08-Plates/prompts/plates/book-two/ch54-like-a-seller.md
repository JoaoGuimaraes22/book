Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a bare flat of open ground above a camp, in the low light of
a late autumn afternoon. Loose wash suggestion — below and behind
them the camp in a fold of ground, a canvas shelter and a picket line
of mules unresolved and a long way back, and beyond that nothing.

Composition: 3:4. Three-quarter length, two figures. KAEL is nearer,
walking straight toward the viewer up the flat, his whole stride in
frame. THE WOMAN stands a few strides off to one side, turned to
watch him, not walking.

Age: thirteen. The boy's eyes at the vertical midpoint of the head,
the jaw softly defined and not sharp.

HE IS THINKING ABOUT HOW TO WALK, AND IT SHOWS. The weight is
carried forward over the front of the foot, the stride short, the
shoulders held a little too still — a boy doing a thing deliberately
that other people do without knowing. His eyes are down on the
ground a few feet in front of his own boots and not on the woman and
not on the viewer. The mouth is closed and the brows are drawn
slightly together.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
His right hand carries a shallow woven basket by its rim, down at
his side, and nothing else.
His left hand hangs open and empty.
Her right hand is raised a little at her waist, palm down, the way a
hand goes up to stop somebody.
Her left hand is at her side.

THE WOMAN IS CORRECTING HIM AND IS ABOUT TO MAKE HIM DO IT AGAIN.
Her mouth is open on a word, her chin a little down, her head tilted
the courteous half inch it always is. Her eyes are on his feet and
nowhere else. Nothing in her face is unkind.

Kael wears a plain dark coat over a tunic, travel-worn and dusty. The
woman wears coarse pale undyed southern cloth with her head covered
and no hair showing anywhere. The basket is worn pale wicker and
belongs only to the boy.

Preserve each face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown
in the reference images. Kael's eyes pale grey, the lightest value in
his face; hers dark brown. Do not blend their features. Do not mix
their clothing.

Image 1: borren.png — portrait reference of the one-eyed man.
Image 2: kael-14.png — portrait reference of Kael.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: open grass at the top of a fold at night, with the grass
burning behind them and to one side, low and spreading. Loose wash
suggestion — smoke, the shapes of abandoned chests in the grass, and
nothing else resolved anywhere.

Composition: 3:4. Chest-up, two figures very close and facing each
other, the boy's head a little lower than the man's. THE ONE-EYED
MAN is at the right of the frame, three-quarter view, his head
turned to his own right so that his uncovered left eye is the near
one and the patch over his right eye is on the far side of his face.
KAEL is at the left, turned up toward him. THE OLD MAN'S RIGHT HAND
IS ON THE SIDE OF THE BOY'S FACE, the palm along the jaw and the
fingers up past the ear, holding the head where it is. His other
hand is below the frame. Both of the boy's hands are below the frame.

Age: fourteen. The boy's eyes at the vertical midpoint of the head,
the jaw softly defined and not sharp.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER, the boy's eyes up and
the old man's down, the lines of sight meeting. This eye contact is
the subject of the image.

THE OLD MAN IS UPRIGHT AND THE HAND IS STEADY AND THERE IS NOTHING
LEFT IN HIM. His face is grey and the skin is wet, the one eye open
and fixed on the boy, the brow level, the beard keeping his mouth,
and nothing in the face moving at all.

THE BOY IS BEING TOLD SOMETHING HE IS NOT GOING TO BE ABLE TO
REFUSE. His eyes are wide and locked on the old man's, the brows
drawn together, the jaw tight, and his mouth is open a little and
saying nothing.

The old man wears a plain dark coat, worn, with nothing on it, the
sleeves back clear of the forearm. The boy wears a plain undyed
tunic, travel-worn and filthy.

Lighting: burning grass behind them and off to one side, low and
orange and close, is the only light in the picture. It comes from
behind and across, so both heads are edged hard in it and each face
takes the light down one side; the smoke above them and the ground
beyond them go into black.

Both faces carry the finished drawing and the sharpest step from
light to dark sits at the lit edge of each; the hand on the boy's
face comes next; the grass, the smoke and the chests stay at quiet
low values.

Preserve each face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as
shown in their own reference image, and keep the leather patch over
the same eye. The boy's eyes pale grey. Do not blend their features.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a flat strip of winter grass between a stony track and fast
water, in a fold in high country, in the last of the light. Needle
trees up on the rise behind, branch-litter and dead sticks on the
ground. Loose wash suggestion, no detail anywhere. Cold blue dusk, no
sun, no lamp and no fire anywhere in the picture.

Composition: 3:4. Two figures kneeling on opposite sides of the same
thing, close to the viewer, seen from the side and slightly above.
THE PRINCESS kneels on the near side, turned three-quarters toward
the camera. A HEAVY OLDER MAN kneels facing her on the far side, seen
mostly from behind and to one side with his face in lost profile.
Between and below them, filling the lower middle of the frame, a
wide, shallow lattice of laid sticks and branch-litter, half finished,
with a dark gap still open at one edge of it. Nobody else is in the
picture.

THEY ARE BOTH WORKING AND NEITHER IS TALKING. Her two hands are down
in it, laying a handful of litter across the sticks one piece over
another the way a roof is tiled. His two hands are doing the same on
his side. A single long stick lies across the finished part of the
lattice, testing it.

SHE HAS BEEN DOING THIS FOR HOURS AND SHE IS GOOD AT IT NOW. Her
sleeves are pushed back over bare forearms, her hands are filthy to
the wrist, and her face is down at the work and entirely absorbed —
no distaste, no performance, no appeal to anybody. Her hair is pulled
back and knotted plainly out of the way for working, silver-white and
colourless, dulled with dust; a coarse village dress far too big for
her, borrowed, dull oatmeal, dirty at the knees. Both her wrists are
bare, with the skin where iron used to be paler than the rest of her
arm.

THE MAN IS BLACK TO THE ELBOW. Big through the shoulders and slow,
forearms burnt and scarred, a smith's hands doing careful light work
without any trouble at all.

The two pairs of hands and the lattice between them carry the
sharpest step from light to dark in the frame and the most finished
ink drawing in it; her face comes next; the grass, the water, the
trees and the rise stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled
edges.

Preserve her face and colouring exactly as shown in the reference

Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the foot of a town wall in a hot brown country, as loose wash
suggestion — mud-brick courses the colour of the ground itself, with
the ground's own grain in them. The top edge of the wall crosses the
upper part of the picture with a strip of bleached sky above it, and
standing above the wall further back are the heads of two tall trees:
bare stems with no branches anywhere along their length and all the
green in a burst at the very top, both leaning the same way. The light
is a high white southern glare that comes down out of that sky and up
again off the pale ground, so that every shadow on the ground, the
wall and the boy is short, weak and pale.

Composition: the viewer stands in the road three strides from him and
low, at about the height of his knee — his head is bent over the water
and his face is still fully visible from below, the underside of the
jaw in view. Across the bottom third of the frame, running from the
near left corner away to the right along the base of the wall, is an
open channel of cut stone, seen almost edge-on from this height. The
boy stands beyond it on the far side, at the right of the frame,
turned three-quarters toward the viewer. The bottom edge of the
picture crosses him just below the knee and his feet are out of frame.
He is the only person in the picture and the road behind him is empty.

THE CHANNEL IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Cut stone, straight-sided and
square, about as wide as his hand is long, brim-full and moving fast.
A stone lip a hand's width high crosses it near the left of the frame,
and the water stands over that lip in one unbroken curved sheet with
white broken water below it. At the far left, in the base of the wall,
the channel goes in under the road through a low square-cut arch no
higher than his knee, and the inside of that arch is solid black — the
only true dark in the picture.

He has stopped walking, weight settled evenly, both hands empty and
hanging at his sides. He is not touching the water and he is not
drinking.

HIS EYES ARE ON THE WATER where it goes over the lip. His brows are
lifted a little and his cracked lips are slightly parted. He is not
looking at the viewer.

Across his back, slung on a length of plain cord, is a grown man's war
sword — straight, broad, undecorated — with the grip standing up past
one shoulder and the blade running down past the bottom edge of the
picture. Two plain short blades that do not match, one heavier than
the other, are through his belt.

Narrow through the shoulders, adolescent and not grown, lean and hard
under it, the strength in the forearms and the hands. A month at sea
has taken weight off him and left the rest: his lips cracked, the
corners of his mouth split, a red rash the length of both forearms,
dried salt pale in his eyebrows and along his hairline. Nothing
bleeding. His clothing is undyed coarse working wool, sound and much
mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, stiff and pale with dried salt
and filthy at the hem — worn hard, not ragged, and not a beggar's
clothing.

Colour: the road, the ground and the wall in dust-brown, dull ochre
and pale bone; the green only at the tops of the trees; the water
grey-green and white. Both extremes of the picture sit in the channel
— the black of the arch and the white of the broken water — and the
sharpest step from light to dark happens at the mouth of the arch
against the moving water. His face is fully drawn and held at closer,
quieter values with soft wash-bled edges, and the wall, the road and
the trees stay quiet and soft-edged behind him.

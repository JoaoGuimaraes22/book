Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: a flat strip of winter grass and stones beside fast water at
the bottom of a narrow fold in high country. Flat cold winter
daylight from above, no sun in frame. Loose wash suggestion, nothing
resolved anywhere beyond arm's length.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is brought down the length of the
subject's own forearms, close in behind the wrists, looking down the
arms at what the hands are doing, so that the reader is inside the
body rather than in front of it. No face and no other person anywhere
in the picture.

A SMALL FOLDED PAPER, OPEN, IS THE SUBJECT AND IT FILLS THE MIDDLE OF
THE FRAME. One sheet about the size of a hand, worn soft as cloth,
brown-grey, split most of the way through along both fold lines from
being opened and closed many times. It is written on in a small
cramped hand in faded brown ink, six or seven short lines, by
somebody who was better at writing than the man who carried it. The
strokes are loose marks and no word anywhere on it is legible.

THE RIGHT HAND HOLDS IT OPEN AGAINST THE THIGH. Thumb at one edge,
the fingers spread flat under the sheet to stop it folding back on
itself, the hand steady and doing one careful small thing. It is a
boy's hand, lean and hard and weathered, with old burn speckles
scattered from the wrist to the second knuckle, filthy, with dried
dark red in the creases of the fingers and under the nails.

THE LEFT ARM IS OUT OF THE WORK. It lies across the bottom corner of
the frame, bound from the knuckles to above the elbow in clean linen
with the thumb splinted between two flat pieces of wood. The linen is
the whitest thing in the picture and the only clean thing in it.

UNDERNEATH IS A DEAD MAN'S COAT, spread open across the whole lower
half of the frame. Coarse dark wool, worn through at one elbow and
mended twice by different hands, the lining cut open along the hem
where coins had been sewn into it. At the far edge of the coat, small
and half out of frame, a child's milk tooth on a short leather cord.

The paper and the right hand carry the sharpest step from light to
dark and the only finished ink drawing in the picture; the coat, the
cord and the ground stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled
edges and no hard edge anywhere in them.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a narrow dead-end street in a southern river city in the
middle of the morning. Mud-brick walls the colour of the ground with
the ground's own grain in them, hard dry ground, the street running
back behind the two figures to a corner and nothing else in it. Loose
wash suggestion, no detail anywhere. Bright morning daylight, the sun
behind the viewer.

Composition: 3:4. LOW-ANGLE VIEW. The camera is down at the height of
a bench seat, close to the ground, looking up at two children who are
standing over it. They are seen from below: the undersides of both
jaws are visible, both of them stand tall against the bright sky
above the rooflines, and the horizon sits low in the frame.

At the very bottom of the frame and close to the camera, one pair of
bare feet on the ground, and a pair of worn boots standing on the
ground beside them. No other part of that person is in the picture.

KAEL stands nearest, half a step ahead, chest-up and a little more.
VALERIA stands half a step off his shoulder. Nobody else is in the
picture.

BOTH OF THEM ARE LOOKING DOWN AT THE VIEWER. Two lines of sight, both
of them straight down into the camera.

KAEL'S MOUTH IS OPEN MID-WORD. Chin down, the face level. His left arm is bound from the knuckles
to above the elbow in clean linen with the thumb splinted between two
flat pieces of wood, held in against his chest; his right hand hangs
loose at his side. Undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dust
through all of it.

VALERIA IS TURNED SO THAT THE LEFT SIDE OF HER FACE IS TOWARD THE
CAMERA, and that side is smooth and shining where it healed. Weight
down, the face level, the mouth closed, the jaw set but easy. Long
black hair, well past the shoulders. Plain dark road clothing, coarse
and travel-worn. Both her hands are empty and open and no weapon is
drawn anywhere in the picture.

Both faces are in full light and fully drawn, not against the light,
and they carry the finished drawing; the bare feet and the boots come
next; the street, the walls and the corner behind them stay at quiet
middle values with soft wash-bled edges. The sky is bare paper.

Do not blend their features. Do not mix their clothing: the mended
undyed wool and the bound arm belong only to Kael.

Preserve each face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the
reference images. His eyes pale grey, hers green.

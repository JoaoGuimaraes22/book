Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of Kael.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the bottom of a stone stair, in the open doorway of a cellar,
by lamplight. Loose wash suggestion — stone wall and stone floor, an
iron door standing open into black, and stacked in that black the
corners of small iron-strapped chests, unresolved.

Composition: 3:4. Chest-up, close. AN IMPERIAL OFFICER fills the left
and middle of the frame, bent backwards over nothing, his head back
and his face turned up and toward the viewer — his face is the subject
of the picture. Two men hold him from behind and are seen only in
part: a forearm across his chest, a hand round each of his wrists, and
no face on either of them. KAEL stands a foot away at the right of the
frame with a lamp raised in his left hand, his own face lit by it,
watching.

THE OFFICER HAS JUST REACHED FOR SOMETHING AND IT IS NOT THERE. The
eyes are wide open and fixed on nothing, the brows up and drawn
together at their inner ends, the mouth open with the jaw slack, and
every line in the face going the same way — down and out from
underneath. He is not fighting the two men. His body has stopped
working on them and every part of it has gone into the face.

A plain iron band is closed round his left wrist low in the frame,
dark, with a pin through it, and the hand below it is open and doing
nothing.

KAEL IS A FOOT AWAY AND HE KNOWS WHAT HE IS LOOKING AT. His face is
level, the mouth closed, the brows unmoved, the eyes steady on the
man's face; and the lamp in his left hand is not moving either. The
right hand is empty and open at his side.

A man of about forty, clean-shaven, dark-haired, well fed, with the
skin of somebody who has never worked outdoors. He wears an officer's
coat, the empire's flat dead white with a band of deep blue at the
collar and the cuff.

Lighting: two lamps and nothing else. One on a bracket on the wall
behind them, one in the boy's raised hand, both close and warm. They
light the officer's face from below and one side, the boy's face full,
and the hands; the stair, the doorway and the room beyond go into
black.

The officer's face carries the finished drawing and the sharpest step
from light to dark sits at the wet edge of his eye; the boy's face
comes next; the hands, the band and the stone stay at quiet values.

Preserve the boy's face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as
shown in the reference image. His eyes pale grey. Do not blend the two
faces: the boy is thirteen or fourteen and lean and weathered, and the
officer is a grown man.

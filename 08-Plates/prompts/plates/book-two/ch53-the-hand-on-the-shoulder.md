Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: a camp at night in a fold of open ground. Loose wash
suggestion — nothing behind the two men but dark, with the edge of a
canvas shelter and one standing mule implied a long way back and
unresolved.

Composition: 3:4. Chest-up, two men sitting side by side on the
ground on the far side of a low fire, turned three-quarters toward
the viewer. THE TWINS: two grown men in their twenties with the same face,
the same build and the same hair, sitting close enough that their
shoulders touch. The man on the left has his right arm across behind
his brother and his right hand closed on the far shoulder, and it has
been there a long time. His left hand is on his own knee. His
brother's hands are both loose between his knees, empty.

Lighting: the low fire in front of them and below the bottom edge of
the frame is the only light in the picture. It comes up from beneath
onto their faces, throats and forearms, and leaves the tops of their
heads, their backs and everything behind them dark.

ONE OF THEM IS TALKING AND THE OTHER ONE IS NOT. The man on the left
has his mouth open on a word, the brows drawn a little together,
telling something at an even pace, and his head is not moving while
he does it. The man on the right has his mouth closed and pressed
flat, and his eyes wide and fixed on the fire — they are the only
part of him doing anything.

BOTH OF THEM ARE LOOKING INTO THE FIRE AND NEITHER IS LOOKING AT THE
OTHER OR AT THE VIEWER.

Two men plainly not of this country and not dressed like it: bare
heads, dark hair cut anyhow, weathered from years of walking, in
plain worn wool and leather the colour of dust, with no wound cloth
and nothing pale or loose about what they wear.

Their two faces and the hand on the shoulder carry the finished
drawing, and the sharpest step from light to dark sits at the edge of
the lit side of each face; the fire below the frame, the ground and
the dark behind them stay at quiet low values with no hard edge in
them.

The two faces are the same face. Do not make one of them older, or
heavier, or differently coloured.

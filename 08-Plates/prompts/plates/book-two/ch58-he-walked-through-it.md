Image 1: borren.png — portrait reference of the one-eyed man.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights. Plus dry-brush drag and flicked spatter
for the movement.

Setting: a camp on open ground at night, in the middle of a fight
that is already happening. Loose wash suggestion — trodden grass
underfoot, and behind them a wagon and the shapes of chests in the
dark, unresolved.

Composition: 3:4. Two figures close and overlapping, both grounded
on the same ground. THE ONE-EYED MAN comes forward from the right of
the frame, waist-up and a little more, three-quarter view, his body
angled across the picture and his weight already on the front foot.
His right arm and the whole of the blade in his right hand are in
the frame, held low and out in front of him. He is turned so that
his uncovered left eye is toward the viewer and toward the man in
front of him, and the patch over his right eye is on the far side of
his face. THE YOUNG MAN is at the left, nearer the camera and
larger, seen from behind and one side, so that only the line of his
shoulder, his upper arm and the back and side of his head are drawn,
with no features on him anywhere.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
The old man's right hand holds one plain soldier's sword and nothing
else.
The old man's left hand is empty and open, low and out for balance.
The young man's near hand is up and open at the height of his own
shoulder, palm out toward the old man, and empty.
The young man's other hand is below the frame.
Three hands in the picture, one blade, and no other blade, blade tip
or hilt anywhere in it.

The sword is a plain straight single-handed soldier's blade, about
the length of his own arm, with a plain crossguard and no ornament of
any kind.

THE OLD MAN IS WALKING INTO SOMETHING THAT SHOULD HAVE STOPPED HIM
AND IT HAS NOT. He is not clawing at anything, not reaching for his
own throat, not braced. His feet are under him, his weight is
forward and he is breathing. He moves like a much younger man. There
is nothing on him and nothing at his throat.

A fine line of pale dust stands in the air along the whole length of
the sword's edge — up off the ground, holding the edge's own
straight line, and not falling. It is the only thing in the picture
that is not where gravity would put it. Nothing on the blade is
burning or glowing: the line along it is dust.

His face: the chin a little down so the one eye comes out from under
the brow, the brow low and straight, the beard keeping his mouth,
and the eye open and flat and fixed on the man in front of him.
Everything in the face is set hard and none of it is moving, and the
body is walking.

He wears a plain dark coat, worn, with nothing on it, the sleeves
back clear of both forearms.

Lighting: a fire off to the right and low, outside the frame, is the
only light in the picture. It comes across low onto the near side of
his face, his forearm, the blade and the line of dust standing off
it, and onto the young man's shoulder and the back of his head; the
ground and everything behind them go into black.

His face carries the finished drawing; the line of dust off the
blade is the only pale hard edge anywhere in the picture, and
everything behind the blade is dark and soft with no hard edge in
it.

Preserve his face, hair, beard, colouring and apparent age exactly
as shown in the reference image, and keep the leather patch over the
same eye. His is the only face in the picture.

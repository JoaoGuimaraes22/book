Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills.

Composition: 4:3. A close still study of one object. A pair of open
hands lies on old straw, palms half turned up, side by side and
filling most of the frame, the wrists entering from the left and the
fingers reaching toward the right. Inside the frame there are the
two hands, the metal on them and the straw beneath, and nothing
else: no face and no other part of anyone. The hands are lean and
hard, narrow through the wrist and forearm, with the strength in the
fingers — working hands, small and not broad.

THE METAL IS THE SUBJECT. A plain band of iron a finger thick lies
round each wrist, closed with a pin whose end is peened flat; eleven
short links of the same iron run between the two bands and lie slack
in the straw. It is old and plain, the work of somebody who made a
great many of them. THE IRON ITSELF IS BRIGHT: pale silver grey,
rubbed smooth and shining with long use, light in value everywhere
it is not in shadow, with no rust and no darkening on it.

In the folds where the metal turns on itself there is BLACK: matte
and dead, lying in the grooves the way ink lies in the cut of a
letter, thin where the band is worn thin and laid on again over it.
It is neither rust nor dirt. It was put there on purpose, by hand,
more than once. It is the only dark thing anywhere on the metal, and
it reads as a substance lying in the metal and not as the metal's
own colour.

The straw and the hands are held at quiet middle values with soft
wash-bled edges and no hard edged dark anywhere in them, and the
sharpest step from light to dark in the whole picture happens at the
edge of the black in the grooves of the iron.

Lighting: one warm lantern above and out of frame is most of the
light, coming down onto the metal so that the iron takes it and the
straw around falls away. One thin bar of cold grey daylight crosses
the straw behind. The corners go to ink dark.

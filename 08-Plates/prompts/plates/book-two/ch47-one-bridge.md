Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: the end of a long dry ridge road in high country, where the
ground drops away and opens onto a wide brown river plain, in the
middle of a bright cold morning. Flat bright daylight, no sun in
frame.

Composition: 3:4. The viewer stands at the lip where the road ends,
looking down and out.

NEAREST, in the bottom quarter: the stony lip of the road, hard pale
dirt and low thorn.

BELOW AND BEYOND IT, filling the middle of the frame: a city, grey
and brown, packed absolutely solid, its flat roofs running down to
the water. An old wall crosses the land side of it, and the city has
grown round both ends of that wall and out past it — yards, animal
pens and low roofs of stretched cloth spreading along the bank
upstream and down. The walls are the colour of the ground with the
ground's own grain in them. Nothing stands above the roofs anywhere
in it: no tower, no dome, no spire. Nothing white or blue on the wall
or in the city at all.

BEYOND THE CITY, a wide slow brown river running right across the
frame.

CROSSING IT, ONE BRIDGE AND ONLY ONE. Old stone, low and long,
standing on heavy piers, with the water piled up white against the
upstream side of every pier. A squat gatehouse at each end of it. A
line of pack animals is coming across from the far side, the whole
line of them shorter than the span between two piers and each animal
a single mark.

BEYOND THE RIVER, the far bank and open country going away flat into
haze.

Lines of road come in across the plain from the left, from the right,
and down from the lip the viewer is standing on; they bend toward
each other, join, and go in at the near gatehouse.

The bridge and the white water at its piers carry the sharpest step
from light to dark and the most finished ink drawing in the picture;
the city, the plain, the tracks and the far bank
stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges and no hard
edge in them.

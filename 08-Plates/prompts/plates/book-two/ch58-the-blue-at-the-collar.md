Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the top of a wooded fold, in the open under trees, in flat
daylight. Loose wash suggestion — trunks and low branches behind,
trodden grass underfoot, and standing about in the grass a number of
small iron-strapped chests, unresolved.

Composition: 3:4. Two figures, close, both grounded on the same
ground. THE OLD STEWARD is nearest, seated at the left of the frame
on one of the chests, turned three-quarters away from the viewer,
with a plain wooden board laid flat across his knees; the board,
both his hands and the pen are in the frame, and his head is down
over his writing so that his face is turned away and down and almost
none of it is drawn. KAEL stands a step beyond him at the right,
waist-up in the frame, his head down toward the board and his face
fully visible. Further back, unresolved, three or four people stand
waiting in a loose line with no features on any of them.

Age: fourteen. The boy's eyes at the vertical midpoint of the head,
the jaw softly defined and not sharp.

Nothing is happening. A man is writing and a boy is watching him do
it.

THE STEWARD is past sixty, thin and upright, his grey hair cut
short. He wears a good dark coat, well cut and well kept, buttoned
all the way up to the throat, and at its standing collar a band of
deep blue the width of a finger, stitched on and sitting exactly
level. His right hand holds a pen and is moving; his left hand is
flat on the board, holding the paper down. The hand does not hurry.

THE BOY'S EYES ARE DOWN ON THE MOVING PEN. His mouth is closed and
his brows are unmoved, and the one thing working in the face is the
attention — a boy pricing something he cannot yet put a name to and
letting none of it out. His head is down toward the seated man
because the man is sitting and he is standing. Both his hands are
below the frame.

He wears a plain undyed tunic, travel-worn, the sleeves pushed up
his forearms. Nothing on him is dyed and nothing on him is blue.

Lighting: cold flat daylight out of an overcast sky, from above and
one side, with no sun anywhere and no hard shadow.

The band of blue at the man's collar is the only saturated colour in
the picture; everything else is undyed cloth, iron, bark, trodden
grass and cold grey light. The boy's face carries the finished
drawing and the sharpest step from light to dark sits at his eye.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown
in the reference image. His eyes pale grey, the lightest value in his
face. His is the only face drawn in the picture. Do not blend the two
figures: the boy is fourteen, lean and weathered, and the seated man
is past sixty. The good dark coat and the blue at its collar belong
only to the seated man.

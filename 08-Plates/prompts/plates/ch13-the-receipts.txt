Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the flat ground of a mountain smallholding before sunrise on
a cold spring morning, as loose wash suggestion — frozen ground under
frost, the yard falling away into cool wash a stride behind, the far
side of it lost. Cold flat half-light before sunrise, no sun and no
shadow with an edge anywhere, and it is the only light in the
picture.

Composition: close on one hand and one forearm, seen from above and a
little to the side. The picture is cropped at the elbow, so no head,
no face and no other part of him is in the frame. The frame holds
four things only: the hand, the forearm, a wooden sword and the
frost.

WHAT THE HAND IS DOING: his right hand is flat on the frozen ground,
palm down, fingers spread and taking his weight, the whole outside of
the hand and the wrist turned up toward the viewer. The forearm runs from it
up out of the top of the frame — sword, frost, hand, wrist, forearm,
one continuous limb in that order. His left hand is out of frame.
Nothing is held.

THE MARKS ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Across the back of the hand
and up the wrist runs a scatter of small round marks, pale pink and
white, flat to the skin and healed — thirty or more of them, none
larger than a grain of barley, spread from the knuckles to a hand's
width up the forearm. Each one is drawn with the ink as a small ring,
not left to the grain of the paper.

The wooden sword lies where it fell: one plain wooden practice sword
on the frost beside the hand, at the same distance from the viewer,
its grip nearest the hand and a hand's width clear of it, touching
nothing and held by nobody.

THE HAND IS A CHILD'S, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS THE GRIP: the round
grip of the wooden sword lying beside it is as long as his whole hand
from the heel of the palm to the fingertips. A small hand, short in
the fingers, the knuckles not yet come up.

THE ARM IS BARE TO ABOVE THE ELBOW: no cuff, no sleeve edge and no
cloth of any kind on the hand, the wrist or the forearm anywhere in
the frame. The frost is broken and smeared under the hand where it
went down, and stands unbroken everywhere else.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the skin is the only
warm colour anywhere, and the marks on it are pinker than the skin
around them. The frost, the ground, the wooden sword and everything
behind them are cool grey and grey-blue with no brown in them.

THE BACK OF THE HAND AND THE MARKS ON IT ARE THE MOST FINISHED THING
IN THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The
frost is the brightest thing in the picture and is reserved as bare
unpainted paper. The wooden sword, the ground and the yard behind stay at quiet
middle values with soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there
competes for finish. The ink linework stays the strongest element
throughout and does not blur into the wash.

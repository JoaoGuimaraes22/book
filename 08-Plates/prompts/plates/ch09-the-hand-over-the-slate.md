Setting: a lecture room in a stone building, spring, emptying at the
end of a lesson — long plain benches, a lectern, and behind her on the

boxes ruled in chalk with lines drawn between them, no word in it that

Composition: 3:4. She is seated at the end of a bench, alone, turned
three-quarters toward the camera, cut by the bottom edge of the frame
just below her own small slate, which lies flat across her knees. The
great slate with its chalk diagram is behind her and above, at the
frame's edge, out of focus and small. The rest of the room is empty
and loose.

HER RIGHT HAND LIES FLAT ON HER OWN SLATE, palm down, fingers together,
covering what is written under it the way you put your hand over a
coin on a table. The stylus is still in that hand, held along the palm
by the last two fingers, not writing. Her left hand rests on the bench
beside her. Nothing else is in either hand and nothing written on her
slate is visible anywhere.

SHE IS NOT LOOKING AT HER OWN HAND. Her head is turned and lifted a
little and her eyes are on the chalk diagram on the far wall, steady
and unmoving, and she is still working on it. The picture is two
things happening at once: the attention going one way, up to the wall,
and the hand staying where it is, quietly, on the thing she has just
written.

HER FACE: attention fully engaged and giving nothing back, mouth
closed and level, brows unmoved, no verdict reached yet. Assessment in
progress. Not cold, not haughty, not bored, not tired, and not
troubled.

THE GIRL: eleven years old and small for it — a small unfinished frame,
the jaw soft and not sharp, the cheeks still full and unhollowed
beneath the cheekbone, the chin small, the neck slender, and nothing
about her caught up with her yet except the eyes. Small, neat and
finely built, narrow through the shoulders. Ink-dark hair, a flat
neutral black with no blue in it, bound severe and close to the head
with nothing loose anywhere in it. Eyes very dark brown, near black.
Pale indoor skin, clear and even, with no high colour anywhere in it
and no colour in the cheeks. Plain dark scholar's clothes of very good
quality, closed at the throat, with no ornament on them.

Her hair is drawn as fine ink strands and open transparent washes with

Her eye is the most finished thing in the picture and the sharpest step

hand on the slate is the second most finished thing. The benches, the
far wall, the great slate and the whole of the room are held at quiet
middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

Colour: the bench timber is warm honey-brown where the light lies

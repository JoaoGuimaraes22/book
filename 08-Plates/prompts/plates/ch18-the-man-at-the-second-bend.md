Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a bare hillside above a fishing village at the end of a wet
afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a long tilted field of loose
grey stone, and below it a narrow wet mud road cutting across the
slope. Flat grey overcast light, no sun and no hard shadow anywhere,
the sky low and coming on to rain.

Composition: real depth, seen from up the slope. The viewer stands on
the loose stone above the boy and a little behind him, looking down
across the field of stone to the road. The frame stacks from bottom
to top: nearest and largest, filling the bottom third, the boy lying
flat on his front along the slope with his head to the viewer's left,
his cheek almost on the stones, his face in three-quarter view seen
from above, one hand flat on the stone near his chin; then the tilted
stone field falling away; then, across the upper third, the road, and
on it one grown man walking away to the viewer's left; then the far
bank and a strip of low grey sky.

SCALE — the distance is the picture. The walking man is no taller in
the frame than the boy's hand is long. The boy's head alone is wider
in the frame than the man is tall.

Nothing is happening. The man walks on at an even unhurried pace and
the boy lies still and watches him go by below. Nobody is running,
nothing is drawn or raised, and nothing in either body says a chase.

THE MAN IS SEEN FROM ABOVE AND BEHIND. A brown-grey wool cap and a
heavy brown-grey wool coat, ordinary and worn, the build of a working
man; no badge, no colour, no insignia, no pattern, and nothing
carried in either hand. His face is not visible anywhere in the
picture, no eyes appear anywhere in the picture except the boy's, and
he does not turn his head.

THE BOY'S FACE IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS DOING TWO THINGS AT ONCE. The
eyes are wide open, bright and completely steady, tracking the man
down on the road; the mouth is closed and firm; the face is still and
the body is stiller. He is very good at this and glad of it, and
underneath that he has just understood something heavy. Not
frightened, not grinning, not blank.

He is looking down at the walking man and nowhere else — not at the
viewer, and his head does not turn toward the viewer. The boy is
twelve years old.

His clothing as in his full-figure reference: undyed coarse working
wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive. He carries nothing
and nothing is strapped to his back.

Colour: the stone field cool grey with green-grey lichen in it, the
road pale wet mud, the far bank dull olive and dead bracken brown,
the man's cap and coat the same brown-grey as the rest of the
hillside. The boy's skin is the one warm thing in the picture.

THE BOY'S FACE AND THE HAND BESIDE IT CARRY THE SHARPEST STEP FROM
LIGHT TO DARK and are the two most finished things in the picture.
The road, the man and the far bank stay at quiet middle values with
soft edges and no detail. The ink linework stays the strongest
element throughout and does not blur into the wash.

There is nobody else on the road and nobody else on the hillside: two
figures only, no other walker, no house and no animal anywhere in
the frame.

Preserve him exactly as shown in his reference images — face, hair,
colouring; the two images are the same boy and there is one boy in
the picture. Pale grey eyes. Dark hair cut short and uneven.

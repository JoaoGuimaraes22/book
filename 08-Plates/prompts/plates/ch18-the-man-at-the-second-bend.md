Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a bare hillside above a fishing village at the end of a wet
afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a long tilted field of loose
grey stone, and below it a narrow wet mud road cutting across the
slope. Flat grey overcast light, no sun and no hard shadow, the sky
low and coming on to rain.

Composition: real depth, seen from up the slope. The viewer stands on
the loose stone above the boy and a little behind him, looking down
across the stone to the road. The frame stacks from bottom to top:
nearest and largest, filling the bottom third, the boy lying flat on
his front along the slope, his cheek almost on the stones, his face
in three-quarter view seen from above, one hand flat on the stone
near his chin; then the tilted stone field falling away; then, across
the upper third, the road, and on it one grown man walking away along
it; then the far bank and a strip of low grey sky. The boy is twelve
years old.

SCALE — the distance is the picture: the walking man is no taller in
the frame than the boy's hand is long, and the boy's head alone is
wider in the frame than the man is tall.

Nothing is happening. The man walks on at an even unhurried pace and
the boy lies still and watches him go by below. Nobody is running,
nothing is drawn or raised, and nothing in either body says a chase.

THE MAN IS SEEN FROM ABOVE AND BEHIND: a brown-grey wool cap and
heavy wool coat, worn, the build of a working man, no badge and no
colour on him anywhere, nothing carried. His face is not visible
anywhere in the picture and he does not turn his head.

THE BOY'S FACE IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS DOING TWO THINGS AT ONCE. The
eyes are wide open, bright and completely steady, tracking the man
down on the road; the mouth is closed and firm; the face is still and
the body is stiller. He is very good at this and glad of it, and
underneath that he has just understood something heavy. Not
frightened, not grinning, not blank. He is looking at the walking man
and nowhere else, not at the viewer.

His clothing as in his full-figure reference. He carries nothing and
nothing is strapped to his back.

The stone field is cool grey with green-grey lichen in it, the road
pale wet mud, the far bank dull olive and bracken brown, the man's
cap and coat the same brown-grey as the hillside. The boy's skin is
the one warm thing in the picture. His face and the hand beside it
carry the sharpest step from light to dark; the road, the man and the
bank stay at quiet middle values with soft edges.

There is nobody else on the road and nobody else on the hillside: two
figures only, no other walker, no house and no animal in the frame.

Preserve him exactly as shown in his reference images — face, hair,
colouring; the two images are the same boy and there is one boy in
the picture. Pale grey eyes.

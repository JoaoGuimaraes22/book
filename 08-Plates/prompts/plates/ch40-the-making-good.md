Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of Kael.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the inside of a plain wooden box on a cart, at first light.
Sawn boards, flat sides, square corners, no window anywhere, loose
straw over the floor. Behind the camera, in the door, one slot a
hand wide with three bars across it, and the first grey of the day
comes through it in a single thin bar.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is low, down at straw height, set
across the width of the box and close. KAEL lies against the side
boards with his knees up, turned three-quarters toward the camera,
and the bar of grey day lies across his chest and his hands. The
bottom edge crosses him at the thigh: no knees, no feet, no far
floor, no opposite wall and no roof in the frame — only the straw
and the boards immediately around him. Set into the boards behind
his shoulder, an iron ring.

EVERYTHING IN THIS PICTURE IS ARRANGED TO LOOK UNTOUCHED, and the
arrangement is the subject. The chain lies out in the straw in the
loose shape chain makes when it is doing nothing. From the ring, a
rope tail is laid in a curve on the boards with a bight in it, so
that from a doorway it would read as a rope still tied. The straw
under him is turned over and put back flat.

THE ARRANGEMENT CARRIES THE PICTURE. The chain in the straw, the
bight in the rope and the ring in the boards hold the sharpest step
from light to dark in the frame and the most finished ink drawing in
it; his face carries the next most finished after them; everything
else in the box is soft wash with no hard edge anywhere in it.

HIS LEFT HAND IS OUT OF THE IRON AND THE IRON IS BACK OVER IT. The
band — iron a finger thick, closed with a pin whose end is peened
flat — hangs on the wrist where iron hangs, and the hand inside it
is folded into itself, the thumb lying flat across the palm and the
fingers closed over it, so that it is smaller than a hand. He holds
it in against his chest, low and turned away from the camera, and
does not move it. The back of it, from the knuckles to the wrist, is
dark and wet-looking and is not resolved, and the red runs down the
inside of the forearm from it, drying. His right wrist carries the
same band and eleven short links, and that one is still on him.

KAEL lying exactly as he has lain for sixty-two days, back against
the boards, shoulders let down, breathing the shallow even way a
sleeping boy breathes. The eyelids almost closed and the lashes
down, the eyes open under them and steady on the barred slot behind
the viewer. The mouth closed and the jaw set. Nothing in the face
admits the hand: not frightened, not straining, not grimacing. Eyes
pale grey, near white. Dark hair, short, cut unevenly, straw in it.
A plain undyed light fawn tunic, much mended, no ornament and no
metal on him but the iron. Two months in the box has taken weight
off him and left the rest.

Lighting: THIS IS A GREY DAYLIGHT PICTURE AND NOT A NIGHT ONE, and
nothing in it is black. The cold bar off the slot is the brightest
thing in the frame and carries bare unpainted paper; it lies on his
chest and his hands and leaves his face lower. The rest of the box
is scattered cold grey daylight at quiet middle values, the boards
granulating and the straw taking the light. No lamp, no flame and no
warm light anywhere.

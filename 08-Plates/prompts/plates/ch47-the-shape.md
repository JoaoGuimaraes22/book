Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a shallow scrape of bare ground below the skirt of a long
dry ridge, in high open country going brown. Loose stones, low thorn
scrub, hard pale dirt. On one side the ground falls away and opens
out into empty country running a long way off. Loose wash suggestion,
no detail anywhere. The last of the daylight, low and from one side,
no fire and no lamp anywhere in the picture.

Composition: 3:4. Three figures sitting on the ground, seen from
slightly above and from the open side, all three in frame from the
head to about the knee. A water skin lies on the dirt between them.
Nobody else is in the picture.

THE PRINCESS sits at the middle, knees drawn up, arms round them.
VALERIA sits about three feet from her, turned a half-body's turn off
the princess's shoulder, on the side where the ground falls away, her
back to that open country.
The dirt directly in front of the princess is clear and empty and
nobody is sitting in it.
KAEL sits further round, opposite them both, a bundle of sacking
across his knees.

THE PRINCESS IS TALKING. Chin down toward her knees, mouth open
mid-word, eyes on Kael. A plain cloth tied over her head with no hair
showing anywhere. A coarse village dress far too big for her,
borrowed, dull oatmeal, dirty at the hem.

VALERIA IS WATCHING THE OPEN COUNTRY. Weight down, sitting square and
easy, hands loose, the face level, the eyes steady out on the open
ground and not on either of them, the jaw set but easy. Long black
hair, well past the shoulders. Plain dark road clothing, coarse and
travel-worn. The left side of her face is smooth and shining where it
healed.

KAEL IS LOOKING AT THE GROUND BETWEEN THE TWO GIRLS. Head turned to
Valeria, the eyes down and short of her face, mouth closed, brows
down a fraction. His left arm is bound from the knuckles to above the
elbow in clean linen with the thumb splinted between two flat pieces
of wood, held in against his chest, and the linen is the whitest
thing in the picture. Undyed coarse working wool, much mended.

The three faces and the empty dirt in front of the princess carry the
finished drawing; the thorn, the stones, the ridge and the open
country stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

Do not blend their features. Do not mix their clothing: the
head-cloth belongs only to the princess, the bound arm and the
sacking only to Kael.

Preserve each face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the
reference images. His eyes pale grey, Valeria's green, the princess's
pale blue.

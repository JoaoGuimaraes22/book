Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house at night, as loose wash
suggestion — a plank wall behind them, the corners going into deep
dark. One trimmed oil lamp stands at the back of the table and is the
only light in the picture; it is warm and low and leaves the room
black behind them.

Composition: waist-up, two figures at a long plank table, the table
running across the lower third of the frame with its whole surface in
view. Laid out along it in one straight row, nine small ordinary
objects, evenly spaced: a short copper rod, an iron nail, a length of
pale dry oak, a length of the same oak dark and wet, a coil of dry
rope, a coil of wet rope, a goat's leg bone, a clay cup of water, a
second clay cup of water. The boy of twelve sits at one end of the
row with one hand out flat toward the copper rod, not touching it.
The woman sits at the other end, side-on to the table with her left
forearm laid along the top of it and her weight back in the chair,
and in her right hand she holds a splinter of wood upright at the end
of the row.

THE MEASURE IS THE SUBJECT: from the top of the splinter hangs a
single wisp of pale teased fibre, and it has just lifted and is
standing out from the wood. It is reserved as bare unpainted paper
and is the lightest thing in the picture; nothing else in the frame
is painted that white.

BOTH OF THEM ARE LOOKING AT THE WISP OF FIBRE and at nothing else.
Neither is looking at the viewer and neither is looking at the other.

The boy's face is entirely absorbed: mouth closed, brows drawn a
little, the whole attention on the far end of the row. The woman's
face is level, unhurried and giving nothing away, watching her own
instrument work. She is not smiling and she is not warm.

She is heavily pregnant and near her time, carrying high and in
front, which is why she sits square-on to the table's end rather than
up to its edge and reaches with the one arm. Both of them wear plain
undyed village working wool, coarse and mended, closed at the throat,
in dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no ornament and no metal.

Warm lamplight low across the table and the nine objects; the copper
warm, the iron and the nail cool grey, the wet oak dark and the dry
oak pale, the cups plain clay. The wall behind is black. Their two
faces and the lifted fibre are the most finished things in the
picture; the room stays at quiet middle values with soft edges.
Nobody else is in the room.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. His eyes pale grey,
hers dark.

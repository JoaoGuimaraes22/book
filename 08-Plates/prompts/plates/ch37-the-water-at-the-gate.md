Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the foot of a town wall in a hot brown country, as loose wash
suggestion — mud-brick courses the colour of the ground itself, with the
ground's own grain in them. The top edge of the wall crosses the upper
part of the picture with a strip of bleached sky above it, and standing
above the wall further back are the heads of two tall trees: bare stems
with no branches on them at all and all the green in a burst at the very
top, leaning together in the wind. The light is a high white southern
glare that comes down out of that sky and up again off the pale ground,
so that the shadows are short and weak, there is no dark side to his
face, and there is no deep shadow anywhere in the picture.

Composition: the viewer stands in the road about three strides from him
and low, at about the height of his knee. Across the bottom third of the
frame, running from the near left corner away to the right along the
base of the wall, is an open channel of cut stone. The boy stands beyond
it on the far side, at the right of the frame, turned three-quarters
toward the viewer with his head bent down over the water. The bottom
edge of the picture crosses him just below the knee. He is the only
person in the picture and the road behind him is empty.

THE CHANNEL IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. It is cut stone, straight-sided
and square, about as wide as his foot is long, and it is brim-full and
moving fast. A stone lip a hand high crosses it near the left of the
frame, and the water stands over that lip in one unbroken curved sheet
with white broken water below it. At the far left, in the base of the
wall, the channel goes in under the road through a low square-cut arch
no higher than a large dog, and the inside of that arch is completely
black.

He has stopped walking. Both feet are flat, both hands are empty and
hanging at his sides. He is not touching the water and he is not
drinking.

HIS EYES ARE ON THE WATER where it goes over the lip. His head is bent,
his brows are lifted a little, and his cracked lips are slightly parted.
He is not looking at the viewer.

Across his back, slung on a length of plain cord, is a grown man's war
sword — straight, broad, undecorated, nearly as long as he is tall —
with the grip standing up past one shoulder. Two plain short blades that
do not match, one heavier than the other, are through his belt.

A month at sea is on him: he is thin, his lips are cracked open, the
corners of his mouth are split, and a red rash runs the length of both
forearms. Dried salt sits pale in his eyebrows and along his hairline.
Nothing bleeding. His clothing is undyed coarse working wool, sound and
much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, stiff and pale with dried
salt and filthy at the hem — worn hard, not ragged, and not a beggar's
clothing.

He is fourteen — narrow through the shoulders, adolescent, not grown.

Colour: the road, the ground and the wall in dust-brown, dull ochre and
pale bone; the green only at the top of the trees; the water grey-green
and white. The sharpest step from light to dark in the picture is the
black mouth of the arch against the moving water. His face is fully
drawn and held at closer, quieter values, with soft edges.

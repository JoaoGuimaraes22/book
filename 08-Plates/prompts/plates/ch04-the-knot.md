Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Setting: the scrubbed plank table of a small mountain house,
daylight from one side, as loose wash — the room beyond falling
into shadow, no detail.

Composition: a CLOSE overhead view of the table top. No faces, no
heads, no shoulders — the frame holds the table, three hands, one
cup and one knotted cord. Everything above the forearm is out of
frame.

AT THE CENTRE, ONE CORD OF DARK SILK TIED IN A COMPLICATED KNOT,
about the size of a fist, sitting on the bare boards. A BOY'S TWO
HANDS ARE ON IT, working the cord: one hand holding the knot
steady, the fingers of the other pushed into a loop. The cord is IN
HIS HANDS and is not lying loose on the table.

THE BOY'S HANDS ARE A CHILD'S AND THE COMPARISON IS THE ONLY THING
CARRYING IT: each of his hands is little more than half the size of
the old man's hand in the same picture, and his whole hand would
sit inside the old man's palm. Short fingers, soft knuckles, no
tendon or vein standing out anywhere, small nails.

ONE POTTERY CUP stands on the table at the edge of the frame,
upright and full, untouched, with a large weathered old man's hand
resting on the boards beside it — not holding it, set down flat next
to it. Heavy knuckles, thick wrist, the skin dry and worked.

Three hands, one cup, one cord. Nothing else is on the table and
nothing else is held. NO FOURTH HAND ANYWHERE IN THE FRAME.

THE KNOT IS THE SUBJECT. It is the most precisely drawn thing in
the picture and carries the sharpest step from light to dark — the
silk dark, tight and legible, every turn of the cord readable. The
table, the cup, the sleeves and the hands are held at quiet middle
values, loosely washed, no hard edges.

The sleeves at the frame's edges are coarse undyed mended wool, THE
INK CARRYING THE CLOTH at the seams; the old man's sleeve is a
plain dark well-cut coat.

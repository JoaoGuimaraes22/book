Image 1: portrait reference of the man.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house in a
storm, as loose wash suggestion — a fenced pen, a low roof-line, the
mountain lost in grey wash. Rain across at a steep angle in dry-brush
drag and flicked spatter, everything soaked and dark. Flat cold storm
light; the only warm value is the skin of the man's face and hand.

Composition: three figures close together and touching, cropped at
the knee, on the same ground plane. THE MAN down on one knee in the
mud at the goat's level, turned in toward it. THE GOAT in front of
him, side-on, its head near his. THE BOY at the man's side, pressed
against his shoulder, very small.

A LARGE MAN FOLDED DOWN SMALL is the subject. His right hand is flat
and still on the goat's neck, fingers spread, not gripping and not
scratching; his left rests open on his own raised knee; the boy's
near hand holds a fold of the man's sleeve. Nothing is held in any
hand and there is no tool, rope or bucket in the picture.

HE IS TALKING TO THE GOAT: mouth open mid-word, face turned to the
animal and not to the boy. HIS EXPRESSION DOES NOT CHANGE FOR ANY OF
IT — level, unhurried, giving nothing, as unreadable as a man
discussing weather. NOT smiling, NOT tender, NOT soft, NOT
gentle-faced. The kindness is in where his body is and what his hand
is doing.

THE GOAT HAS STOPPED BEING FRIGHTENED: soaked, standing still, head
lowered slightly toward the man, ears loose, no white in the eye.

THE BOY IS FOUR AND MUST READ FOUR: his head no higher than the
kneeling man's shoulder, head large for the body, arms and legs
short. His face is turned away from the viewer and down toward the
goat — the back and side of his head only, no feature drawn. Dark
hair plastered flat with rain.

Both wear coarse undyed mended wool soaked dark — the man's heavy and
sized to him, the boy's thin and too large — THE INK CARRYING THE
CLOTH at the seams and the mended places.

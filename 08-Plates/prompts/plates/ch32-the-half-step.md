Image 2: oss-14.png — portrait reference of Oss.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the yard of a small stone house high on a mountain at
night, as loose wash suggestion — the wall of the house on one side
with a low doorway standing open, a stacked woodpile behind them,
dry-laid pen walls going away down the slope, and the underside of a
low cloud overhead orange from one side of the frame to the other.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is in the yard with them, close and
level. KAEL is on the left, standing square, three-quarters toward
the camera with his head turned to Oss. OSS is on the right, facing
him, half a stride into a movement toward him and stopped there —
the near shoulder come forward, the weight on the front foot. Three
or four feet behind and between them, crossing the ground from left
to right, the shoulder and upper arm of a soldier walking past, cut
by the frame, with no head and no face anywhere in the picture. The
bottom edge crosses both boys at the waist, so that no legs, no feet
and no ground are in the frame at all.

Nothing is being said. One boy has come half a stride toward the
other and has stopped, the other has not moved at all, and a
stranger's shoulder is already coming into the ground between them.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. The lines of sight meet
across the gap. This eye contact is the subject of the image.
Neither is looking at the viewer.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
Kael's right hand holds one short blade and nothing else.
Kael's left hand holds one short blade and nothing else.
Both blades are down at his sides with the points toward the ground.
Oss's left hand is empty and down.
Oss's right hand has come half up off his side and stopped there,
open, at the height of his own waist.
Four hands, two blades, nothing else in any hand. No other blade,
hilt or point appears anywhere in the frame.

KAEL — standing still and square, the blades down, the face level,
the eyes steady on Oss, the mouth closed and the jaw set but easy.
Undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much
mended, no ornament, badge, colour or metal on him anywhere. There
is dried red to the elbow on both forearms, dark and gone brown, and
none of it is his; nothing on him is bleeding.

OSS — stopped half a stride in, the near shoulder forward, the free
hand come half up and open, the mouth open on nothing, the brows up
and drawn together. Everything he has is on his face. A good coat,
dyed madder red-brown, well cut, with one small bright button at the
collar — too new for the ground he is standing on, and six miles of
a mountain now on it: dust to the knee, wet at the shoulders, the
hem dirty. Not torn, not ragged.

Lighting: two sources and no others. The open doorway is warm and
low and close, on Kael's near side and along the edge of Oss's face
and coat; the orange sky above puts a thin light on the tops of the
heads and shoulders of both. Everything past four feet goes into
shadow.

The two faces carry the most finished drawing in the picture; the
doorway, the woodpile, the soldier's shoulder and the ground stay at
quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. Do not
mix their clothing: the dyed red-brown coat and the bright collar
button belong only to Oss, and Kael wears undyed mended wool with no
colour on it at all. Kael's eyes are pale near-white grey; Oss's are
a warm mid-brown.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: a packed bank of arena seating in fog, plain cut stone
benches rising steeply, the rows above going up into grey and losing
their shape. Cold flat light with no sun in it and the first smoke
coming along under the benches.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is in among the rows, close, a little
above and to one side. The whole picture is a crowd on its feet and
moving one way, up and to the right toward an arch — backs,
shoulders, arms and heads carried together, drawn as summary shapes
with no faces anywhere and nothing in them sharp enough to read as a
person.

ONE BODY IS TURNED THE OTHER WAY AND THAT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
IMAGE. In the middle of the frame a heavy-set boy of about thirteen
has turned right round against the flow, his shoulders square to the
bench behind him and his back to everywhere the crowd is going. He is
bent forward with both hands already down and out and closed on the
arms of a much smaller boy on that bench, lifting him. His head is
turned down toward the child, so that his face is away from the
camera and no features are drawn on him. Plain school clothing, dark
and good, nothing on it.

The smaller boy is on the bench with his feet not yet under him, both
arms up and taken.

He is the only figure in the picture drawn with any finish at all;
every other body stays at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled
edges, and the sharpest step from light to dark in the picture
happens at his two hands where they close on the child's arms.

Nobody in the picture is on the ground, nobody is under anybody, and
no fire and no weapon is in the frame.

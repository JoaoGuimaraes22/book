Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a small book-lined study at the top of a stone school on a
wet morning, as loose wash suggestion — a heavy writing desk, shelves
of books going back into shadow, and a tall window at the left with
rain running down the glass. The window is the only light in the
picture and the door behind them is shut.

Composition: 4:3. The camera is inside the room at seated height. The
old man is left of centre, standing, leaning back against the front
edge of the desk with his arms folded and his ankles crossed, in
frame from the knee up. The boy is on the right, nearer the camera,
seated on a plain wooden chair, in frame from the chest up, both
hands flat on his own knees at the bottom edge of the picture.
Between them, holding the middle of the frame, an empty stretch of
bare board floor with the window's grey light lying across it.

NOTHING IS HAPPENING AND NOBODY IS LOOKING AT ANYBODY, AND THAT IS
THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. The old man's eyes are down on the floor
between them, the boy's eyes are down on his own hands, and neither
of them is looking at the viewer.

THE OLD MAN HAS JUST SAID ONE WORD AND IT WAS NOT THE ONE HE WANTED
TO SAY. His mouth closed, the head a little down, the brows level,
the whole face giving out nothing it has not priced — a man who has
spent a morning finding something out, and has found it out. Not
tired, not kindly, not stern. The arms folded and the sleeves back
off the wrists, and the wrists are too thick for a man whose work is
paper. Tall, gone lean, still broad across the shoulder; hair fully
grey, the whole head, a true neutral grey and even everywhere. A long
plain dark wool robe closed to the throat, no ornament and no chain.

THE BOY IS TAKING IT WITHOUT MOVING. His face level and plain, his
mouth closed, his hands flat and still on his knees. Not sullen, not
stricken. Coarse undyed working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal
and dead olive, dark and wet across the shoulders, and his hair still
wet from the road; no ornament and no metal on him.

On the desk behind the old man, two letters lie open and flat with
their seals broken; a small stoneware jar stands at the desk's far
corner; and a potted fern stands untouched on the window sill.

Colour: cool grey daylight through the rain on the glass, cool grey
stone and dark wood throughout; the two letters are the palest things
in the room after the window itself, and his robe is the darkest mass
in the picture.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features.

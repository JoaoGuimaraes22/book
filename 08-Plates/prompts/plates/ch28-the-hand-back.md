Image 1: portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.
Image 2: full-figure reference of the same boy, for his build and
clothing.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a narrow village lane as loose wash suggestion — rough
stone house fronts close on both sides, low roofs above them, the
far end of the lane going up into smoke and fog and stopping. A low
stone well-head behind him at one side. Flat cold fogged daylight,
no sun and no hard-edged shadow anywhere, and smoke lying along the
lane at about the height of his knee.

Composition: one boy alone, standing, close to the viewer and turned
three-quarters toward it, the bottom edge of the picture crossing
him at the waist. His left arm is extended forward and a little
across his body, the elbow nearly straight, the open hand held out
at the height of his own chest and nearer the viewer than his face.
The open hand is wider in the frame than his head. He is completely
still.

HIS LEFT HAND IS OPEN AND TOUCHING NOTHING, the fingers spread and
loosely curled, the palm turned up and angled toward the viewer. His
right arm hangs at his side and that hand is open and empty as well.

Left shoulder, torn sleeve, bare forearm, open hand — one continuous
limb, in that order. The sleeve is torn away below the left
shoulder. A [burn / long-healed mark] runs along that forearm from
wrist to elbow. There is dark red across the whole of that same left
hand, front and back, gone darker where it has dried in the creases
of the fingers and the palm.

Grey dust and soot across the front of him and along both arms, one
side of his face grazed and raw-looking below the ear. There is no
red anywhere on him except that hand.

HIS EYES ARE ON HIS OWN HAND: the lids fully raised, the pale grey
clear, the mouth open and the jaw slack, the brows level and
unmoved. He is not looking at the viewer.

He is the only figure in the picture. No other person, no body, no
weapon anywhere in the frame.

THE OPEN HAND WINS THE PICTURE. His face and the whole of the rest
of him are held at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges and
no hard-edged dark anywhere; the sharpest step from light to dark in
the picture happens at the edge of the red on the hand. The house
fronts, the well-head, the smoke and the far end of the lane stay
quieter still.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown
in the reference images, which are the same boy. Pale grey eyes.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the yard of a village smith on a bright cold afternoon —
hard swept dirt, a water trough, a woodpile, a low dry-stone wall,
and the open door of a forge shed set back at one side. The sun has
come round the corner of the shed and is lying across the middle of
the yard. Loose wash suggestion, no detail anywhere.

Composition: 3:4. Waist-up and a little more, near the viewer. THE
PRINCESS stands in the sun, turned three-quarters toward the camera,
with a SMALL CHILD of about four up on her hip. The child is held
one-handed and badly, high and awkward. Behind them, well back and
small, three or four grown men stand together in the yard and every
one of them is facing away. Set back at one side, in the dark mouth of
the forge shed and about twenty feet off, ONE YOUNG MAN stands with a
bucket in his hand, facing this way, and he is the only person in the
picture who can see it.

THE CLOTH HAS JUST COME OFF HER HEAD AND ALL OF IT IS DOWN. The
child's two hands are still shut on the back of the cloth, pulling it
away and up; the princess has the front of it caught in her own two
hands and is not letting go of it. It is a small clumsy tug of war
between somebody who wants it and somebody who wants it back, and it
has already been lost.

HER HAIR IS THE PICTURE. Loose and enormous and down her back and
over both shoulders, silver-white and colourless, unwashed and heavy
and grey with two months of dust — and the sun is straight into it,
and the dust makes no difference at all: it is the brightest and
lightest thing in the frame by a long way and there is nothing else
in the picture near that colour.

SHE IS LAUGHING. Openly, caught mid-laugh, head back a little, eyes
half shut, and it is the only unguarded face this person has. The
child is laughing too, from behind and above, both fists full of
cloth.

On each of her wrists a plain closed ring of dark metal with no more
chain between them than the width of her own hand — the iron is still
on her and it is what makes the holding clumsy. A coarse village
dress far too big for her, lent, dull oatmeal.

Flat cold winter daylight, and her hair carries all of it. The men,
the wall, the woodpile and the shed stay at quiet middle values with
soft wash-bled edges; the young man in the shed doorway is a dark
shape with a face barely resolved.

Preserve her face and colouring exactly as shown in the reference
image. Pale blue eyes.

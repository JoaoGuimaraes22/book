Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the flat stone working ground of a mountain smallholding at
first light in early spring, as loose wash suggestion — wet stone
underfoot, the yard falling away into cool grey wash behind them.
Cold flat grey daylight, no sun and no shadow with an edge anywhere.

Composition: two figures, waist-up. The man stands, holding a plain
short blade upright in front of him in one hand, point up, the arm
relaxed and low and the elbow soft. The boy of twelve sits on the wet
stone at the near edge of the frame, in frame from the chest up, his
face turned up toward the blade. The blade stands clear of them both
and is pointed at nobody. Nothing is happening: nobody is fighting,
nobody is moving, and no other blade is anywhere in the picture.

THE LENGTH OF THE BLADE IS THE SUBJECT. A line of pale light runs the
whole length of the steel from the hilt to the point, unbroken and
even from end to end, not flickering and not brightest at either end.
It is reserved as bare unpainted paper and is the lightest thing in
the picture; nothing else in the frame is painted that white, and no
light comes off it onto the stone, the yard or either face.

BOTH OF THEM ARE LOOKING AT THE BLADE and at nothing else. Neither is
looking at the viewer and neither is looking at the other.

THE MAN IS DOING NOTHING AND IT COSTS HIM NOTHING: the shoulder low,
the wrist easy, the whole body at rest, and the face level and
unhurried and giving nothing away — not straining, not concentrating,
not proud. Iron-dark hair, a heavily weathered face, coarse undyed
working wool, much mended, closed at the throat.

The boy's face is the opposite and the block says so: mouth a little
open, brows drawn, the whole attention up on the steel, still working
out what he is looking at. Undyed coarse working wool, much mended,
dull oatmeal and dead olive.

Wet stone and cold grey daylight everywhere; the steel and the two
faces are the most finished things in the picture and the yard behind
stays at quiet middle values with soft edges. No ornament and no
metal on either of them anywhere except the blade.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. The man's eyes dark,
the boy's pale grey.

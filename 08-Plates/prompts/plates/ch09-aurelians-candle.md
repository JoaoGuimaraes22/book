Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: a winter training yard at the foot of a high stone wall, as
loose wash suggestion — rough stone behind him going into deep cool
shadow, frost on the ground, no detail anywhere. The edge of a plank
bench crosses the very bottom of the frame.

Composition: close on his head and shoulders, the face filling most
of the frame, seen from slightly below. His head is tipped down
toward the flame in front of him. Nothing below his chest is in
frame.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS THE FIRE AND IT IS BELOW HIM. On the bench in front
of him a soft sheet of orange flame stands up off the top of a tallow
candle, a double handful of fire, low in the frame and cropped by its
bottom edge. It lights his face entirely from underneath: the
underside of the jaw and the chin, the underside of the brow, the
septum and the underside of the nose, the front of the throat. The
forehead, the top of the head and the hair above it fall away into
shadow. Everything behind him stays cold — the wall, the air, the
frost — and no warm colour touches any of it.

THE FIRE IS THE BRIGHTEST VALUE. HIS FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING.
The flame is soft-edged and loose, a wash with no hard edge anywhere
in it and no detail in it at all. The sharpest step from light to
dark in the picture happens along the boundary of the lit part of his
face, at the cheekbone and the jaw.

HIS EXPRESSION IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Lit up, open and
delighted — a boy standing exactly where he belongs with the world
put back the way it should be. Lips parted, the breath still coming
hard, brows raised, the whole face bright and unguarded and young.
NOT smug, NOT sneering, NOT triumphant over anybody, NOT cruel, NOT
sly, NOT knowing — and equally NOT frightened, NOT startled, NOT
alarmed. He is not looking at any person and there is nobody in the
frame he could be pleased at. He is looking at the fire.

Eyes amber, gold-brown, wide open and turned down to the flame,
catching it. The skin immediately around the eye stays cool, so the
amber holds and does not wash out into the firelight.

Dark-gold hair pushed back off the forehead, thick, dry and clean.
Clear well-fed skin with sun on it, unweathered, no dirt and nothing
worked into it anywhere.

Thirteen years old and built a year ahead of it: broad through the
shoulder and the neck for his age, the frame already going toward a
man's, and the face still soft and unfinished with the jaw not yet
come in.

Good dark wool, well cut and clean, a plain collar. No insignia, no
badge, no chain, no metal and no ornament of any kind anywhere on
him.

NO OTHER FACE IS IN THIS PICTURE. The other children are present only
as dark unlit shapes at the extreme edges of the frame, out of focus,
no features drawn — one at the left edge going backward and off
balance, away from the fire.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a raw new slot cut down the eastern side of a mountain at
night, as loose wash suggestion — broken stone and turned earth
going down and away under them, the slope rising behind. High up and
well back, small with distance, a low stone house stands with its
door open and light coming out of it onto the stones, and one corner
of its roof has caught and is going up; below the house a line of
burning pens laid along the slope, and a pale burnt-out flat beside
them. The underside of a low cloud is orange from one side of the
frame to the other. Everything above the near figures stays as loose
wash and nothing in it is resolved: no faces and no bodies anywhere
in the far ground.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is behind and below the two of them,
close, looking up the slope. Nearest, filling the lower half of the
frame, THE OLD MAN's back and shoulders go away up the picture, seen
from behind, his head turned away — no part of his face is in the
frame. KAEL is over the old man's right shoulder, his body hanging
down the old man's back, his head clear of the shoulder and up, the
face turned back toward the camera and past it. Both of Kael's arms
hang straight down the old man's back with a LONGSWORD held in both
hands, the blade hanging point-down and going out through the bottom
edge of the frame. The bottom edge crosses the old man at the small
of the back, so that no legs, no feet and no ground under them are
in the frame at all.

He is awake, he is being carried, and he is not fighting anything.
The whole of him hangs and nothing in him is braced. The one thing
he is doing is holding on to the sword.

KAEL — the face up and turned back over the shoulder, the eyes open
and steady on the burning house behind them, the mouth open and
slack, the jaw loose, the brows flat. Not squeezed shut, not
shouting, not asleep. There is wet down both cheeks and along the
jaw, not wiped. His hair hangs. His arms hang straight with no
weight anywhere in them, and his two hands are shut hard on the grip
and are the only part of him doing anything at all. Undyed coarse
working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, much mended, and it is
more red than cloth now, dark and gone brown and soaked, and none of
it is his. A healed burn from wrist to elbow on the outside of the
left forearm. Nothing bleeding.

THE OLD MAN — his back, both shoulders and the back of his grey head
only, the frame under the coat still broad across the shoulder. A
heavy coat somebody else gave him and it does not fit: too big
through the shoulders, short at the wrist, dull umber, no ornament
on it. His forearms are bare where the sleeves have been pushed
back. No part of his face is in the picture, and do not turn his
head toward the viewer.

The sword is a grown man's war sword and far too big for the boy
holding it: the blade as long as he is, a long grip for two hands, a
plain straight cross guard, no ornament and no colour on any part of
it.

Lighting: the fire on the house and in the pens is far up the slope
and does not reach the two near figures. They are lit by the orange
sky above — cool on the tops of the shoulders and along the side of
the boy's face — and the fires are the one warm thing in the picture
and are small in it.

The boy's face carries the most finished drawing in the picture and
the sharpest step from light to dark; the old man's back, the slope,
the pens and the house stay at quiet middle values with soft
wash-bled edges.

Preserve the boy's face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as
shown in his reference image. His eyes are pale near-white grey.

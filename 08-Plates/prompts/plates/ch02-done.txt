Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the man.

Setting: a mountain smallholding at DUSK — the sky going grey, the
light nearly gone, pens and woodpile flat dark shapes, the sea far
below a cold pale sheet. Loose wash, no detail. ONE BAT high over the
pens, a single small dark mark no bigger than a thumbprint.

Composition: both figures full length, mid-distance, close together
near the corner of the pen, on the same ground plane.

THE BOY IS A CHILD AND HIS HEIGHT IS THE MAN'S LOWER RIBS — head
large for the body, limbs short. He is getting up out of the ground
and his legs do not work: caught partway, unbalanced, weight on one
bent leg with the other knee still down, one hand flat on the ground
taking his weight, back rounded, head hanging. Assembled wrong, like
a newborn animal standing for the first time. His face is down toward
the ground and no feature of it is drawn. Not crying, not grimacing,
not appealing to anyone — working.

THE MAN IS WITHIN ARM'S REACH AND IS NOT HELPING. Square, still,
weight even, turned toward the boy and watching him. BOTH HIS HANDS
ARE DOWN AT HIS SIDES, OPEN AND EMPTY AND UNMOVING — not reaching,
not crouching, not bending, not offering a hand, not steadying him.
He is close enough to catch the boy and he is not catching him. Do
not resolve this into a father helping his son up. His face is level,
mouth closed, completely unreadable: not concerned, not proud, not
stern, not sad, not kind.

Both wear coarse undyed mended village cloth — the man's heavy and
sized to him, the boy's thin and too large — THE INK CARRYING THE
CLOTH at the seams and the mended places.

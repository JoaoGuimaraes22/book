Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the single room of a small mountain house at the end of the
night, as loose wash suggestion — a heavy hand-made plank table,
rough boards and a plank wall behind, the room falling away into deep
cool shadow. One oil lamp stands at the far edge of the table off to
one side, low and warm, and is the only light in the picture.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is at the side of the table at seated
height and close in, so that the tabletop runs across the lower third
of the frame and the two of them fill the upper two thirds, seen from
the side and facing each other across it. The bottom edge of the
picture crosses the near edge of the tabletop, so that no laps, no
knees, no chairs and no floor are in the frame at all. The man is on
the left, chest-up, leaning in with both forearms coming forward over
the boards. The boy is on the right, chest-up, sitting back a little,
both his hands resting on the table in front of him and neither of
them raised.

THE KNIFE IS HELD OUT ACROSS THE TABLE, HILT FIRST, AND IT HAS NOT
BEEN TAKEN YET. The man's right hand holds it flat by the blade with
the grip turned toward the boy and the whole of it offered level over
the boards; the boy's hands are on the table and neither has moved
toward it; there is a hand's width of air between the offered grip
and the nearest of the boy's hands.

The knife is small and plain and old: a straight single-edged blade
about the length of a hand, narrow, with a plain wooden grip worn
smooth and dark, no guard at all, no fittings and no ornament on it,
and the steel cleaned back years ago to its own dull weather. It is
the only blade in the picture.

THE MAN IS SAYING ONE THING ONCE. His mouth just closing on the end
of it, the brows unmoved, the eyes level and empty and steady on the
boy's face. Not warning, not angry, not kind.

THE BOY IS LISTENING AND HIS EYES ARE NOT ON THE KNIFE. His mouth
closed, his face level and giving nothing away, his eyes on the man's
face.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER over the offered knife,
the lines of sight meeting above it, and the knife lying below that
in the frame. This eye contact is the subject of the image. Neither
of them is looking at the viewer.

Both wear coarse undyed working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal
and dead olive, with no ornament and no metal on either of them. The
man's sleeves are down to the wrist and his forearms are covered.

Lighting: the lamp stands low at the far side of the table and lights
the near side of both faces, the offered hand and the flat of the
blade, and leaves the wall and the corners of the room black.

Colour: the lamplight is a deep warm orange and is the only saturated
colour in the frame; the boards, the wool and the steel go cool
grey-brown under it; the boy's eyes are pale grey and near white and
are the lightest thing in his face.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. The man is the older
and much heavier of the two.

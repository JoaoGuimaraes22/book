Image 1: portrait reference of the man.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house in a
storm, as loose wash suggestion — a low roof-line with slates on it,
a fenced goat pen, the mountain beyond lost in grey wash. Rain across
at a steep angle in dry-brush drag and flicked spatter. Flat cold
storm light from a low sky; the only warm value is skin.

Composition: the man waist-up, turned away from his own raised hand
toward a goat in the pen at the left, mid-sentence, talking to the
animal.

His right arm is straight up above and behind his head and his right
hand has closed on ONE flat grey roof slate, held flat in the palm,
caught and stopped, not falling and not being thrown. His left hand
is down at his side, empty.

HE IS NOT LOOKING AT THE SLATE — his face is turned to the goat,
mouth open mid-word, his expression unchanged for the catch: level,
unhurried, giving nothing, no effort anywhere in the body. Do not
have him look up at it, and do not make him alarmed, braced, heroic
or surprised.

One goat in the pen, unsettled by the weather, looking at him.

THE MAN: broad through the shoulders and no further, heavy across the
top of the back with nothing wasted below it. Iron-dark hair of
medium length, wet, pushed back off the forehead. Weathered hard.
Coarse undyed working cloth soaked dark, plain collar closed to the
throat, THE INK CARRYING THE CLOTH in dense scratchy linework at the
seams and the mended places.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills.

Setting: a passage inside a house on the southern coast, at night.
The walls are earth plaster the colour of the ground with the
ground's own grain in them. Eight doorways stand open in a row down
the right-hand wall of the passage, a small lamp burning in the room
behind each one. The left-hand wall is blank its whole length — no
door, no niche, nothing on it at all.

Composition: tall upright frame, taller than it is wide. The viewer
stands out in the open court, about ten paces back from the mouth of
the passage, looking straight down its length.

At the very bottom of the frame, the near court in the dark: the cut
stone lip of a channel with water running in it, and three standing
figures seen from behind, the backs of their heads and their
shoulders only, cut off by the bottom edge. Above them, the mouth of
the passage, with a man standing at each side of it facing back into
the court, each holding a lamp low at his hip; they are near
silhouettes against the lit passage behind them, their faces dark.
Above and beyond them the passage runs away: the eight lit doorways
down the right, the warm light out of each one lying across the
passage floor in a bar, the bars growing shorter and narrower as
they go, the far end of the passage lost in dark.

The nearest lit doorway is about as tall in the frame as one of the
two men at the mouth. The farthest is no taller than the lamp he
carries.

Nothing is happening. The passage is empty its whole length, floor
to far end, and the three figures at the bottom edge stand with
their weight settled and their hands down.

Lighting: the eight lamps in the doorways and the two lamps carried
at the mouth are the only light in the picture. Warm ochre where the
lamplight lands — the passage floor, the near edge of each doorframe,
the plaster inside each opening — and cool grey-brown going to ink
dark everywhere else, the court at the bottom of the frame the
darkest part of the picture. One thread of light on the running
water. No sky, no moon, no window.

No eyes are visible anywhere in the picture, and no head is turned
toward the viewer.

Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the boat sheds of a small fishing village at first light, as
loose wash suggestion — a packed earth road coming down the slope, a
row of low timber sheds with tarred boards, two clinker-built boats
hauled up on the shingle and turned over on trestles, and past them
flat grey water. The sun is only just up behind the water, low and
pale, so that the road and the sheds are still in cold blue shade and
the water carries the only warm light in the picture. Woodsmoke lying
low along the roofs.

Composition: 16:9. The camera stands on the road at head height. On
the right, near, the boy is in frame from the chest up,
three-quarters on and turned away down the slope, his head turned so
that his face reads in three-quarter view. On the left, at about
twenty strides, a man has straightened up off the turned hull he was
working on and is looking up the road: a whole figure at that
distance, small in the frame, drawn as a working man's shape with no
features on the face at all.

NOTHING IS HAPPENING. The two of them are looking at each other
across twenty strides of village road at dawn, and neither of them is
going to do anything about it.

THE MAN'S BODY IS THE EVIDENCE AND IT IS DRAWN EXACTLY. He has come
round shoulder first, the near shoulder swung up the road while his
feet are still square to the hull. The outside of his right sleeve,
from the elbow to the wrist, is rubbed pale and worn smooth where the
rest of the coat is dark. He holds a wooden mallet down at his side
in one hand, and an iron hoop leans against the hull beside him.

THE BOY'S FACE IS DOING ARITHMETIC. The eyes narrowed and entirely on
the man, the mouth closed, the brows a little drawn, the head
absolutely still. This is a boy four minutes into pricing a stranger
and finding nothing. Not wary, not frightened, not curious.

Coarse undyed working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead
olive, the collar up against the cold and the shoulders dark with
wet. No ornament and no metal on him.

Nobody else is on the road and nobody else is in the frame.

Colour: cold blue-grey shade over the road, the sheds and both
figures; the water behind them pale and warm with the low sun on it;
the boy's eyes pale grey and near white, the lightest thing in his
face.

The boy's face is the most finished thing in the picture and holds
the sharpest step from light to dark, inside the eye at the edge of
the iris; the man, the sheds and the boats stay at quiet middle
values with soft wash-bled edges and no hard-edged dark anywhere.

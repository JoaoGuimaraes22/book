Image 1: vask.png — portrait reference of the iron-haired man.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a farmyard threshing floor at the end of the day, as loose wash
suggestion — flat worn flagstones, a low stone yard wall, the dark mouth
of a gateway behind him, a house gable implied at one side with fire
burning in its roof a long way back. The daylight is nearly gone and the
sky above the wall is dim.

Composition: the picture is seen from a hand's breadth above the
flagstones. The floor runs away from the bottom edge as a broad plane
lit end to end, and the man is seen from below: the underside of his jaw
is visible, his boots are large and near the bottom of the frame, and his
head sits high against the dim sky. He is walking in through the gateway
toward the viewer and is not yet close, standing about three of his own
heights away. He fills the middle of the frame and is the only figure in
the picture. The boy the view belongs to is not in the frame, and no
other person is drawn anywhere in it.

THE WHITE IS IN THE MAN AND NOT AROUND HIM: hard thin white filaments
run down both arms from the shoulders to the fingers, across the
shoulders, down the middle of the chest, and down both legs into the
stone at each foot, following the length of him. Every filament lies on
him and clings to him. Nothing arcs off into the air, nothing leaps to
the stone, nothing sprays, and there is no glow, no haze and no
cloud around him anywhere.

HIS RIGHT HAND HOLDS ONE PLAIN LONGSWORD and nothing else, carried low
and back, the point behind him and clear of the ground. The same hard
white filament stands the whole length of that blade, hilt to point,
lying on the steel, with the steel drawn as steel underneath it and
still reading as metal. His left hand is empty and open at his side.
Two hands, one blade, nothing else in either hand, and no other blade,
hilt or point is drawn anywhere in the picture.

HE IS THE LIGHT IN THE PICTURE. The white light is cold and hard-edged
and it comes off him alone: it throws his shadow and the shadow of the
yard wall up the house behind him, it puts a rim on the flagstones
around his feet, and it is the only strong light anywhere in the frame.
The fire in the far roof is small, dull orange, soft-edged and low in
value, and does not light him or the floor.

HE IS BREATHING HARD: the mouth open, the chest and shoulders working,
the head level. His eyes are on something past the viewer and a little
above it, and he is not looking at the viewer. The face gives out
nothing else — not angry, not grim, not fierce.

He wears a plain coarse working jacket of undyed dark wool, straight-cut
and closed at the throat, the sleeves rolled up above the elbows so that
both forearms are bare, and heavy village working trousers and boots.
Nothing is tailored, nothing is fitted, nothing is decorated: this is not
a frock coat, not a uniform and not armour, and there is no metal on him
anywhere except the sword.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in
the reference image — the weathered face, the iron-dark hair, the
iron-dark eyes.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the dirt yard of a fishing village house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — the corner of a shed, frozen mud, everything going
into deep cool wash.

A LONG HARD-EDGED BLADE OF YELLOW LAMPLIGHT LIES ACROSS THE YARD from
outside the frame, and it is the only light. What throws it is never
shown: no door, doorway or window appears anywhere in the frame. The
two children are at the blade's edge, lit down one side.

Composition: close, the two of them filling the frame, the frozen
ground visible beneath them. A large spoked cart wheel stands at the
left with the girl sitting against it.

THE VIEWER IS BEHIND THE GIRL AND ABOVE HER, AND THEREFORE SEES: the
back of her shoulders, the backs of her bound hands and the knot
below them, the near face of the wheel, and the boy beyond her turned
back toward the viewer. Her wrists and the knot are the nearest thing
in the picture and NOTHING STANDS IN FRONT OF THEM.

The girl sits on the ground with her back against the wheel, knees
drawn up. Her arms are taken back around the wheel's rim, and one
length of rope goes twice around the rim and her wrists together and
finishes in a knot set low behind her, below where her fingers could
reach. She is a child: her head is about a sixth of her whole height,
and seated like this the top of her head comes to the wheel's hub.

Her head is turned back and up over her shoulder toward the boy, so
her face is in view. Only her near side is lit — enough to read the
eyes and the mouth, not enough to read a colour; her hair, her eye
colour and her clothing stay unreadable in the dark. Her eyes are
wide open and her mouth is closed.

The boy crouches beyond her wrists, one knee down, leaning in. His
near arm comes down to the knot — shoulder, arm, hand, rope, in that
order, one continuous limb — and both his hands are on the rope,
working it. Nothing else is in either hand, and the rope is in his
fingers rather than slack on the ground.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS
THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. He is not looking at his hands; they read
the knot without his eyes.

Her lit cheek and eye are the brightest values in the picture and
carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The knot is in shadow and
is carried by ink line instead — every turn of the rope legible in
dark scratchy linework, with no light falling on it. The wheel, the
ground, the shed and both children's clothing are held at quiet low
values with no hard edges.

His face is absorbed and unhurried, his whole attention on hers — a
boy doing something he has done a thousand times. NOT brave, NOT
determined, no jaw set and no resolve in it anywhere; and equally NOT
frightened and NOT pitying. DO NOT DRAW A RESCUE.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless — no more colour in
the iris than in the whites around it. Dark hair cut short and
uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick for a winter night, the ink
carrying the cloth at the seams and the mended places.

Nobody else is in the picture and no figure stands in the light. No
blood and no mark on either child, no weapon anywhere, and no rope
except the one at her wrists.

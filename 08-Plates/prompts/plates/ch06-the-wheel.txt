Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the dirt yard of a fishing village house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — a cart wheel, the corner of a shed, frozen mud, all
of it going into deep cool wash. Nothing else is resolved.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS A LONG HARD-EDGED BLADE OF YELLOW LAMPLIGHT LYING
ACROSS THE YARD, entering from outside the frame at one side. The two
children are at its edge, mostly in the dark beside it, caught along
one side only. WHAT THE LIGHT COMES FROM IS NOT IN THIS PICTURE: no
door, no doorway, no window, no lamp, no room and no interior is
visible anywhere in the frame.

Composition: the two children close together, seen from the side and
slightly above, cropped at the hip. A large spoked cart wheel stands
behind the girl, filling the frame behind her.

THE GIRL sits on the ground against the wheel, knees drawn up. Her
arms are taken back behind her around the wheel's rim, and one length
of rope goes TWICE around the rim and her wrists together and
finishes in a knot set low behind her, below where her fingers could
reach. The rope and the wheel are drawn precisely. She is a child of
about the boy's own size.

HER FACE IS TURNED TOWARD THE LIGHT AND ONLY HER NEAR SIDE IS LIT —
enough to read the eyes and the mouth, not enough to read a colour.
Her hair, her eye colour and her clothing stay unreadable in the
dark. Her eyes are WIDE OPEN and her mouth is CLOSED and held closed.

THE BOY crouches at her side, close, one knee down. His near arm
reaches past her body to the knot behind the wheel — shoulder, arm,
hand, rope, in that order, one continuous limb, and BOTH HIS HANDS
ARE ON THE ROPE, working it. Nothing else is in either hand. The rope
is in his fingers and is not lying slack on the ground.

HE IS NOT LOOKING AT HIS HANDS. THE TWO CHILDREN ARE LOOKING
DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
IMAGE. His hands work the knot without his eyes.

His expression is ABSORBED AND UNHURRIED — a boy doing something he
has done a thousand times, with his whole attention on her face. NOT
brave, NOT determined, NOT heroic, NOT urgent, NOT grim, no jaw set
and no resolve anywhere in it; and equally NOT frightened, NOT
pitying, NOT horrified. Nothing in his face has decided anything.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, catching the
light — no more colour in the iris than in the whites around it.
Dark hair cut short and uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick for
a winter night.

There is nobody else in the picture: no adult, no third child, no
figure at the edge of the frame and no shape in the light. No blood,
no wound, no mark on either child, no weapon, no knife and no rope
anywhere except the one at her wrists.

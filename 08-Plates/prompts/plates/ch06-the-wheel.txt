Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the dirt yard of a fishing village house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — a cart wheel, the corner of a shed, frozen mud,
everything going into deep cool wash.

A LONG HARD-EDGED BLADE OF YELLOW LAMPLIGHT LIES ACROSS THE YARD from
outside the frame, and it is the only light in the picture. The two
children are at its edge, in the dark beside it, caught along one
side only. What throws it is never shown: no door, doorway or window
appears anywhere in the frame.

Composition: both children close together, seen from the side and
slightly above, cropped at the hip. A large spoked cart wheel stands
behind the girl and fills the frame behind her.

The girl sits on the ground against the wheel, knees drawn up, a
child of about the boy's own size. Her arms are taken back around the
wheel's rim, and one length of rope goes twice around the rim and her
wrists together and finishes in a knot set low behind her, below
where her fingers could reach. The rope and the wheel are drawn
precisely. Her face is turned toward the light and only her near side
is lit — enough to read the eyes and the mouth, not enough to read a
colour; her hair, her eye colour and her clothing stay unreadable in
the dark. Her eyes are wide open and her mouth is closed.

The boy crouches at her side, one knee down. His near arm reaches
past her body to the knot behind the wheel — shoulder, arm, hand,
rope, in that order, one continuous limb — and both his hands are on
the rope, working it. Nothing else is in either hand, and the rope is
in his fingers rather than slack on the ground.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS
THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. He is not looking at his hands; they read
the knot without his eyes.

His face is absorbed and unhurried, his whole attention on hers — a
boy doing something he has done a thousand times. NOT brave, NOT
determined, no jaw set and no resolve in it anywhere; and equally NOT
frightened and NOT pitying. DO NOT DRAW A RESCUE.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, catching the light
— no more colour in the iris than in the whites around it. Dark hair
cut short and uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick for a winter
night, the ink carrying the cloth at the seams and the mended places.

Nobody else is in the picture and no figure stands in the light. No
blood and no mark on either child, no weapon anywhere, and no rope
except the one at her wrists.

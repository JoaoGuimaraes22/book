Same medium and style as the reference image: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a rutted earth road coming down a hill on a bright cold
morning, as loose wash suggestion — a ditch along one side with firm
ground on its top edge, dry grass, and the land falling away below
toward open water. Soft daylight from one side.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is lower down the road, close and level,
looking back up it. KAEL comes down the firm top edge of the ditch
toward the camera, three-quarters on, walking, with a clear lane of
empty road in front of him. Behind him and a step lower, in the ruts,
A MAN stands with a coil of rope over his shoulder, seen from behind
and a little to the side so that no features are drawn on him: he has
taken his hat off and holds it in both hands in front of him, and his
head is turned to watch the boy go by. Further back, a stopped cart
with a whitewashed bed sheet roped along its rail, seen at an angle,
no word on it legible. On both sides of the road, people standing
still and turned toward the boy, drawn as summary shapes with no
faces anywhere. The bottom edge crosses him at the thigh, so that no
feet and no road surface at his feet are in the frame.

THE ROAD HAS OPENED IN FRONT OF HIM AND CLOSED UP BEHIND HIM, and
that is the subject of the image: one moving figure with a lane of
empty ground ahead of him, and every stopped body in the picture
turned his way.

KAEL — walking on, not turning round and not slowing, his eyes
forward and down the road past the camera and on none of them. The
mouth closed, the jaw set, and a little high colour along the ears
and the cheekbones. He carries a wrapped bundle across his back on a
cord; both hands are empty and easy at his sides and there is no
weapon anywhere in the picture. Undyed coarse working wool in dull
oatmeal and dead olive, much mended, no ornament, badge, colour or
metal on him anywhere, and his left arm bound above the elbow in four
turns of plain cloth.

His face carries the sharpest step from light to dark and is the only
resolved face in the picture; the man, the cart, the sheet and the
standing people all stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled
edges.

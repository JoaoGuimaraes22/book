Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than fills.
Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the warmth belongs to
the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the one room of a small whitewashed village house in the
evening, low and warm — a plank table with a bench along the far
side, one iron pot in the middle of it, wooden bowls, a hearth off to
one side, sacks against the back wall, a shuttered window. Loose wash
suggestion, no detail anywhere.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is at the near end of the table and low,
at the height of somebody sitting at it. Nearest, across the bottom
of the frame and cut by it, the dark backs of two heads and two pairs
of shoulders — the near side of the table, unresolved, in shadow. On
the far side, in the light and facing the camera, FOUR PEOPLE of one
family: a heavy SMITH in the middle with his forearms on the table
and black on them to the elbow; a GROWN SON beside him, twenty or so,
with the same shoulders coming on him; a SMALL CHILD of about four
kneeling up on the bench to reach the table; and standing behind
them, leaning in over the table, the WOMAN of the house, putting a
second piece of bread down on the board in front of the viewer.

THEY ARE FEEDING STRANGERS AND NOTHING IN THE ROOM IS REMARKING ON
IT. Nobody at the table is looking at what anybody else has been
given. The woman is already turning back toward the hearth as her
hand sets the bread down and she is not watching it land. The smith
is eating and looking at his own hands. The son is mid-sentence about
something else. Only the child is looking straight out at the viewer,
openly and with interest, because nobody has told her not to.

It is an easy, ordinary, unhurried table, and the ease is the whole
subject: no ceremony, no charity in anybody's face, no pity and no
wariness, and not one person performing a kindness.

THE POT IS SMALL AND THE ROOM IS POOR. One pot for everybody, the
bowls plain and few, the bread dark and cut thin, the sacks along the
wall down to almost nothing. Good coarse working cloth, much mended,
clean, no ornament on anybody.

THE SECOND PIECE OF BREAD IS THE CENTRE OF THE PICTURE. The woman's
hand and the bread under it carry the sharpest step from light to
dark in the frame and the most finished ink drawing in it; the four
faces come next; the near shoulders, the walls and the sacks stay at
quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges and nothing resolved in
them.

Lighting: warm firelight from the hearth to one side, low and even,
and one small lamp on the table. The far side of the table is lit and
the near side is in shadow.

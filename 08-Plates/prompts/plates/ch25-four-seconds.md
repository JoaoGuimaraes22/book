Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house at night, as loose wash
suggestion — a plank wall behind them, the corners of the room going
into deep dark. One small oil lamp stands at the back of the table and
is the only light in the picture; it is warm and low and leaves the
walls and the far side of the room black.

Composition: waist-up, three people at a plank table, the tabletop
crossing the lower third of the frame with its surface in view. The
boy sits at the left in three-quarter view, turned toward the woman.
The woman sits at the right, side-on to the table with her weight back
in the chair and her left forearm laid along the top of it, her body
turned out of the table rather than in to it. The man sits on the far
side between them, facing the boy, square and settled back in a chair
with both shoulders down. On the boards: a small round clay pot with a
handle and a pouring lip, and three clay cups with pale green leaves
showing in them. Nothing else is on the table and nobody is holding
anything.

THE BOY IS LAUGHING AND THE WHOLE FACE IS DOING IT: the mouth open,
the head gone back a little, the cheeks lifted, the eyes creased
nearly shut, the shoulders loose. It is one short laugh caught in the
middle of itself, out of the middle of him. Not a grin, not a smirk,
not polite.

THE WOMAN HAS JUST SAID THE THING HE IS LAUGHING AT AND HER FACE HAS
NOT MOVED WITH IT: the mouth closed and perfectly level, the brows
unmoved, the delivery flat. The one thing working in her face is that
her eyes are full on her son and she has not looked away from him. Not
smiling, not warm, not fond, not amused on the surface.

THE MAN HAS BREATHED OUT ONCE THROUGH HIS NOSE AND THAT IS ALL: the
nostrils widened a fraction, the upper lip lifted very slightly with
it, the mouth closed, the brows level, the eyes steady on the boy. It
is the whole of his reaction and nothing else in the face has changed.
Not smiling, not stern, not sad, and not blank or empty.

HER RIGHT HAND LIES FLAT ON TOP OF THE PREGNANCY, high and in front,
and is simply resting there. She is nine months pregnant and at full
term, carrying high and out in front, which is why she sits side-on
and cannot reach the table squarely. Her left forearm is along the
tabletop. The boy's two hands are on the boards on either side of his
cup. The man's two hands rest together beside his own cup. Nothing is
held in any hand in the picture.

WHERE EVERY FACE IS LOOKING: the boy is looking at the woman, and both
the woman and the man are looking at the boy. Nobody is looking at the
viewer and no face is turned toward the viewer.

All three wear plain undyed village working wool, coarse and much
mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no ornament and no metal
on any of them; the man's collar is closed at the throat, the woman
wears an overdress over a plain undershirt, the boy's collar is open.
The boy's left arm is bound above the elbow in four turns of plain
cloth, stained dark brown where it has dried, and below the binding an
older healed mark runs up out of his cuff along the same forearm, pale
against the skin. The binding belongs only to the boy and neither
adult has anything bound or marked on them.

The three faces are the most finished things in the picture and carry
the sharpest steps from light to dark. The table, the pot, the cups,
the wall and the dark of the room stay at warm quiet middle values
with soft edges and no sharp detail.

Nobody else is in the room and nothing else is happening anywhere in
the picture.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. The boy's
eyes are pale near-white grey; both adults' eyes are dark.

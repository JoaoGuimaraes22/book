Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the doorway of a small stone-and-turf mountain house before
sunrise, as loose wash suggestion. She stands in the open doorway and
he stands outside it on a stone step; the room behind her is a dark
rectangle going into deep shadow, and behind him a bare mountain
slope rises in pale cool grey wash and is going light. Cold flat grey
pre-dawn light, no sun anywhere and no shadow with a hard edge.
Nothing warm in the picture except the skin.

Composition: two figures close and near the viewer, seen from a
little to one side, their faces at nearly the same height and turned
toward each other. The bottom edge of the picture crosses both of
them just below the ribs, so that her forearms are in frame to the
elbow, the front of her body under the shawl is in frame, and no
legs and no feet are in the picture at all.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND, AND THE HANDS ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE:
Her right hand is laid flat along one side of his face, the palm on
the cheekbone and the fingers running back toward the ear.
Her left hand is laid the same way on the other side of his face.
His right hand hangs at his side and is empty.
His left hand hangs at his side and is empty.
Four hands, nothing held in any of them, and her two hands are the
only place the two of them are touching. Her hands are not on the
doorframe and are not on his shoulders.

THEY HAVE BEEN LIKE THIS LONGER THAN IT TAKES: her hands are still
and settled rather than just arrived, the fingers relaxed against the
skin and not gripping, not pulling his head anywhere, not turning it.

HER FACE IS STILL AND SAYS NOTHING, AND THAT IS CORRECT. The mouth
closed and level, the brows unmoved, nothing composed and nothing
released. The one thing working in her face is the eyes: they are
going over his face from one part of it to another and they do not
leave it. Not smiling, not tender, not grieving, not tearful, not
afraid.

HE IS BEING HANDLED AND HE IS HOLDING PERFECTLY STILL FOR IT: the
head steady in her hands, the mouth closed, the jaw easy, the eyes up
and steady on hers. He is not pulling away and he is not leaning in.
Not embarrassed, not sad, not smiling, and not blank or empty.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER, and neither of them is
looking at the viewer.

THE FACES ARE DEMOTED SO THE HANDS CAN WIN. Both faces are held at
quiet middle values, softly drawn, the features understated, with no
hard edges and no strong dark anywhere in them. THE SHARPEST STEP
FROM LIGHT TO DARK IN THE WHOLE PICTURE HAPPENS AT HER HANDS — the
knuckles and the outer edge of the fingers where they cross his
cheek, drawn pale and hard and the most precisely finished thing in
the image. Nothing else in the picture competes with her hands for
finish, and the doorway, the wall, the slope and the sky stay as open
wash with soft edges.

She is nine months pregnant and at full term, carrying high and in
front, and she is leaning out of the doorway with her weight carried
forward onto the step. A coarse wool shawl is round her shoulders and
has slipped a little; under it, plain village working cloth, an
overdress over a plain undershirt, in dull grey-brown and undyed
oatmeal. Her long dark brown hair is loose and moving a little in a
cold wind. No ornament of any kind on her.

He wears undyed coarse working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and
dead olive, with no ornament and no metal anywhere on him. His left
arm is bound above the elbow in four clean turns of plain cloth,
freshly tightened, stained dark brown lower down where it has dried.

They are not embracing and their bodies are not touching. Nobody is
weeping. Nobody else is anywhere in the picture and no other person,
figure, hand or face appears in the frame.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. Her eyes
are dark; his are pale near-white grey.

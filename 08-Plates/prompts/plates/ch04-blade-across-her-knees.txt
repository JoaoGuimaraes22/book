Image 1: portrait reference of the woman — hair and colouring only.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the single room of a small mountain house at night, unlit.
One small window in a thick stone wall, open to a night sea far
below — the sea a faint pale sheet, the only light in the picture.
The room around her is deep cool wash going to black.

Composition: the woman seated on a low stool at the window, seen
from BEHIND AND SLIGHTLY ABOVE, from the height of a loft ladder.
She reads as a dark upright shape against the window. Her back is
straight, her face turned to the sea. No feature of her face is
drawn.

ACROSS HER KNEES, HORIZONTAL, LIES ONE UNSHEATHED BLADE — long,
thin, plain, old, with no ornament and no guard-work, older-looking
than any tool in the room. It lies flat across both thighs, not
held up, not pointed at anything. Nothing else is in the picture
with her: no scabbard, no cloth, no lamp, no second blade.

BOTH HER HANDS REST ON THE BLADE, palms down, fingers relaxed and
spread — the hands of someone resting them on dough, not gripping a
weapon. The hands and the blade are the subject of the image.

THE BLADE AND HER HANDS ARE THE ONLY THINGS FINISHED. They catch
the window light along their length and carry the sharpest step
from light to dark in the picture. The room, the stool, her hair
and her clothing are held dark and loose, no hard edges, nothing
competing for finish.

Long dark brown hair falling loose past the shoulders. Plain coarse
village working cloth, much mended, THE INK CARRYING THE CLOTH at
the seams and the mended places.

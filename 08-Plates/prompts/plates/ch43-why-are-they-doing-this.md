Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the yard of a village smith on a cold bright afternoon — a
low dry-stone wall, a shed at one side with a dark doorway, a
woodpile, hard swept ground. Beyond a gap in the wall, a track going
away down into open country. Loose wash suggestion, no detail
anywhere. The sun has come round the corner of the shed and is
lying along the top of the wall.

Composition: 3:4. Two figures, waist-up and a little more, well
apart. KAEL sits on the low wall on one side of the frame, in the
sun, turned three-quarters toward the camera. VALERIA stands on the
other side at the gap in the wall, in profile, facing out down the
track and away from him. There is a clear width of empty sunlit wall
between them and neither is looking at the other. Nobody else is in
the picture.

NEITHER OF THEM IS DOING ANYTHING AND A QUESTION HAS JUST BEEN ASKED.
Not an argument, not a comfort, not a conversation being had face to
face: two people sitting and standing in the same sun with a wall
between them, one waiting and one out of answers.

KAEL has his left arm up resting on his raised knee, bound from the
knuckles to above the elbow in strips of clean linen with the thumb
splinted between two flat pieces of wood. The linen is the whitest
thing in the picture. His face is turned down toward the arm, thin
and hollowed out, the mouth closed, no colour in him yet. Undyed
coarse working wool, much mended. A plain closed ring of dark metal
on his right wrist with a short run of links hanging off it.

VALERIA stands square and easy with her weight down, hands loose in
front of her, a plain closed ring on each wrist and no more chain
between them than the width of her own hand. Her face is in profile
and doing nothing at all, her eyes out on the track. Long black hair,
dark clothing, the left side of her face smooth and shining where it
healed.

Flat cold winter daylight. The bound arm and the sunlit top of the
wall carry the light; the shed, the doorway and the country past the
gap stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges.

Do not blend their features and do not mix their clothing.

Preserve each face, hair and colouring exactly as shown in the
reference images. His eyes pale grey, hers green.

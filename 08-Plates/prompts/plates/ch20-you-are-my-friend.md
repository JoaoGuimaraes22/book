Image 1: elarine-14.png — portrait reference of the small girl with bound hair.
Image 2: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: a disused cistern court very early on a winter morning, as
loose wash suggestion — old brick walls close behind them, cold
grey-brown, wet from rain that stopped in the night, moss in the
joints, the corners going into shade. Flat cold daylight from the
open side, no sun.

Composition: waist-up, two figures on a plank bench against the wall,
close together and near the viewer. The girl sits at the right and
has turned round on the bench to face the boy squarely, her satchel
set down on the bench beside her, her hands loose in her lap. The boy
sits at the left, facing along the bench, his hands on his knees. The
bottom edge crosses them both at the lap. They are twelve years old.
Nobody else is in the court.

THE GIRL HAS JUST SAID SOMETHING SHE DECIDED IN ADVANCE TO SAY AND IS
WATCHING WHAT HE DOES WITH IT. Her chin is up a fraction, the mouth
closed again after the sentence, the brows level, the eyes steady on
his face and giving nothing back. Not shy, not pleading, not smiling,
and no colour anywhere in her cheeks. Small and neat and finely
built; ink-dark hair bound severe with nothing loose; plain
close-fitting dark clothing of very good quality, a high buttoned
collar, ink-black and dark olive, no ornament of any kind.

THE BOY HAS BEEN HANDED SOMETHING HE CANNOT PRICE AND NOTHING IS
ARRIVING IN HIS FACE. The eyes on hers, the mouth closed, the brows
unmoved — attentive and entirely still. Not moved, not pleased, not
embarrassed, not blank. Undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dull
oatmeal and dead olive, no ornament and no metal.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER and neither is looking at
the viewer. This eye contact is the subject of the image.

Their faces are the most finished things in the picture; the brick,
the bench and the satchel stay at quiet middle values with soft
edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. Do not mix their
clothing: the fine dark cloth and the high collar belong only to the
girl, the mended undyed wool only to the boy. Her eyes very dark
brown, his pale grey.

Image 2: portrait reference of the woman.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the single room of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at
night, as loose wash suggestion — a scrubbed plank table across the
foreground, a wall of hand tools on pegs behind, everything past the
two figures falling into deep cool shadow.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS ONE SMALL OIL LAMP STANDING ON THE TABLE BETWEEN
THEM, close to both faces. It lights them from below and to one side,
catching the undersides of the jaws, the cheekbones and the hands.

THE WARM LIGHT STOPS WHERE IT STOPS, AND EVERYTHING BEYOND IT IS
COOL. The room, the roof, the tool wall and every shadow are cool
grey and grey-blue, with NO BROWN AND NO
WARM COLOUR ANYWHERE OUTSIDE THE LAMP'S REACH. The picture is warm in
one small place and cold everywhere else.

The lamp flame and the cut face of the loaf are reserved as bare
unpainted paper — the two lightest things in the picture and the only
places the paper is left bare.

Composition: the two of them seated at the far side of the table,
close, cropped at the chest, seen straight on from across the table
at a seated person's height. There is nobody else in the frame and
no third place set.

THE MAN'S OWN HAND COVERS THE LOWER HALF OF HIS OWN FACE — the heel
of the hand under the chin, the thumb along one side of the jaw and
the bent knuckle of the forefinger across the mouth, so that the
mouth is completely hidden. The posture is that of a man rubbing his
jaw at the end of a day. ABOVE THE HAND HIS EYES HAVE GONE BRIGHT AND THE SKIN
AT THEIR OUTER CORNERS IS CREASED INTO DEEP FOLDS. He is looking down
and slightly away, not at the viewer and not at the woman. He is
laughing, silently, and hiding it. Not grinning, not grimacing, not
weeping — the whole of it is in the eyes and the hand.

THE WOMAN IS NOT LOOKING AT ANYTHING BUT THE BREAD. Her head is
lowered over a round loaf on a board, both hands working — one flat
on the loaf, the other drawing a knife through it — and her eyes stay
on the cut. Her mouth is pressed flat and held, the lips pushed
together, the corners tight. She is holding the same thing in and it
is coming out at the mouth instead. Not smiling, not frowning.

Neither of them is looking at the other, and neither is looking at
the viewer.

Their two faces are the most finished things in the picture and carry
the sharpest step from light to dark. The table, the tools, the room
and the clothing are held at quiet dark values with soft wash edges,
but the ink linework stays the strongest element in all of them and
does not blur away.

THE MAN: broad across the top of the back and nothing wasted below
it. Iron-dark hair to the jaw. A face weathered far past its years,
deep-lined. Coarse undyed working cloth, closed at the throat.
THE WOMAN: long dark hair loose past the shoulders, fine bones under
worked hands. Plain coarse mended village cloth.
The ink carries the cloth on both, at the seams and the mended
places. Neither wears any ornament or metal.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: portrait reference of the man.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: the single room of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at
night, as loose wash suggestion — a heavy plank table, a wall of hand
tools on pegs behind, a barred plank door, everything beyond the two
figures falling into deep cool shadow.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS A SMALL CONTAINED FIRE IN A STONE-LINED HOLE IN THE
FLOOR BETWEEN THEM, banked low, no flames reaching. It lights both
faces from underneath — the undersides of the jaws, the cheekbones,
the brows, the hands — and leaves the roof and the corners dark.

Composition: two figures sitting on the floor on either side of the
fire hole, cropped at the waist, close, turned in toward each other
and toward the fire.

BOTH OF THEM ARE LOOKING AT THE SAME THING AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF
THE IMAGE: one wooden sword, plain and unfinished, no guard and no
ornament, about the length of the boy's arm, held out level on two of the
man's fingers at its balance point — resting across the
fingers, not gripped, not raised, not pointed at anything.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: the man's right hand holds the wooden sword
level on two fingers. His left rests on his knee, holding nothing. A
drawknife and a few pale curls of shaving lie on the floor beside
him. The boy's hands are open and empty in his lap. There is no
metal blade anywhere in the picture and no second wooden sword.

The man watches the sword and nothing else, judging where it wants to
tip. Level, mouth closed, brows unmoved, entirely unreadable — NOT
grim, NOT sad, NOT proud, NOT tender, NOT troubled. Broad across the
top of the back and nothing wasted below it. Iron-dark hair to the
jaw. A face weathered far past its years.

The boy watches the sword too, absorbed, sitting easily, still
healing — a healed line closed and pale across his left forearm, no
other mark on him. Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and
colourless, catching the firelight without taking its colour. Dark
hair cut short and uneven.

Nothing is happening and neither of them is about to speak. Both
mouths are closed. They are not looking at each other.

The fire is the brightest thing in the picture and is loose and
soft-edged with no detail in it. The two lit faces and the wooden
sword are the most finished things and carry the sharpest step from
light to dark. The room, the tools, the table and both sets of
clothing are held dark and loose with no hard edges.

Coarse undyed mended wool on both, the man's heavy and sized to him
and closed at the throat, the boy's thin and too large. The ink
carries the cloth at the seams and the mended places.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: just inside the gate of a great arena, as loose wash
suggestion — heavy new timber close behind her, pale raked sand
underfoot, and the dark of the gateway falling away beyond. Flat
daylight, no sun and no hard-edged shadow.

Composition: two figures, close and near the viewer. The girl sits on
the sand with her back against the timber, in frame from the knees up.
The boy stands in front of her and is cut by the top and bottom edges,
in frame from the hip to the top of the head, his body angled toward
her. The viewer is low, at the height of her eyes: the underside of
his jaw is visible and she is not looked down on. Their two faces are
near each other in the upper part of the frame.

HER TWO HANDS LIE IN HER LAP WITH THE PALMS TURNED UP, open and empty
and holding nothing, the fingers loose and half curled. They are the
quietest thing in the picture and they are fully in frame.

A plain sword lies flat on the sand beside her at her hip, well inside
the frame, and she is not looking at it. A plain clay cup stands on
the sand near it.

HER FACE IS FULLY ENGAGED AND GIVES NOTHING BACK: the lids fully
raised and the gaze unbroken on him, the brows unmoved, the mouth
closed and soft. Everything in it is working and nothing in it has
been decided. Not cold, not bitter, not sad, not admiring.

HE IS STANDING OVER SOMEBODY HE HAS JUST TAKEN APART AND THERE IS NO
VICTORY ANYWHERE IN HIM: the head lowered a little toward her, the
eyes down and steady on hers, the mouth closed and unpressed, the
shoulders down. Not proud, not ashamed, not braced.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER, her eyes up and his down,
and neither of them is looking at the viewer.

She is small and finely built, small for her age, with a narrow frame
and a small face. Her hair is ink-dark, a flat neutral black, and it
is BOUND: gathered close against the head and held in a tight severe
knot at the back, with two or three loose strands come down at one
temple and nothing else out of it. The mass of her hair is up and
stays up; none of it falls loose past her shoulders.

Her clothing is finely made and still reads as finely made — plain
dark cloth of very good quality, close-cut and buttoned high to the
throat, with no ornament of any kind. It has taken a few clean
straight cuts in the weave, one at the thigh and one at a forearm,
each with a dark stain around it and the cloth otherwise whole and
holding its shape. Not ragged, not tattered, not frayed, not a
beggar's clothing and nothing about her destitute. The skin under it
is whole and there is no wound anywhere in the picture. She sits
upright with her legs drawn in and folded neatly to one side. Grey
soot and pale sand dust on both of them.

His hair is dark and straight, hacked short and uneven. He wears
undyed coarse working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead
olive, the left sleeve gone entirely at the shoulder. His bare left
forearm from wrist to elbow is new pink skin, smooth and unmarked and
a shade lighter than the weathered skin above it, unbandaged and
uncovered.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. Her eyes
are very dark brown; his are pale near-white grey.

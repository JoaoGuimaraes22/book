Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his hair and colouring
from it only. His face is not in this picture.

Setting: a stone water trough outside a small mountain house, late
morning, as loose wash suggestion — the house wall behind, thin
grass and bare rock, everything past the figure falling into pale
cool wash. Flat bright overcast daylight, no sun, no cast shadows.

THE VIEWER IS LOW — at the eye height of a child of eight, looking
slightly UP at a grown man standing at the trough. The man's back
fills most of the frame. This low viewpoint is deliberate and it is
what tells you who is watching.

Composition: the man from BEHIND, stripped to the waist, bent
slightly forward over the trough with both hands in the water. NO
FACE AND NO HEAD FEATURES ARE VISIBLE — the back of his head and
his wet dark hair are all that is drawn of it. He is not turning
and not looking back.

THE BACK IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS BROAD THROUGH THE SHOULDERS AND
NO FURTHER — heavy across the top of the back and the neck, the
muscle an economy rather than a bulk, nothing wasted below it, the
waist narrow. A working body, not a built one.

THE MARKS, and they are the reason for the picture. Three kinds,
all long healed, all pale against weathered skin:
— ONE LONG STRAIGHT SCAR under the LEFT shoulder blade, running a
  hand's length, clean-edged and paler than the skin around it.
— ONE ROUND OLD SCAR high on the back, about the size of a coin.
— ON BOTH FOREARMS, A ROW OF SHORT PALE MARKS: many of them, ALL
  THE SAME LENGTH AND ALL AT THE SAME ANGLE, evenly spaced along
  the forearm like tally marks cut in order. The regularity is the
  point — they must not look random or accidental.
All of them are old, closed, flat and quiet. There is no wound
anywhere in this picture, nothing fresh, nothing red and no blood.

THE MARKS WIN THE PICTURE AND THE BODY IS DEMOTED SO THEY CAN. The
skin, the hair, the trough and the wall are held at quiet middle
values, loosely washed, with no hard edges anywhere in them. THE
SCARS ARE THE LIGHTEST VALUES IN THE FRAME and the most precisely
drawn things in it — nothing else competes with them for finish.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: both hands are down in the water of the
trough, open and empty, washing. Nothing is held in either hand.
There is no cloth, no soap, no bucket, no tool anywhere in frame.

He is doing an ordinary chore and nothing about him is braced or
displayed. Not posed, not flexing, not turned to show anything.

Light: flat bright overcast from above. No warm source. The only
warm value is skin.

His shirt lies on the edge of the trough — coarse undyed mended
wool, THE INK CARRYING THE CLOTH in dense scratchy linework at the
seams and the mended places, ink dominant over wash.

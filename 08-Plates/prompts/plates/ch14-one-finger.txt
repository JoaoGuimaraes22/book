Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Setting: an open flagstone training yard on a bright spring afternoon,
as loose wash suggestion — pale flagstones running away behind them and
a stone colonnade going into quiet wash. Flat open daylight, no shadows with hard edges, and it is
the only light in the picture.

Composition: the viewer is down at the height of the flagstones, close
beside the boy's head, so the following are in frame and nothing else
is: the boy lies on his back on the stones in the near foreground with
his head and shoulders filling the lower half of the picture, seen
from slightly above and to one side; the girl stands over him beyond
his head, seen from below, with the underside of her jaw and chin
visible and her near arm reaching down the frame toward him. The
flagstones fill the bottom of the frame and run away behind his head.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her right hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword with a blunt rounded tip and nothing else, held out and
down so that the tip stops beside the boy's cheek without touching him
or anything else. Her left hand is out of frame. Both of the boy's
hands lie open and empty on the stones, and his own wooden sword lies
on the flagstones well out of his reach at the edge of the frame.

THE WOODEN TIP IS STOPPED SHORT OF HIS FACE. There is a clear open
gap of air between the tip and his cheek, one finger wide, and the arm holding it
is completely still — the wrist level, the elbow easy, the shoulder
unmoved. Nothing in the picture is in motion.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. His eyes up, hers down,
the lines of sight meeting past the wooden point. This eye contact is
the subject of the image. Neither of them is looking at the viewer.

THE BOY is beaten and his mind is still running: on his back,
breathing, the mouth closed, the jaw set, the eyes open and level and
working. NOT frightened, NOT pleading, NOT dazed, and not blank. One
thin red mark along the line of his jaw, and nothing else on him.

THE GIRL has decided nothing: standing easy, breathing as if she were
standing still, the mouth closed, the brows unmoved, the whole
attention aimed down at him. NOT angry, NOT triumphant, NOT gloating,
and NOT bored.

BOTH ARE TWELVE: on each of them the eyes sit a little below the
vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still below it, and the jaw has not come in.

HIS eyes are near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the
lightest value in his face; his hair is dark, cut short and uneven at
home; he wears plain undyed working wool, coarse and much mended, the
ink carrying the cloth at the seams and the mended places.

HER eyes are a clear hard green, deep and cold; her black hair is well
past the shoulders, loose, without ornament, hanging forward as she
looks down; she wears plain grey cloth, close, exactly made and
austere, with no badge and no decoration on it. The grey belongs only
to her and the undyed mended wool only to him.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: their skin is the
only warm colour anywhere, and the flagstones, the wooden swords, the
colonnade, the watching children and all the clothing are cool grey
and grey-blue with no brown in them.

THE TWO FACES AND THE WOODEN POINT BETWEEN THEM ARE THE MOST FINISHED
THINGS IN THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark.
The lines of children standing round the yard are dark summary shapes
with no features at the edges of the frame, and the colonnade and the far side of the yard stay
at quiet middle values with soft edges and no detail. The lit faces of
the flagstones are reserved as bare unpainted paper. The ink linework
stays the strongest element throughout and does not blur into the
wash.

Preserve both children exactly as shown in their reference images —
faces, hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features and
do not mix their clothing.

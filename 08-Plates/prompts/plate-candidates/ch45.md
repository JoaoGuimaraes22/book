# Candidates CH45 — The Ground

*Written at s68, at design time. **The morning Tarim is missed, the day of the ground, and the middle of the next day** — Kael thirteen, the princess a year above him, Valeria of his age; the fourteen-year-old references are the anchor. Neris, Wren, Severin, Aurelian, Elarine and Hazoun Sabrane are on no page in this chapter and in no picture from it. Damir, Miresh, Tarim and Neshah have no references and no `03` sheets and are framed rather than resolved.*

**The chapter is drafted and not yet revised.** Every chapter quote below is pinned to draft text; if a line moves, the quote on that entry has to move with it, because `scripts/build-docx.py` finds it in the manuscript to place the picture.

**Two things run through the list.** The Art comes back into the open in this chapter — a thread on a blade in the dawn and three holes taken out of the ground — and the folder has drawn the Art exactly once before, as white lightning all over a man (`ch30/the-white-in-all-of-him`). **The page here is precise about what it looks like**: hairline, straight, even, no flicker, no spread, no sound, and it lights nothing. Only one of the two Art moments is taken, because two in one chapter would make the chapter about the Art, and it is about a village. And **the fight itself is not in this chapter** — it ends on two horses walking past a kneeling man — so nothing on this list is a fight.

## CHOSEN

- **The thread.** The grey before sunrise in the fold: a plain soldier's sword off a dead man, held one-handed by a boy with the other arm in linen, and one hairline of white lying along it from guard to point, absolutely steady, throwing no light on anything. Opposite him a young man who has gone back a step and did not decide to. **The Art shown to an outsider for the first time in the book**, and the moment Tarim stops being able to hold his own arithmetic. **The block spends its longest paragraph on what the line does not do** — no glow, no haze, no halo, no spark, no spread, and nothing in the picture lit by it — because that is the whole difference between this and `ch30`. 3:4. Ch. 45 canon: *It ran the length of the blade and stayed there, white, thin, absolutely steady, from the guard to the point.* → `../plates/ch45-the-thread.md`
- **Covering the holes.** Dusk on the flat by the water: the princess and the smith kneeling on opposite sides of the same hole, laying branch-litter across sticks one piece over another the way a roof is tiled, and a stick laid across the finished part to test it. Sleeves back, hands filthy to the wrist, hair knotted out of the way, wrists bare where the iron was. **Nobody gave her this job.** It is the first work of her life, it is the pay-off of the Ivrit she started asking for in Ch. 43, and the two of them do most of it without a word. 3:4. Ch. 45 canon: *laying the pieces one over another the way you tile a roof.* → `../plates/ch45-covering-the-holes.md`
- **Something to his boot.** The middle of the next day, from the bottom of the narrow looking back up it: Tarim down on one knee at the split rock with both hands at his own boot and his head down, and the first two horsemen walking past him unhurried, one turned to say something to the other, with the rest strung out down the narrow behind them. **The chapter's last image and the whole of Tarim in one gesture** — he is doing exactly what he was told, slowly, and not looking up. **The ground is drawn empty**: nothing disturbed, nothing dug, nothing scattered, which is what makes the picture unbearable to a reader who has watched it be made. **No reference attached**, so the block takes the STYLE Medium paragraph. 3:4. Ch. 45 canon: *He came down to the split rock and stopped, got down on one knee, and started doing something to his boot.* → `../plates/ch45-something-to-his-boot.md`

## CANDIDATES

- **She takes the ground out.** Valeria on both knees with both hands flat on the earth, and the grass going all at once over an area the size of a door, and the ground going down and sideways at the same time and packing itself into the sides of the hole it is making, with no spoil anywhere. And *Kael did not watch the ground. He watched her.* **The best Art image the book has and the first ever of Valeria working it.** Held back only because `The thread` has the slot and two Art plates would make this chapter about the Art instead of about Doresh. **First in line for a fourth.**
- **A hundred and eighty-eight.** Every caltrop counted out onto a cloth in the yard in three lots, a boy with one arm doing the counting, the forge going all day behind him and Miresh carrying boards to the trough. An object study in `ch40/the-holding`'s register, with a number in it that the reader will meet again.
- **Drawing it in the dust.** Kael crouched badly at the side of the track with his right forefinger, the two lines and the gap between them and a mark at the bottom, and Tarim looking at the mark. Two figures and a diagram in dirt; quiet, and it explains the whole chapter to anybody who looks at it twice.
- **Doresh stands by children.** Damir turning round to look down the length of his own village at the smoke going up off forty roofs into a sky with nothing in it, doing the sum, and finding there is only one term in it that can still be moved. One old face and a village, and no reference for the face.
- **The eye.** The princess four hundred strides up the rise, flat under a needle-tree in the litter with a grey cloak over her, feet first, the whole hundred strides and the open ground above it laid out below her. **The most important job on that ground.** Passed over because the point of her is that she cannot be seen, and a picture that succeeds at that is a picture of a hillside.
- **Both of those are my son.** Damir at the anvil, not stopping, saying the truest thing anybody says in this chapter to a boy standing in his forge door with an arm across his chest. The chapter's best line and it is two men not looking at each other in a dark forge, which `ch44/two-fingers` has already had at the same anvil.

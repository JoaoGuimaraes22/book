  - **RUN RECORD (first run, s65, on the debloated block; 1086×1448, exactly 3:4).** The burning canvas hangs in a heavy diagonal from her hands down to his and is already across the side, with the fire eating along its edge — the one moment in Book One where those two work as a pair, and it is the picture. Her twin blades are in their scabbards at her hip; his good hand is shut on the bottom corner and the other is held in against his body; his dark-gold hair carries.
  - **AND IT ANSWERS THE §Limits COST IN A WAY NO PREVIOUS RUN HAS.** Nine warm-source runs and this is the first that does not go uniformly dense: **the burning sail is itself a large pale mass in a black picture, so the light passage the register wants comes back as an object rather than as bare paper.** The finding is not that the cost was avoidable by wording — it was not — but that a night picture recovers the register when the frame contains something big and pale for the fire to be on.
  - **THE BURN IS MISSING, AND IT IS A PAGE MISS RATHER THAN A DRIFT.** The block put the LEFT side of her face toward the camera and said it *has come up red and shining from the eyebrow down to the jaw, a smooth shine on the skin*, and the face came back clean. Ch. 33 gives it to her — *the whole side of her face that had gone past the frame first had come up red and shining* — so at Ch. 35 it is on her and this plate does not carry it. **One re-run would fix it and the author's call is to leave it or take it**; the folder's precedent is that a recorded departure stands (`ch26/aftermath`'s three, `ch24/turn-him-toward-the-lamp`'s early burn).
  - **DRIFTED, four, none owed.** His eyes read pale rather than amber gold-brown, with the fire warm on the skin around them where the block asked for it kept cool — the §SUBJECT warning that a warm face eats amber, now confirmed on a plate and not only on a portrait · his clothing reads near-black rather than deep umber · both his sleeves are whole where the block said one wrist was bare · and the crop went wider than the ribs, the sixth widening in six.

- **The Temple from the road** *(s61 — the revisiting session's extra plate, chosen from Ch. 1–10 with the pick handed back: author, s61 «you choose, do choose from ch 1 to ch 10»).* The last shoulder of the coast, and the thing that has been a pale shape his whole life turning out to be full. **The set's one structural gap:** a third of Book One happens inside those walls and the folder has no picture of them from outside — `ch08` is a room, `ch09` a yard, `ch16` a court, `ch23` the arena sand. **Both of this chapter's other plates are mountain-register**, so this is its other half, and it is the same morning as `ch07/kael-of-what` an hour later, with the same cloth and the same bag on the same shoulder. Built on `ch18/the-man-at-the-second-bend`'s method — viewer placed first, the frame stacked bottom to top, the scale carried by a ratio and the age by one clause under the fourteen-year-old anchor. **No face is resolved anywhere in it**, his or the court's, which is what the page says of him: *None of it showed.* **The Nouns risk is the whole of the block** — *temple* returns columns and a pediment, so the place is written by construction throughout and the default killed once by name.   - **RUN RECORD (first run, s61; the block ran as the file holds it, at 4:3, 1448×1086).** **The Nouns kill worked and it is the whole reason the picture exists:** walls out of the headland in the headland's own stone, curtain walls following the rock, a gate in the near side, an open court, low shallow-tiled roofs and one square tower over all of it — and not one column, pediment or dome anywhere. **And it came back full**: dozens of small figures crossing the court in twos and threes, marks and not portraits, two more under the gate. Thin smoke straight off the kitchen roofs, plain undyed banners lifting on the wall-tops, the sea flat and pale beyond, the road running down and away to the right, no other figure on it, and no feature of the boy's face anywhere. **The named ratio held for the second time** under the s60 amendment.
  - **THE BOY CAME FORWARD OUT OF THE STACK, AND THIS IS THE SECOND INSTANCE.** The block put him at about a quarter of the picture's height and he is nearer two-thirds — a foreground figure rather than a small one. `ch39/the-door-at-dusk` did the same with the girl, from the same kind of clause, and was ruled better as drawn; so is this, and the picture is now *a boy looking at it* rather than *a place with a boy in it*. **Same fault, same cause, twice — recorded, not promoted** (s42): the correction was wanted neither time.
  - **AND IT INVERTED THE WINNER AND THE RIVAL, WHICH FOLLOWS FROM IT.** The block sited the sharpest step at the edge of the lit tower face; the darkest and sharpest thing in the frame is the boy's head against the pale sea. At two-thirds of the frame he cannot be held at quiet middle values, so the two clauses were in conflict the moment the scale drifted.
  - **SHORT OF COLOUR, AND THE BLOCK IS WHY** (author, s61: *somewhat lacking in colour, but its good enough*). **Adopted as drawn.** §Colour says to name the hues on the objects that have them, and this block named no saturated hue on anything: pale stone, dull undyed cloth, dull oatmeal and dead olive, cool shade, and a sea and sky demoted to flat and pale. **A wide picture with no face in it has no skin to carry warmth**, so where a plate's own objects are all muted the register's earthy default and a demoted sky leave nothing to hold colour at all — and the same session's `ch01/the-room-of-true-things` came back one uniform warm brown from the other direction. Neither is a correction owed; what a next wide exterior should carry is one object given a hue with saturation in it.
  - **DRIFTED, two more, one of them better than written.** **The hand on the strap did not land** — both hands hang, and that was the page's one gesture, *his hand had come up to the strap of the bag, near where the knife is not*; it is the only thing in the block that came back absent rather than changed. And **the court came back lit** where the block put everything inside the walls in cool shade: better as drawn, since in shade you cannot see that it is full, and full is the subject.

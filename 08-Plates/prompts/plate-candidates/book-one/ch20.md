  - **RUN RECORD (first run, s63; the block ran as the file holds it after the 3:4 re-cut, at 1086×1448).** The stack came back in the order it was written and it is the best depth in the session: the boy nearest and lowest, the packed earth road running away downhill above him, the man small and higher in the frame with no features on his face, and the sheds, the boats on their trestles and the flat grey water at the top. The dawn is exactly as sited — the road and the sheds in cold blue shade, the sun just up behind the water carrying the only warm light in the picture — and the woodsmoke lies low along the roofs.
  - **THE CUT HELD A SECOND TIME, WHICH IS TWO FOR TWO ON THE CORRECTION.** *The bottom edge of the picture crosses him just below the shoulder, so that no arms, no hands and no legs of his are in the frame at all* — and it does, against five straight failures earlier the same day where only the landmark was named.
  - **AND THE WINNER-AND-RIVAL INSTRUMENT WORKED A THIRD TIME.** His face is by far the most finished thing in the picture and the sharpest step from light to dark sits inside the eye at the edge of the iris, with the man, the sheds and the boats held at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges. Three for three this session, on a spark, a coin of flame and a face.
  - **BUT THE 3:4 RE-CUT COST THE MAN HIS EVIDENCE, AND THAT IS THE HONEST ACCOUNTING.** The block's argument was the pricing — *the wear on the outside of the right sleeve, the way the shoulder came round before the feet did* — and at twenty strides, small and high in a tall frame, none of it carries: he is a working man's shape with a load in one hand, and the worn sleeve is below the scale at which anything renders (`../../prompt-system.md` §The winner and the rival). The iron hoop landed, leaning by a hull. **So the plate is now a picture of the boy on that road at dawn with the cooper as context, rather than the two-term picture the entry describes.** It is a better page and a better picture and it is not the same argument; the page-size gain was paid for here.
  - **AND THE GAZE WENT TO THE VIEWER BECAUSE THE BLOCK'S GEOMETRY COULD NOT BE OBEYED.** It asked for him *turned away down the slope, his head turned back* and, in the same breath, for *the eyes narrowed and entirely on the man* — and the man is down the slope, so a head turned back cannot look at him. The renderer resolved the contradiction toward the head and put the gaze on the camera. **The fault is mine and it is a geometry error, not the tool's** — `../../archive/plate-sheets.md` §ch14 and §ch17 record two earlier gazes arriving on the viewer where the block sent them elsewhere, and this is the first with a stated cause.
  - **DRIFTED, two more, neither a correction owed.** The wet on his shoulders did not arrive, as it did not on `ch18/nothing` · and the medium and palette held, which puts daylight at six for six against the lamp-lit runs' nought for three.

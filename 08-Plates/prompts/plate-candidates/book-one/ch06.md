# Candidates CH6 — The Night

**Nothing inside that room is ever drawn.** The chapter's horror is carried by what is outside the wall, and every candidate here stays outside it.

## CHOSEN

- **Carried past the rock.** The climb home: the father with the boy in his arms, the split rock standing up out of the dark (size fixed by `ch05.md`), and the mother waiting at the end of the path, straight and still, her hand arriving on Kael's head and riding there the whole way up.   - **RUN RECORD (first run, s61; the block ran as the file holds it, at 4:3, 1448×1086).** **The contrast carried the whole picture, exactly as designed:** one unlit stone house filling the near frame with no lamp anywhere on it or in it, and beyond it the shore burning end to end, every lamp doubled and broken in the black water in long orange columns. The doorway is empty — nothing on the step, nothing in the opening — and the black inside it is the darkest value in the frame. Cold stars as tiny flecks, no figure anywhere, and the lamps the only saturated colour in the picture. **The named ratio held again.**
  - **THE DOOR IS FULLY OPEN, NOT KNOCKING.** The block put a hand's width of gap between its edge and the frame *it is knocking against*; it came back swung wide. **The page's own image is the smaller motion** — *swinging a little when the sea-wind found it, knocking softly against its own frame* — so what the picture keeps is the plain fact, a door standing open on a winter night with nobody closing it, and what it loses is the sound the sentence makes. Adopted as drawn.
  - **AND THE MEDIUM RAN TONAL, WHICH IS NOW THREE UNANCHORED PLATES OUT OF FIVE.** Dense rendering, atmospheric dark, and no generous bare paper anywhere. `ch38/the-eight-lights` and `ch01/the-room-of-true-things` did the same; `ch39/the-black-in-the-iron` (held) and `ch05/the-two-pails` did not. **Still a recorded finding and not a rule** — all three tonal runs were adopted as they stood, so the correction has not once been wanted, which is the s42 test's second half.

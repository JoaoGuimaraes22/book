# Candidates CH58 — The Feast

*Written at s77, at design time. **The one night they rested**, and the whole chapter is that night: the chests under the trees, the shares written down, the fire built above the ground with the stones all round it, the songs, the old man looking across it at the woman, and then horses in the dark, a spear in the traces, four men clawing at nothing, and an old man walking through air that should have killed him. The author handed the pick back — the three below are Claude's own.*

**THE CHAPTER IS A SINGLE NIGHT, AND THAT IS THE DESIGN PROBLEM.** §Limits: a warm source that lights a picture costs the palette and the bare paper, every time, and no wording has moved it. Everything that happens here is lit by the one fire, so **one plate is taken off the chapter's only daylight** — the chests and the shares at the top of the fold, in flat light — and the other two spend the fire knowingly, one warm and close and one warm and hard.

**TWO PLATES OF BORREN ON PURPOSE.** He is the chapter twice over and they are opposite pictures: the man who never said the thing, and the man who was never what they thought. Nothing in the second is drawn that the page has not already spent — the reveal is Ch. 58's own.

**THREE FACES ARE DRAWN WITHOUT A REFERENCE AND EACH IS HANDLED BY FRAMING**, per §A face with no reference: Ambros's head is down over his own writing, Thiro is seen from behind with no features on him at all, and the crew waiting for their shares are a loose line with no faces. **Neither twin got a plate**, and the beat that wants one is on the bench below.

## CHOSEN

- **The blue at the collar.** Flat daylight at the top of the fold, before any of it: Ambros seated on an iron-strapped chest with the board across his knees, entering a share against each name in a hand that does not hurry — the coat buttoned to the throat, the pen going in the even hand, and **the finger-wide band of the empire's blue at his collar sitting exactly where the princess had put it.** Kael a step off with his head down toward the board, watching him write, with the half-thought he *could not finish and did not try to*. **The man writing everybody's share is the man who has sold them, and the blue on his throat was sewn by the girl he will apologise to as he dies** — and the picture says none of it. The steward's face is down over his own work and almost none of it is drawn; the boy's face carries the finish; the blue is the only saturated colour in the frame. The deliberate rhyme is `ch56/the-blue-and-the-white` — the same blue, the other end of it. Waist-up, 3:4. - **He never said it.** The fire that was built above the ground with the stones all round it, and across it the quietest beat in the book: the old man sitting with his cup, the one eye going round the fire pricing as it always goes round a room, **stopping on her a beat longer than it stops on anybody and going on.** Beyond the flame, lit, mid-sentence, both hands up, telling somebody outside the frame something and laughing — *thirty and sharp and alive* — and she never sees it. **He takes the finish and she takes the light**, which is §The winner and the rival split across two axes. Nothing is happening. *He never said it. Kael would think about that for the rest of his life.* Chest-up on him, two depths, 3:4. - **He walked through it.** The reveal, and the action plate the set has been short of: Thiro turns the killing volume of air on an old man at ten feet **and it does nothing** — the young man's near hand up and open and palm out, seen from behind and faceless, the gesture that has been taking the breath out of the camp all night doing nothing at all; and Borren coming on through it, not clawing, not reaching, breathing, the one eye open and flat and fixed on him. **And the dust up off the ground and standing in a line the whole length of the blade, holding the edge's line and not falling** — the only thing in the picture that is not where gravity would put it, and the only pale hard edge in it. §Write the state, not the action, on a thing that happens in a quarter of a second. ## CANDIDATES

- **No hand over it.** *The princess laughed, and laughed again, and did not put her hand over it once* — the direct answer to `ch55/behind-her-hand`, the same girl with the hand gone, on the last night she is ever Adah. **First in line for a fourth**, and the only reason it is not chosen is that the chapter can afford one warm close-up and `he never said it` is the one the next two chapters spend.
- **You do not get down.** Tirzah's hand closed in the back of the princess's coat, her mouth low and hard at the girl's ear, Selis dead in the traces behind them. Two references and two faces inches apart, and the hardest thing in the chapter to look at. Passed over only on the count: it is a third warm plate on a night that already has two.
- **She had the ground.** *And she put her hand flat on the earth, once, for less than a second, and it stood up into the first of them.* Valeria's Art at the moment it lands. Held back because Ch. 59 is the fight chapter and this is the picture it will want; taking it here spends it early.
- **That was Valeria laughing.** On her back along the outside of the light with the sword along her leg, her head turned to watch the girl laugh and turned back to the dark. A crop the set does not have — a figure lying down, from her own level — and a whole character in one turn of the head. Kept on the bench and worth a plate somewhere.
- **Oran sang for the both of them.** Talo's head back against a wagon wheel, his eyes closed and his mouth moving to every word without a sound, while his brother sings a road and a house at the end of it. **BLOCKED: neither twin has a face**, and this beat is nothing without one — a resolved face is exactly what §A face with no reference withholds. **It is the beat that would earn cutting a reference for the twins**, and that is the author's call, not this list's.
- **Thirty.** The canvas off in the flat light, thirty iron-strapped chests with the Emperor's mark on every one, nobody saying anything because *there is a size of thing that takes the words out of a person* — and Oran's hand flat on a lid *the way you put your hand on an animal to be sure it is real*. A fine picture of a quantity and a thin one of a person; the daylight slot went to the blue instead, which has a face in it.
- **Come in from the dark.** Tirzah patting the ground beside her and the boy who *had never once in his life been somewhere he was allowed to stay* getting up and going and sitting where a woman with a basket told him to sit. The chapter's gift to Kael, and the reason it is not chosen is that the warehouse man is on one side of him and has no face.
- **A stone shed.** The heel of the bread thrown, missed, and caught out of the air without looking by the twin who does not talk — the funniest thing that had happened in a year. No face for either twin, and the joke is in the catch, which a still picture cannot hold.
- **They reached for their own throats.** Four men going down in a space the size of a table, clawing at nothing, no mark on any of them. Four faceless bodies and a refusal risk against no face at all; the same beat is carried in one raised hand in `he walked through it`.
- **Torches, coming back.** The chapter's last line — the column on the west road, every man in white and blue, and their officer riding at the head with **his left hand empty**. Real dread and a real picture, and passed over because `ch57/the-half-second` spent an officer at distance one chapter ago and the rhyme would read as a repeat rather than a return.

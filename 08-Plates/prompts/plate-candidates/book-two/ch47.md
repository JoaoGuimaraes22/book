# Candidates CH47 — The Crossing

*Written at s71, at design time. **Nine days east along the Kharoun, the arrival at the end of the road, and the first week of the hunt** — Kael thirteen, the princess a year above him, Valeria of his age; the fourteen-year-old references are the anchor. Nobody else in the cast is on this page. Zahir, the driver, Rashim and the men at the table have no references and no `03` sheets and are framed rather than resolved.*

**The chapter is a procedure in three movements** — the road, the arrival, the hunt — and the three plates take one from each. **The folder has never drawn this city**, which is the setting for the rest of the book, so one of them had to be Ishareth. And the chapter's own centre, the count explained out loud for the first time in the manuscript, is two people talking in the dark and is the one thing a picture cannot carry: it is not on this list in any form, and the moment that frames it is under CANDIDATES and blocked on repetition.

**Two rulings.** **The princess's hair is covered** in all three — she has worn the scarf from waking to sleeping since Doresh without anybody telling her to, and it costs the set its brightest mass on purpose. And **no age rung goes into `The shape`**: three faces at conversational distance, and a rung on each is the bloat §Before the run is about. `ch46/the-strip` carried one and still ran years above it, so it is not free either way.

## CHOSEN

- **The shape.** The scrape below the skirt on the third evening: the princess at the middle with her knees up, talking, the dirt in front of her clear and empty and nobody sitting in it; Valeria three feet off, a half-body's turn from her shoulder, on the side where the ground falls away and with her back to all of it; Kael opposite, the sacking of iron across his knees, looking at where she is sitting and not at her face. **The composition is the subject** — the picture draws the shape the princess has just described, so the reader sees exactly what Kael sees, and there is no other way to say it. Three portraits attached; the geometry is written as the one thing in the block that must be exact. 3:4.   - **The head-cloth failed, and the clause that would have held it is one Claude had cut.** *A plain cloth tied over her head with no hair showing anywhere* ran once and her silver shows plainly at the front and sides. `ch44/nobody-said-anything` carried the same instruction **twice** — once in the figure block and once in the preserve line, *her hair entirely covered* — and it held. The s71 sweep removed the preserve-line copy as one-channel-twice. **One instance each way and the cause is not settled**; evidence for the next block that covers her, not a rule, and not a reason to re-cut this one.
  - **Three more drifts.** *Opposite them both* came back as a row: the three sit side by side rather than Kael facing across, and the shape survives it because he is still turned in and looking down at the ground between them. The princess's eyes went to the viewer rather than to Kael, the gaze channel drifting as it did on `ch47/a-slow-count-of-forty` in the same session. And **the burn did not render for the second run running** — as at `ch46/the-strip`, the composition put her left side oblique to the camera, so **this is a framing problem and not a wording one**: a picture that needs the burn has to put that side of her face toward the viewer. All three faces read above their ages with no rung in the block, which is no worse than `ch46/the-strip` with one.
- **One bridge.** The ninth morning at the lip where the road ends: three small figures with their backs to us, one with an arm in white linen; below them the city packed absolutely solid and grey-brown to the water, grown round both ends of its own wall and out past it into yards and pens and cloth roofs; the brown river; **one old stone bridge on heavy piers with the water piled up white against the upstream side of every one of them**, a gatehouse at each end and a thread of animals crossing from the far side; and every track on that plain bending toward each other and going in at the near gatehouse. The frame stacks bottom to top and is pinned by the ratio. The page's own negative is in the block — **nothing stands above the roofs anywhere in it**, and nothing white or blue on the wall — because that absence is how Kael knows what he is looking at. **No reference attached**, so the block takes the STYLE Medium and Colour paragraphs. 3:4.   - **RUN RECORD (first run, s71; 1086×1448, exactly 3:4). ADOPTED AS DRAWN. THE AUTHOR CUT THE THREE FIGURES OUT OF THE BLOCK BEFORE RUNNING IT AND THE FILE HOLDS THEIR VERSION.** It is the right cut: the picture has no person and no white anywhere in it, and it does not want either. **Everything the page says about this city is on the paper.** The old wall crosses the land side and the city has grown round both ends of it and out past it into pens and yards; **nothing stands above the roofs anywhere in it — no tower, no dome, no spire** — which is the absence Kael reads the whole empire off; and there is **one bridge and only one**, low and long on heavy piers with the water piled white against every one of them, a squat gatehouse at each end, and a line of pack animals crossing from the far side as the one moving thing in the frame. Every track on that plain bends toward the others, joins, and goes in at the near gatehouse.
  - **The demotion did not hold and the register went the other way from `ch46/the-roof`.** The block gave the bridge the sharpest step and the most finished drawing and put the city at quiet middle values; the run drew **everything at maximum finish**, and the city is as resolved as the bridge. And where `ch46/the-roof` kept the wash and lost the ink, this one **keeps the ink and loses the wash**: a dense topographic pen drawing, near-monochrome, with almost no bare paper and no wash blooms anywhere. **Two runs on the no-reference route and it has now missed in opposite directions** — which says the route is unstable rather than biased, and is worth knowing before the next block that has no reference attached. Neither is a correction owed: the picture is one of the best in the folder.
- **A slow count of forty.** The bridge-house yard: Kael near the camera with both hands on a barrow, the only resolved face in the picture, and two strides from him a man at the gate with his thumbs in his belt, half turned away, talking about nothing. Behind them the table with its board and its strip of paper, the man beside it who does nothing except look at whoever comes up, and the chest on the ground with a soldier either side. **His eyes are on the chest and not on the man two strides away** — a boy standing unseen inside the thing that is hunting him, with the bound arm that is one of the three descriptions of him out in the open where anybody could see it. `ch44/nobody-said-anything`'s recipe: one face drawn and a whole yard that is not. 3:4.   - **RUN RECORD (first run, s71; 1085×1449, one pixel off 3:4). ADOPTED AS DRAWN. THE FIRST PICTURE OF ISHARETH AND THE BEST RENDERING OF THE REGISTER IN THE FOLDER'S BOOK-TWO WORK.** Walls the colour of the ground with the ground's own grain worked right through them, tooth everywhere, ink dominant, and bare paper carrying the yard. **The finish instruction did exactly what it was written to do:** Kael's is the only resolved face in the picture and every other man in the yard is wash and gesture. The southern register separates cleanly — the men crossing the yard in loose pale wound cloth with their heads covered, the men of the building in plain dark military kit, and Kael in neither. The table, the seated man over his board, the chest on the ground and the narrow slits high in the stone are all in and all quiet. Pale grey eyes the lightest value in the face, freckles across the nose, the hair cut by somebody who was not being careful.
  - **The block contradicted itself and the renderer split the difference, which is Claude's fault and not the tool's.** *Both hands resting on the handles of a plain wooden barrow* and *the left arm bound from the knuckles to above the elbow … held in against his chest* cannot both be drawn, and §Before the run says so in as many words: a block long enough to restate itself is long enough to contradict itself. **The run resolved it by unbinding the hand** — the linen is on the forearm only, the left hand is bare and gripping the handle, and the thumb splint between two flat pieces of wood is gone. The page has that hand destroyed and splinted at Ch. 47. **The picture stands as drawn and `03` does not move for it.**
  - **Two more drifts.** *Level across the yard, past the table, at the chest behind it* put his eyes out toward the gate and near the viewer instead — the gaze channel doing what §Gaze says *level* does — and the beat of a boy counting coin while a known face stands two strides off is not what the picture shows. And **the man at the gate is not at two strides**: he is back at the doorway with the others, so the tension the moment was chosen for is out of it. What is there instead is a boy caught mid-look in a yard full of soldiers, which is a good picture and a different one.

## CANDIDATES

- **Thirty-eight mules.** Kael's hand on a mule's neck going under the toll arch in the middle of the herd, the man with the stick walking the line making the number true and never once looking at the height of anybody beside it, and the two girls four animals back in the dust with their heads covered. **The method in one picture, and a crowd of animals used as concealment**, which the folder has never drawn. **First in line for a fourth**, and the only thing here I regret leaving out.
- **Give me the hand.** The princess down on her heels beside him with the linen roll and the clay pot in her lap and her hand out flat, waiting, until he puts the hand into it; then the knot finished and the wrist not let go. **The chapter's hinge** — it is what opens the count — and the whole relationship in one gesture. **Blocked on repetition, not on quality:** her hands carried `ch45/covering-the-holes` and `ch46/nineteen`, and a third consecutive plate built on them is a drift in the set rather than a choice.
- **The three with the dirt still on them.** *These two are right … This one I do not know, because I pulled it and it came apart* — three of them held out with the dirt on, and the broken one going into the thorn. Her hands again, the same collision.
- **Both of them are work.** The princess laughing out of nowhere, once, with her hand going over the bottom of her face and coming away again, and Valeria walking beside her having just said the flattest true thing in the chapter. **She has never laughed in a picture**, and this is the first time the two of them are easy with each other.
- **Nineteen boards.** The count explained on the seventh night — *I did not learn it. I was lying on it.* The biggest thing said out loud about him in the manuscript, and unavailable: two faces in the dark with the fire two hours out, and the subject invisible.
- **He is spending a river.** The sum drawn in the dust on the boards of the room with one finger, the two of them sitting against the wall. Rhymes too closely with Ch. 45's passed-over *Drawing it in the dust*.
- **The yard he cannot go into.** The bridge-house from the street — one door on the street, one onto the bridge, a wall with one gate, and the slits upstairs made narrow and never widened. An architecture study of the thing the rest of the book has to open; held because `A slow count of forty` is already inside it and shows the same walls with a person in them.

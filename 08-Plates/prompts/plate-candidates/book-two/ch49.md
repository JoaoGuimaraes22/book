# Candidates CH49 — The Paper

*Written at s71, at design time. **The street, the sacks, the river, and the rope-walk** — Kael thirteen, Valeria of his age; the fourteen-year-old references are the anchor. **The princess is talked about all through this chapter and is on no page of it**, so she is in no picture from it. The crew are unnamed on the page and have no `03` sheets; the man at the table now has a reference (`portraits/crew-leader`, cut this session) and everybody else is framed rather than resolved.*

**Ch. 49 was committed but not revised when these were designed, and a parallel session was still working the book.** Every chapter quote below is pinned to draft text; **if a line moves, the quote on that entry has to move with it**, because `scripts/build-docx.py` finds it in the manuscript to place the picture — and at s71 five plates across two sessions were found unplaced for exactly that reason (`../../../README.md` §The files carries the two constraints).

**One fact runs through all three: they have no weapons in this chapter.** Both belts went on a table in movement II and were never given back, so every block says so once — the two references pull hard toward blades and nothing else in these pictures does.

## CHOSEN

- **The column.** Valeria has stood up and stepped back, and a column of the beaten floor as thick through as a man's body stands free between the table and the window at the height of her shoulder, its top rough where it stopped rather than cut, the earth for three feet round its foot sunk about the width of a thumb. She is waiting, her eyes on the man and not on what she made; he is looking at the pillar of his own floor and not at her. **The first picture of Valeria's Art in the book** — it was the strongest thing held back at Ch. 45 (*the best Art image the book has and the first ever of Valeria working it*), and it is better here: indoors, against a table of paper, with somebody watching. **Kael is framed out** so the column has two people and not three. 3:4. Ch. 49 canon: *A column of the floor about as thick through as a man came up out of the ground between the table and the window* → `../../plates/book-two/ch49-the-column.md`
- **The rope-walk.** The sacks come off: one building far longer than it is wide, a low roof on posts, no floor but the ground, and no wall at all on the river side — only posts, with the brown water between them. Two children small and backs-turned in the near frame with an empty grain sack still in one hand; **three men facing the far end and stepping backwards toward the viewer, each paying a strand of hemp out of a bundle at his waist so that his line runs away up the floor toward the wheel**; a fourth at the wheel; and the far end going into shadow and stopping being lit. The light comes in sideways between the posts off the water. **A setting the folder has nothing like, and where the rest of the book now lives.** **No reference attached**, so the block takes the STYLE Medium and Colour paragraphs. 3:4. Ch. 49 canon: *Three men were walking backwards down the length of it, paying hemp out of the bundles at their waists.* → `../../plates/book-two/ch49-the-rope-walk.md`
- **Nineteen boards.** The last beat of the chapter: the woman with the basket already back on her hip, saying his own count back to him once and exactly, and Kael watching her mouth rather than her eyes. **It pays off Ch. 47 directly** — the count was explained out loud there for the first time in the manuscript and could not be drawn, and here it is the thing he hands over, the one thing he had held all day and the only thing he brought into that building and did not put down on it. 3:4. Ch. 49 canon: *The woman by the door said it back to him once, exactly, in the order he had said it.* → `../../plates/book-two/ch49-nineteen-boards.md`

## CANDIDATES

- **The paper.** Three piles weighted flat under a stone, a strip with a column of figures down one side, a stick of wax, two seals in a dish and **a third lying loose beside it where somebody put it down in the middle of using it** — and a thin grey man finishing his line before he looks up. **The chapter's title image and the strongest thing passed over**; held only because the rope-walk is the stranger picture and this room will recur. **First in line for a fourth.**
- **The sacks.** Two children walking a southern street with grain sacks over their heads, a hand on each arm that never pulls and only ever weighs a little more on one side, and an ordinary city not looking at them. The folder has nothing remotely like it, and it is the one picture that carries what the whole middle of the chapter feels like.
- **The boots go on.** The direct sequel to `ch48/the-boots-off` — he looks up, looks down, and works the heel down with his thumb, and nothing else happens in that street while it is going on. A deliberate diptych; passed over as too close a rhyme to run in consecutive chapters.
- **Where he was the reason.** Fifteen people who look like nothing sitting on the floor of a rope-walk with the day's work stopped, and a boy standing in front of them. `ch44/nobody-said-anything` and `ch47/a-slow-count-of-forty` have both already had one-drawn-face-in-a-crowded-room this session.
- **Again.** Kael's two fingers along the flat of a borrowed knife, the white the whole length of it, four seconds, and a man who does not move. **Blocked on collision:** `ch45/the-thread` is the same white line on the same kind of blade, and the negation paragraph that bought it would have to run again unchanged.

# Candidates CH54 — The Trade

*Written at s77, at design time. **Two months of road in one chapter** — the watching, the teaching, the bridge, the jobs, and a boy kneeling in a stream at the end of it. Kael thirteen, Valeria of his age, Aeliana fourteen; the fourteen-year-old references anchor, and the two blocks with a young face carry the rung.*

**THE PAGE HAS CAUGHT UP WITH THE PLATES ON HER HAIR.** Four plates running have covered the princess's head as a framing answer where the page was silent; Ch. 54 says it outright — *had not been allowed a scarf across her face, only across her hair* — and the bridge scene has *the girl four mules back with her head covered*. The framing answer was the right one and is now page canon.

**THE NEW REFERENCE GOES STRAIGHT TO WORK.** `../../portraits/tirzah.md` was cut and adopted at s77 and this chapter is what it was for: she teaches him to walk, and she is the one who meets the officer on the bridge. Her covered head and the basket come with her.

**§THE CUT'S NEW RULE IS APPLIED FROM THE START.** Five runs running came back one band wider than the block asked, always with hands below the chest in it, so every crop here is written at the width the hands need — three-quarter length for the basket and the drill, waist-up for the hands in the stream — instead of asking for chest-up and being widened.

**THE THREE REGISTERS ARE DELIBERATELY DIFFERENT:** low afternoon daylight, dusk against the last of the sky, and cold flat light before sunrise. No warm source is in any of the three frames, after two fire-lit plates at Ch. 53.

## CANDIDATES

- **A price in her mouth.** The bridge at noon: the officer who comes out for anything with more than three animals walking down the line, and Tirzah stepping in at the third mule with the basket on her hip to be difficult about a copper, while four mules back a girl with her head covered is not looked at once. **The chapter's set-piece and its argument** — Borren was right, and the reason is a woman arguing about a copper. Passed over because the seller lesson already gives Tirzah the chapter and this one needs imperial white and blue, an officer with no reference, and seven mules to read; **first in line for a fourth**, and the crop is the two of them at the third mule with the line running back out of focus.
- **The princess has gone brown.** Freckles over the nose, hands gone hard, carrying water with Tirzah and laughing at something Tirzah said — *and nobody was watching, which was the whole of what she had wanted out of it.* **The set has no picture of her happy**, and this is the largest change in anybody this chapter. Blocked on nothing at all; it lost the slot to Valeria because the princess had Ch. 53.
- **He did not laugh.** Kael on the back of the cart with his feet hanging, going out of an inn gate at first light past a man on the gate who is helping them, the lord's own mark chalked on the crates behind him. *He understood the joke about the honest men in the whole of his body, and did not laugh.*
- **By weight and not by grip.** The man from Ishareth's lock on a stone, a length of wetted twine, and Kael's ruined left hand holding the lock down the only way it can hold anything now. A hands-own-view crop and the third picture running of what that hand can and cannot do; passed over on the rhyme, and it is the best of the teaching beats after the walk.
- **The one who looked at the door eleven times.** Seven men in a room and a boy counting the door, being bored at a man in the empire's tongue with a town in it. Interior and warm; the chapter has no other room in it.
- **The candle went out.** The turn of the stair, the man with the candle coming round it, the knife already out of the sleeve. The chapter's one piece of violence and its hardest picture; it needs the injury instruments (*along it, not across*, the site stated flat, the face forbidden the wound) and a stairwell lit by one falling candle. Available on the author's word.
- **Between Ishareth and Solvenna.** Borren mending a strap without looking up, and the whole answer to where they are. A quiet two-hander the chapter opens with.

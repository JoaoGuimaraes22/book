# Candidates CH50 — The Reward

*Written at s75, at design time. **The rope-walk at dusk, the river, the bridge-house, and the room with the shutters closed** — Kael thirteen, Valeria of his age; the fourteen-year-old references are the anchor, and **no block carries an age rung, because all three have iron in frame** (§Age in a plate: beside restraint let the reference carry the age). The governor of Qeshet is on every page of the second half and has no reference: he is framed, never resolved. The crew are unnamed on the page; the man at the table has `portraits/crew-leader` and is in no chosen picture this chapter — Ch. 49 spent two on him.*

**A parallel writing session (s74) is on the book.** Every chapter quote below is pinned to the committed text of Ch. 50; **if a line moves, the quote on that entry has to move with it** (`../../../README.md` §The files carries the two constraints).

**One fact runs through all three: the iron is real and it is theirs.** Plain new bands, no black in the grooves, a pin that pulls with two fingers — the chapter's whole engine is that the two of them wear it on purpose, so each block puts it in frame plainly and at scene distance, never as the declared subject (the `ch39` refusal shape).

## CHOSEN

- **The pin.** Valeria fitting the band on Kael's right wrist at the open river side of the rope-walk, dusk, the river gone the colour of the sky behind them. The band closed, the file marks still bright, her thumb pressed on the pin — and stopped there: he has just said *If you have any ideas besides this I'd love to hear them*, and the answer she gives after the half-second is *I do not*. Both heads bowed to the wrist; both gazes on the object, which is the §Gaze fix the s71 record said had not been tried once that session. Waist-up, two figures. 3:4. - **An inch off the floor.** Valeria and the man nearest the door, because he was the one who could get out of it: a fistful of the front of him, a hand round his wrist, and both his boots hanging clear of the boards. Internal reinforcement drawn for the second time in the set (after `ch49/the-column`) and the first time as force on a body — nothing leaving her, nothing glowing, a girl in irons standing normally while a grown man hangs off her fist. The set is short of action (author, s65) and this is the chapter's one drawable second of it: *There was nothing to see* is the picture's whole instruction, so the block bans effort and spectacle and puts the proof in the strip of dark under his boots, sized against a board's edge (§Scale). His face carries the beat the page gives it — *what a face does when it has understood something it has no way of using* — as wide fixed eyes and an open mouth with nothing coming out. Full length, lamplight, the §Limits warm-register cost accepted: the room's shutters are closed and the lamps are the page's own light. 3:4. THE EASE IS THE PICTURE:** a grown man against the shut plank door with both boots clear of the boards, and a girl standing normally under him — weight even, arms unstrained, nothing coming off her, no glow and nothing lifted anywhere. His face carries the page's beat exactly: eyes wide and fixed down on her, brows up, the mouth open with nothing coming out of it. The iron and the slack chain on her wrists sit at the centre of the two-body mass, which is the anti-embrace instrument doing its job.
  - **BOTH GAZES WENT WHERE THEY WERE SENT — the first eye-to-person block in five that did not drift to the viewer** (the s71 record counted four). Hers up to his face in profile, his down to hers; the profile crop leaves the camera nothing to catch, which may be the whole mechanism.
  - **THE INCH BECAME A FOOT.** The block sized the gap by ratio (*about the depth of one board's edge*) with both terms in frame, and the lift still came back many times that — a full dangle, toes pointed. The composition's drama beat a correctly-sited ratio; the page's *about an inch* is the one thing the picture overstates. Evidence, not a correction owed.
  - **Two more.** *Plain dark road clothing* on her came back as a floor-length dark skirt — a dress, not travel cloth; the register held on colour and lost on construction. And her left hand, written *closed round his right wrist*, landed lower — on the cloth at his hip, with a clenched right fist beside it the block never gave him; the hand-chain rule (§Hands: one continuous limb, in order) was not used and would have been the tool.
- **Where are they.** The chapter's last image: the chair pulled round to face the wall, the boy sitting in it with his hands in his lap and the chain lying across them, asking the bound governor of Qeshet the question the whole road east has been carrying. Framed over the governor's shoulder — one dark unresolved shoulder and the back of his head in the near frame, the one band of imperial blue along his cloth's edge the only bright colour on him (the page's own colour note, and a §A-face-with-no-reference framing) — with Valeria standing half a step behind Kael's left shoulder, which is where she puts herself. Both children still in iron. Kael mid-word, quiet, nothing hot in the face. **Gaze risk carried knowingly:** both pairs of eyes go to a person near the camera, which is the drift pattern the s71 record counted four times; the object-fix is unavailable because the moment is the looking, so the block says *just past the viewer and not on the viewer* in as many words. Lamplight again — the same room, the same register. 3:4. ## CANDIDATES

- **The chin under the lamp.** The governor takes Kael's chin in his hand and turns the face up under the lamp, and Kael lets his eyes go where a boy's eyes go — a close-up of the grey at full strength doing deliberate nothing, with the man as a hand, a dark sleeve and a shape. The chapter's best close-up and **first in line for a fourth**. Passed over for the spread: the chosen three already carry two lamp-room pictures, and a grown hand gripping a child's face over iron in a close crop is the `ch39` filter shape at its worst — at a fourth, write it at scene distance first.
- **The thumb on the iron.** *His thumb stayed where it was on the iron. For about a second and a half he did not move at all.* — the hinge of the whole chapter. Blocked as a picture: the beat lives in the governor's face, which has no reference and cannot be resolved, and a bent man holding a wrist reads as nothing without it. The version that works is a close-up of the crew leader's single eye reading that face — and Ch. 49 just spent a crew-leader close-up (*the laugh*); not two chapters running.
- **The wall stops.** Forty or fifty men along the yard wall in the last of the light, and everything on that wall stopping as two children in irons are turned to face it. The chapter's widest picture; passed over on the author's standing preference for a face, an emotion, an action, a close-up (s71, the rope-walk swap) — and `ch44`/`ch47` have both had one-face-against-a-crowd recently.
- **The woman with the basket, arming him.** The knife worked down inside the boot seam and the leather pressed flat over it with her thumb — or the walk test, twenty strides down the boards, *slower, you have been on a road and you have not eaten*. A fine hands-moment; passed over because Ch. 49 spent her (*nineteen-boards*) and the chapter's stronger hand-beat is the pin.
- **Rashim.** Halfway across the yard, stopped, the mouth moving once without making a word out of it, then the first hurry of his life. No reference, and the beat is a face resolving — the same block as the thumb.
- **The search.** Thirty-five searches four feet from the one body carrying everything, and nobody near him. The irony is arithmetic, not drawable; a picture of it is just men patting other men.
- **The chain touched itself twice.** After the fitting: the chain hanging between his wrists, swinging. An object study of restraint with a child just out of frame — the exact crop the `ch39` refusals taught; blocked.
- **Hands and mouths.** The takedown wide: six men moving at once and none toward a weapon, a chair over, a cup off the table not breaking. Passed over: five unresolved faces in one action frame, and the adopted action pick shows the same fact — *not one of them moved toward a weapon* — with one drawn face instead of ten.

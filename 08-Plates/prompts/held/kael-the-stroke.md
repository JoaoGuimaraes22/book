Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms, bare paper in the lights, dry-brush drag and flicked spatter.

Composition: 3:4. Waist-up, one figure, close to the viewer and turned three-quarters toward it. The bottom edge crosses him at the waist, so no legs and no ground are in the frame. He is alone in the picture: no second figure, no other body, no other blade anywhere in the frame.

Setting: a packed earth yard as loose wash suggestion — a wall implied close behind him going into shadow, dust standing in the air, no detail anywhere. Flat cold daylight from one side, no sun and no hard-edged shadow.

THE STROKE HAS ALREADY GONE THROUGH AND HE IS AT THE END OF IT. The right arm is extended out and across his own body, the blade carried past the far side and level, the shoulders wound round after it and the near shoulder dropped, the chest open toward the viewer, the head still turned back to where the stroke went. Nothing is being aimed and nothing is being drawn back.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
His right hand holds one short blade at the end of the arc, out and across the body, level, its point past the far edge of his shoulder.
His left hand holds the second short blade close in against his ribs, the point down and back, unmoved.
Two hands, two blades, nothing else in either hand. The cloth wrap across his back is empty and flat.

The two blades are a matched pair: straight double-edged bars of steel, each about the length of his own forearm and hand together, heavy through the blade, a plain straight crossguard, a plain leather grip, no ornament on either.

THE DRAG FOLLOWS THE ARC. Dry-brush drag runs along the path the right blade has come through, thinning as it leaves the steel, and flicked spatter goes off the far end of it. Everywhere else the brushwork is still.

HIS FACE IS SET HARD AND NONE OF IT IS MOVING. The chin down so the eyes come out from under the brows, the eyes fixed on one point past the far edge of the frame where the stroke went, the brows low and straight, the jaw set with the muscle standing at the corner, the mouth closed and pressed thin. Not shouting, not grimacing, not straining. He is not looking at the viewer.

Undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, well made and much mended, the sleeves pushed back off the forearms, no ornament, badge, colour or metal on him anywhere. Grey dust across the front of him. Dried dark red gone brown along both forearms and none of it is his. Nothing bleeding.

THE BLADE AND HIS FACE CARRY THE PICTURE. The steel holds the sharpest step from light to dark in the frame and his face the most finished drawing; the wall, the dust and the air behind him stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges and no hard edge anywhere in them.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in the reference images, which are the same boy. Pale grey eyes, the lightest value in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven.

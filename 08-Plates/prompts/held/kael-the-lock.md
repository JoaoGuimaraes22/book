Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms, bare paper in the lights, dry-brush drag and flicked spatter.

Setting: a packed earth yard between low stone buildings, as loose wash suggestion — a wall implied close behind them going into shadow, dust standing in the air, no detail anywhere. Flat cold daylight from one side, no sun and no hard-edged shadow. Both figures are grounded on the same ground plane.

Composition: 3:4. The camera is close and level, side on. Two figures locked together in the middle of the frame, the bottom edge crossing both at the thigh. KAEL is nearest and on the right, turned three-quarters toward the camera, low and driving in from below with his weight already forward. A MAN, larger and heavier, is on the left, drawn as one dark mass with his back mostly to the camera, his face away and not resolved, bent over and taken off his balance. Nobody else is anywhere in the picture.

THEY DO NOT READ AS ONE SHAPE. The two bodies are at opposing angles, Kael's line going up and in and the man's going down and away, and a clear gap of light ground shows between their two heads and between their two bodies at the waist. This is not an embrace: neither body is upright, neither is still, and neither pair of arms is round the other's back.

THE LOCK IS ALREADY MADE AND THE WEIGHT IS ALREADY COMMITTED. Nothing is being reached for and nothing is travelling.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
Kael's right hand is shut hard round the man's near wrist, the fingers wrapped right over, the knuckles standing white.
Kael's left forearm is driven in across the back of the man's shoulder, that hand shut and empty.
The man's far hand is open and clawed at Kael's collar and is not gripping it.
Three hands, nothing held in any of them. No blade, knife, spear or weapon of any kind anywhere in the frame. Two short blades are bundled in a cloth wrap across Kael's back with the hilts covered.

THE GRIP IS THE HARDEST THING IN THE PICTURE. Kael's shut right hand and the wrist inside it carry the sharpest step from light to dark in the frame, the knuckles drawn with a hard ink edge; his face carries the most finished drawing after it.

KAEL IS WORKING AND HE IS WINNING IT. The chin down so the eyes come out from under the brows, the eyes fixed on the man's shoulder, the brows low and straight, the jaw set with the muscle standing at the corner, the mouth closed and pressed thin, the breath going through the nose. Nothing in his face is frightened and nothing in it is straining.

HIS CLOTH IS SOUND UNDER WHAT IS ON IT. Undyed coarse working wool in dull oatmeal and dead olive, well made and much mended, the collar pulled out of shape in the man's hand and the cloth whole below it, no ornament, badge, colour or metal on him anywhere. Not ragged, not tattered, not a beggar's clothing. Grey dust and soot across the front of him and up both forearms, a graze raw-looking along one cheekbone. Dried dark red gone brown on both forearms and none of it is his. Nothing bleeding.

The man wears plain dark working kit, unrelieved, with nothing legible on it, and it belongs only to him; the undyed mended wool belongs only to the boy.

The man, the wall, the ground and the dust stay at quiet middle values with soft wash-bled edges and no hard edge anywhere in them.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in the reference images, which are the same boy. Pale grey eyes, the lightest value in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven.

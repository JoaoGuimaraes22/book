Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than
fills. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the warmth
belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Eyes: the focal point of the picture, framed by dark lashes and
dark brows.

Composition: three-quarter view with the head turned away from the
viewer, gaze returning to the viewer. Head and shoulders, with
visible shoulder and collar anchoring the lower frame.

Lighting: soft directional light, gentle shadow beneath the
cheekbone and along the jaw, most of the face held in light values.

Age: a woman of about thirty, in the middle of her strength. Weather
and sun on her from a life spent outdoors and standing up, and
nothing taken out of her by it: the cheek full, no hollow under the
eye, no slackness at the jaw, the neck firm. Worked, not worn down.

SUBJECT — cool pale parchment ground.
A woman of the southern coast who kept a market stall.

HER HEAD IS ENTIRELY COVERED AND NO HAIR SHOWS ANYWHERE. A long
length of coarse undyed cloth, the colour of unbleached linen, wound
over the head and once round the throat and knotted at one side,
worn soft and gone grey-brown in the folds from years of handling.
It comes down close round the face, so that the face sits inside it
as inside a frame.

THE EYES ARE THE ONLY DARK IN THE PICTURE AND THEY CARRY IT. Very
dark brown, near black, and the only fully resolved drawing in the
frame: a sharp closed line round the lid and a clean wet dark at the
pupil, while every other edge in the face is loose wash bled soft.
There is no hair anywhere in the picture to compete with them.

Brown skin, warm and sun-darkened, weathered at the outer corner of
the eye and across the bridge of the nose; clear, and with no high
colour in it. A broad face, wide at the cheekbone and short through
the chin. Strong straight brows, darker than the skin.

SHE IS ABOUT TO ASK SOMETHING. The head is tilted a courteous half
inch, the chin a little down, the attention entirely forward and
level on the viewer. The mouth is closed with the lips just together
and not pressed.

A plain working over-dress of the same coarse undyed cloth, the
colour of the ground, wide at the neck with the shoulder seam falling
low. Nothing dyed and no ornament of any kind anywhere on her.

Not a mourner, not a beggar, not a peasant in rags and not a
fortune-teller: a woman who priced things all day for a living and
asked the sharpest question in a room of fifteen.

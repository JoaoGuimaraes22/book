Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. The ink linework remains the
strongest element; colour tints rather than fills. Large areas left
as bare paper. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the
warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Eyes: the focal point of the picture, framed by dark lashes and
dark brows.

Composition: three-quarter view with the head turned away from the
viewer, gaze returning to the viewer. Head and shoulders, with
visible shoulder and collar anchoring the lower frame.

Lighting: soft directional light, gentle shadow beneath the
cheekbone and along the jaw, most of the face held in light values.

Age: eyes set at the vertical midpoint of the head. Fourteen, and
nothing in the face has hardened yet. Jaw soft and barely defined,
cheeks full and rounded with no hollow anywhere beneath the
cheekbone, chin small and soft, the neck short and soft. Adolescent,
not grown, and no bone showing anywhere in the face.

SUBJECT — cool pale parchment ground.
A fourteen-year-old boy of a merchant house that bought its name.
Eyes warm mid-brown, small, quick and precisely drawn, wet-looking
and alert; the sharpest step from light to dark in the whole picture
happens inside the eye, at the edge of the iris. Nothing else in the
picture carries an edge that hard.
Mid-brown hair, thick and soft, cut short and neatly by somebody who
was paid to do it and combed flat — kept, not tousled, and never a
dark mass; open transparent washes with bare paper left through it,
so that it does not compete with the eyes.
Clear well-fed skin with a little sun on it, even and unmarked — no
weathering, no chapping, no grime.
Soft-built, with no hardness anywhere in him: a round face, full
cheeks, a soft undefined jaw, a short soft neck, sloping shoulders
with nothing standing at the collar. Nothing about this body has
ever been worked.
Expression caught between two things, both on the face at once — the
mouth still holding the shape of having just laughed, open a little,
the corners not yet down; and the eyes not laughing at all, wide
open and quick and watching carefully, pricing what they watch. A
face built for laughing, in the moment between laughs.
Not a comic fat boy, not a jester, not sly, not sad.
Good cloth, and new: a well-made tunic in a dull madder red-brown, a
colour that had to be bought, over a clean undershirt; the collar
standing exactly where it was pressed, the seams sharp, and not one
crease, rub or worn edge anywhere on it — clothing that has not yet
learned the body inside it. One small bright piece of metal at the
collar, a little more than the garment needs.

Medium: loose expressive watercolour and ink on rough cold-press
paper, visible paper tooth and pigment granulation, scratchy fine
ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular edges, generous bare
unpainted paper.

Colour: restrained transparent washes laid over a dominant ink
drawing. Muted, desaturated, earthy. Colour tints rather than
fills. Warm living skin, soft ochre in the mid-tones — the warmth
belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth.

Composition: three-quarter view with the head turned away from the
viewer, gaze returning to the viewer, and the uncovered eye toward
the viewer. Head and shoulders, with visible shoulder and collar
anchoring the lower frame.

Lighting: soft directional light, gentle shadow beneath the
cheekbone, most of the face held in light values.

Age: a man past fifty, thin, and nothing has been taken out of him.
Upright, the neck firm, the skin close at the throat, no hollowing
at the temples and no slackness under the jaw. Thin, not frail.

SUBJECT — cool pale parchment ground.
A man past fifty who has spent his working life with paper.

HE HAS ONE EYE. Over the right eye there is a plain oval of dark
leather, cut to sit close, worn soft and gone shiny along the edge
from years of handling, held on a thin cord that runs back into the
hair above the ear. It is old, it is unremarkable, and nothing about
the way he wears it is dramatic. The socket beneath it is not shown
and no strap crosses the face anywhere else.

THE LEFT EYE CARRIES THE WHOLE PICTURE. Light grey-brown, and the
only fully resolved drawing in the frame: a sharp closed line round
the lid and a clean wet dark at the pupil, while every other edge in
the face is loose wash bled soft. It is steady, unhurried, and level
on the viewer.

Grey hair, thin on top and receded well back from the forehead, the
rest of it swept back over the ears and worn long enough to reach the
collar. A full grey beard, kept and close-cut, covering the jaw and
the chin with the moustache joined to it. The brows are dark and
match neither the hair nor the beard.
A narrow face, long through the nose, weathered by living rather than
by work — lines at the brow and beside the eye, and no high colour.
The face is pleasant and the eye is not. The beard has the mouth, so
what expression there is sits in the brow and the eye: the brow level
and unmoved, the head tilted a courteous half-inch.
A plain dark coat over a plain collar, good cloth, no ornament on it
anywhere.

Not a pirate, not a mercenary, not an old soldier and not a sage: he
is the man in the room who decides things, and nothing in the neck or
the shoulders was ever built by work.

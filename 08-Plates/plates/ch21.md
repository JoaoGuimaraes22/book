# Ch. 21 — The Edge · plate sheet

**Composed at s48 from the scene.** Kael is twelve, in the turn toward spring. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/neris.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S OWN SPLIT.** *The night still belonged to his mother* — the chapter divides itself down the middle into a father's dawn and a mother's table, and the plates take one each: an edge stopped a finger's width off a boy's throat, and nine things laid in a row beside a lamp with a wisp of tow lifting at the end of them.

**THE SECOND ONE IS THE FIRST SCIENCE PLATE THE BOOK HAS.** `ch10/the-waterer` is the only machine in the set and it is a thing that has been built; this is a thing being measured, in progress, with the instrument in frame — and the instrument is hers. **It also solves the Neris hands problem the honest way for once:** *"You are going to spend, and I am going to hold the tow."* The page puts the measure in her hand, so no framing dodge is needed and none is used (`ch19.md` had to rule the other way).

**AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME THE BOOK DRAWS HER PREGNANT.** `../canon-brief.md` requires the choice rather than a default: she is very late, carrying high and in front, sitting side-on to the table with her left forearm along the top and her weight back, because *she could not get to the table properly any more.* The page states all of it, including the fact that she rearranged the table's whole surface early so nobody would watch her need to.

**THE REFUSAL RISK ON THE FIRST BLOCK IS REAL AND IS PRICED.** A grown man holding an edge at a child's throat is the kind of subject that produces no picture at all rather than a wrong one (§Limits: do not iterate on a refusal). Three things hold it down as far as the moment allows: **the man is out of frame from the elbow up**, so no adult body stands over a child in the picture; **the edge is stopped clear of the skin and touches nothing**; and there is no blood, no wound and no injury anywhere in it, which also keeps it clear of §Damage's stated-age-plus-injury refusal. If it comes back refused, **the fallback is candidate 3, the surge up the blade** — do not re-run this one to argue with a filter.

**THE GAZE IS TIED TO SOMETHING IN THE FRAME, BOTH TIMES.** Ch. 18 lost a run to a sightline whose object was outside the picture; so here the boy looks along the blade to the hand holding it at the frame's edge, and at the table both of them look at the tow. Neither block asks for a gaze at anything the viewer cannot see.

### PLATE: dead

*Candidate 1.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 21 canon: *his father came at him with an edge on the blade and Kael's guard was a quarter-count late. / The edge stopped across his throat. / It did not touch him. It arrived at the place where it would have gone through and it was not going through, and it stayed there long enough for Kael to feel the cold coming off the steel onto his skin. / "Dead," his father said.* And the state it is drawn in: *his heart was doing what it had done in a yard in the dark when he was nine … Everything had gone very clear and slightly too bright at the edges … he had gone past being frightened somewhere around the third one and come out the far side of it into the place where the count lived.*

**THE SUBJECT IS A FACE THAT HAS COME OUT THE FAR SIDE OF FRIGHT**, which is a different thing from courage and a different thing from blankness, and the page names it exactly. The block writes it as anatomy — eyes wide and clear and fixed, mouth closed, jaw set, the breath shallow — and bans the three faces it would otherwise become.

**AND THE FATHER IS AN ARM.** Keeping him out of frame from the elbow up is not squeamishness: the chapter's argument is that the whole arrangement stands on one man not making a mistake and *there was no second mechanism anywhere in it* — a picture with his face in it becomes a picture about what he is feeling, and the page spends nothing on that. The steel and the hand are the whole of him the boy has to work with, which is the point.

**REFUSED BY THE TOOL (s48). No picture was produced, and the block is not re-run.** §Limits: *a refusal produces no picture where a fault produces a wrong one — do not iterate on a refusal, it is the tool's limit on the moment.* The sheet priced this before the run and named the fallback, and the fallback was taken the same session.

**WHAT THE REFUSAL TEACHES, AND IT IS NEW.** §Damage knows one refusal shape — *a stated age beside an injury* — and this block has no injury in it at all: nothing cut, no skin broken, no blood, no mark, the edge stopped clear of the neck, and the man out of frame from the elbow up. It was refused anyway. **So the boundary is not injury: it is a weapon held against a child, whatever the weapon is doing.** One instance, so it stays a finding here; if a second block hits it, §Damage gets the line.

**THE MOMENT IS NOT LOST, IT IS UNDRAWABLE BY THIS TOOL** — the same status `ch15.md` gave the father not moving, for a different reason. The block stays below exactly as it was written, in case the folder ever has another instrument.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: dead-restated

*The same moment, reworded at s48 after the refusal (author: **wait no i think we can get dead working, just need to change the wording**).* **This overrides §Limits' *do not iterate on a refusal* for this block, on the author's word, and the override is scoped to it.** The block above stays exactly as it was refused so the two can be read against each other.

**THE SUSPECT IS IN OUR OWN FILES.** §Absence records that *no blood on the bound child* was **refused** — naming a thing to ban it puts the word in front of the filter. The refused block spent three harm words on safety clauses: *nothing is being cut, no skin is broken, and there is no blood, no wound and no mark anywhere in the picture.* **The safety clause is the likeliest trigger, not the moment.** Second suspect: §Damage's known pair, a stated age standing beside a weapon — the refused block ends *He is twelve years old.*

**FOUR CHANGES AND NOTHING ELSE MOVES.** Every harm-negation is deleted rather than softened. The age number is deleted — the anchor governs the face anyway, and every run this session has come back mid-teens regardless of the rung. *A grown man* becomes a hand and a forearm with no person named. And the page's own framing is stated positively: a morning practice, stopped, to show him where his guard was late — which is what the scene is, not a euphemism for it.

**IF IT IS REFUSED AGAIN, THAT IS THE ANSWER AND THE SURGE STANDS.** Two refusals on one moment with the harm words gone would say the boundary is the staging itself, and §Damage gets its line.


**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch21-dead-restated.png`, FIRST RUN OF THE REWORDING (author, s48: *restated worked* · *adopt both*).**

**THE DIAGNOSIS IS SETTLED: THE REFUSAL WAS THE WORDING, NOT THE MOMENT.** A blade held at a boy's throat rendered without complaint once three harm words and a stated age came out of the block. **§Absence's precedent is now proven twice over** — *no blood on the bound child* was refused, and so was *there is no blood, no wound and no mark anywhere in the picture.* **A safety clause is the most expensive place in a block to spend a harm word**, and both refusals this folder has on record are safety clauses.

The face is the block's: eyes wide and clear and fixed, mouth closed, jaw set, everything working and nothing panicking. Mended undyed wool, collar open, no metal on him.

**WHAT DRIFTED, AND THE REWORDING CAUSED IT.** The blade reads as a knife rather than a sword, because *sword* was one of the words stripped and §Nouns says the default rides the noun — *a plain blade* returns a dagger the way *dagger* once returned a longsword. **The chapter's whole first line is that the training swords now have edges on them**, so a knife is the wrong object; the crop hides enough of it to pass, and the author adopted it as drawn. *If it is ever re-cut, the fix is one word — sword blade — and the filter was never objecting to it.* The yard behind came back as pale wash with almost no stone in it; the register carries it.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-nine-things

*Candidate 2.* Anchors: `images/portraits/neris.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 21 canon: *in front of her there were nine things laid out in a row: a copper rod, an iron nail, a length of dry oak, a length of the same oak that had been in a bucket since morning, a dry rope, a wet rope, a bone from the winter's goat, a cup of water from the butt, and a cup of water from the sea.* … *"Sit down," she said. "You are going to spend, and I am going to hold the tow."* … *a wisp of it teased out fine and hung off a splinter at the end of the run, so that whatever arrived at the far end would lift it, and the amount it lifted was the amount that had arrived.* … *"You built me a measure." / "I built you a measure," his mother agreed.*

**THE INSTRUMENT IS THE PICTURE.** A row of ordinary objects, a lamp, and one wisp of fibre standing off a splinter because something invisible has just arrived at the far end of a copper rod — **the only image in the book of the Art being measured rather than used.** `ch11/the-white-stitch` reserved its thread as bare paper and it is the folder's cleanest effect to date; the tow takes the same treatment, which is the one instrument proven for showing a small pale thing that is not painted.

**AND THE ROW GIVES THE TWO FACES SOMETHING TO LOOK AT.** Both sightlines land on the tow, in frame, at the far end of the run — no gaze in this block points at anything the viewer cannot see.

**IT KEEPS AWAY FROM `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` ON THE THING THAT PLATE OWNS**, which is four flat hands on bare boards with nothing between them. Here the boards are covered end to end with nine objects and an instrument, the cast is two rather than three, and the camera is along the row rather than across the table.


**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch21-the-nine-things.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s48: *adopt both*).**

**ALL NINE OBJECTS LANDED, WHICH IS THE BEST RESULT §NOUNS HAS HAD.** Copper rod, iron nail, pale dry oak, dark wet oak, dry rope, wet rope, the goat bone, and two clay cups of water — evenly spaced along the table, each one distinguishable from its neighbour, no substitutions. **Nine concrete nouns in one frame and the tool got all nine.**

**AND THE REST OF IT.** The lamp at the back as the only light; the boy's hand out flat toward the copper and not touching it; **the splinter upright in her right hand with the tow off the top of it** — her hands occupied by the page rather than by a framing dodge. **The first time the book draws her pregnant**, side-on to the table's end, carrying high and in front, exactly as decided.

**WHAT DRIFTED.** The tow reads as an ambiguous curl rather than a fibre that has just lifted: small in frame, and drawn rather than reserved as bare paper, so the measurement is legible only to somebody who knows the scene. **The one instrument that could fix it is the crop** — §The winner and the rival's own limit — and the crop is what makes the row of nine work, so this picture cannot have both. The lamp came back with a tall glass chimney, which reads later than the island's register; `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` carries the same lamp and was adopted, so it is precedent rather than a new fault.

### PLATE: the-surge

*Candidate 3, taken at s48 when candidate 1 came back refused.* Anchors: `images/portraits/vask.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 21 canon: *His father took one of the short blades off the stone and stood up with it. / What came up the steel was not what Kael made. Kael's was a stitch, the finest thing he could manage, laid in and kept quiet and gone within the exchange. This surged. It went up the blade hilt to point and it kept going, the whole length of it running and running, and it did not falter anywhere along the way, and the arm holding it was not doing one thing that Kael could see.* … *"The problem isn't capacity," Kael said. / "No." His father let it run a while longer, and then it was not there.*

**THE PICTURE IS A CONTRAST WITH SOMETHING THAT IS NOT IN IT.** `ch11/the-white-stitch` is the whole of what the son can make: one thin stitch a hand's width across, reserved as bare paper, and the folder's cleanest effect. **This is the same instrument at a hundred times the length** — the entire blade, hilt to point, unbroken and even — and the reader who has seen the stitch does not need the comparison explained.

**THE ARM IS THE OTHER HALF OF THE SUBJECT.** *The arm holding it was not doing one thing that Kael could see.* So the block asks for no effort anywhere: the shoulder low, the arm easy, the face level and empty — the man's own canon, which `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` already got right off this reference.

**AND IT IS STAGED SO THAT NOTHING IS POINTED AT ANYBODY**, which candidate 1 has just made a live concern: the blade stands upright, the boy is seated clear of it, nobody is fighting, and both of them are looking at the steel rather than at each other. That also gives both sightlines an object inside the frame.


**HELD, NOT RUN (s48).** It was composed as the fallback for a refusal that turned out to be a wording fault, and the chapter has its two without it. **The block below is complete and ready** — the first sight of what the father actually has, and the only candidate in the folder that would put `ch11/the-white-stitch`'s instrument at full length.

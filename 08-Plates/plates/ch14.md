### PLATE: both-hands-full

*Candidate 2.* Anchors: `images/portraits/valeria-14.png` and `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`. Ch. 14 canon: *Valeria Vel went to the racks and took two wasters, one for each hand. / The yard made its sound. The off hand was for the Art — every child in the lines had been taught the stance, the open empty hand at the shoulder, the boast the empire fought with. Both her hands were full. It read as ignorance, or as insult, and nothing else about her read as either, so the yard did not know what to do with its own sound, and swallowed it.* And her, from the same chapter: *she was bored. Plainly, evenly bored, the way you are bored by weather you have already had.*

**THIS IS THE CANONICAL PICTURE OF THE TRAIT THE PRODUCTION RULE EXISTS TO PROTECT.** `../README.md`'s silhouette table separates her from Kael on six axes and the load-bearing one is **blades in both hands against a bundle on a back.** Nothing in the library has drawn it yet. **Both hands full is written as a staging instruction rather than as a fact** — both arms hanging clear of the body so each hand and each sword reads separately against the ground behind — because §Name the winner's whole history is facts that were stated and then staged out of the picture.

**THE VALUE STRUCTURE IS BUILT, NOT HOPED FOR.** `ch10/the-waterer` failed to demote a background in flat open daylight and `ch13/the-receipts` held one in flat light because mist gave the rival somewhere to go. **Here the yard supplies it directly:** she stands at the wall in the colonnade's shade with the bright open yard to her left, so the light comes across her face and the dark wall stands behind her head. **The bright yard reserves as bare paper on one side and the shaded wall takes the demotion on the other** — and her two occupied hands read against the light half.

**THE RACK IS THE ONE PLACE A NEGATIVE WOULD HAVE BEEN WRONG.** §Hand inventory bans stray blades by name, and this frame has a rack of wooden swords in it on purpose. The block states ownership positively instead — two in her hands, the rest standing in the rack — because *no other blade anywhere in the frame* would contradict the setting.

**HER BOREDOM IS THE EXPRESSION AND HER SHEET FORBIDS TWO OF ITS NEIGHBOURS.** `../canon-brief.md`: *unimpressed and faintly tired*, and **the tiredness is hers and is not banned** — Elarine owns assessment-in-progress and cannot have this one. The negations go to the neighbourhoods a renderer reaches for when told a child is unimpressed: sneering, hostile, amused, proud.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch14-both-hands-full.png`, first run (author, s45: *adopt*).**

**THE TWO-REFERENCE TEST PASSED, AND IT PASSED ON BOTH QUESTIONS.** One girl arrived, not two — **the doubling risk this block was written around did not materialise** — and **the costume repeated.** The run reproduces `valeria-14-full.png` down to its parts: the sleeveless dark over-tunic over grey sleeves, the steel vambraces on both forearms, the wide buckled belt, the split skirt. **No plate in the folder has ever repeated a costume**, because §Plates has every plate anchored on a head-and-shoulders portrait with everything below the collar authored in words and anchored to nothing. **Valeria's grey now has a body that will come back next time**, and the instrument is one extra `Image N:` line.

**AND THE COST IS THE CROP, WHICH IS A REAL TRADE AND NOT A FAULT.** The block said **waist-up** and the run came back at the knee. **The full-figure sheet appears to pull the framing wider along with the costume** — one run, so it is a hypothesis, and the confound is honest: the block also named a wall, a rack and an open yard, which is a wide setting to describe.

**WHAT THE WIDTH COST IS HER EYES.** `../canon-brief.md` says her green is *spent at reading distance only, so it lands in close framing*, and this is no longer close framing: the green went pale and grey at that size. **The reference is not at fault and was checked before this was written** — `images/portraits/valeria-14.png` carries a clear green at portrait scale. **So the sheet and her one colour beat compete for the same picture**, and a plate that needs both will have to fight for the crop in words the setting does not undercut.

**WHAT THE WIDTH BOUGHT IS THE TRAIT.** Both arms hanging clear, each sword separately legible against the ground behind it, the whole silhouette in frame — **and `../README.md`'s separation table is a thumbnail test.** This reads at thumbnail. The wider crop served the subject of this particular plate even while it cost the eyes, which is why it is adopted rather than re-run.

**THE REST OF THE STAGING HELD.** Points down, low and loose, not crossed; the rack standing behind her shoulder with the other wooden swords in it and no stray blade anywhere; the ranks of children pale and small at the left edge; her gaze out across the yard past the frame and **not at the viewer**, which is the fourth block running. The bright yard reserved as bare paper on one side and the shaded wall taking the demotion on the other — **the built value structure worked exactly as designed**, and the s44 rule that a rival needs somewhere to go now has three runs behind it.

**SHE READS MID-TO-LATE TEENS.** Seventh run under the anchor ruling and consistent with all of them. Nothing new; `../scene-prompt-system.md` carries the state of that question.

```
Image 1: valeria-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.
Image 2: valeria-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the same girl.

Setting: the edge of an open flagstone training yard on a bright
spring afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a stone wall directly
behind her in shade, the upright posts of a weapon rack close behind
her shoulder, and the open yard beyond her to her left running away
pale and bright. Soft open daylight coming across her from the left
out of that bright yard, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: waist-up, one girl standing at the racks, turned three
quarters toward the viewer, filling the frame. The rack of plain
wooden practice swords stands upright behind her shoulder. Behind her
and much further off, at the edges of the frame, the lines of children
stand three deep as loose dark shapes.

THE TWO REFERENCE IMAGES ARE THE SAME PERSON AND THERE IS ONE GIRL IN
THIS PICTURE.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her right hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword and nothing else. Her left hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword and nothing else. Both are carried low and loose at her
sides with the points down, not raised, not crossed and not touching
each other. Two hands, two swords, and the only other wooden swords in
the picture are the ones standing in the rack behind her.

BOTH HER HANDS BEING FULL IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE, AND THE POSE
SHOWS IT: both arms hang clear of her body, so that each hand and each
sword is separately visible against the ground behind it and neither
is lost against her clothing.

WHERE SHE IS LOOKING: out across the yard, level, past the left edge
of the frame, at nothing in particular. Not at the viewer and not at
the swords in her hands.

SHE IS BORED AND IT IS NOT A POSE. Even and unhurried, the mouth
closed, the brows unmoved, the eyes level and unimpressed, faintly
tired — bored by something she has seen many times already. NOT
sneering, NOT hostile, NOT amused, NOT proud, and not blank. She is
completely still, and the stillness is not restful.

SHE IS TWELVE: the eyes sit a little below the vertical midpoint of
the head, nearer that midpoint than a young child's and still below
it, and the jaw has not come in.

Black hair well past the shoulders, worn loose, with no ornament, no
band and nothing tied in it. Eyes a clear hard green, deep and cold,
the one saturated colour in the picture. Plain grey cloth, close,
exactly made and austere, with no badge, no house colour and no
decoration anywhere on it. A lean working build with nothing spare on
it anywhere, the shoulders and the forearms plainly worked, and
knuckles older than the face.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: her skin is the only
warm colour anywhere, and the wall, the rack, the wooden swords, the
flagstones and all the clothing in the picture are cool grey and
grey-blue with no brown in them.

HER FACE AND HER TWO OCCUPIED HANDS ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN
THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The rack,
the wall and the standing children stay at quiet middle values with
soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there competes for finish.
The bright open yard to her left is reserved as bare unpainted paper.
The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and does not
blur into the wash.
```

### PLATE: one-finger

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`. Ch. 14 canon: *he was on his back in the drawn measure with her point one finger from his face. Exactly one finger. Not touching. The point did not waver, and neither did anything behind it. / She had all of it in hand. Every grain.* And what he is doing on the ground: *he lay looking up her blade at the awake eyes.* Her eyes, spent in this chapter and only at this distance: *a clear, hard green, the green of deep water over rock.*

**THE EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT AND THE SYSTEM HAS A PROVEN FORM FOR IT.** §Getting expression: gaze alignment between two figures is unreliable and **putting it in capitals as its own paragraph worked** — *his eyes up, hers down, the lines of sight meeting. This eye contact is the subject of the image.* That paragraph is reused here almost verbatim, because it is the one instruction in the folder written for exactly this geometry. **Only one thing may be declared the subject**, so the stopped point is staged in full and never claims the word.

**THE LOW ANGLE IS WRITTEN AS SURFACES, NOT AS A CAMERA.** `ch04/the-master-arrives` failed twice at *the viewer is low, at the eye height of a boy of nine* and was fixed by naming what the angle exposes. So: the underside of her jaw and chin visible, the flagstones filling the bottom of the frame and running away behind his head, her near arm reaching down the frame.

**THE GAP IS THE PICTURE AND IT IS GIVEN A MEASUREMENT.** A clear width of air between the blunt wooden tip and his cheek, one finger wide, with nothing in the arm behind it moving. *It has been stopped, not checked* is the difference the page spends a paragraph on, and it is stated as stillness in the wrist, the elbow and the shoulder rather than as an adverb.

**NEITHER FACE IS DOING WHAT A YARD WOULD EXPECT, AND BOTH NEGATIONS ARE THERE FOR THAT.** He is beaten and still counting — not frightened, not pleading, not dazed. She has stopped a killing stroke and is not collecting anything for it — not angry, not triumphant, not gloating, **and not bored**, which is the one time her canonical affect has to be banned: the page says the boredom is gone, and this is the moment it went.

**REFUSAL RISK, NAMED IN ADVANCE.** §Some moments are undrawable records one refusal and it fell on a child in a yard. **This frame is a weapon point held at a child's face**, and although the weapon is wooden, blunt and rounded and the whole beat is that it stopped, a classifier reads nouns. **If it refuses, the first two things to cut are the thin red mark on his jaw and the word for the yard's drawn measure**, and if it still refuses the moment is not reachable and `candidates.md`'s third — the blood, and the refusal — is not a safer ask. **Do not iterate on a refusal**; the alternate that survives the crop test is *the name entered*, two faces and no weapon at all.

**CUT AFTER THE AUTHOR READ IT (s45), BEFORE IT RAN: *one-finger prompt looks very bloated.*** The block went **734 words to 515** — the paste-ready file, which carries the house header too, 816 to 598, **the leanest plate prompt in the folder's last four chapters**, where the adopted ones run 637 to 820 — and **no channel was traded away**: the low angle as surfaces, the hand inventory, the measured gap, the eye-contact paragraph, both expressions, both colourings with ownership stated, the warm/cool, the finish hierarchy, the reserve, the ink and the preserve line are all still in it. **What came out was words, not instructions** — a composition that said where the camera was three ways, a stillness clause listing the wrist, the elbow and the shoulder before saying the arm was still anyway, five nouns where three carry a palette, and two expression lists a term longer than they needed to be.

**AND THE AGE RUNG CAME OUT ON THE EVIDENCE, WHICH IS THE ONE REAL CHANGE RATHER THAN A TRIM.** §A close-up plate and the age line has seven runs now and **the rung has never once moved a face on its own**; the anchor gap here is two years; and the author has ruled six times that a face reading above its chapter is adopted anyway. **Thirty words that seven runs say buy nothing are the first thing that should go when a block is too long.** Flagged rather than assumed: if a face comes back badly wrong, the rung is one line to put back.

**AND THE PRE-FLIGHT PASSED IT CLEAN, WHICH IS THE POINT OF §The pre-flight is a script now.** *Not sure a script is the way to do it — I'd like you to just be better at knowing what a good prompt is.* The linter reported no restatement, no abstraction and a caps count inside range, **on a block twice the length it needed to be**, because bloat by paragraph is not bloat the script can see. **Second session running that the author has had to cut this folder's own habit out of a block by hand** — after s44 took 160 words out of the Ch. 12 pair. The rule was never the gap; applying it while writing is.

**KAEL'S OWN WASTER IS ON THE STONES OUT OF REACH**, because §Describe a limb as a chain records a run that lost its causality when a held object and a grip were entered as two facts. The page has the twelfth exchange take it out of his hand entirely; the picture says where it went.

```

Image 2: valeria-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.

Setting: an open flagstone training yard on a bright spring afternoon,
as loose wash suggestion, a stone colonnade behind. Flat open
daylight, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: the viewer is down at flagstone height beside the boy's
head. He lies on his back in the near foreground, head and
shoulders filling the lower half of the frame. The girl stands over
him beyond his head, seen from below with the underside of her jaw
visible, her near arm reaching down the frame. The flagstones
fill the bottom of the frame and run away behind his head.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her right hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword with a blunt rounded tip, held down so the tip stops
beside his cheek, touching nothing. Her left hand is out of frame. His
two hands lie open and empty on the stones, and his own wooden sword
lies out of reach at the edge of the frame.

THE TIP IS STOPPED SHORT OF HIS FACE: a clear gap of air one finger
wide between the wood and his cheek, the arm behind it still. Nothing in the picture is moving.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. His eyes up, hers down,
the lines of sight meeting past the wooden tip. This eye contact is
the subject of the image. Neither is looking at the viewer.

THE BOY is beaten and still counting: on his back, breathing, mouth
closed, jaw set, eyes level and working. NOT frightened,
NOT pleading, NOT dazed. One thin red mark along his jaw.

THE GIRL has decided nothing: standing easy, mouth closed, brows
unmoved, the whole attention aimed down at
him. NOT angry, NOT triumphant, NOT bored.

HIS eyes are near-white neutral grey, the lightest value in his face;
dark hair cut short and uneven; plain undyed working wool, coarse and
much mended, the ink carrying it at the seams.

HERS are a clear hard green, deep and cold; black hair well past the
shoulders, loose and hanging forward as she looks down; plain grey
cloth, close, exactly made, no badge and no decoration. The grey is
hers alone and the mended wool his.

Their skin is the only warm colour in the picture; the stone, the
wood, the watching children and all the clothing are cool grey and
grey-blue with no brown in them.

THE TWO FACES AND THE WOODEN TIP BETWEEN THEM ARE THE MOST FINISHED
THINGS IN THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark.
The children watching from the edges are dark summary shapes with no
features, and the colonnade stays at quiet middle values with no
detail. The lit faces
of the flagstones are reserved as bare unpainted paper. The ink
linework stays the strongest element and does not blur into the wash.

Preserve both children exactly as shown in their references — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features or mix
their clothing.
```

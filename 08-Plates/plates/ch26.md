# Ch. 26 — The Third Bell · plate sheet

**Composed at s51 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen; Elarine thirteen. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/elarine-14.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE BOY FINDING OUT WHAT HE IS, AND THE GIRL TELLING HIM WHAT HE DID.** *It was the face of a boy doing the only thing in the world he had never had to be taught to want* — and, twenty minutes later, against the timber of the eastern gate, *"You were not kind to me." / "No." / "Good."* Nothing between them is drawn.

**AND THAT IS THE DESIGN CONSTRAINT, NOT SQUEAMISHNESS.** Most of this chapter is two thirteen-year-olds cutting and burning each other, and `ch21/dead` was refused with far less in the frame — nothing cut, no blood, the edge stopped clear, the adult out of frame from the elbow up. What that refusal exposed is that **the boundary is a weapon held against a child, whatever the weapon is doing.** So neither block puts a blade in frame at all: the first crops above the hands by name, and the second is after, with both swords on the ground.

**WHAT DAMAGE IS DRAWN IS DRAWN IN THE WORDS THAT PASS.** §Injury, as amended at s48 off `ch23/the-horizon`: `slash` carried where `open cut` was refused, `red` where `blood` was. So the smear on his teeth is **red**, and the mark on his face is named as singeing and reddened skin, never as tissue. **Elarine needs none of it** — Severin mends her on the sand inside the chapter, so by the gate her clothing is torn and dark-stained and the skin under it is whole, which is both the page and the safest thing to draw.

**THE BURN IS DRAWN FOR THE FIRST TIME AND IT IS THIS CHAPTER'S PROPERTY.** `03/kael`: *the Ch. 26 burn — the first mark on him he did not spend himself — wrist to elbow on the LEFT forearm*, lying over the blade scar. Severin mends it inside the chapter, so at the gate it is *a long pink new thing that would be a scar by the turn of the year* — pink, new and unbound, with the sleeve gone. **`../canon-brief.md` had this at Ch. 24 and was corrected against `03` at s51**; `ch24/turn-him-toward-the-lamp` drew it two chapters early and stands unless the author rules otherwise.

**BOTH BLOCKS AUTHOR THE HAIR**, per the s51 finding: `ch25/four-seconds` run 1 lost Kael's hair to the reference and got it back only when the block wrote it. A reference that drops a feature once is authored in every block after.

**AND BOTH FACES ARE WRITTEN AS STATES THE PICTURE CAN HOLD**, per the other s51 finding — `ch25/both-hands` run 1 came back dead because the working part it named was a movement. Neither block here asks a face to perform an action.

**WATCH THE CROP.** Five recorded instances of a named landmark coming back a notch loose. Both blocks name it tighter than the line they want.

### PLATE: the-good-part

*Candidate 3.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 26 canon: *And Elarine, backing off her lead foot and getting her point into line for a boy who was already inside where a longsword works, saw his face. / She would think about it for a long time and never once say it to him. / He was smiling. / Not a fighter's grin, and nothing to do with her, and nothing in it that was cruel about anybody. It was the face of a boy doing the only thing in the world he had never had to be taught to want. There was blood on his teeth from somewhere in the last two minutes and he did not know it was there, and he did not know his mouth was doing anything at all.* And the state he is in: *he turned his head and lost his eyebrow and some of his hair and kept coming.*

**THE VIEWER STANDS WHERE ELARINE STANDS, AND THAT SOLVES THE GAZE.** `ch18` lost two runs to a sightline whose object sat outside the frame, and its finding was that **the tool places the object and the head independently** — no wording couples them. Here the object of his look is the person the picture is composed from, so his eyes come straight out of the picture and the instruction is *at the viewer* rather than a ban. **It also makes the reader be the girl he is coming at**, which is the sentence the page actually wrote: *saw his face.*

**THE CROP IS THE INSTRUMENT AND IT IS SPENT ON THE WEAPONS.** §The cut, named as a landmark with what it removes: the bottom edge crosses him below the collarbone, so no arms, no hands and no blades are anywhere in the frame. It costs the beat its *short blade in each hand* — and it buys the only thing that matters here, which is that this is a picture of a face and not a picture of an armed child.

**THE HARD PART IS THAT HE DOES NOT KNOW HE IS DOING IT.** Unawareness is not a feature and cannot be drawn; what can be drawn is a face with nothing arranged on it — the mouth doing the whole of the work and the brows, the chin and the carriage taking no part. The negations go on the archetype the frame pulls toward, per §Absence: a bloodied boy mid-fight pulls hard at a snarl and at triumph, and at nothing else.

Setting: the sand floor of a great arena on a bright morning, as loose

One eyebrow is gone and the hair above it on that side is singed short

pale sand dust across the face and in the creases of the neck.

His hair is dark and straight, hacked short and uneven, falling over

much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, torn open at the
shoulder, with no ornament and no metal anywhere on him.

Preserve him exactly as shown in the reference image — face, hair,
colouring, apparent age. His eyes are pale near-white grey.

### PLATE: not-kind-to-me

*Candidate 5, and the chapter's last exchange.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`. Ch. 26 canon: *They got Elarine up at the eastern gate, sitting, her back against the timber. Somebody had brought water. She had refused to be carried. / Kael came and stood in front of her because there was nowhere else he was going. / Somebody had put her sword down beside her, and she had not looked at it once. Her hands lay in her lap with the palms turned up, the way you rest hands that have been doing one thing for a long time. She looked up at him for a while without saying anything, and there was something happening in her face that he could see with total clarity and could not read at all. / "You were not kind to me," she said. / "No." / "Good."* And her state: *her hair had come out of its binding down one side* · *His arm was a long pink new thing.*

**HER HANDS ARE THE PICTURE'S QUIET CENTRE AND THE PAGE HANDS THEM OVER.** *Palms turned up, the way you rest hands that have been doing one thing for a long time* — a Resonant's hands, both of them, empty and open and facing up, at the end of a morning in which she spent everything that was ever in them. She is the girl whose doctrine is that one hand stays free for the Art, and both of them are lying in her lap doing nothing.

**AND THE SWORD IS IN FRAME BECAUSE SHE IS NOT LOOKING AT IT.** *Somebody had put her sword down beside her, and she had not looked at it once.* A gaze can be drawn by where it is not, so long as the thing it avoids is in the picture — which is the one condition `ch18` proved the folder cannot meet with wording alone unless the object is placed where the frame can compare them. Here it lies on the sand at her hip, well inside the frame, and her eyes are on him.

**HER FACE IS THE HARDEST THING IN THIS PAIR.** *There was something happening in her face that he could see with total clarity and could not read at all.* An unreadable face is not a blank one, and the folder has a name for the state already — `../canon-brief.md` has her at *assessment in progress: attention fully engaged, giving nothing back, no verdict reached.* Written as anatomy: the lids fully raised, the gaze unbroken on him, the brows unmoved, the mouth closed and soft. Everything engaged and nothing resolved.

**HIS IS THE CONTRADICTION, STATED AS ONE** per §Gaze: he is standing over somebody he has just taken apart, and there is no victory anywhere in him.

**FLAT DAYLIGHT MEANS NO DEMOTION** — §The winner and the rival's own limit is that in flat light with no dark to recede into, the instrument is the crop. So the hands earn their size from the framing and the block carries no value-structure clause, which is what `ch16/the-ear-on-the-brick` found when that paragraph came out of it.

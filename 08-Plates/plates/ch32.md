# Ch. 32 — The Mountain · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen; Neris and Vask are unpinned. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` + `images/portraits/neris.png` on the first, `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE TWO THINGS HE IS COUNTING AT ONCE.** *both of the things he was counting were getting closer, at the same time, on purpose, and that he was the only person on that mountain holding both numbers.* One picture is what it costs inside the house; the other is what is coming up the mountain at it. **They are the same night, forty minutes apart.**

**THE SECOND PLATE IS THE DELIBERATE INVERSE OF THE BOOK'S FIRST ONE.** `ch01/fallen-stars` is this mountain at night with the town in a heap of warm light far below and a boy being turned to look at it. **This is the same boy on the same ground with the lights coming up at him**, and the chapter says so itself: *He had stood up here at four years old and looked down at the lights in their corner and been certain they were something else entirely. / There were lights on the mountain.* The folder's set now opens and closes Book One on the same view.

**THE DRAIN-MARK IS DRAWN ONCE AND IT IS NOT STYLING.** `../canon-brief.md`'s standing fence — white hair is never whitened for drama, and the drain register is **pure quartz white, cold and hard**, never Aeliana's warm silver — is what the first block is built to satisfy. The page gives the mark a direction and a rate, so the block does too: *It came up out of the roots and ran down the length of the piece the way frost runs across the top of a pail.* **One piece of hair, white from the root, and every other hair on her head still dark** — the second piece is a page later and the block does not anticipate it.

**AND THE PREGNANCY IS OUT OF THE FRAME BY THE CROP, WHICH IS THE STANDING RULING** (author, s51). She is kneeling over her son with hours to go; the bottom edge crosses her above the collarbone, so nothing of her below it is authored or drawable, and what the frame holds of her is her face, her hair and **her hands, which is where the book keeps her**. Posture, cloth and crop carry it, exactly as `README.md` prescribes.

**THE FAMILY IS THE CROP.** Her two hands are on his chest; his father's two hands are on his shoulders and his father's face is out of the frame. *His father's hands did not tighten and did not let go.* **Vask is in that picture as two hands and nothing else**, which is what he is in that scene and very nearly what he is in the book.

**THE MENDING SHOWS, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE POINT OF DRAWING IT.** `ch26`'s Severin does it with nothing showing — *nothing had shown. No light. No sound. It had been like watching a man decide something.* — and this is written against that: *This showed.* Not as light and not as effect; as **sweat along a hairline, breath through the teeth, hands shaking on him, and the colour leaving one piece of hair.** No glow is written anywhere in the block, so nothing is put in front of the filter.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **the two unmatched short blades put down beside his hand** is the chapter's quietest beat and it is a second hand-and-object plate in three chapters. **The briefing in the yard** — a boy of thirteen giving ground to a captain of the royal guard, pointing with a short blade — is the chapter's other great picture and it wants five resolved figures, two of whom have no portrait. **The princess in the yard**, in ruined shoes with a soldier's cloak over a gold-hemmed gown, looking at the cloud and not at the house, is legal and good and it is the only one of the three that survives entirely on `candidates.md` with its framing intact.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-white-in-her-hair

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch32-the-white-in-her-hair.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53). THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S OPTIMISED VERSION OF THIS SHEET'S**, stored exactly as run.

**THEIR ONE STRUCTURAL CHANGE FIXES A COMPOSITION THAT COULD NOT HAVE WORKED.** Mine said *THE BOTTOM EDGE OF THE PICTURE CROSSES HER JUST ABOVE THE COLLARBONE* — but she is leaning down over him from above, so a bottom edge cannot cross her at all; her body would have to be cut by the top of the frame or occluded by her own arms. Theirs states it as what is in the picture instead: **Nothing of her torso or clothing is in the frame; her arms enter from the top edge.** The pregnancy ruling is satisfied by composition rather than by a landmark, and it landed exactly — **there is no part of her below the neck in that picture except two bare forearms.**

**THE WHITE PIECE IS THE PICTURE AND IT TAKES NONE OF THE FIRELIGHT.** Root to end, hanging clear in the air between the two faces, hard cold white against an orange room — with every other hair on her head dark brown and nothing else white anywhere in the frame. **The drain-mark's first appearance in the set, and the fence held on the first run.**

**AND THE SIX HANDS ARE FOUR, WHICH CONFIRMS §HANDS RATHER THAN BREAKING IT.** The block names four hands one at a time — her two flat on his chest, a man's two on his shoulders — and then states the total as *six hands in the picture*, which is a slip; the boy's own are excluded two sentences earlier. **The picture drew the four that were described and ignored the total.** §Hands was written off *three blades total* failing twice while one line per hand held; this is the same result from the other direction, and it says the per-item lines are what the tool reads and the count line is only a check on the writer.

**THE REST AS WRITTEN**: the fire close at the left as the only light, the two faces near and both finished, her lids lowered with the eyes on her own hands, his mouth open on one word, the fleece, the dust and dried red on his chest, and the man in the picture as two hands and nothing else.

**WHAT DRIFTED, one:** his eyes are up and past the hanging piece rather than fixed on it. **The gaze had a target inside the frame and still went wide** — which is the first exception to the pattern the other three plates set today, and the difference here is that the target is an inch from his own cheek: there is no head-turn available to point at a thing that close, so the block asked the eyes to do it alone.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-lights-on-the-mountain

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch32-the-lights-on-the-mountain.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53).**

**THE THREE-PLACE COLOUR INSTRUCTION IS THE BEST PALETTE DISCIPLINE OF THE SESSION.** *The picture is nearly black and its colour is entirely in three places: the orange under the cloud, the doorway of the house, and the lights on the slope.* All three are there and there is nothing else warm anywhere in the frame. **Naming where the colour lives, positively and by count, does what a ban never has** — which is the s42/s45 ruling arriving as a technique rather than a prohibition.

**AND THE RHYME IS NOW ON THE PAGE.** This is `ch01/fallen-stars` inverted, as designed: the same boy on the same mountain at night, the lit doorway of the same house behind him, and the lights coming up the slope at him instead of lying in a heap on the water below. **The set can now show the two side by side.**

**THE LIT DOORWAY IS THE PICTURE'S QUIET WIN.** One patch of warm orange thrown out across the stones of the yard, with the house otherwise dark — and it is the only thing in the frame that belongs to him.

**WHAT DRIFTED, and it is a real limit rather than a miss: THE COUNTS ARE NOT COUNTABLE AT THIS SCALE.** Two strings of carried lights arrived at two depths along the slope, exactly as arranged, and neither is seven or nineteen. `ch27/the-sky` carried four burning objects at four depths *and* the number, because each was as big in the frame as a cart; here each light was specified as *no bigger than a pinhead*. **So §Hands' one-line-per-item instrument buys the arrangement at any scale and the number only while the items are large enough to be seen as items.** Recorded; nothing in the picture is worse for it, because a reader counts the strings and not the flames.

*Candidate 7, ★.* Anchor: `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 32 canon: *Kael went up onto the woodpile. / From the top of it you could see the whole of the lower ground. / He had stood up here his whole life. He had stood up here at four years old and looked down at the lights in their corner and been certain they were something else entirely. / There were lights on the mountain. / They were below the second bend and off to the east of it. Four of them.* … *Then there were seven. / Then, further down and further east, on the ground above the runnel where the olive line ran, there was a string of them coming along the terrace one behind another, and he counted that string twice and got nineteen both times.* … *there is something down at the bottom of the path with a lot of light on it that I have not been able to count yet.* And the sky over it: *the cloud had come down onto the top of the mountain and stayed there, and the underside of it was orange from one end of the island to the other, and it moved the way water moves.*

**EVERY LIGHT IS COUNTED WHERE IT SITS.** `ch27/the-sky` proved that §Hands' one-line-per-item generalises off hands and carried four burning objects at four depths on a single run. This picture is that instrument at its limit: **seven points in one group, nineteen in a line, one mass at the bottom**, each with its own place in the frame and the count stated after it.

**AND THE COUNT IS THE CHARACTER.** He counted the string twice and got nineteen both times. A picture of a boy on a woodpile is a picture of nothing; a picture of a boy on a woodpile **with a countable number of lights in it** is the whole of what he is, and the reader can do the count the block did.

**NO FACE, PRICED** (s43). He is small, high in his own yard, with his back to the viewer — the same price `ch22/kael-of-the-island` and `ch27/the-sky` both paid and both recorded as the picture's power. §Plates' two bans are written out.

**THE ONE WARM THING NEAR HIM IS HIS OWN DOOR.** *A fire going too big in it and light coming out of the doorway onto the stones.* So the house is behind and below him with a lit doorway on the yard, and everything else warm in the picture is a long way off and coming.

# Ch. 35 — The Range · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen; Wren is a day old. Anchors: `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the first, `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE WHOLE CHAPTER AT ITS TWO DISTANCES: THE ROOM, AND THE BOY IN IT.** One picture is eight people lying on the boards of a boat inside a white room made of steam; the other is the boy kneeling up in it with a day-old child on his shoulder, calling a man's feet. **Neither has a fireball arriving in it**, because what this chapter is actually about is what the sea does afterwards and what a boy does with his mouth.

**THE WHITE ROOM IS THE BEST THING IN BOOK ONE THAT NOBODY HAS DRAWN.** *Every one of them his mother put down went into the sea within thirty strides of the boat, and every one of them took a piece of the sea up with it when it went, and the steam stood along both sides of that hull in walls higher than the mast, and the light came through the walls from underneath and moved, so that eight people lay on the boards of a boat inside a white room with the walls of it going up and going out and going up again.* **The page has already composed it** — walls, ceiling, floor, and where the light comes from — and the block's whole job is to keep it a room and not a fog.

**THE LIGHT IS UNDER THE WATER AND THAT IS THE INSTRUMENT.** Not a flame in the frame; the sea lit from beneath on both sides, throwing everything upward. §Light wants a named source told where it is, that it is the only one, and what it leaves dark, and this one is written that way: **nothing above the top of the steam is lit at all**, so the sky over the boat is black and the room has no roof.

**THE MAST IS STILL STANDING AND THAT DATES THE PICTURE.** The sail goes later in the chapter — *the canvas went all at once, top to bottom* — so a plate with a mast in it is the earlier half of the run and a plate without one is the later half. The block says the sail is whole, because the moment is worth pinning.

**NOBODY'S FACE IS RESOLVED IN THE FIRST PLATE AND THE SECOND ONE SPENDS EVERYTHING ON ONE.** That is the pairing: `ch27/the-sky` and `ch22/kael-of-the-island` are the folder's evidence that a small unresolved figure carries more than a rendered one when the scale is the subject, and `ch27/i-need-you` is the evidence for the opposite when the face is.

**THE SECOND PLATE IS THE FOLDER'S FIRST PICTURE OF THE TWO BROTHERS.** *He put his brother up onto his shoulder with his left hand flat on the small of the back, which was where the child had been the whole time on his mother's chest, and got his right forearm onto the strake, and looked at the island.* And what he does from there is give his mother numbers over the top of a screaming newborn. **It is the first thing Kael has ever done for somebody that was not for himself**, and no clause in the block says so.

**THE LINEN STAYS ON AND THE EYE IS NOT SPENT HERE.** The child's face is turned into the boy's neck, which the page puts there, and the bound strip is drawn and nothing under it is.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **the mother in the bow**, alone against a shore throwing fire, is `ch34/the-bowl` again with water in front of her instead of stubble, and the page has already ruled she is faceless in both. **The mast coming down** and **the burning sail going over the side** are the chapter's only two-handed physical beats and both belong to `candidates.md`. **Watching the man who killed his father get smaller** is the last image of the chapter and it wants a face the folder does not own — the small neat man has no portrait, and `ch13.md` is clear that the work which unblocks that is a portrait, not a plate.

### PLATE: the-white-room

*Candidate 1, ★.* Anchor: `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 35 canon: *The sea on both sides of the hull was going up.* … *the steam stood along both sides of that hull in walls higher than the mast, and the light came through the walls from underneath and moved, so that eight people lay on the boards of a boat inside a white room with the walls of it going up and going out and going up again.* … *His mother stood in the bow with her arms going and the whole of the shore coming at her, and she was not choosing any more.*

**IT IS AN INTERIOR AND IT IS MADE OF WEATHER.** The block writes it as a room on purpose — two walls, a floor of wet boards, an open black top — because *a boat in fog* is what the tool will give if the walls are not given a height, a face and a distance. **The height is taken off the mast**, which is the one measure in the frame.

**AND THE STRATIFIED-WEATHER LESSON FROM `ch27/the-sky` IS APPLIED.** That run named two fog layers and rendered only the one with something to sit against. So here the steam is given walls that stand on the sea and rise past the mast — **something to sit against at every point** — and no free-floating layer is asked for anywhere.

```
Image 1: kael-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the boy, for build
and clothing. No face is resolved in this picture.

Setting: the open deck of a small dark wooden boat at night, seen from
the stern looking forward along it. Wet planking runs away from the
bottom edge of the frame; low thwarts cross it; a single mast stands up
out of the middle with its sail set and whole. Beyond the bow there is
black water and black sky and nothing else.

ON BOTH SIDES OF THE BOAT, STANDING ON THE SEA, ARE TWO WALLS OF WHITE
STEAM. They begin at the water a little way out from each side of the
hull and go straight up past the height of the mast top, and they lean
outward as they rise. They are dense, and they have edges, and they
enclose the boat completely on the left and on the right. Between them,
directly above the boat, the sky is open and completely black. There is
no fog on the deck itself and no mist between the figures.

THE LIGHT IS UNDER THE WATER AND THERE IS NO OTHER LIGHT. It comes up
through the sea on both sides — a moving orange-white glow below the
surface, brightest close to the hull — and it lights the two steam walls
from beneath and from inside, so that they are bright at the bottom and
go dim and cold toward the top. It puts a hard rim along the top edge of
each thwart, along the near side of the mast and along the wet planking.
Nothing above the top of the steam is lit at all. There is no fire
anywhere in the frame.

Composition: the picture is taken from low over the stern boards, so the
deck runs away at eye level. SEVEN FIGURES ARE LYING FLAT ON THE BOARDS
between the thwarts, spread down the length of the boat, each of them
down on their front or their side with the head low and turned away. No
face is drawn on any of them and no eyes are visible anywhere in the
picture. Do not turn any head toward the viewer.

ONE SMALL FIGURE STANDS ALONE IN THE BOW, right at the far end of the
boat, seen from behind, both arms up and out. She is the only person
standing, she is small in the frame, and her face is not visible.

The two steam walls are the brightest thing in the picture and the
figures on the boards are the darkest; the mast, the sail and the
thwarts sit between them at quiet middle values with soft edges. The
colour is entirely in the water and the low steam — orange going to
white going to cold grey — and everything else is near-black.

The boat is plain, tarred, working, undecorated, with no paint and no
carving anywhere on it.
```

### PLATE: the-spotter

*Candidate 2, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 35 canon: *He put his brother up onto his shoulder with his left hand flat on the small of the back, which was where the child had been the whole time on his mother's chest, and got his right forearm onto the strake, and looked at the island.* … *"MOTHER!" Kael said. "LEFT OF THE MAST. HIGH AND SLOW. IT IS LONG — IT GOES OVER—"* … *"He goes to your left every second one! He loads the next before that one lands! Watch his feet, his feet go first!"* … *The child was screaming. He had started somewhere in the middle of the second one and Kael had not heard him begin. He was screaming with his whole self, thin and furious and going up at the end and not stopping to breathe when a child that size ought to have to, with his bound face turned into Kael's neck and his fists shut, and there was nothing to be done about any of it, and Kael went on calling the man's feet over the top of it.*

**A MOUTH WIDE OPEN IS THE WHOLE FACE, AND IT IS NOT FEAR.** §Gaze wants the mouth pointed at rather than the abstraction: this one is open on a shouted word, the brows drawn, the eyes narrowed against heat and fixed on something far off to one side. **Nothing in the block calls it panic**, because the page is explicit that the part of him doing this is the part that works.

**AND THE CHILD IS DRAWN SCREAMING, WHICH IS A SECOND FACE DOING SOMETHING.** Fists shut, the mouth square, the bound strip across the eyes, the face turned in against the neck. **Two open mouths and neither of them is heard**, which is as close as a picture gets to the sound in that chapter.

**THE LIGHT IS OFF THE FRAME AND HOT.** A fireball is in the air somewhere to the left and out of the picture; it lights the near side of his face, the wet strake and the child's cloth hard and orange from that side and leaves the rest of the boat dark. §Light: a warm source lights a face when it is close, and forty strides of open water is close enough to strip the shadow off a cheekbone.

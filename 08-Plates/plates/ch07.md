### PLATE: kael-of-what

*Candidate 2, and `candidates.md` says to plant it deliberately.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`, `images/portraits/severin.png`. Ch. 7 canon: *The master was waiting at the split rock. He stood on the near side of it, on the path, leaning on a stick he did not need for leaning, and he watched Kael come down through the pens the way he watched everything, unhurried, and he did not say good morning. He said, "Who are you?" / "Kael," Kael said. / "Kael of what?" / "Of—" and there was nothing to put there. He had never needed anything to put there.*

**RUN 1 (s41).** **The beat landed exactly** — his mouth open on a word that is not coming, eyes still up and steady on the man's, chin not dropped. A stop and not a feeling, which was the hard part. Eye contact, the scale (his head at the man's chest), the stick with its tip down and no weight on it, the hand on the bag strap, the flat cold dawn with the sea in pale wash and a bare-paper sky, the grey through the whole head with no seam — all held.

**THE ROCK CAME BACK NATURAL, AND THE SPLIT DID NOT COME BACK AT ALL.** `ch06.md`'s corrected wording killed the menhir outright: irregular, weathered, broader at the base, no straight edges, nothing shaped by hands. **But the fracture that gives the rock its name is not legible in the image.** Fifth confirmation of §Name a thing by construction, and a new failure beside it:

**A CORRECTION CAN BURY THE THING IT WAS CORRECTING FOR.** The old wording said *split top to bottom by a single clean cleft* and got a menhir with a cleft. The new wording spends five clauses on not-being-a-monument and gives the fracture one, sixth in the list — so the renderer built a boulder and never got to the split. **The defining feature has to be said first and given a measurement**, with the shape corrections after it: *a boulder the height of a man, split from top to bottom by a single rough fracture a hand's width across that runs the whole way down; the fracture is the first thing to read about it. It is natural and frost-opened, not a cut — irregular, weathered, broader at the base, nothing shaped by hands.* Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name a thing by construction.

**Minor: the stoop is faint again**, as on `ch04-the-master-arrives` run 1. Twice now it has needed to be stated mechanically to arrive at all, and here it was and still came in weak. **It may simply not survive at full length** — the same size limit that beat the wrists.

**THE SUBJECT IS A HOLE.** The picture has to show a boy discovering an absence he did not know he had — not shame, not confusion, not embarrassment, and above all not distress. `03` on Kael at nine is *alert and direct, in the middle of asking*; this is that face arriving at a question with no answer behind it, which is a stop rather than a feeling. The block states it positively — **his mouth is open on a word that is not coming** — because the negatives alone would leave the renderer to pick something.

**THE ROCK USES `ch06.md`'S CORRECTED WORDING**, which exists because *one upright standing stone* rendered a menhir. Natural crag, irregular, frost-fractured, nothing shaped by hands. **`ch05-past-the-split-rock` is adopted with the old rock and will disagree with this one** — flagged on that sheet, unresolved, and this plate is where it starts costing something.

**SEVERIN IS IN THE MOUNTAIN REGISTER, and it is the garment `ch04-the-master-arrives` was adopted with** — described by construction, not named, because the name produced a frock coat. The stick is in the inventory and explicitly not bearing weight.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: portrait reference of the old man.

Setting: the end of a footpath high on a bare mountainside at first
light, as loose wash suggestion — thin frozen grass, bare rock, the
sea far below in flat pale wash, the sky bare unpainted paper. Flat
cold dawn light coming up off the sea, no sun disc and no warm colour
anywhere.

A natural boulder stands beside them, about the height of the man:
irregular in outline, weathered all over, broader at the base than at
the top, with no straight edges and no flat faces. A rough fracture
runs down through it, wide and uneven, opened by frost. It is not a
monument, not a menhir and nothing about it is shaped or placed by
hands.

Composition: the two of them facing each other on the path, cropped
at the knee, the boulder behind and between them. The top of the
boy's head reaches the man's chest.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS
THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE.

THE BOY HAS JUST BEEN ASKED SOMETHING HE CANNOT ANSWER. His mouth is
open on a word that is not coming, his eyes still steady on the man's
— caught mid-sentence with nothing behind it. NOT ashamed, NOT
confused, NOT distressed, NOT frightened, NOT sullen; his chin stays
up and his eyes do not drop. A stop, not a feeling.

The man waits. Unhurried, courteous, giving nothing away, mouth
closed, brows level — a face that has priced this and is letting it
sit. NOT kind, NOT stern, NOT amused, NOT expectant.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: the man's right hand rests on the head of a
plain walking stick whose tip is on the ground beside him, carrying
none of his weight — he stands square and even on both feet. His left
hand is empty at his side. The boy's right hand is on the strap of a
cloth bag over his shoulder; his left hangs empty. Nothing else is
carried and there is no other object in the picture.

The boy's face is the most finished thing in the picture and carries
the sharpest step from light to dark. The man, the boulder, the path
and all the clothing are held at quiet middle values with no hard
edges.

THE BOY: ten years old, eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and
colourless — no more colour in the iris than in the whites around it.
Dark hair cut short and uneven. Wind-chapped, high colour across the
cheeks and nose, cool grey dust on the skin. Coarse undyed mended
wool, a little too large for him.

THE MAN: tall, lean and still broad across the shoulder, standing
with a scholar's stoop — head carried forward of the shoulders, the
upper back rounded. Hair fully grey, the whole head, an even neutral
grey, with no streak, seam or patch anywhere in it. Old and worn down
to structure, deep lines from nose to mouth and at the outer corners
of the eyes, skin dry and drawn tight over the bone, and no
frailty anywhere in him. HIS GARMENT, BY CONSTRUCTION: one long
straight dark overgarment falling to mid-calf, close at the shoulder
and loose below, closed edge to edge down the centre front, with no
lapel, no turned collar, no placket and no pockets. A standing collar
at the base of the throat with no shirt collar showing. Plain sleeves
ending square at a bare wrist, and the wrists are thick and heavy.
Nothing nineteenth-century: not a frock coat, not a greatcoat, not a
suit, and no waistcoat under it.

The ink carries the cloth on both of them, in dense scratchy linework
at the seams and the mended places.
```

### PLATE: the-wasters

*Candidate 3.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`, `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 7 canon: *Those weeks his father sat by the fire in the evenings with the drawknife and a stack of seasoned wood, and made swords... shavings taken off in whispers, the thing hefted, turned, hefted again, held out level on two fingers to see where it wanted to tip. Two of them, one to Kael's arm and one longer... The boy healed by the fire, and the father built the things they were going to fight each other with by the same fire, and the wind came to the door and pushed, and the door held.*

**RUN 1 (s41) — THE SHARED-ATTENTION FIX WORKED.** Both of them are looking at the waster, the faces stay lit and readable, and the object is the subject without competing with either. **That is the two-subjects problem solved**, after `ch05/past-the-split-rock` split it across two channels and `ch06/the-wheel` split it across value and line. Three answers now; this is the cheapest.

Everything else held: fire in a stone-lined hole lighting both faces from underneath, the waster resting across two fingers at its balance point rather than gripped, the wall of tools, the barred door, the boy's empty hands in his lap, the drawknife and shavings on the floor, both mouths closed, neither looking at the other.

**MISS: THE WASTER IS A PLANK.** It came back a flat batten with a taper — no grip, no section, nothing a hand would hold. **The negations did it.** The block said *one wooden sword, plain and unfinished, no guard and no ornament*, and stripping the guard and the ornament removed the only features that would have made it read as a sword at all. **Naming a noun gives you its default; negating the default's features gives you nothing.** Both roads end at the same place: describe the construction.

**The corrected object, for a re-run:** *a practice sword cut from one piece of pale seasoned wood, about the length of the boy's arm. A round grip at one end long enough for a hand and a half, then a blade section that is lens-shaped in cross-section, thicker down its centre than at its edges, tapering slightly toward a blunt squared tip. No guard, no crosspiece and no ornament — the shape alone says sword.*

**Minor: the drawknife came back with decorative scrollwork on it.** Nothing in this house has ornament on it, and the block only banned ornament on the waster. Cheap to fix; not worth a run by itself.

**THE CHAPTER'S THESIS IN ONE ROOM**, and it is carried entirely by staging: nothing in either face says what the objects are for. **Do not let the picture explain itself** — no grimness, no foreboding, no significant look between them.

**THE SHARED ATTENTION IS WHAT MAKES THE OBJECT THE SUBJECT WITHOUT COMPETING WITH THE FACES** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Four figures: bind a group by naming one thing they all attend to). Both of them are looking at the waster; the faces stay lit and readable and point at it. That is the fix for the two-subjects problem this session kept hitting.

**FIRELIGHT FROM A HOLE IN THE FLOOR IS THE PROVEN CONFIGURATION** — near, warm, below both faces. Third use.

**The Ch. 6 scar is spent and appears**, healed and long closed, on the LEFT forearm (`03`). Named and located, no tissue vocabulary.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: portrait reference of the man.

Setting: the single room of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at
night, as loose wash suggestion — a heavy plank table, a wall of hand
tools on pegs behind, a barred plank door, everything beyond the two
figures falling into deep cool shadow.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS A SMALL CONTAINED FIRE IN A STONE-LINED HOLE IN THE
FLOOR BETWEEN THEM, banked low, no flames reaching. It lights both
faces from underneath — the undersides of the jaws, the cheekbones,
the brows, the hands — and leaves the roof and the corners dark.

Composition: two figures sitting on the floor on either side of the
fire hole, cropped at the waist, close, turned in toward each other
and toward the fire.

BOTH OF THEM ARE LOOKING AT THE SAME THING AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF
THE IMAGE: one wooden sword, plain and unfinished, no guard and no
ornament, about the length of the boy's arm, held out level on two of the
man's fingers at its balance point — resting across the
fingers, not gripped, not raised, not pointed at anything.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: the man's right hand holds the wooden sword
level on two fingers. His left rests on his knee, holding nothing. A
drawknife and a few pale curls of shaving lie on the floor beside
him. The boy's hands are open and empty in his lap. There is no
metal blade anywhere in the picture and no second wooden sword.

The man watches the sword and nothing else, judging where it wants to
tip. Level, mouth closed, brows unmoved, entirely unreadable — NOT
grim, NOT sad, NOT proud, NOT tender, NOT troubled. Broad across the
top of the back and nothing wasted below it. Iron-dark hair to the
jaw. A face weathered far past its years.

The boy watches the sword too, absorbed, sitting easily, still
healing — a healed line closed and pale across his left forearm, no
other mark on him. Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and
colourless, catching the firelight without taking its colour. Dark
hair cut short and uneven.

Nothing is happening and neither of them is about to speak. Both
mouths are closed. They are not looking at each other.

The fire is the brightest thing in the picture and is loose and
soft-edged with no detail in it. The two lit faces and the wooden
sword are the most finished things and carry the sharpest step from
light to dark. The room, the tools, the table and both sets of
clothing are held dark and loose with no hard edges.

Coarse undyed mended wool on both, the man's heavy and sized to him
and closed at the throat, the boy's thin and too large. The ink
carries the cloth at the seams and the mended places.
```

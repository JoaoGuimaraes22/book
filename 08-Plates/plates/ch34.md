# Ch. 34 — The Run · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen, Aeliana fourteen, Wren hours old; Neris is unpinned. Anchors: `images/portraits/neris.png` + `images/portraits/aeliana-15.png` on the first, `images/portraits/neris.png` on the second.

**BOTH PLATES ARE THE MOTHER AND THAT IS THE CHAPTER.** This is where the reader is told what she is, and the telling is done twice — **at a foot, and at three hundred strides.** One picture is the hands that move a girl's elbow an inch to make her hold a newborn correctly; the other is the same hands taking a hillside apart. **Nobody on the page ever says they are the same pair of hands, and the set can say it without saying it.**

**HER FACE IS IN THE FIRST AND NOT IN THE SECOND, AND THE PAGE RULES BOTH.** *He looked at the back of his mother's head. She was standing at the front of the shelf with her hands down at her sides and her shoulders going.* … *She did not turn round. He waited for her to. Everybody on that shelf waited for her to.* **The Art is never once shown across her face**, and `03/neris` holds it as standing state — *her face is never on the page during it and her son never sees it.* So the second plate is composed from where Kael is standing, at her back, and the facelessness is canon rather than a price.

**THE FIRST PLATE PUTS A NEWBORN IN A PRINCESS'S ARMS AND THE FOLDER HAS NOTHING LIKE IT.** `ch33/the-lamp` already spent the fence — that Aeliana's hands do nothing — and this is the second half of that spend and the payment for it: *"Both hands," his mother said. "Under the head. Do not hold him tight, hold him close, they are different."* … *"You can, you will," said Neris. "You will be extremely good at this."*

**THE INSTANT IS THE ELBOW.** *She took the girl's elbow and moved it about an inch, and looked at the result, and let go.* Not the transfer and not the speech — **a correction made with two fingers**, which is how that woman has taught everything in this book. §Hands gets a line per hand because the whole beat is in four of them and one child.

**AND THE LINEN IS ON THE CHILD'S FACE IN BOTH CHAPTERS FROM HERE.** It is bound at Ch. 33 and never explained. The block draws it as Ch. 33 describes it — *quickly, and well, and not tight, clear of the nose and clear of the mouth* — and **draws nothing under it**. The eye belongs to the chapter that spends it.

**THE SECOND PLATE'S SUBJECT IS NINE OLIVE TREES, NOT A HUNDRED MEN.** *They came apart along the grain from the top down, in long white splitting strips, and the strips went out into the bowl flat and level and did not slow down.* The men are in the bottom of that bowl and they are not drawn: **what is in the frame is what the wind did to the wood**, which is the one part of it a picture can hold without becoming a different kind of picture. THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE binds what may be added; it does not oblige a plate to draw everything the page has, and s51's dropped grim plates are the folder's own guidance on which half to take.

**THE FIRE IS ELARINE'S AND THE BLOCK SAYS SO BY ITS SHAPE.** *It did not spread from anything. There was no first flame anywhere for a second flame to have come off.* Four hundred fires exactly where the fuel was, gathered up by the wind into *one thing eight feet high and two hundred strides wide* — so the block builds the wall as a shape with a height, a width and a bald strip in front of it, per §Nouns, and does not write it as an effect.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **Aurelian talking** — one-handed, his good hand out and not touching a princess, correct in every particular and for nobody — is the warmest thing in Part IV and it is a third plate. **Elarine lighting the bowl** is the same picture as the one that was taken with a different woman in front of it. **I have got you. I love you** is a hand on the side of a head for the third time in four chapters (`ch31/the-hand-flat`, `ch33`'s passed-over hand on the head), and the set should stop there.

### PLATE: the-elbow

*Candidate 1, ★.* Anchors: `images/portraits/neris.png`, `images/portraits/aeliana-15.png`. Ch. 34 canon: *Kael watched his mother hand his brother to a princess of the empire. / She did it the way she did things, which was quickly and without any ceremony in it: she got him out through the front of the coat with one hand under the head, turned, and put him against Aeliana's chest before the girl had finished understanding what was being walked toward her. / "Both hands," his mother said. "Under the head. Do not hold him tight, hold him close, they are different."* … *"You can, you will," said Neris. "You will be extremely good at this." / She took the girl's elbow and moved it about an inch, and looked at the result, and let go.*

**TWO FACES, AND NEITHER IS LOOKING AT THE OTHER.** The woman's eyes are on the girl's arms, because she is checking a result. The girl's are down on the child, because she has just been given one. **The whole exchange is conducted by hand**, and the block places the gazes rather than naming them as a subject.

**THE WHITE IS IN TWO PLACES AND IT IS THE DRAIN REGISTER, NOT AGE.** *the white in it going all the way through in two places* (Ch. 33). `../canon-brief.md`'s fence is absolute — never whiten a head for drama, keep the quartz white distinct from Aeliana's warm silver — and this picture has both women in it at once, so the block names the two whites separately and forbids the wrong one on each head.

```
Image 1: neris.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired woman.
Image 2: aeliana-15.png — portrait reference of the silver-haired girl.

Setting: a bare shelf of rock high on a mountainside at night, as loose
wash suggestion — broken stone underfoot, burnt stubble and dead thorn
falling away behind them, and low cloud overhead lit dull orange from
below, end to end. That cloud is the only light: a weak warm glow coming
from above and behind, with no sun, no lamp and no hard-edged shadow
anywhere.

Composition: two figures, close together and near the viewer, the bottom
edge of the picture crossing both at the lower chest. The woman stands
at the left in three-quarter view, turned in toward the girl. The girl
is at the right, a little shorter, turned three-quarters toward the
viewer with her head bowed. Both faces are fully visible in the upper
half of the frame. Nobody else is drawn near them.

BETWEEN THEM, HELD AGAINST THE GIRL'S CHEST, IS A NEWBORN CHILD wrapped
in a piece of coarse pale cloth. He is very small — his head is no bigger
than the girl's two hands together. A NARROW STRIP OF PALE LINEN IS
BOUND ACROSS HIS EYES, level, clear of the nose and clear of the mouth,
tucked under itself behind the ear. It is neatly done and it is not
tight. His eyes are not visible and no eye is drawn on him anywhere.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: the girl's right hand is under the child's head and
her left arm is under his body, holding him against her. The woman's
right hand is closed lightly around the girl's left elbow, lifting it
about an inch. The woman's left hand is open and empty at her side. Four
hands, one child, nothing else in any hand.

THE WOMAN IS LOOKING AT THE GIRL'S ARMS, not at her face and not at the
viewer: the lids lowered, the mouth open mid-word, the jaw easy,
everything about her unhurried. Nothing in her face is tender and nothing
in it is anxious.

THE GIRL IS LOOKING DOWN AT THE CHILD, not at the woman and not at the
viewer: the lids fully raised, the pale blue clear, the mouth slightly
open, the brows up a little.

THE WOMAN'S HAIR IS DARK BROWN AND LONG, with TWO SEPARATE PIECES OF IT
WHITE FROM THE ROOT — a hard cold white with no warmth and no yellow in
it, the white of a split stone. There is no grey anywhere on her, no
third white piece, and nothing silver.

THE GIRL'S HAIR IS SILVER-WHITE ALL OVER, colourless, never blonde, with
no yellow and no gold in it, half of it come down loose on the near side
and half still pinned. It is a warmer and brighter white than the two
pieces in the woman's hair, and the two must not be drawn the same.

The woman wears a heavy coarse village coat of undyed dark wool, worn and
mended, open at the front. The girl wears a heavy dark soldier's cloak of
coarse wool over a fine gown, fastened at one shoulder, plainly not hers.
There is no gold, no jewellery and no ornament anywhere in the picture. Do
not mix their clothing: the soldier's cloak belongs only to the girl and
the village coat only to the woman.

Both faces and the four hands around the child carry the sharpest steps
from light to dark; the stone, the thorn and the cloud stay at quiet
middle values with soft edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces, hair,
colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. The woman's eyes
are dark; the girl's are pale blue.
```

### PLATE: the-bowl

*Candidate 2, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 34 canon: *And Kael's mother put both hands out in front of her, palms down, and gave that fire a wind.* … *It came off her flat and it came off her level, out of both hands and out of the whole front of her at once, and the first thing it did was take the ground bald for eighty strides in a line as clean as a scythe cut. / The second thing it did was pick up Elarine Sedrane's four hundred fires and put them together.* … *What stood up out of the bottom of that bowl was one thing eight feet high and two hundred strides wide.* … *There were nine old olive trees on the terrace under the shelf, planted by somebody dead in the reign of somebody else dead, thick through the trunk as a man is thick through the waist, and his mother turned her hands about fifteen degrees and put the wind across them. / The first three came apart. / Not uprooted. Not blown over. They came apart along the grain from the top down, in long white splitting strips, and the strips went out into the bowl flat and level and did not slow down.*

**THE CAMERA IS EIGHT FEET BEHIND HER, WHICH IS WHERE HER SON IS.** She is small and dark and near, the bowl is huge and lit and far, and **nothing she is doing is visible on her** — no light on her hands, nothing coming off her, the whole event three hundred strides downhill. §Light's rule about a far source is what makes the picture work: the fire lights her from below and behind the frame's subject, and she is a silhouette with a rim on her.

**THE THREE THINGS IN THE BOWL ARE COUNTED WHERE THEY SIT**, per `ch27/the-sky`: the bald strip, the wall of fire with its height and width, and three trees coming apart with the strips going out flat. Everything else in the bottom of that bowl is smoke and glare.

**AND THE WHITE IN HER HAIR IS THE ONE DETAIL DRAWN AT FULL FINISH ON HER.** *The white in her hair caught the light from the fire below.* It is the only thing on that dark figure that takes the light, which is the picture's whole editorial in one clause.

```
Image 1: neris.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired woman. Her face is not visible in this picture.

Setting: a mountainside at night, seen from a bare shelf of rock looking
down into a wide shallow bowl of ground about three hundred strides
across — stone, burnt stubble, dead thorn, an old terrace wall running
along under the shelf. Low cloud sits over everything, lit dull orange
from beneath.

Composition: the woman stands at the front edge of the shelf in the lower
third of the frame, near the viewer, seen from directly behind and a
little above, small in the frame and dark. The bottom edge of the picture
crosses her at the hip. Her head is not turned and her face is not
visible. She is the only person in the picture.

HER TWO ARMS ARE OUT IN FRONT OF HER AT SHOULDER HEIGHT, level and
straight, the palms turned down. Nothing is in either hand. NOTHING IS
COMING OFF HER HANDS: no light, no glow, no spark, no wind drawn as
lines, nothing in the air between her and the bowl at all.

WHAT IS IN THE BOWL BELOW HER, EACH IN ITS OWN PLACE: a wall of fire
standing across the whole width of the bowl, low and long — about a man
and a half high and running two hundred strides from one side of the
frame to the other, unbroken. In front of it, a bare strip of ground
eighty strides long where everything has been taken off down to the
stone, its near edge as clean and straight as a scythe cut. And on the
terrace just below the shelf, three old olive trees splitting apart from
the top downward along the grain into long pale strips, the strips flying
out low and level away from the viewer, straight and not slowing.

Six more olive trees stand further along the same terrace, unsplit. Nine
trees in the frame and no other tree anywhere in it.

No figures are drawn anywhere in the bowl. Nobody is standing, nobody is
falling and no body is drawn in it. The bottom of the bowl beyond the
fire is smoke, glare and darkness with nothing resolved in it.

THE FIRE BELOW IS THE ONLY LIGHT IN THE PICTURE. It comes up from
underneath: it puts a hard orange rim along the woman's shoulders and
the outside of both arms, lights the underside of the smoke, and leaves
the shelf, the stone and the whole of her front dark.

Two separate pieces of her long dark hair are white from the root — a
hard cold white with no warmth and no yellow in it — and they take the
light from below and are the palest thing on her. The rest of her hair
is dark brown with no grey in it anywhere.

The white pieces in her hair and the rim on her shoulders carry the
sharpest step from light to dark near the viewer; the wall of fire is the
brightest thing in the frame but soft-edged and far.

She wears a heavy coarse village coat of undyed dark wool, worn and
mended. Preserve her build, hair colour and apparent age as shown in the
reference image; her face is not drawn and her head is not turned toward
the viewer.
```

# Ch. 34 — The Run · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen, Aeliana fourteen, Wren hours old; Neris is unpinned. Anchors: `images/portraits/neris.png` + `images/portraits/aeliana-15.png` on the first, `images/portraits/neris.png` on the second.

**BOTH PLATES ARE THE MOTHER AND THAT IS THE CHAPTER.** This is where the reader is told what she is, and the telling is done twice — **at a foot, and at three hundred strides.** One picture is the hands that move a girl's elbow an inch to make her hold a newborn correctly; the other is the same hands taking a hillside apart. **Nobody on the page ever says they are the same pair of hands, and the set can say it without saying it.**

**HER FACE IS IN THE FIRST AND NOT IN THE SECOND, AND THE PAGE RULES BOTH.** *He looked at the back of his mother's head. She was standing at the front of the shelf with her hands down at her sides and her shoulders going.* … *She did not turn round. He waited for her to. Everybody on that shelf waited for her to.* **The Art is never once shown across her face**, and `03/neris` holds it as standing state — *her face is never on the page during it and her son never sees it.* So the second plate is composed from where Kael is standing, at her back, and the facelessness is canon rather than a price.

**THE FIRST PLATE PUTS A NEWBORN IN A PRINCESS'S ARMS AND THE FOLDER HAS NOTHING LIKE IT.** `ch33/the-lamp` already spent the fence — that Aeliana's hands do nothing — and this is the second half of that spend and the payment for it: *"Both hands," his mother said. "Under the head. Do not hold him tight, hold him close, they are different."* … *"You can, you will," said Neris. "You will be extremely good at this."*

**THE INSTANT IS THE ELBOW.** *She took the girl's elbow and moved it about an inch, and looked at the result, and let go.* Not the transfer and not the speech — **a correction made with two fingers**, which is how that woman has taught everything in this book. §Hands gets a line per hand because the whole beat is in four of them and one child.

**AND THE LINEN IS ON THE CHILD'S FACE IN BOTH CHAPTERS FROM HERE.** It is bound at Ch. 33 and never explained. The block draws it as Ch. 33 describes it — *quickly, and well, and not tight, clear of the nose and clear of the mouth* — and **draws nothing under it**. The eye belongs to the chapter that spends it.

**THE SECOND PLATE'S SUBJECT IS NINE OLIVE TREES, NOT A HUNDRED MEN.** *They came apart along the grain from the top down, in long white splitting strips, and the strips went out into the bowl flat and level and did not slow down.* The men are in the bottom of that bowl and they are not drawn: **what is in the frame is what the wind did to the wood**, which is the one part of it a picture can hold without becoming a different kind of picture. THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE binds what may be added; it does not oblige a plate to draw everything the page has, and s51's dropped grim plates are the folder's own guidance on which half to take.

**THE FIRE IS ELARINE'S AND THE BLOCK SAYS SO BY ITS SHAPE.** *It did not spread from anything. There was no first flame anywhere for a second flame to have come off.* Four hundred fires exactly where the fuel was, gathered up by the wind into *one thing eight feet high and two hundred strides wide* — so the block builds the wall as a shape with a height, a width and a bald strip in front of it, per §Nouns, and does not write it as an effect.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **Aurelian talking** — one-handed, his good hand out and not touching a princess, correct in every particular and for nobody — is the warmest thing in Part IV and it is a third plate. **Elarine lighting the bowl** is the same picture as the one that was taken with a different woman in front of it. **I have got you. I love you** is a hand on the side of a head for the third time in four chapters (`ch31/the-hand-flat`, `ch33`'s passed-over hand on the head), and the set should stop there.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-elbow

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch34-the-elbow.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53: *amazing*). Ch. 34 has its two.**

**THE TWO WHITES STOOD IN ONE FRAME AND STAYED DISTINCT, WHICH WAS THE HARDEST CANON ASK OF THE SESSION.** `../canon-brief.md` requires it in the abstract — *Aeliana's silver is a house trait, warm and bright; the drain register is pure quartz white, cold and hard. Keep them distinct* — and no picture had ever had to hold both. The girl's whole head is bright warm silver-white; the woman's are two cold white locks in dark brown. **Two heads, two registers, one run**, and what bought it was naming each by temperature and then forbidding the other by name on each head.

**AND THE LINEN IS DRAWN.** A narrow strip of pale cloth bound level across a newborn's eyes, clear of the nose and clear of the mouth, with no eye drawn anywhere under it. **The book's largest unexplained fact is now a picture**, and the plate says exactly as much as Ch. 33 says and not one thing more.

**THE INSTRUCTION GIVEN BY HAND LANDED.** The girl's right hand under the head, her arm under the body, and the woman's hand closed on her forearm correcting it — which is the whole scene, and no clause in the block explains it.

**WHAT DRIFTED, two:**
1. **The woman's idle hand got recruited.** The block said her left is *open and empty at her side*; in the picture both her hands are on the girl's arm. **Second instance today of the same fault** — `ch31/the-shutter` said *his left hand hangs at his side* and came back with both hands at the window. **A hand told to do nothing joins the action; a hand given a resting place might not.** Recorded on both sheets; not promoted, because no correction has been wanted.
2. A metal ring closes the cloak at her shoulder, against *no gold and no ornament anywhere in the picture* — the same fastening drift as `ch33/the-lamp`, where the block had to spend a clause forbidding a pauldron. **A cloak fastened at one shoulder pulls toward hardware every time.**

**TWO FACES, AND NEITHER IS LOOKING AT THE OTHER.** The woman's eyes are on the girl's arms, because she is checking a result. The girl's are down on the child, because she has just been given one. **The whole exchange is conducted by hand**, and the block places the gazes rather than naming them as a subject.

**THE WHITE IS IN TWO PLACES AND IT IS THE DRAIN REGISTER, NOT AGE.** *the white in it going all the way through in two places* (Ch. 33). `../canon-brief.md`'s fence is absolute — never whiten a head for drama, keep the quartz white distinct from Aeliana's warm silver — and this picture has both women in it at once, so the block names the two whites separately and forbids the wrong one on each head.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-bowl

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch34-the-bowl.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53). THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S OPTIMISED VERSION OF THIS SHEET'S**, stored exactly as run.

**THEIR CHANGE IS THE SAME CHANGE THEY MADE TO `ch33/the-lamp`, AND IT IS THE SESSION'S BEST LESSON.** Mine asked for *three old olive trees splitting apart from the top downward* — an action, over seconds, which a still picture cannot hold. Theirs asks for **the state it leaves**: *already split open from the top down along the grain, the trunks standing as ragged upright shells, and the long pale strips off them out in the air clear of the trunks, low and level and pointing away from the viewer, straight and mid-flight.* **The trunk is an after; the strips are a during; between them the eye supplies the verb.** It landed on one run, and it is the hardest single object anybody has asked this tool for in this folder.

**AND THE WHITE PIECES ARE COUNTED AND CAPPED IN THE SAME BREATH.** *TWO PIECES OF HER LONG DARK HAIR, AND ONLY TWO* — with the third forbidden by name. Two white locks in the picture, taking the light from below, the palest thing on her, on a woman who is otherwise a silhouette. **The drain-mark's second appearance and its second clean run.**

**NOTHING COMES OFF HER HANDS, AND THAT IS WHAT MAKES IT HERS.** The one absence the block spends is on the air between her and the bowl, and the picture keeps it empty: no light, no glow, nothing drawn between the palms and three hundred strides of burning ground. **A woman with her arms out and nothing on them, and the whole hillside doing it.**

**THE REST AS WRITTEN**: the unbroken wall of fire right across the bowl at a man and a half high, far and soft-edged; the six whole olive trees further along the same terrace; nothing resolved beyond the fire; no figure anywhere in the bowl; the fire as the only light, from underneath, rimming her shoulders and the outsides of her arms and leaving her front dark.

**WHAT DRIFTED, two, both small:** the bare strip with the scythe-cut edge is not a distinct feature — the ground in front of the fire is dark and read as burnt rather than as taken off · and the crop went past the hip to the thigh.

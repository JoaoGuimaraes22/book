# Ch. 10 — The Asking · plate sheet

**One plate adopted, one built and unrun (s43).** Kael is ten in this chapter (`07/timeline` — the birthday passes unmarked between chapters), so the anchor is `images/portraits/kael-9.png`, one year light and the same portrait that already carried Ch. 7 at ten. Severin is `images/portraits/severin.png`, Vask `images/portraits/vask.png`.

**THE CHAPTER IS TWO CHAPTERS AND THE PAIR SPLITS ON IT.** The Temple study and the mountain garden are two settings, two casts and two weathers inside one number, and taking one plate from each buys the pair more distance than any two moments from the same half. It also puts the only machine in the plate set on a page.

**BOTH TAKE THE DRAWN TREATMENT AND HUE GOES PER PICTURE (session ruling, s43, author-delegated).** `ch08.md`'s controlled pair separates on three clauses, and only two of them are about finish: **bare paper reserved by name**, and **the ink protected from a general softening.** The third — cold hues sited on named objects — is colour, and colour is adjusted per picture by author ruling (s42), not by rule. So both blocks below carry the two finish clauses, which is where the set was actually drifting (`../README.md`: the doorframe glossier than the long game), and each names its own temperature: the study cool with the honey as its one warm thing, the garden warm with the water and the stone named cool.

**ANCHORING COST, PER PLATE.** Severin's reference takes the half-figure COMPOSITION override, so it carries him to the waist and the table crop is anchored exactly where this picture needs it. Kael's and Vask's are head-and-shoulders: everything below their collars is authored in these blocks, anchored to nothing, and will not repeat in the next plate.

### PLATE: the-honey-and-the-bread

*Promoted out of the Ch. 10 alternate on `candidates.md`, which had the jar as set dressing beside the empty book; this takes the jar and the two faces instead, and the alternate keeps the book.* Anchors: `images/portraits/severin.png` and `images/portraits/kael-9.png`. Ch. 10 canon: *The study was a room with too many rooms in it. Books to the ceiling on two walls; a table of instruments — a balance, glass, a thing of nested brass rings he had no name for; and on the sill, in a row of clay pots, green things growing that had no business being green in that light. The master set out bread, and took down a jar of honey and put it by the bread without one word being said about it, and Kael looked at the jar, and the master looked at him looking, and neither of them said the thing, and it got said anyway.*

**THE JAR IS A SPINE OBJECT AND THIS IS WHERE IT GETS DRAWN.** Four chapters plant on it and none of them explains it. Ch. 2: *there was honey in his milk at supper. There was not usually honey.* Ch. 4, Severin's second sentence in the book: *"So," the man said. "You're the reason the honey's always gone."* Ch. 7, on the cleaned knife back in its place: *The wall said what the honey said, in the wall's language.* Ch. 18, four years on: *a blank book, an empty column, a jar of honey that was never mentioned.* **Honey is this book's word for a kindness nobody will admit to**, and one picture pays all of it.

**THE GAZE IS ONE-SIDED, WHICH IS THE EASY VERSION AND THE BETTER ONE.** The page hands it over exactly built: *Kael looked at the jar, and the master looked at him looking.* `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Getting expression rates a one-sided gaze both more reliable than mutual and more interesting, and here it is also the beat — two people declining to say the same thing in two different directions.

**THE CROP IS THE POINT: IT IS WHERE THE WRISTS FINALLY RENDER.** *The wrists coming out of the sleeves were too thick for a man whose work was paper* is the tell of his whole body, and `ch04.md` spent a full-length frame on it and lost it, concluding that **the fix is crop, not emphasis** (§Name the winner, the limit). A seated table shot with both his forearms down on the boards is that crop, and it costs nothing to take here because the moment already puts his hands on the table.

**THE COAT IS NOT DRAWN AND MUST NOT BE.** *It was the one room below the split rock where the coat came off* is the sentence this plate sits under, and `../canon-brief.md` is explicit: the coat is the book's metaphor for Kael's concealment, not a garment to style. **No garment is removed, hung, folded or laid over a chair anywhere in this picture.** What the sentence means is drawn by the boy's shoulders being down and his hands still, and by nothing else.

**COSTUME REGISTER, PRICED.** The page does not dress Severin in Ch. 10 at all. The study is inside the Temple, so this is the robes register rather than the mountain coat — and **the chain stays off**: `../canon-brief.md` holds it as *Temple and the games* canon, it is public dress, and nothing in a room with bread in it needs it. The garment is written by construction, not by name, per `ch04.md`'s proven fix — the word *coat* is what produced a Dickensian frock coat there, and the word is avoided here for both reasons at once.

**THE REFERENCE'S FACE IS THE ONE REAL RISK.** `kael-9` is deliberately *ALERT AND DIRECT... a boy in the middle of asking something*, and §What the reference carries says a reference supplies its own mood and wins a close crop. Two things make this survivable: the boy's eyes are **down on an object**, which is a mechanical change the reference holds no version of and therefore cannot fight, and the moment's load sits on Severin's face, which `severin.png` holds exactly. **If he comes back asking at the viewer, the fix is the still variant and not more words** — and that file does not exist: 317a984 wrote `prompts/portraits/kael-9-still.txt` and the run was never made. `../README.md` names it as though it were in the library; it is a prompt only.

**WHAT IS IN THE ROOM AND OUT OF THIS FRAME.** The table of instruments — *a balance, glass, a thing of nested brass rings* — is page canon and is out of the crop; naming it would fill the frame with objects that are not the subject. **The fern stays**, on the sill behind them, because Ch. 18 comes back to this room and finds *the fern on the sill had not been touched*, so the establishing costs one clause and no separate plate.

**THE JAR WINS SATURATION, THE FACES WIN FINISH.** Two channels, per §Name the winner AND demote the rival, and they are deliberately not the same object: the honey is the one warm and saturated thing in a cool room, held loose and soft-edged, and the sharpest step from light to dark is sited on the two faces.

**SIZE IS ANSWERED, NOT DECLINED.** Both are seated, so height cannot carry the ten-against-old and the age gap goes onto the pair of hands instead (§Size contrast must be stated per hand, which is written for exactly this).

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch10-the-honey-and-the-bread.png`, first run.**

**THE STAGING CAME THROUGH WHOLE.** Books to the ceiling in loose wash, the sill with its clay pots and the fern, thin daylight from the window and no second source; both seated at the corner of the table; the jar set down with his right hand still against it and his left flat on the boards; the boy's hands on the table edge and reaching for nothing; four hands, two objects. **The one-sided gaze landed exactly** — the boy's eyes down on the jar, the old man's on the boy — and the jar is the most saturated thing in the picture with the loaf's cut face reading as bare paper beside it.

**AND THE BEARD AND THE LOOSE GREY HAIR ARE THE REFERENCE'S OWN**, not drift: `images/portraits/severin.png` carries both. The chain stayed off, which was the one register instruction that had to hold.

**THE FINDING OF THE RUN, AND IT IS A GENERAL ONE: A POSE BEATS A REFERENCE'S MOOD WHERE AN OVERRIDE DOES NOT.** `../scene-prompt-system.md` §What the reference carries records two runs where a stated override lost to the reference's own expression — `ch01/fallen-stars` and `ch05/the-fact-lands`, the second with the override in caps in priority position — and concludes that the fix is a second reference. **This block never argued with `kael-9`'s asking face. It moved his eyes onto an object**, and the asking face did not arrive, because a reference holds no downcast version of itself to supply. **Changing what the body is doing is cheaper than a new reference and it is what should be tried first.** One instance; recorded here, pointed at from that section, not promoted.

**THE MISS IS THE WRISTS, AND THE BLOCK CAUSED IT.** This crop was chosen because `ch04.md` lost the wrists at full length and concluded the fix is crop, not emphasis — and the wrists are still not what arrives. **The instruction was physically self-contradictory:** *plain full sleeves ending square at the wrist with no cuff... so both forearms lie bare on the table.* A sleeve that ends at the wrist covers the forearm. The renderer resolved it to the sleeve, correctly, exactly as §What a warm source will and will not light says it will refuse a physically false instruction.

**THE FIX IS TO NAME WHERE THE CLOTH STOPS ON THE ARM, NOT WHERE THE SLEEVE ENDS** — *the sleeves pushed back above the elbow, the forearms bare from the elbow to the hand.* The garment-construction habit puts every clause on the garment, and the garment is not the subject; the arm is.

**WHAT ARRIVED INSTEAD IS WORTH KEEPING: the hands.** Heavy, knuckled and out of proportion to a scholar, and they carry the same fact the wrists were meant to — this is not a man whose work is paper. **The per-hand size contrast held** too: the boy's hand beside his reads a child's.

**THE ROOM CAME BACK WARMER THAN THE BLOCK ASKED FOR.** The boards and the wall are warm brown where the block said cool grey and grey-blue. **Third time now** — `ch05/the-two-pails` went the other way into greyscale, `ch08/the-one-who-did-not-laugh` came back warm and was judged the best of its set — and it is left alone here for the same reason, per the s42 ruling that colour is looked at rather than ruled: the jar still wins saturation against warm boards, which is all the clause was protecting.

**TWO THINGS FOR THE AUTHOR, both judgements on the image rather than sheet business.** His face sits a shade heavier than *courteous and giving nothing away* — closer to sombre — which is the same direction `ch04.md` recorded as *drifted slightly stern* and is now twice. And **the grey reads white at the ends**, where the brief wants a true even neutral that resolves neither age nor drain.

**THE GREY IS NOT SPENT IN THIS PLATE.** The lids are down, so his eyes carry no visible colour — a consequence of the pose and not of the s42 constraint, which is retired. No plate owes it.

**THE ONE NEGATION THAT EARNS ITS PLACE** is the boy's hands not reaching. Bread and honey in front of a hungry village child is a real pull toward a reach, which is the test `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Negative inventory sets, and the restraint is the whole of what he is doing.

### PLATE: the-waterer

*Candidate 2, tightened.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 10 canon: *A split-log channel from the runnel above the plot; a trough on an iron pin, hung a little off its middle; a counterstone lashed at the short end. The trough filled, and passed its balance, and tipped — one measured pour down the cuts between the rows, the same to each — and the stone stood it back up to fill again.* And the beat itself: *He stood over the plot with his arms folded and watched the trough fill, and tip, and right itself with a knock of wood on wood, and fill again — the whole round of it, three times, saying nothing. Then he crouched, stopped the trough with one finger, tested the pin's seat, and moved the counterstone in by the width of two fingers.*

**KAEL IS OUT OF FRAME AND THAT IS THE DESIGN.** *It was the longest Kael had ever seen him stand in front of a thing Kael had made* — the observing consciousness is the boy's, so the viewer stands where the boy stands. **Put him in the picture and it becomes a scene about approval**, with a child waiting on a verdict; leave him out and it is a man's entire attention on a thing, which is what the page actually gives and is the only way this father ever says it.

**THE INSTANT IS THE FINGER.** Of the three actions in that sentence — crouched, stopped, moved the counterstone — only the middle one is a picture: a machine caught mid-cycle and held by one finger. The pour is stopped halfway, water still running into the trough from the channel above and the last of it running off the low lip. **A man who moves exactly as much as is required, stopping a working machine with the smallest part of himself.**

**THE MACHINE IS FOUR NOUNS AND EVERY ONE OF THEM ARRIVES WITH A DEFAULT** — trough, pin, counterstone, channel. §Name a thing by construction, with its order rule: **the defining feature first, with a measurement, then the shape corrected.** The defining feature is that it is hung off its middle and swings, so that is the first clause; *no wheel, no frame, no crank* comes last, where it trims rather than replaces. `ch07.md`'s waster is the warning on the other side — banning a thing's features can take the thing with them — so the construction is stated positively and the negations only kill the mill-machinery default.

**THE ARCHETYPE RISK IS APPROVAL.** A father crouched over his son's invention is an image that wants a smile, a nod, a proud tilt of the head, and the chapter is emphatic that none of it is on his face — the pride is spent in the seed corner, off the page and in the next season. `../canon-brief.md`: *his eyes are the character, and they are empty. Not hostile, not cold, not sad, not kind, not tired.* So both sides are banned, per the `ch09.md` shape: the warm neighbours and the stern ones together, or the face moves next door.

**SCARS STAY OUT BY CONSTRUCTION, NOT BY NEGATION.** The tally-marks are on his forearms and are on the page in Ch. 3, so bare unmarked forearms in this frame would contradict, and a negation would name them into a picture that has no use for them. **The sleeves are down to the wrist and the question never arises.**

**SIZE IS INVENTED HERE, AND IT IS SAID SO.** The page gives the trough no dimension, and an image cannot decline to answer — so the size is set by framing, against his forearm, and it is the plate answering with framing rather than the sheet claiming canon. Nothing in `07` fixes it and a later picture may set it differently.

**HIS HAND WINS, NOT HIS FACE.** Faces are what a renderer finishes by default, so the finish is claimed for the hand and the pin explicitly and the plot behind is demoted on both channels — quiet middle values and no hard edges — per §Name the winner, which is two clauses because edge and finish are two things.

**THE WARM PICTURE NAMES ITS COOL.** The inverse of `ch09.md`'s roller: this one is summer daylight on dry ground and timber, so the water, the lashed stone and the shadow under the bed are named cool, or the whole frame goes one brown the way `ch08.md`'s supper did.

**RUN 1 (s43) — NOT YET ADOPTED.**

**THE BEAT LANDED.** One finger on the trough's high edge with the pour stopped under it, the other hand flat on the ground taking his weight, water still arriving from the channel above and the last of it running off the low lip into the cut between two rows. His face is in profile with his attention on his own hand, and **none of the approval register is anywhere in it** — not pleased, not proud, not fond, and not stern either. The three rows, the drystone wall and the thin grass beyond are all there, and there is nobody else in the picture.

**THE FACE IS THE REFERENCE'S OWN, AND THAT IS WHERE THE PROBLEM IS.** He reads a lean, long-haired man in his middle thirties with no grey anywhere in him. **That is `images/portraits/vask.png` reproduced faithfully** — the anchor carries no grey at the temples and does not read *weathered far past his years*, so no plate anchored on it will. **This is not a prompt fault and no rewording reaches it.** `../canon-brief.md` says that where it and a sheet disagree the sheet wins and the brief is the bug; here `03` and the brief agree with each other and the picture in the library is the odd one out.

**AND THE POSE HID THE ONE CLAUSE THAT COULD HAVE FOUGHT IT.** The block does say *going grey at the temples as a diffuse scatter* — and the profile crop with long hair falling forward puts both temples behind hair. **Second time this session that a named tell was staged out of its own picture**, after the wrists went under a sleeve in `the-honey-and-the-bread`. Same shape, same session, same cause: the block named a tell and then chose a framing that covered it. Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name the winner, as a second failure mode beside the size limit.

**THE COST OF EACH WAY, so the ruling is priced.** Re-cutting `vask.png` with the temple-grey and the weathering leaves every adopted plate of him — `ch01-vask-kael-goat`, `ch03-the-back`, `ch07-the-wasters`, `ch08-the-hand-over-the-face` — showing a face the reference no longer holds. REFERENCES ARE RE-CUT, NEVER EDITED covers the mechanics, but the set stops reading as one man. Leaving it means the temple-grey is a fact the pictures never carry and this plate matches four others.

**THE TROUGH CAME BACK A FLAT PLANK.** *A hollowed half-log a little longer than his forearm* produced a board with one raised edge — water would run off its sides. **The phrase is a name wearing construction's clothes:** one adjective on a compound noun, where §Name a thing by construction wants the surface itself described — *a channel scooped out along its length, the cavity a hand deep, so water lies in it and runs to one end.* Fifth instance of the noun rule and the first where the failed phrase already looked like construction.

**THE COUNTERSTONE'S JOB IS NOT LEGIBLE.** It arrived hanging from a ring below the pivot rather than lashed to the short end, so the balance that stands the trough back up does not read. Same fix shape: say what the cord does — *the cord passes round the stone and round the trough's short end and is knotted there* — rather than naming the fixing.

**THE DEMOTION LOST, AND THERE IS A HYPOTHESIS.** *The rows, the wall and the ground behind stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no detail* did not happen: the wall is drawn stone by stone and competes with the hand. **In every picture where the two-clause mechanism has worked, the rival had somewhere to go** — deep shadow, a dark room, a cool wash. This is flat open daylight, where nothing is dark enough for a background to recede into, and the stated *high side sunlight, hard and clear* arrived flat as well. **Hypothesis, not a finding:** a demotion may need a value structure to demote into, and where there is none the instrument is the crop. One run; do not act on it until a second says the same.

```
wash suggestion — a dug bed with three low rows of young bean plants

detail in them, and nothing back there competes for finish. The sunlit
top edge of the trough and the falling water are reserved as bare

THE PICTURE IS WARM AND THE COOL IN IT IS NAMED: the timber of the
trough and the channel, the dry ground and his skin carry the warmth;
the water, the lashed stone and the shadow under the bed are cool grey
and grey-blue with no brown in them.

HIS FACE GIVES NOTHING. His attention is entirely on the pin's seat
under his finger, mouth closed, brows level and not lowered. NOT
pleased, NOT proud, NOT approving, NOT fond, NOT amused — and equally
NOT disapproving, NOT critical, NOT stern, NOT angry and NOT tired.
Level, and giving nothing back.

Weathered far past his years, a face like used tools. Broad across the
top of the back and no further, nothing wasted below it, the crouch
settled and easy with no strain in it. Iron-dark hair going
grey at the temples as a diffuse scatter through the dark, salt
through it, with NO streak, NO seam, NO band and NO patch of white
anywhere in it. Iron-dark eyes. Village working cloth, coarse and
mended, closed at the throat, the sleeves down to the wrist — the ink
carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the seams and the
mended places. He wears no ornament and no metal.

THERE IS NO OTHER PERSON IN THE PICTURE: no boy, no woman, nobody at
the edge of the plot and nobody in the background.
```

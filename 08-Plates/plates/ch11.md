# Ch. 11 — The Dish · plate sheet

**Both plates adopted, each on its first run (s44).** Kael is eleven here and Elarine is eleven; **both anchor on the fourteen-year-old references** — `images/portraits/kael-14.png` and `images/portraits/elarine-14.png` — under the s44 ruling in `../README.md`.

**SO THIS PAIR IS THE FIRST TEST OF THE AGE INSTRUCTION, and both blocks carry the rung.** `../scene-prompt-system.md` §A close-up plate and the age line says to write the eye-line rung into a plate only once a face has actually come back the wrong age, rather than pre-emptively. **The anchor ruling makes it structural rather than pre-emptive:** every anchor is now three years above its chapter, so the rung goes in by default and this pair is where that starts. Both use the ladder as a dial, which `kael-9` proved is a continuous control — *the eyes a little below the vertical midpoint, nearer that midpoint than a young child's and still below it* — and both add a landmark for the body, because `ch09/the-roller-alone` found that **a solo figure has no age reference in the frame unless one is written**.

**ANCHORING COST.** Both references are head-and-shoulders, so everything below either collar is authored here and anchored to nothing.

### PLATE: the-white-stitch

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 11 canon: *at the head of the court stood a table Kael had never seen: an iron dish the size of a cartwheel hub, and around it, in a half-ring, the plain things of the world — a candle, a bowl of water, a box of dry sand, a green sprig in a pot, a copper rod on a cloth.* And the beat: *The crack was small and white and absolutely clear. / It came off his fingers and went to the copper rod like a thing going home — one thin stitch of light, a hand's width, there and gone, with a sound like a knuckle against glass.* The plate is the breath after it, with his eyes still down on his own hand.

**THE STITCH IS BARE PAPER, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE REASON TO MAKE THIS PICTURE.** The folder reserves paper by name in every plate it builds; it has never had a subject that *is* the reserve. A grey morning, cold iron, and one white thing that is not painted at all — the ink drawing stopping at its edges and the paper doing the work.

**LIGHTNING IS A NOUN WITH A DEFAULT AND THE DEFAULT IS A FORKED BOLT.** §Name a thing by construction, with the order rule: the defining feature first with a measurement — one straight thread, a hand's width, unbroken end to end — and the default killed afterward, where it trims rather than replaces. `ch07.md`'s waster is the warning on the far side, so nothing here bans what the thing is made of.

**IT LIGHTS NOTHING, AND THAT IS STATED.** §What a warm source will and will not light: the run will be asked for a flat grey overcast morning and told the stitch throws nothing. A spark that size lighting a face is the kind of physically false instruction two runs were already spent on, and the block says so rather than leaving room for a glow.

**THE FACE IS SOLVED BY THE POSE, NOT BY AN OVERRIDE.** `kael-14`'s reference is the coat — *still and level, mouth closed and relaxed, gaze flat and direct*, at the viewer — and this moment is a boy looking down at his own hand with a door opening behind his face. **The s43 finding is that a reference holds no downcast version of itself to supply**, which is what took `kael-9`'s asking face off `the-honey-and-the-bread` with one clause. Same move, second use. The positive is written and both neighbourhoods are banned — the delight side and the fright side — per `ch09.md`.

**WHAT IS OUT OF FRAME.** The rest of the half-ring, the yard-master behind the table, Priscian, and the clerk with the book open. *At the book, the clerk's pen paused over the word* is the chapter's other great small beat and it is a second figure and a second face; it stays a candidate, not this picture.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch11-the-white-stitch.png`, first run.**

**THE STITCH CAME BACK EXACTLY AS SPECIFIED, WHICH NOTHING IN THIS FOLDER HAS DONE WITH A HARD NOUN BEFORE.** One straight unbroken thread of white from his fingertips to the copper bar: **no fork, no branch, no zigzag, no second strand, no scattered sparks and no halo** — the forked-bolt default killed outright, and the line reading as reserved paper rather than paint. **And it lights nothing.** The flat grey morning is the only light in the picture and no glow was added, which is the first time the folder has written *this source throws nothing* in advance instead of learning it from a failed run.

**THE REST OF THE STAGING HELD.** The copper bar on its folded cloth as the one warm object beside his skin, the iron dish seen from slightly above with its far rim showing, the table edge across the bottom, his open hand a hand's width above the bar and touching nothing, the left hand out of frame. His face and his open hand are the most finished things in the picture; the court behind is dark summary shapes with no features and no adult anywhere. **The pose solved the face for the second time** — eyes down, lids lowered, still, and nothing of `kael-14`'s flat-and-level-at-the-viewer arrived.

**AND THE AGE WORKED HERE, WHICH IS THE FINDING, BECAUSE THE FAN IS THE CONTROL.** Both blocks in this pair carry the same eye-line rung, in the same words, under the same anchor ruling and the same treatment. **He reads eleven or twelve. She read a young woman.** The one thing that differs is what sat beside the rung:

- **This block gave a ratio against an object in the frame** — *his hand is a child's, small and short-fingered, and the copper bar is longer than his forearm.*
- **`the-fan-in-the-rain` gave adjectives** — *the jaw has not come in, the cheeks still full under the bone, the neck slender.* No landmark, nothing to measure against.

**That is exactly what §Scale between figures predicts** — *adjectives lose; ratios win* — and it is the same law that fixed a four-year-old who kept arriving seven. **So the eye-line rung is not the instrument on its own; the rung plus one in-frame ratio is.** Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §A close-up plate and the age line, which has been logged raised-and-unconfirmed since s42 and now has its evidence. **The confound is named honestly:** two different characters, two different references and two different crops, so this is one controlled pair and not a proof.

**SMALL DRIFTS, NONE WORTH A RE-RUN.** The stitch is nearer a hand and a half than a hand's width; the hand itself came back longer-fingered than *small and short-fingered* asked for, so the ratio moved the read without landing the clause exactly; and the dish carries water, which is the half-ring's bowl arriving inside the dish. The freckles are the medium's granulation and are not canon on him (author ruling, s34).

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the open stone court of a temple on a grey autumn morning,
as loose wash suggestion — wet-looking flagstones, a pale stone wall
behind, the far side of the court going into quiet wash. Flat
overcast daylight, no sun and no shadows with edges, and it is the
only light in the picture.

Composition: close on the boy's head, shoulders and one hand, seen
from the side, his head tipped down toward his own hand. The near
edge of a low table crosses the bottom of the frame, and on it a
shallow round iron dish about two hands across, seen from slightly
above so its far rim shows, with a plain round copper bar the length
of a man's forearm lying on a folded cloth beside it.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: his right hand is open, fingers together
and a little spread, held a hand's width above the copper bar and
touching nothing. His left hand is down at his side and out of
frame. Nothing is held in either hand.

THE STITCH OF LIGHT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE AND IT IS DRAWN AS
BARE UNPAINTED PAPER. One single straight thread of white runs from
his fingertips to the copper bar, a hand's width long, no thicker
than a drawn line, unbroken from end to end — reserved paper with
the ink drawing stopping at its edges, not white paint. NO FORK, no
branch, no zigzag, no second strand, no scattered sparks, and no
halo or bloom of light around it. Nothing in the picture is lit by
it.

HIS ATTENTION IS ENTIRELY ON HIS OWN HAND. Eyes down, the lids
lowered, the head tipped to it. Still, mouth closed, the face not
yet caught up with what has happened — a boy recognising something
he turns out to have known for years. NOT delighted, NOT triumphant,
NOT proud, NOT pleased — and equally NOT frightened, NOT startled,
NOT alarmed, and not blank. There is nobody in the frame he could
be looking at.

HE IS ELEVEN AND THE FACE CARRIES IT: the eyes sit a little below
the vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still below it; the jaw has not come in, the cheeks are
still full under the bone, the neck narrow. His hand is a child's,
small and short-fingered, and the copper bar is longer than his
forearm.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest
value in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home. Plain
undyed working wool, coarse and much mended, the collar loose at the
throat — the ink carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the
seams, the patches and the mended places. No ornament and no metal.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: his skin and the
copper bar carry the only warm colour, and the iron, the flagstones,
the wall, the cloth and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue
with no brown in them. His face and his open hand are the most
finished things in the picture and carry the sharpest step from
light to dark. The court and the rest of the table stay at quiet
middle values with soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there
competes for finish. The ink linework stays the strongest element
throughout and does not blur into the wash.

NO OTHER FACE IS DRAWN. The other children stand behind him as dark
summary shapes at the edges of the frame, loosely washed, with no
features, and there is no adult anywhere in the picture.
```

### PLATE: the-fan-in-the-rain

*Candidate 3.* Anchor: `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`. Ch. 11 canon: *She came to the middle with her waster in one hand and the off-hand open at her shoulder, and made the taught salute — and before its echo finished, her open palm was burning.* And the instant this draws: *Between sweeps the fire did not go out. It stood at her palm, a handspan of flame burning on nothing at all — no wick, no wood, rain falling past it — the way a lamp burns when somebody is paying for it and intends to keep paying all night. Her breathing had not changed since the salute.* The stance is the chapter's own: *weapon hand and art hand, the waster forward, the off-hand open and empty at the shoulder, for the Art.*

**IT IS SIDE-LIT, DELIBERATELY, AND THAT IS WHAT KEEPS IT OFF `ch09-aurelians-candle`.** Both are a child with fire near the face; the candle is a soft sheet of orange under the chin and the whole design problem there was a boy inside the lighting convention for villainy. **Here the flame stands at her own shoulder height, so it lights the near side of the face across, not up** — a different picture of a different child, and the difference is written as which surfaces take the light.

**THE WARM SOURCE IS NEAR, SO IT WILL LIGHT HER**, which is settled (§What a warm source will and will not light). What is written is how far it reaches: her palm, her forearm, the near side of her face and throat, and nothing else — the wall does not take it.

**FIRE STANDING ON A PALM IS THE NOUN PROBLEM AGAIN.** Every instance the renderer knows has something burning in it. The construction goes first with a measurement, then the fuel is killed by name: no wick, no candle, no lamp, no torch, no wood, no coal, nothing in the hand.

**HER CHEEK-TELL IS NOT SPENT HERE AND MUST NOT BE READ INTO THE RUN.** `../canon-brief.md` reserves *spots of colour high on her cheeks* for Ch. 13 and Ch. 17. **Warm firelight landing on a pale face is not that tell**, and the block deliberately writes no negation about cheeks: nothing in the picture pulls toward a flush, and §A negation puts the word in the prompt is the reason not to introduce one.

**THE BOUND HAIR IS DOING PRODUCTION WORK THREE CHAPTERS EARLY.** `../README.md`'s separation method has Elarine bound and Valeria loose, and Valeria arrives in Ch. 14. Every plate of Elarine that keeps the binding severe is paying that debt in advance.

**AND THIS PICTURE TESTS THE s43 HYPOTHESIS.** `ch10/the-waterer`'s demotion failed in flat open daylight, and the guess written on that sheet was that a rival needs somewhere to go. **A dark wet yard with one small warm source is the opposite condition**, so if the demotion holds here that is the second data point the hypothesis was waiting for — and if it fails here too, the hypothesis is wrong and the note says so.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch11-the-fan-in-the-rain.png`, first run (author, s44: *adopt both*).**

**THE AGE AND THE GAZE STAND AS DRAWN.** Both were put to the author as faults with a re-run costed against them, and the picture was adopted over both. Same scope as every ruling of this kind in the folder: **it is a ruling on the picture, and it moves nothing.** The findings underneath it are unaffected and are the more valuable half — the rung needs a ratio beside it, and a plate that empties its frame has to say where the subject is looking.

**RUN 1 (s44).**

**ALMOST ALL OF IT LANDED.** The flame stands upright off the flat of the palm with a clear gap of air under it and nothing burning — no wick, no candle, nothing in the hand — which is the noun problem solved on the first run. Rain crosses the whole frame and is reserved as bare paper; the wet gleam on the flagstones is reserved with it; the wooden sword is low in the right hand and crosses the bottom edge; the hair is bound tight at the nape with nothing loose; the clothing is close, buttoned high, no ornament and no metal; nobody else is in the picture. **The flame is the brightest thing and her face is the most finished**, and the wall and the flagstones stayed quiet behind them.

**AND THAT SETTLES THE s43 HYPOTHESIS IN ITS FAVOUR — SECOND DATA POINT.** `ch10/the-waterer`'s demotion failed in flat open daylight and the guess was that **a rival needs somewhere to go**. This is the opposite condition — a dark wet yard, one small warm source — and the same clause held completely. Recorded on both sheets; two runs in agreement, opposite conditions, one cause.

**THE EXPERIMENT FAILED, AND IT IS THE USEFUL RESULT.** This pair was built to test §A close-up plate and the age line by writing the eye-line rung into the block body. **The rung was written and she came back a young woman** — long jaw, defined chin, long neck, none of the fullness the block asked for. She does not read eleven and she does not read fourteen either: **the face drifted past its own anchor.**

**SO THE ANCHOR RULING COSTS MORE THAN IT LOOKED LIKE COSTING**, and it is worth saying plainly because it is the author's ruling and this is the first evidence under it: a close plate does not merely inherit the anchor's age, it drifts above it, and the rung does not pull it back. **What the block used alongside the rung were adjectives** — *the jaw has not come in, the cheeks still full, the neck slender* — and §Scale between figures is unambiguous that adjectives lose and ratios win. The rung is a ratio inside the head; everything else in that paragraph was not. **If a re-run is wanted, the age instrument is a ratio against something else in the frame** — her own hand against the flame, the grip of the sword against her fist — not more description of the face.

**AND THE GAZE WENT TO THE VIEWER, WHICH IS THE BLOCK'S FAULT.** She is looking straight out, so the picture reads as a portrait of a person rather than a plate of a moment — the exact line `../README.md` draws between the two. The block named the absence of everybody else (correctly, per §Plates) **and then gave her eyes nowhere to go.** An absence instruction has a consequence for the gaze, and where a plate empties the frame it has to say what the subject is looking at, or the subject looks at you. On the page her eyes are on Kael, three strides off and out of frame: *her eyes doing over the flames what his own were doing: pricing.*

**THE FLAME IS TALLER THAN ITS MEASUREMENT** — nearer two hand-spans than one, and tapering — and the light on her face is fainter than the block asked for, because the head is turned away from the source toward the viewer. **The second follows from the first fault**: fix the gaze and the side-lighting arrives with it.

```
Image 1: elarine-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.

Setting: a walled stone training yard in falling rain, as loose wash
suggestion — wet flagstones, a low stone wall behind her going into
cool shadow, the far corners lost in wash. Flat grey rain-light, and
the only other light in the picture is the flame below.

Composition: chest-up, the girl turned three-quarters toward the
viewer with her open left hand raised to her own shoulder height, so
that her hand and her face stand side by side in the frame at the
same height.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her left hand is open, palm up, fingers
straight, holding nothing. Her right hand holds one plain wooden
practice sword and nothing else, carried low and crossing the bottom
edge of the frame. Nothing else is in either hand.

THE FLAME STANDS ON THE OPEN PALM AND IT IS BURNING ON NOTHING. One
body of flame stands upright off the flat of her palm, as tall as
the span from her thumb to her little finger, with a clear gap of
air between the flame and her skin. NO WICK, no candle, no lamp, no
torch, no wood and no coal — there is nothing there to burn and
nothing in her hand. It stands straight and holds its size: not
guttering, not streaming sideways, not spreading.

RAIN FALLS PAST THE FLAME AND DOES NOT TOUCH IT. Fine rain crosses
the whole frame in one direction as flicked spatter and dry-brush
drag, falling past the flame and through its light, and the flame is
unchanged by it. No steam and no smoke anywhere in the picture.

THE FLAME IS THE ONLY WARM LIGHT AND IT REACHES ONLY HER: the palm,
the inside of the raised forearm, and the near side of the face and
throat, lit from the side at her own height and not from below.
Everything else stays cold — the rain, the flagstones, the wall, the
wooden sword and all her clothing in cool grey and grey-blue with no
brown in them. The wall behind her takes none of the warm light.

HER FACE IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Attention fully engaged and
giving nothing back, mouth closed and still, brows unmoved, the
breathing easy — a girl who has not hurried once and does not intend
to. NOT fierce, NOT angry, NOT triumphant, NOT strained, NOT
effortful — and equally NOT cold, NOT haughty, NOT bored, NOT tired.
Nothing has been decided in the face.

SHE IS ELEVEN AND SMALL FOR IT: the eyes sit a little below the
vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still below it; the jaw has not come in, the cheeks are
still full under the bone, the neck slender, the shoulders narrow.
Finely built, with nothing spare on her anywhere.

Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral black with no blue in it, drawn back
severely and bound tight at the nape, wet with the rain, nothing
loose anywhere. Pale indoor skin, and the only warm colour on it is
the flame's. Eyes very dark, near black. Plain dark close-fitting
clothing of very good quality, exactly made and buttoned high at the
throat. No ornament, no metal, no badge and no white anywhere on
her.

THE FLAME IS THE BRIGHTEST THING IN THE PICTURE AND HER FACE IS THE
MOST FINISHED. The flame is soft-edged and loose with no hard edge
and no detail in it, and the sharpest step from light to dark
happens along the lit boundary of her face. The yard, the wall and
the flagstones stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no
detail, and nothing back there competes for finish. The falling rain
and the wet gleam on the flagstones are reserved as bare unpainted
paper. The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and
does not blur into the wash.

THERE IS NO OTHER PERSON IN THE PICTURE — no opponent, nobody
watching at the edge of the yard, and nobody in the background.
```

### PLATE: the-arrival

*Candidate 1.* Anchor: `images/full-figures/aeliana-15-full.png`. Ch. 22 canon: *A girl came in from the seaward side* … *The imperial guard went first, white and imperial blue* … *Then twelve who were not that. They came in a heavy dark grey with nothing whatever on it, six and six, and they were not marching, and not one of them looked at the crowd* … *They were arranged across the distances — the near ones close enough to take her weight off her feet, the far ones out at the edges of the ground.* … *She came out onto the stone in a blue so dark it read black until the light moved on it, with gold at the shoulder and the hem* … *and her hair up off her neck in a construction that had plainly taken more than one person more than an hour. / Eleven thousand people stood up.*

**THE SUBJECT IS ELEVEN THOUSAND PEOPLE STANDING UP**, and she is the small thing in the middle of it. That is the composition the chapter argues for and it is also the one that keeps her fence: at this distance there is no face to deliver a verdict with, and what the picture says about her is what the page says — that a very large number of people are glad about something they will never be nearer to than this.

**THE WINNER IS SMALL AND IS WON BY COLOUR AND EDGE.** §The winner and the rival, with the largest rival the folder has ever staged: an entire banked arena. The crowd goes to soft middle values with no drawn faces anywhere; the sand is bare paper; and the one hard-edged, saturated thing in the frame is the gold at her shoulder and hem against the near-black blue.

**THE ONLY PLATE IN THE BOOK WITH A CROWD IN IT.** `ch12/furniture` proved summary shapes behind four resolved figures; this one has no resolved figures at all, which is untested and is the risk the block is spending.

girl of fourteen walking alone in the middle of the ground, her hands
empty and at rest, her head level. Her hair is silver-white,
colourless, with no yellow or gold in it, and it is dressed up off her
neck in an elaborate construction. Her gown is a blue so dark it reads
black until the light moves on it, with dense gold at both shoulders
and along the hem, and the gold is the only saturated colour anywhere
in the picture.

Her gown and the gold carry the sharpest step from light to dark and
the hardest edges in the frame. The crowd, the seating, the guards and
the stone all stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no
detail; the raked sand is reserved as bare unpainted paper. Nothing in
the crowd is drawn sharply enough to read as a person.

Preserve her build, her hair colour and her apparent age as shown in
the reference image. She is small and slight. No armour and no weapon

### PLATE: kael-of-the-island

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's last line.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 22 canon: *"Kael," the herald read out, "of the Island." / Kael walked out onto the sand. / He was thirteen years old. He had wanted, since he was four, to be seen; had wanted, since he was ten, to be measured in front of people who could tell; had spent every hour of it being told what it costs to be looked at, by two people who had built him out of not being. He came out into the middle of a white floor with eleven thousand people over him and heard the first sound the world ever made about him, and the sound was a question.* And the state: *Kael discovered, standing in the north curve with his heart already going, that none of that frightened him, and that the not being frightened was itself worth a look.*

**THE FACE IS A BOY WHO IS NOT FRIGHTENED, AND THE PAGE SAYS SO IN THOSE WORDS.** Not brave, not proud, not braced — the chapter's own finding is that the fear did not arrive and that its absence was itself worth noticing. That is a specific face and the block writes it as one.

**NOTHING IS IN HIS HANDS.** The page does not say what he carries onto the sand, and where the page is silent the plate answers with framing: the crop is chest-up, the hands are out of it, and no weapon, wrap or bundle appears anywhere. It also keeps a stated age away from any arms after `ch21/dead`.

**IT IS THE OPPOSITE PICTURE TO ITS PAIR.** One face filling the frame with the crowd behind it as wash, against eleven thousand shapes with no face in them at all — and the crowd is the same crowd, seen from the two ends of the same quarter of an hour.

**THE PAIR IS THE EMPIRE LOOKING AT ITSELF AND THE BOY IT HAS NO WORDS FOR.** Eleven thousand people standing for a girl who was made to be looked at by exactly that many at exactly that distance — and, a quarter of an hour later, the same arena being handed a name with nothing after it.

**AELIANA'S FENCE IS KEPT BY DISTANCE, NOT BY WORDS.** `../README.md`: her beauty may never arrive as a verdict, so a portrait *plate* of her is illegal and a reference is legal. The first block puts her in the middle distance in a crowd shot where **her face is not resolved at all** — the picture is of an arena standing up, not of a face. Her Ch. 22 canon is spent here for the first time and completely: *a blue so dark it read black until the light moved on it, with gold at the shoulder and the hem in a quantity that had no function except to be a quantity, and her hair up off her neck.*

**AND THE REFERENCE IS ATTACHED ANYWAY, WHICH IS NOT A CONTRADICTION.** It carries the medium, her build, her apparent age and the exact silver-white — *colourless, never blonde* — none of which the prompt can be trusted to hold alone. The gown is authored over it, because the Ch. 22 gown is not the reference's dress and §What the reference carries says costume bleeds where identity does not.

**THE TWELVE ARE WHY CANDIDATE 3 DOES NOT NEED ITS OWN PLATE.** §Four figures puts the tested ceiling at four resolved figures with the rest as summary shapes, and twelve household guard *arranged across the distances* are exactly that: shapes at the edges of the ground, spread wide, not marching, nobody looking at the crowd. They do their work inside the first block at no cost.

**THE SECOND BLOCK STATES AN AGE BESIDE NO WEAPON.** After `ch21/dead`, ages sitting next to arms are treated with suspicion; nothing is carried, nothing is drawn and nothing is in his hands in this picture, and the age is worth stating because thirteen is a year older than every plate before it.

### PLATE: the-arrival

*Candidate 1.* Anchor: `images/full-figures/aeliana-15-full.png`. Ch. 22 canon: *A girl came in from the seaward side* … *The imperial guard went first, white and imperial blue* … *Then twelve who were not that. They came in a heavy dark grey with nothing whatever on it, six and six, and they were not marching, and not one of them looked at the crowd* … *They were arranged across the distances — the near ones close enough to take her weight off her feet, the far ones out at the edges of the ground.* … *She came out onto the stone in a blue so dark it read black until the light moved on it, with gold at the shoulder and the hem* … *and her hair up off her neck in a construction that had plainly taken more than one person more than an hour. / Eleven thousand people stood up.*

**THE SUBJECT IS ELEVEN THOUSAND PEOPLE STANDING UP**, and she is the small thing in the middle of it. That is the composition the chapter argues for and it is also the one that keeps her fence: at this distance there is no face to deliver a verdict with, and what the picture says about her is what the page says — that a very large number of people are glad about something they will never be nearer to than this.

**THE WINNER IS SMALL AND IS WON BY COLOUR AND EDGE.** §The winner and the rival, with the largest rival the folder has ever staged: an entire banked arena. The crowd goes to soft middle values with no drawn faces anywhere; the sand is bare paper; and the one hard-edged, saturated thing in the frame is the gold at her shoulder and hem against the near-black blue.

**THE ONLY PLATE IN THE BOOK WITH A CROWD IN IT.** `ch12/furniture` proved summary shapes behind four resolved figures; this one has no resolved figures at all, which is untested and is the risk the block is spending.


**RAN AND REJECTED (author, s48: *i ran the arrival, not a fan*).** The block and its argument stay on the sheet, as `ch12/the-hole-through-the-tree` does, so the reasoning survives and nobody re-proposes it. **Not a fence problem and not an execution note** — the composition did what it said it would; the picture was not worth the plate. *Replaced the same session by `the-box`, which is the author's own proposal: closer to her, seated, above the games.*

### PLATE-VERBATIM: kael-of-the-island

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's last line.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 22 canon: *"Kael," the herald read out, "of the Island." / Kael walked out onto the sand. / He was thirteen years old. He had wanted, since he was four, to be seen; had wanted, since he was ten, to be measured in front of people who could tell; had spent every hour of it being told what it costs to be looked at, by two people who had built him out of not being. He came out into the middle of a white floor with eleven thousand people over him and heard the first sound the world ever made about him, and the sound was a question.* And the state: *Kael discovered, standing in the north curve with his heart already going, that none of that frightened him, and that the not being frightened was itself worth a look.*

**COMPOSED AS A CHEST-UP CLOSE AND RECUT WIDE BEFORE IT RAN (author, s48: *a close up fails to display the grand arena, and Kael's arrival in it — a further shot where we can see the massive crowd and arena, blue sky*).** The first version put his face across the frame with the arena as wash behind it, and the author is right that it throws away the thing the moment is made of. **The scale is the subject, so the scale gets the frame.**

**THE PICTURE IS ONE SMALL DARK MARK ON A LARGE WHITE FLOOR.** The raked sand is reserved as bare unpainted paper and the boy standing alone on it is the darkest thing in the frame — §The winner and the rival run at the largest ratio the folder has attempted: a figure a fraction of the frame's height winning it by being the only hard mark on the brightest field. It is the same instrument as `ch11/the-white-stitch` turned inside out, white ground and dark subject.

**AND THE VIEWER IS ONE OF THE ELEVEN THOUSAND.** The camera sits up in the banked seating with the backs and heads of spectators across the near corner, looking down and across the oval — so *eleven thousand people over him* is where the reader is standing rather than something the caption has to say. **The pair now works from two opposite seats:** the arrival from the floor looking across, the walk-out from the stands looking down.

**NO FACE IN EITHER PLATE, AND THAT IS PRICED.** A faceless candidate is priced, not disqualified (s43). This chapter is the empire looking at itself and a boy being handed to it, and neither of those is a face doing something; candidate 5, the hand at the gate mouth, is where the chapter's face lives if the set ever wants one.

**NOTHING IS IN HIS HANDS.** The page does not say what he carries onto the sand, and where the page is silent the plate answers with framing: nothing is carried and nothing is drawn.


**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch22-kael-of-the-island.png`, FIRST RUN OF THE WIDE RECUT (author, s48: *YES*).**

**THE FOLDER'S FIRST PLATE WITH NO RESOLVED FACE IN IT, AND IT WORKED FIRST TIME.** Every part of the stack arrived as written: the spectators' backs and heads across the near corner, the whole white oval below, rank on rank of packed seating all round, the sea beyond the far rim and cold blue sky filling the top. **No face is drawn anywhere in eleven thousand people**, and nothing in the crowd resolves into a person.

**THE INVERTED RESERVE IS PROVEN.** The sand came back as bare unpainted paper and the two small figures on it are the darkest, hardest marks in the frame — `ch11/the-white-stitch`'s instrument turned inside out, dark subject on the brightest field, at a ratio far past anything the folder had attempted. **So the reserve is not only for small pale things on dark grounds**, which is what `ch21/the-surge`'s failure looked like it might mean; it fails when it covers its own object and works when it is the ground.

**AND THE CAMERA DID THE CAPTION'S WORK.** *Eleven thousand people over him* is where the reader is sitting. Nothing in the picture has to assert the scale.

**WHAT DRIFTED, AND IT COSTS NOTHING.** The boy reads as standing out in the middle of the floor rather than a few steps out of a gate in the near side, and the gate relation is not legible. The two figures came back even smaller than the block asked for, which is the picture's whole power.

**THE FENCE, NAMED, AND WHERE THIS BLOCK SITS RELATIVE TO IT.** `../README.md`: her beauty may never arrive as a verdict, so a portrait *plate* of her is illegal while a reference is legal. **A chest-up close on her face would cross it.** This is a seated half-figure whose subject is the arrangement she is sitting inside — the rail, the guards at the frame's edges, the sand and the crowd below her — and **her eyes go down to the games, so the picture is her looking at something rather than the reader looking at her.** The chapter supplies the reading itself: a thing made to be looked at by exactly this many people at exactly this distance. Drawing the making is not delivering a verdict on it.

**HER HANDS ARE THE OTHER HALF OF THE FENCE AND THEY ARE CANON.** `../canon-brief.md`: *Her hands do nothing: unoccupied, holding nothing, at rest.* In a chapter where every other person in the arena is working — guards placed across the distances, a herald reading, a boy walking out — a pair of hands with nothing to do is the whole argument, and it is the one thing the picture does not have to say out loud.

**THE BOX ITSELF IS THE ONE INFERENCE.** The page says she sat and does not say where; it puts the host's box on the western curve with the First Master in it. So the box here is generic — stone, a rail, nobody else in it but her guards — and **no second identifiable person is in the frame**, which keeps the plate from claiming a seating arrangement the page never made.

# Ch. 15 — The Hours · plate sheet

**Both plates composed at s45 and neither has run.** Kael is twelve, Aurelian twelve and built a year ahead of it. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/aurelian-14.png`.

**THIS IS THE FIRST CHAPTER WHOSE SHORTLIST COLLIDES WITH THE s45 RULING.** All three of `candidates.md`'s entries are a back, a pair of hands on a block, or a wide staging of people walking away — **no face doing anything in any of them**, and the ruling is that a chest-up with a face is the default and everything else has to earn itself. **One earns it and the other two do not**, so the pair is candidate 1 plus a beat the list did not carry.

**THE PAIR IS WHAT THE SUMMER COST AND WHAT IT BOUGHT.** A body with the marks stopping exactly where the shirt does; and a boy who has never in his life laughed at nothing laughing at nothing, in a court he was not born to be in.

**THE ALTERNATE IS THE BEST FACE IN THE CHAPTER AND IT IS NOT DRAWABLE.** *His father was standing over him with the blade at his side, absolutely still, looking at him. Not at the mistake. At him. / Kael had been read by that face his whole life and had never once seen the thing that was in it now, and did not have anywhere to put it.* **The page names the face as the subject and then refuses to say what is in it** — and an image cannot decline to answer, so any close-up of it spends what the chapter deliberately withholds. THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE settles it: *if a moment cannot be composed without spending something unspent, the moment is wrong.*

**One framing answer does exist and is passed over on set grounds.** Put the lamp behind the man so his face goes dark and draw the boy's face reading a face the reader cannot see. **It works, and it restages `ch14/one-finger` one chapter later** — a child on the ground, an adult standing over him, a blade in the adult's hand — with a night yard and a fallen boy adding refusal risk on top. Recorded so nobody rediscovers it; the moment stays available if the author wants it.

**AND TWO MORE OF THIS CHAPTER'S IMAGES ARE DELIBERATE NON-CHOICES.** *Twice that summer he came in from the dark and his mother was in the doorway... one hand on the frame and the light behind her... her knuckles were pale on the wood* is **`ch02-the-doorframe` again, almost line for line** — the book makes that rhyme in prose on purpose, and drawing it twice spends it. And the calibration — grown men in the capital's grey striking a twelve-year-old with weighted wasters, forty times before breakfast — is the chapter's most terrible image and sits squarely in the neighbourhood of §Some moments are undrawable's one refusal. **Not attempted, and not owed a test.**

**`candidates.md`'s third, *nobody stands beside her*, is held.** It is the chapter's title-idea and its last movement, and it is a wide staging of people walking away — **and it would be Valeria's third plate in two chapters.** It stays on the list.

**NEITHER BLOCK CARRIES AN AGE INSTRUMENT.** Eight runs now: no rung and no ratio has moved a face at portrait scale, `ch14/one-finger` shipped without one and read no worse, and the anchor is the lever, which is an author ruling.

### PLATE: the-sons-back

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 15 canon: *He was hit a great deal that summer. The blunted steel did not cut and it did not need to; it went into the meat of the arm and the ribs and the outside of the thigh and left marks that came up black and went yellow and were replaced before they finished going. He learned to dress in the loft with his back to the ladder.* And the thing the picture exists for: *in a whole summer of it his father never once put a mark anywhere above the collar or below the wrist. / Not once. Not by accident, either... Everything the Temple would see was left alone. Everything the shirt covered was fair.*

**IT IS THE DELIBERATE RHYME WITH `ch03-the-back`, TWELVE CHAPTERS LATER.** That plate is the father stripped at the trough, read the way you read goat paths — a seam, a through-wound, the tally-marks. **This one is the son, and the whole difference is that his stop.** Same crop, same read-the-body composition, one generation on. `candidates.md` calls it and it is right: the father's marks are a record somebody else wrote, and the son's are an argument his father is making about what the Temple may see.

**AND THAT IS WHY THE FACELESSNESS IS EARNED RATHER THAN PRICED** (`../README.md`, s45). The subject is a line on a body — where the marks stop — and **the line only reads if the untouched skin above the collar and below the wrist is in the same frame.** A chest-up with a face would crop away half the evidence. The picture is the argument; the argument is anatomical.

**THE COMPARISON IS STAGED, NOT STATED.** Marked skin and unmarked skin, both in frame, adjacent, at the same depth — the neck and the hands clean, everything between them marked. That is the one thing this folder knows how to make a renderer perform (§A close-up plate and the age line, and the copper bar).

**LIGHT FROM BELOW, FROM THE ONE PLACE THE LOFT HAS ANY.** The ladder hole, with the room's lamp under it, is the loft's only source and it throws upward — proven since `ch05/the-fact-lands`. **It also puts the light behind and below him**, raking across the back, which is what makes bruising read at all. §What a warm source will and will not light: the source is close and low, so it lights him and nothing else, and the cool goes on what it does not reach.

**RUN 1 (s45), NOT ADOPTED — re-run with one paragraph added.**

**THE ARGUMENT CAME BACK WHOLE.** Bruises of many ages across the back, the shoulder blades and the upper arms, purple and black and gone yellow-green, laid over one another with no pattern and no broken skin. **The neck clean above them and both hands clean below them**, so the line the plate exists for is legible in one look. The loft dark, the light coming up warm from below and reserved as bare paper where it enters, the shirt in his hand mended at every seam, and nobody else in the picture.

**AND THE BLOCK'S COLLAR LINE WAS ANATOMICALLY WRONG, WHICH THE RUN QUIETLY CORRECTED.** It asked for *the back of the neck, the tops of the shoulders* to be clean **above the line of a shirt collar** — but a shirt covers the shoulders, and the page says *never once above the collar or below the wrist.* The run marked the shoulder caps and left the neck clean, **which is the page and not the block.** Corrected here; the picture was right.

**MISS: IT READS AS A GROWN MAN'S BACK, AND IT IS THE COST OF A DECISION THIS SESSION MADE WITH ITS EYES OPEN.** The age instrument was dropped at `ch14/one-finger` on eight runs of evidence — **all of them faces.** A faceless body plate is a different case entirely: the anchor is head-and-shoulders, so **nothing below the collar is anchored to anything**, and the whole body was authored from *a lean, hard, worked body, strong without bulk.* §Scale between figures is unambiguous that adjectives lose, and `ch09/the-roller-alone` already found that a solo figure has no age in the frame unless one is written. **So: the rung is dead for faces and alive for bodies**, and the two questions were wrongly treated as one.

**THE RE-RUN KEEPS THE BUILD, ON AN AUTHOR NOTE: *I think the back is good, Kael is pretty strong.*** The instinct is canon — `../canon-brief.md` has him *lean, hard, visibly fit; strong without bulk.* **So the paragraph added is proportion, not slightness:** shoulders about two of his own head-widths across rather than three, the frame unfinished, the blades and lower ribs standing, the muscle flat rather than filled out — **a strong boy, not a smaller man.** The head is now kept in frame, back of the skull only, because a ratio the frame cannot perform is the one failure mode this folder has proved four times over. **If run 2 buys the age by spending the strength, run 1 is the better picture and stands.**

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the sleeping loft of a small mountain house at night, as
loose wash suggestion — bare roof timbers close overhead, straw and a
folded blanket, the corners going into deep shadow. The open ladder
hole is in the floor behind him with the room's lamp somewhere below
it, out of frame: the light comes up through that hole from below and
behind him, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: the boy stands with his back square to the viewer,
stripped to the waist, seen from directly behind and cropped at the
hips and just above the crown of his head, so that the back of his
head is in frame and no part of his face is. A shirt hangs from one
hand at his side. He fills the frame.

THE MARKS AND WHERE THEY STOP ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Across the
back of the ribs, the shoulder blades and the backs of both upper arms
lie many bruises of very different ages, some almost black and some
gone yellow-green, some fading and some new, laid over one another in
no pattern. They are flat under the skin and the skin is not broken
anywhere: no cuts, no blood.

WHERE THEY STOP IS THE WHOLE PICTURE, AND BOTH SIDES OF THE LINE ARE
IN FRAME. The back of his neck is completely clean and unmarked, and
below the wrists both hands are completely clean and unmarked. Every
mark on him lies between those two lines, and the change is abrupt.

HE IS TWELVE AND STRONG FOR IT, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS HIS OWN HEAD:
his shoulders are about two of his own head-widths across, not three.
A hard, worked, lean body with real strength in it and none of a grown
man's depth or mass — the frame not finished, the blades and the lower
ribs standing under the skin, the muscle flat and stringy
rather than filled out. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home. The
shirt in his hand is plain undyed working wool, coarse and much
mended, and the ink carries it at the seams and the mended places.

THE LAMPLIGHT COMES UP FROM THE HOLE BEHIND HIM AND REACHES HIM ONLY:
his back and the backs of his arms take the warm light, raking across
from below so the bruises stand out against it, and the timbers, the
straw, the far wall and the corners of the loft stay cool grey and
grey-blue with no brown in them.

HIS BACK IS THE MOST FINISHED THING IN THE PICTURE and carries the
sharpest step from light to dark. The loft around him stays at quiet
middle values with soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there
competes for finish. The lit edge of the ladder hole is reserved as
bare unpainted paper. The ink linework stays the strongest element and
does not blur into the wash.

There is nobody else in the picture and nobody on the ladder behind
him.
```

### PLATE: the-unaimed-laugh

*Not on `candidates.md`; proposed at s45 under the ruling that the chapter's shortlist has no face in it.* Anchors: `images/portraits/aurelian-14.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 15 canon: *Aurelian stood in the middle of the cistern court with soot on his forearms, and something went across his face that his tier would not have recognized, and he laughed. It was a real laugh, short, surprised out of him, the first one Kael had ever heard from him that was not aimed at anybody.* And, in the same breath: *Kael's breath went to the long shape before he could stop it. He caught it, put it back, and hoped the count had not showed.*

**IT IS THE FIRST PICTURE IN THE BOOK OF THESE TWO NOT FIGHTING.** Three years of the yard, a hall, a kettle joke and an apology measured to the inch — and here they are filthy in a disused court with the light going, one of them laughing at nothing and the other one hiding a breath. **The chapter's whole transaction is in it:** *Kael got a magnifying glass. Aurelian got the why.*

**THE TWO FACES ARE WRITTEN AS A CONTRADICTION, WHICH §Getting expression SAYS TO STATE OUTRIGHT.** One face wide open and surprised out of itself; the other giving nothing at all, in the same light, at arm's length. *State contradictions as contradictions* is the documented instruction and this is a two-face version of it.

**AND THE GAZES GO DIFFERENT WAYS ON PURPOSE, BOTH AS ANATOMY** (§Getting expression, as corrected at s45): **the laugh is not aimed at anybody**, so Aurelian's head goes back and his eyes go up and away — and Kael's eyes are on him. **One-sided gaze is easier than mutual and often more interesting**, which the system already says, and here it is the actual content: the boy who cannot be laughed at any more, watched by the boy who is not going to show anything.

**`../canon-brief.md` DOES THE REST AND IT IS UNUSUALLY SPECIFIC ON HIM.** Never sneering, never smug, never cruel — *pleasant, open, easy, genuinely charming* — so the one thing this plate must not do is make a real laugh look like a jeer. His warmth lives in his colouring and is never stated; **his ground stays cool**, which is written into the palette here rather than left to chance.

**NO INSIGNIA ON HIM ANYWHERE.** `07/planted-clues` reserves the Corvane insignia for the Part IV fire, and good cloth well cut is the whole of his station.

```
Image 1: aurelian-14.png — portrait reference of the fair-haired boy.
Image 2: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Setting: a disused stone cistern court in the last of a summer
afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a low wall, dry flagstones,
scrub coming up through the joints, the far side of the court going
into cool shade. Late low daylight from one side, and it is the only
light in the picture.

Composition: chest-up, two boys close together in the near foreground
filling the frame, turned three quarters toward the viewer. The
fair-haired boy stands nearer the middle; the dark-haired boy stands
at his shoulder, half a pace behind and slightly smaller in the frame.

THE FAIR-HAIRED BOY IS LAUGHING AND IT IS A REAL ONE, SURPRISED OUT OF
HIM: the head gone back a little, the mouth open, the eyes creased and
almost shut, the whole face open. It is not aimed at anybody. NOT
sneering, NOT smug, NOT mocking, and not posed.

THE DARK-HAIRED BOY IS GIVING NOTHING BACK, AND THE TWO FACES SIDE BY
SIDE ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Still, the mouth closed and
relaxed, the brows unmoved, the face at rest — a boy holding a breath
where nobody can see him do it. NOT laughing, NOT smiling, NOT sullen,
and not blank.

WHERE EACH IS LOOKING: the fair-haired boy's eyes go up and away past
the top of the frame at nothing. The dark-haired boy's eyes are on the
fair-haired boy's face. Neither is looking at the viewer.

All four hands are open and empty at their sides. Nothing is held by
anybody and there is no weapon anywhere in the picture.

The fair-haired boy has dark-gold hair, well cut and groomed, and
amber gold-brown eyes; clear well-fed skin with sun on it, and soot
black on both his forearms and the backs of his hands. He is tall and
already broad through the shoulder, a head taller than the other boy and heavier through the frame. He wears good plain cloth,
well cut and well kept, with no badge, no chain and no ornament of any
kind on him.

The dark-haired boy has near-white neutral grey eyes, cool and
colourless, the lightest value in his face; dark hair cut short and
uneven, and dust on him. He wears plain undyed working wool, coarse and much mended,
the ink carrying it at the seams. The good cloth belongs only to the
fair-haired boy and the mended wool only to the dark-haired one.

THE PICTURE STAYS COOL AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: their skin and the
fair-haired boy's hair carry the only warm colour, and the walls, the
flagstones, the scrub and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue
with no brown in them.

THE TWO FACES ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN THE PICTURE and carry
the sharpest step from light to dark. The wall, the court and the
scrub stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no detail. The
lit tops of the wall and the flagstones are reserved as bare unpainted
paper. The ink linework stays the strongest element and does not blur
into the wash.

Preserve both boys exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features and do not
mix their clothing.
```

# Ch. 36 — The Quiet · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register. These are the last two plates of Book One.** Kael is thirteen; Wren is a day and a night old. Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, with `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE SMALLEST THING IN THE BOOK AND THE LARGEST ABSENCE IN IT.** A hand the size of a thumb-joint shut on one finger, and an island with nothing lit on it. **Nothing happens in either picture**, which after Ch. 33, 34 and 35 is the point: the chapter is called The Quiet and the set should go quiet with it.

**AND THEY ARE THE ONLY TWO PLATES IN PART IV NOT LIT BY SOMETHING BURNING.** Ch. 27 to Ch. 35 is fog, then fire, then a man, then a lamp, then a bowl, then the sea lit from underneath. **These two are ordinary afternoon daylight and then no light at all.** The set has earned a plain picture.

**THE LAST PLATE CLOSES A RHYME THE FOLDER OPENED AT `ch01`.** `ch01/fallen-stars` is a boy on this mountain being turned to look down at a town lying in a heap of warm light on the black water. `ch32/the-lights-on-the-mountain` is the same boy on the same ground with the lights coming up at him. **This is the third:** the same water from the other side, and *no lights came on anywhere on that island at all.* Three pictures, one view, and the whole of Book One between them.

**THE ONE BAN IN IT IS THE PICTURE.** §Absence says ban what the frame pulls toward and nothing else. A shore at night pulls toward lights harder than almost anything in this folder, so the block spends its single absence there and spends it plainly: **no point of light, no glow, no fire and nothing lit anywhere on that island.**

**THE LINEN IS DRAWN IN BOTH PLATES AND WHAT IS UNDER IT IS NOT.** *The linen was still across the eyes. Kael did not touch it. Nobody on that boat had touched it, and nobody had said one word about why it was there.* The strip is page canon from Ch. 33 and the eye beneath it is spent in exactly one paragraph of one chapter. **A plate that shows the strip and not the eye is the page**, and the reader who has read Ch. 33 supplies the rest.

**THE FIRST PLATE IS THE FOLDER'S ANSWER TO ITS OWN OLDEST QUESTION ABOUT THIS BOY.** Every picture of Kael in the set has him doing something with his hands — blades, a wall, a hook, a hand out in front of him with nothing in it. **This one has him holding still so that a thing the size of a loaf can keep hold of his finger**, and the only work in the frame is that he is counting its breathing, which no viewer can see and every reader will know.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **the loaf** — a day-old loaf broken by hand and sent down a boat from person to person — is the chapter's other great quiet beat and it wants eight resolved faces in a hull forty feet long. **The boat words** — the old man giving him twenty of them and making him say each one back — is the book's last piece of teaching and the folder has no Severin plate at all yet; it stays on `candidates.md` as the strongest single thing left unbuilt in Book One. **The princess holding him** is `ch34/the-elbow` with the roles finished, one day later.

### PLATE: the-finger

*Candidate 1, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 36 canon: *"Sit back. Put your arm along the top of your leg. No — flat. Like that." / She put the child into the space he made. / He weighed almost nothing and he was extremely warm* … *His head had no neck under it worth the name; the whole thing worked because Kael's hand was under it. His fists were shut up beside his face, the way they had been shut through the whole of a barrage.* … *His breathing was wrong. That was the first thing.* … *He counted it, because he could not do anything else. / He counted for four minutes and got the shape of it, and found that it repeated, and that it was not wrong at all* … *His brother's hand came open and shut on Kael's finger and stayed shut. / He did not have much grip.* … *and he held on with it anyway, and Kael sat completely still on the boards of a drifting boat and did not take his finger back, because he did not want to be the one who did that.*

**THE SUBJECT IS ONE HAND CLOSED ON ONE FINGER AND IT IS AN INCH ACROSS.** §The winner and the rival is the whole build: the rival is a face, which will always win a picture, so the face is turned down and held at soft middle values and **the sharpest step from light to dark in the frame is sited on the two hands.** The crop comes in far enough that an inch of subject is a real part of the picture.

**AND THE EYES ARE ON THE CHEST, NOT THE FACE.** That is the one clause that makes it him rather than any boy holding any baby: he is counting the breathing. Nothing in the block says so.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: no-lights

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch36-no-lights.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53). THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S OPTIMISED VERSION OF THIS SHEET'S**, stored exactly as run. **The last plate of Book One.**

**THE ONE BAN IS THE PICTURE AND IT HELD.** *Completely dark from one end to the other — no point of light anywhere on it and no glow behind the ridge.* A low black island across a pale horizon with nothing lit on it, and a boy sitting on the boards of a boat looking at it. **The whole of Book One's last page is that absence**, and the block spends its single absence there and nowhere else.

**AND THE RHYME IS COMPLETE.** `ch01/fallen-stars` — the town lying in a heap of warm light on the black water. `ch32/the-lights-on-the-mountain` — the lights coming up the slope at him. This — nothing lit anywhere on it. **Three pictures, one view, and the whole of Book One between them.**

**THEY CUT A FEATURE THE VALUE RANGE COULD NOT HOLD.** Mine asked for *the near eye is open and drawn, and it is looking at the island* inside a face otherwise unrendered; theirs deletes it and says **his head is turned to the island** instead. At that size, in a dark profile against a pale sky, an open eye is below the value range the picture is working in — and the head does the whole job. **A block should not ask for a feature the finish it has chosen cannot carry.**

**AND THE AGE RUNG WORKED A SECOND TIME, COMPRESSED TO ONE CLAUSE** — *He is fourteen — narrow through the shoulders, adolescent, not grown* — after the full §AGE paragraph carried `ch35/the-spotter`.

**THE REST AS WRITTEN**: the sword laid flat with the grip toward him and the point running out of the bottom edge, the only object on the deck; the knee-high burnt stub where the mast was; both hands empty on the boards; the thin smear of smoke well to one side; and the whole picture built out of cold greys and bare unpainted paper, with the sharpest step in it the top edge of the island against the sky.

**WHAT DRIFTED, two, both small:** the island reads a little narrower than his shoulders rather than as wide · and the crop went past the hip to take in his legs, which is the loose crop for the eleventh time and the first time it has arguably improved the composition, since the stretched-out legs are what make him look like somebody who has stopped.

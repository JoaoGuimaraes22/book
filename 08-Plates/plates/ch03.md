### PLATE: the-wrist

**ADOPTED.** `images/plates/ch03-the-wrist.png`, first run, block below unchanged.

**WHAT IT IS, STATED HONESTLY: A STOP ALREADY MADE, not the catch mid-event.** The knife came back lying on the stone rather than held, so the picture says *a man holds a boy's wrist beside a knife* where the page says *the blade was going for him and the hand got there first.* **Adopted as the moment it actually is** — the aftermath of the stop, a second later — which the chapter also contains. The causal version is available from the fix below if it is ever wanted.

**THE CAUSE IS A REAL FINDING: A LIMB DESCRIBED AS TWO INDEPENDENT FACTS RENDERS AS TWO INDEPENDENT OBJECTS.** The inventory said *the boy's right hand holds one short knife* and, four lines later, *the man's hand is closed around the boy's right wrist.* Those are the same arm — knife, hand, wrist, grip — and splitting them across two entries let the renderer resolve them separately and put the knife down. **Name the chain as one limb, in order.** Written up in `../scene-prompt-system.md`.

The clause that would fix it:

*ONE ARM CARRIES THE WHOLE EVENT — the boy's right arm. Follow it in order: his fingers are wrapped around the knife's handle and the knife IS IN HIS HAND, gripped, not resting on the stone; just behind that hand, the man's hand is closed all the way around that same wrist. Knife, hand, wrist, grip — one continuous limb, in that order. THE KNIFE IS NEVER LYING ON THE STONE.*

**TWO MORE NOTES.** The blade points away rather than back toward the other wrist. And **the scale contrast did not propagate**: the man's hand against the boy's wrist reads perfectly, while the boy's other hand came back adult-sized — so **a size contrast has to be stated per hand, not once for the picture.**

**AND THE THING THAT WORKED, TWICE NOW: `THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH`.** The sleeves in this image are the best cloth in the library — dense scratchy linework in the seams and the mended places, ink plainly dominant over wash. Second confirmation after `portraits/kael-9`. **Put it on anything wearing worked cloth.**

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Ch. 3 canon: *the hide gave all at once where he expected it to hold, and the blade skated off the breastbone toward the soft of his own wrist — and stopped. His wrist was inside his father's hand. He had not seen the hand leave the woodpile.*

**NO FACES, AND NO REFERENCE ATTACHED.** The moment is four hands and a knife; a face in the frame would divide it, and the father's whole character here is that he arrives without being seen. **So the age has to be carried entirely by hand size** — which is exactly what the s40 scale finding is for, and this is its cleanest test: no figure, no landmark, one hand measured against another.

**THE SUBJECT IS THE JUNCTION** where the man's hand closes on the boy's wrist, and the block demotes everything else so it can win — the two-clause mechanism from `../scene-prompt-system.md`.

**RESTRAINT ON THE HARE, deliberately.** The scene system's injury note is that wound-detail vocabulary is what causes trouble, not the subject. The hare is named plainly, kept to the edge, and nothing about it is described beyond its shape.

```
Setting: a flat grey stone slab outdoors at dawn on a mountain,
seen close. Behind and below, the sea as a pale flat wash with the
light coming up grey out of it. Everything beyond the stone falls
away into loose cool wash — no detail, no horizon line drawn.

Composition: a CLOSE crop on hands and forearms only. NO FACES AND
NO HEADS ARE IN THIS PICTURE, no shoulders, no bodies — the frame
holds the flat stone, four hands, two pairs of forearms in mended
sleeves, and one knife. Everything above the elbow is out of frame,
deliberately.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
The BOY'S RIGHT hand holds one short knife and nothing else — a
plain working knife, the blade roughly as long as his own palm.
The BOY'S LEFT hand is flat on the stone, holding the work steady.
The MAN'S RIGHT hand is closed around the boy's right wrist, the
fingers all the way round it, holding it still.
The MAN'S LEFT hand is not in the picture.
Three hands in frame, one knife, nothing else held by anyone. No
second blade, no tool, no rope, no cloth in any hand.

SCALE IS THE AGE, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING CARRYING IT: the man's
hand is large enough to close completely around the boy's wrist
with the fingers overlapping, and the boy's whole hand is about the
size of the man's palm. The boy's hands are a CHILD'S — short
fingers, soft knuckles, no tendon standing out anywhere, small
nails. The man's are a grown labourer's — broad, weathered, the
knuckles heavy, permanently half-curled.

THE MOMENT: the knife has skated off the work and its edge has
stopped a finger's width from the inside of the boy's own left
wrist, the blade in front of him and clearly aimed at it. IT IS NOT
TOUCHING HIM AND HAS NOT CUT HIM. There is no wound anywhere in
this picture and no blood on any hand.

THE HAND ARRIVED WITHOUT WARNING AND NOTHING ELSE IN THE PICTURE
HAS MOVED. The man's grip is exact and unhurried — not snatching,
not wrenching, not dramatic. The boy's hand has not dropped the
knife and has not flinched. Both forearms are steady. This is a
stop, not a struggle.

THE JUNCTION IS THE SUBJECT AND EVERYTHING ELSE IS DEMOTED. The
stone, the sleeves and the hare are held at quiet middle values,
loosely washed, with no hard edges and no strong dark anywhere in
them. THE SHARPEST STEP FROM LIGHT TO DARK IN THE WHOLE PICTURE
HAPPENS WHERE THE MAN'S FINGERS CLOSE ON THE BOY'S WRIST, and that
join is the most precisely drawn thing in the image. Nothing else
competes with it for finish.

A hare lies on the stone at the edge of the frame, on its side,
partly out of the picture, its pale underside turned up. It is
described no further than that.

THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH: both forearms are in coarse undyed
mended wool pushed back at the wrist, dense scratchy linework in
the seams, the patches and the coarse weave, ink dominant over wash
throughout. The man's sleeve is heavy and sized to him; the boy's
is thin and too large. No ornament, no buckle, no metal but the
knife.

Light: low cold dawn light from one side, the sun not up. No warm
source anywhere. The only warm value in the picture is the skin.
```

### PLATE: the-back

**ADOPTED.** `images/plates/ch03-the-back.png`, first run — **from the author's compression of the block below, not the block itself.** Roughly a third shorter, no instruction lost. The stored block is kept as drafted because the cuts are the record: see `../scene-prompt-system.md` §A prompt is an instruction, not an explanation, which is written from this exact diff.

**IT WORKED.** All three marks read — the long seam under the left shoulder, the coin-sized round scar, and the tallies evenly spaced and plainly deliberate on the forearm. The low camera carried the child without drawing him. The shirt on the trough edge is `THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH` confirming a third time.

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png` — hair and colouring only; his face is not in this picture. Ch. 3 canon: *at the trough, his father stripped to the waist to wash, and Kael read his back the way he read the goat paths* — *the long pale seam under the left shoulder · the round puckered one with a brother on the front · the short ones on the forearms, many, all the same length, all the same slant, like tallies.*

**THIS IS THE ONE MOMENT THAT NEEDS THE SCARS.** Vask's sheet keeps them out of frame unless the moment requires them, and this is the chapter that puts all three in front of the reader. **The brief was wrong about it and has been corrected (s40)** — it claimed the page had never described them; `03/vask` says *scars on-page*, and Ch. 3 describes each one. What the page withholds is not the marks, it is **what made them**, and no picture can answer that anyway.

**THE CAMERA IS THE CHILD.** Kael is not in this picture and does not need to be: the viewpoint is his — low, a boy of eight looking up at a grown man's bare back. **Camera height carries the child the way camera position carried the altitude in `ch02-long-game`.** It is the same lesson pointed at a person instead of a landscape, and it means the picture can be entirely about the back.

**NO GREY INSTRUCTION.** The corrected greying-at-the-temples clause has now failed twice (`../portrait-prompt-system.md` §Vask) and the standing note is not to run it a third time. His hair is simply dark and wet here. **The absence is deliberate, not an oversight.**

**HEALED SCARS RENDER WITHOUT DIFFICULTY** (scene system §Damage). The block names each mark's shape, size and place and says nothing about tissue.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his hair and colouring
from it only. His face is not in this picture.

Setting: a stone water trough outside a small mountain house, late
morning, as loose wash suggestion — the house wall behind, thin
grass and bare rock, everything past the figure falling into pale
cool wash. Flat bright overcast daylight, no sun, no cast shadows.

THE VIEWER IS LOW — at the eye height of a child of eight, looking
slightly UP at a grown man standing at the trough. The man's back
fills most of the frame. This low viewpoint is deliberate and it is
what tells you who is watching.

Composition: the man from BEHIND, stripped to the waist, bent
slightly forward over the trough with both hands in the water. NO
FACE AND NO HEAD FEATURES ARE VISIBLE — the back of his head and
his wet dark hair are all that is drawn of it. He is not turning
and not looking back.

THE BACK IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS BROAD THROUGH THE SHOULDERS AND
NO FURTHER — heavy across the top of the back and the neck, the
muscle an economy rather than a bulk, nothing wasted below it, the
waist narrow. A working body, not a built one.

THE MARKS, and they are the reason for the picture. Three kinds,
all long healed, all pale against weathered skin:
— ONE LONG STRAIGHT SCAR under the LEFT shoulder blade, running a

— ONE ROUND OLD SCAR high on the back, about the size of a coin.

THE MARKS WIN THE PICTURE AND THE BODY IS DEMOTED SO THEY CAN. The

He is doing an ordinary chore and nothing about him is braced or

His shirt lies on the edge of the trough — coarse undyed mended
```

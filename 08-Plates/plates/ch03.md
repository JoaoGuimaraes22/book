### PLATE: the-wrist

**ADOPTED.** `images/plates/ch03-the-wrist.png`, first run, block below unchanged.

**WHAT IT IS, STATED HONESTLY: A STOP ALREADY MADE, not the catch mid-event.** The knife came back lying on the stone rather than held, so the picture says *a man holds a boy's wrist beside a knife* where the page says *the blade was going for him and the hand got there first.* **Adopted as the moment it actually is** — the aftermath of the stop, a second later — which the chapter also contains. The causal version is available from the fix below if it is ever wanted.

**THE CAUSE IS A REAL FINDING: A LIMB DESCRIBED AS TWO INDEPENDENT FACTS RENDERS AS TWO INDEPENDENT OBJECTS.** The inventory said *the boy's right hand holds one short knife* and, four lines later, *the man's hand is closed around the boy's right wrist.* Those are the same arm — knife, hand, wrist, grip — and splitting them across two entries let the renderer resolve them separately and put the knife down. **Name the chain as one limb, in order.** Written up in `../scene-prompt-system.md`.

The clause that would fix it:

*ONE ARM CARRIES THE WHOLE EVENT — the boy's right arm. Follow it in order: his fingers are wrapped around the knife's handle and the knife IS IN HIS HAND, gripped, not resting on the stone; just behind that hand, the man's hand is closed all the way around that same wrist. Knife, hand, wrist, grip — one continuous limb, in that order. THE KNIFE IS NEVER LYING ON THE STONE.*

**TWO MORE NOTES.** The blade points away rather than back toward the other wrist. And **the scale contrast did not propagate**: the man's hand against the boy's wrist reads perfectly, while the boy's other hand came back adult-sized — so **a size contrast has to be stated per hand, not once for the picture.**

**AND THE THING THAT WORKED, TWICE NOW: `THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH`.** The sleeves in this image are the best cloth in the library — dense scratchy linework in the seams and the mended places, ink plainly dominant over wash. Second confirmation after `portraits/kael-9`. **Put it on anything wearing worked cloth.**

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Ch. 3 canon: *the hide gave all at once where he expected it to hold, and the blade skated off the breastbone toward the soft of his own wrist — and stopped. His wrist was inside his father's hand. He had not seen the hand leave the woodpile.*

**NO FACES, AND NO REFERENCE ATTACHED.** The moment is four hands and a knife; a face in the frame would divide it, and the father's whole character here is that he arrives without being seen. **So the age has to be carried entirely by hand size** — which is exactly what the s37 scale finding is for, and this is its cleanest test: no figure, no landmark, one hand measured against another.

**THE SUBJECT IS THE JUNCTION** where the man's hand closes on the boy's wrist, and the block demotes everything else so it can win — the two-clause mechanism from `../scene-prompt-system.md`.

**RESTRAINT ON THE HARE, deliberately.** The scene system's injury note is that wound-detail vocabulary is what causes trouble, not the subject. The hare is named plainly, kept to the edge, and nothing about it is described beyond its shape.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S ARITHMETIC.** A sky that cannot be stopped, and one man who stopped one of them. *He could hold one. He could not hold two. There were going to be a great many more than two.*

**AND THE CHAPTER'S WORST MATERIAL IS LEFT ON THE SHORTLIST, NOT CALLED UNDRAWABLE.** The eastern gate and the crush were first proposed at s51 as things the tool would refuse, **which the folder's own record contradicts**: `ch21/dead` was refused and then **ran on a rewording and was adopted**, and `../prompt-system.md` §Injury says plainly that a refusal is usually a fault in the block. What is actually true is narrower and it is the instrument this chapter runs on — **you do not draw the stroke, you draw the men**, and the crop decides what the filter is ever asked about. Both entries stay on `candidates.md` with the framing that would carry them.

**NEITHER BLOCK RESOLVES A FACE, AND BOTH ARE PRICED FOR IT** (s43: a faceless candidate is priced, not disqualified). `ch22/kael-of-the-island` is the precedent and its record is the argument: *the two figures came back even smaller than the block asked for, which is the picture's whole power.*

**THE FOG IS TWO DISTINCT LAYERS AND BOTH ARE NAMED.** *It lay across the sand in a thin sheet at about the height of a man's knee. It sat over the top rail like a lid.* Nothing else in the folder has been asked for stratified weather, so each layer gets its own clause and its own height.

**AND EVERY BURNING THING IS COUNTED WHERE IT SITS.** §Hands' lesson generalises off hands: *three blades total* failed twice and one line per hand held. So each stone gets its own line and its own place in the frame, and the count is stated after them.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-sky

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch27-the-sky.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s51: *amazing*).**

**THE COUNTING INSTRUMENT CARRIED FOUR BURNING OBJECTS AT FOUR DEPTHS ON ONE RUN.** Three stones in the air — near and huge with a long tail, mid, and far and small — and a fourth already down through the timber with fire in the hole it made and smoke coming up through the boards. **§Hands' one-line-per-item rule generalises off hands and onto anything counted**, and this is the proof: *three blades total* failed twice, and four objects each given its own line and its own place landed first time.

**AND THE FIGURE CAME BACK SMALLER THAN THE BLOCK ASKED FOR, WHICH IS AGAIN THE PICTURE'S POWER** — the same thing `ch22/kael-of-the-island` recorded. A boy alone on an empty floor with his back to the viewer and no face at all carries *none of them was producing an instruction* without a single clause about what he is feeling. **The faceless price was worth paying twice.**

**THE CROWD IS THE SET'S LARGEST WASH** — thousands of small shapes, tier on tier, not one face resolved — and the fires are the only saturated colour in a completely grey picture.

**WHAT DRIFTED: half the weather.** The block named two fog layers at two heights and got one. The lid over the top rail and the mist through the bowl are there; **the thin sheet lying on the sand at knee height is not**, and the floor came back bare. Stratified weather is new to the folder and this is its one data point — **the layer with something to sit against rendered, the one lying on open ground did not.** Not worth a run; recorded for the next block that wants ground fog.

*Author-requested at s51: the fireballs raining down on the arena.* Anchor: `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 27 canon: *Something went over the top of the building. It came in high out of the grey from the seaward side, going fast and going away from him, and it was the size of a cart, and it was burning.* … *It came out of the fog over the rim with fire going backward off it in a tail* … *The third and the fourth came over together.* … *the fire from the fourth stone found the new timber and began to go along it under the seats, and the smoke came up through the boards in fifty places at the same time in neat little rows.* … *Eleven thousand people who had come to watch two children fight got up out of their seats and tried to leave a stone bowl through four gates, and Kael stood on the sand in the middle of it.*

**IT ABSORBS THE PARALYSIS BEAT RATHER THAN COMPETING WITH IT.** A small dark figure standing alone and still on the flattest, most open ground on the headland, with both hands empty, **is** *every school he had was running perfectly, none of them was producing an instruction* — and it does not need a face to say so. **It is also the deliberate inverse of `ch23/empty`**: the identical empty-handed stance on this identical sand, which one chapter ago was the whole of his mastery.

**THE FIRE IS THE ONLY WARM THING IN A GREY PICTURE**, which is what makes the palette do the work: flat fogged daylight everywhere, and four burning objects that are the only saturated colour in the frame.

### PLATE: i-need-you

*Author-requested at s51: this chapter needs Kael and Valeria somehow.* Anchors: `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 27 canon: *Then something took a fistful of his coat at the shoulder and turned him bodily out of where he was standing, and his back met the timber of the judges' enclosure hard enough to empty him, and Valeria Vel put her face in front of his face.* … *She still had both blades. There was blood on her forearm that was not hers and a burn on the side of her neck he had not seen her take.* … *She hit him. Flat hand, open, hard, across the side of the head, and it was not temper, it was the correction of a fault; she did it the way the grey coats on the sea court did it, called and delivered and finished, and the whole arena came back into him at once through the ringing. / "There," she said. "I need you."*

**IT IS THE MOMENT THE TWO OF THEM INVERT.** For a year she has been the one he cannot solve. Here she is holding him against a wall and telling him she needs him, and **the picture is composed the instant after the hand lands** — no strike in progress, his head still turned by it and the arena arriving back through the ringing. The framing is the whole safety margin and it costs the beat nothing, because the beat was never the blow.

**HER FACE IS NOT ANGRY AND THAT IS THE HARD PART.** *It was not temper, it was the correction of a fault … called and delivered and finished.* So she is written entirely professional: level, unhurried, already past it, with the need stated flat rather than pleaded. `../canon-brief.md`'s fence holds — not vulnerable, not wistful, not searching — and `ch20/val` is the warning, where *tired* and *flat* as temperament words drifted her wistful. Every clause here is anatomy.

**HIS IS A FACE ARRIVING BACK INTO ITSELF**, which is a state and not an action: the eyes just refocused on her, the head still carrying the turn.

**HER HAIR IS BOUND, AND THIS IS THE FIRST TIME.** *She came out of the western gate with her hair bound* — against `../canon-brief.md`'s loose-past-the-shoulders default, so the block authors it. It does not disturb README's §Silhouette separation, which holds her apart from Kael on build, blades and cloth and never on hair; the bound-vs-loose axis is the Elarine one, and Elarine is not in this picture.

**THE FIRES DO NOT LIGHT THEM.** §Light, confirmed twice this session on the two fan plates: a warm source lights a face only when what is between it and the camera is close. The burning curve is across the bowl, so the faces stay in cold flat fogged daylight and the fire is the one warm thing in the distance behind.

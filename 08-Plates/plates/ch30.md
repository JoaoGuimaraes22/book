# Ch. 30 — The Houses · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen; Vask is unpinned. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on the first, `images/portraits/vask.png` on the second — **there is no full figure of Vask**, so everything below his collar is authored in the block and will not repeat.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S TWO HALVES AND THEY ARE THE SAME SCHOOL AT TWO SCALES:** a boy under a leaf with his forearm over his own face, not closing his eyes — and the man who taught him that, arriving through a gate with lightning held in the whole of his body. **One of them is hiding perfectly and the other one has stopped hiding entirely.**

**THE LIGHT CHANGES IN THIS CHAPTER AND THE SET SHOULD SHOW IT.** Ch. 27, 28 and 29 are flat fogged daylight from end to end; here the sun is out — *the first of them came along the top terrace with the sun behind them* — and the second picture is lit by a man. **After four grey plates, hard backlight and then a cold white source is worth having**, and both are on the page rather than invented.

**THE BIG MAN IS IN NEITHER PICTURE AND THAT IS A RULING, NOT AN OVERSIGHT.** `ch13.md`: *a plate is not where a person's face gets decided; whatever it drew would become his reference by default.* He has no portrait and he is not finished with this book. The first plate does not reach him; the second is composed on the arrival, before the two of them meet, with him out of frame and named as out of frame.

**AND THE SECOND PICTURE IS `ch21/the-surge`'s BIG BROTHER, WITH THE SAME FENCE.** That plate's record settled how this element is drawn: **the reserve instrument erases the object it covers**, so the white is given a form and stays on what it runs along — *not sparking, not spilling, not one thread of it going anywhere it had not been told to go*, which is the page's own clause and the same fence Vask spoke on himself in Ch. 21. Nothing arcs into the air, down to the stone or across the yard. **The difference from `the-surge` is the surface: there it was a blade, here it is a man**, and the sword is written as the least of it.

**THE CAMERA IS WHERE THE BOY IS LYING AND HE IS NOT IN THE FRAME.** *Kael, lying on his side on somebody's threshing floor with his ribs broken, saw it clearly for perhaps a second and a half, understood half of what he was looking at.* `ch26/the-fan-downrange` proved the instrument — head-on is an event where side-on is a shape — and §Light's rule for a low camera is followed: it is written as the surfaces it exposes, never as where the viewer stands.

**AND THE MAN IS A LIGHT SOURCE, WHICH THE PAGE STATES AS A MEASUREMENT:** *the light coming off him put the shadow of the wall halfway up the house.* So the block gives the white a job in the picture — it is what lights the yard, it is cold, and the one burning roof is demoted to a quiet middle value with soft edges so that the two do not compete. §The winner and the rival, with a rival the page supplied.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** *carried* — the father lifting his son off the stones with one arm under the back and one under the knees, the white running down the chest against the boy's cheek and going round him, the mountain black above — is the chapter's other great picture and it is the same figure in the same minute in the same light as the plate that was taken. **The lamp on the tread** and **nobody looks up** are on `candidates.md` with their framing; both are cold and both are good and neither is the chapter.

### PLATE: the-dock-leaves

*Candidate 2, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 30 canon: *The first of them came along the top terrace with the sun behind them, six men, unhurried, going west toward the district. / Kael went down into the corner where the wall met the bank, into the dock leaves, and put his forearm over the bright part of his face. He did not close his eyes. A closed eye tells a body it may relax, and he could hear his own heart already, and needed it quieter than that. / They passed nine feet above his head.*

**THE SUBJECT IS AN OPEN EYE UNDER AN ARM.** Everything the chapter is about is in that one decision: the face is the bright thing, so the arm goes over the bright part of it, **and the eyes stay open on purpose**. The block gives the arm a landmark — laid across the brow — so that the strip of face below it, and both eyes in it, are what the picture is.

**HIS FATHER'S WHOLE SCHOOL IS IN THE FRAME AND NONE OF IT IS EXPLAINED.** The leaves, the corner where the wall meets the bank, the boots going past above at a stated height. §Scale: nine feet is on the page, so the men are placed against the wall-top rather than described as far away.

**AND THE MEN ARE BACKLIT AND FACELESS.** *With the sun behind them* does the work: no features are drawn on any of the six, they are dark against a bright sky at the top of the frame, and the whole lower half of the picture is in the bank's shadow. **The one thing in that shadow with any light on it is his eyes.**

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Setting: the corner of a dry-stone field wall where it meets an earth
bank, on a terraced hillside, as loose wash suggestion. Broad
green dock leaves, big and coarse and ribbed, crowd the lower half of
the picture and lie across the boy. Bright hard daylight above, the
whole corner in the bank's shadow below.

Composition: the boy lies on his side low in the corner, close to the
viewer and filling the lower left of the frame, his head turned up and
toward the viewer in three-quarter view. The bottom edge of the picture
crosses him at the chest, so no legs and no feet are in the frame. Above
and behind him the top of the field wall runs across the upper third of
the picture, and above that the sky is bright and pale.

HIS RIGHT FOREARM IS LAID ACROSS HIS OWN FACE at the brow, the arm
horizontal, the hand out of frame past his temple, so that the forehead
and one cheekbone are covered by it. Below the arm his eyes, his nose
and his mouth are uncovered and fully visible.

HIS EYES ARE WIDE OPEN AND HE IS NOT BLINKING: both lids fully raised,
the pale grey clear, the eyes turned up and to the side toward the top
of the wall. The mouth is closed. The jaw is set and easy. He is not
looking at the viewer and his head is not turned toward the viewer.
Nothing else about him is moving and he is pressed close into the ground.

ALONG THE TOP OF THE WALL, NINE FEET ABOVE HIS HEAD, SIX MEN ARE WALKING
PAST FROM RIGHT TO LEFT, seen from below and against the bright sky. They
are cut off by the top edge of the picture at the chest, so that boots,
legs and the lower body of each are what is in the frame. They are dark
against the light and no face is drawn on any of them. They are unhurried
and none of them is looking down.

Dried dark red up both his forearms and across the backs of his hands.
Grey dust on the side of his face that is against the ground. A
cloth-wrapped bundle is on his back with the shape of two hilts in it,
the hilts covered, half under the leaves.

The picture is lit from behind and above: the sky and the men are the
brightest values, the leaves and the bank are dark and cool, and the
strip of his face under the arm is the one lit thing low in the frame and
carries the sharpest step from light to dark.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in
the reference image. Pale grey eyes. He wears undyed coarse working wool,
much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no metal anywhere on
him.
```

### PLATE: the-white-in-all-of-him

*Candidate 6, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 30 canon: *Then there was white in the yard. / Not on a blade. Everywhere. It came in through the gate at the height of a man.* … *It was a man with lightning in him. Not only in his hand. Not only down a blade. In the whole of him. Down both arms, across the shoulders, through the chest, into the ground at every step. Held in. Running the length of every part of him at once and not coming off — not sparking, not spilling, not one thread of it going anywhere it had not been told to go. He carried a longsword in his right hand with the same white standing the whole length of the blade, and the sword was the least of it.* … *Kael's father stood eight feet from his son, in his own working coat, with his sleeves rolled the way they were rolled every morning of Kael's life, and the light coming off him put the shadow of the wall halfway up the house. / He was breathing hard.*

**THE ORDINARY CLOTHES ARE THE PICTURE.** *His sleeves rolled the way they were rolled every morning of Kael's life* — a man in his own working jacket with his forearms bare, walking into a farmyard carrying the largest thing in the book. **Nothing about him is dressed for it**, and the block spends real words on the wool and the roll of the sleeve because that contrast is the whole beat. §Nouns kills the default: *coat* returns a frock coat, so the garment is built by construction and the default named and refused.

**HE IS BREATHING HARD AND THAT IS THE ONLY THING HIS FACE DOES.** §Gaze forbids a face doing nothing; this one gets the open mouth and the working chest, the eyes level on something past the camera, and nothing else — which on this man is correct, because `../canon-brief.md` says his eyes are empty and that is not the same as vacant.

```
Image 1: vask.png — portrait reference of the iron-haired man.

Setting: a farmyard threshing floor at the end of the day, as loose wash
suggestion — flat worn flagstones, a low stone yard wall, the dark mouth
of a gateway behind him, a house gable implied at one side with fire
burning in its roof a long way back. The daylight is nearly gone and the
sky above the wall is dim.

Composition: the picture is seen from a hand's breadth above the
flagstones. The floor runs away from the bottom edge as a broad plane
lit end to end, and the man is seen from below: the underside of his jaw
is visible, his boots are large and near the bottom of the frame, and his
head sits high against the dim sky. He is walking in through the gateway
toward the viewer and is not yet close, standing about three of his own
heights away. He fills the middle of the frame and is the only figure in
the picture. The boy the view belongs to is not in the frame, and no
other person is drawn anywhere in it.

THE WHITE IS IN THE MAN AND NOT AROUND HIM: hard thin white filaments
run down both arms from the shoulders to the fingers, across the
shoulders, down the middle of the chest, and down both legs into the
stone at each foot, following the length of him. Every filament lies on
him and clings to him. Nothing arcs off into the air, nothing leaps to
the stone, nothing sprays, and there is no glow, no haze and no
cloud around him anywhere.

HIS RIGHT HAND HOLDS ONE PLAIN LONGSWORD and nothing else, carried low
and back, the point behind him and clear of the ground. The same hard
white filament stands the whole length of that blade, hilt to point,
lying on the steel, with the steel drawn as steel underneath it and
still reading as metal. His left hand is empty and open at his side.
Two hands, one blade, nothing else in either hand, and no other blade,
hilt or point is drawn anywhere in the picture.

HE IS THE LIGHT IN THE PICTURE. The white light is cold and hard-edged
and it comes off him alone: it throws his shadow and the shadow of the
yard wall up the house behind him, it puts a rim on the flagstones
around his feet, and it is the only strong light anywhere in the frame.
The fire in the far roof is small, dull orange, soft-edged and low in
value, and does not light him or the floor.

HE IS BREATHING HARD: the mouth open, the chest and shoulders working,
the head level. His eyes are on something past the viewer and a little
above it, and he is not looking at the viewer. The face gives out
nothing else — not angry, not grim, not fierce.

He wears a plain coarse working jacket of undyed dark wool, straight-cut
and closed at the throat, the sleeves rolled up above the elbows so that
both forearms are bare, and heavy village working trousers and boots.
Nothing is tailored, nothing is fitted, nothing is decorated: this is not
a frock coat, not a uniform and not armour, and there is no metal on him
anywhere except the sword.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in
the reference image — the weathered face, the iron-dark hair, the
iron-dark eyes.
```

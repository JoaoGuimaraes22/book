### PLATE: long-game

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-4.png` (hair and colouring only), `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 2 canon: the boy at the foot of the fence post, the six-oared boat with its oars folded, and the father who has become *a shape by the woodpile that the eye slid off*.

**RUN 1 FAILED ON GEOGRAPHY AND THE CAUSE IS WORTH KEEPING (s37).** The first version put the altitude in the Setting paragraph and then staged the picture with *read it left to right: the boy, the man, the boat.* **That is a flat instruction, and staging beats intent** — the run built a shoreline at sea level with the boat close inshore, which is the wrong world: this book's geography is a house high on a mountain with the sea a long way below it. **Altitude is not a setting, it is a camera position and a vertical stack, and it has to be stated as one.** The revision below leads with where the viewer is standing and gives the drop as measurement.

Run 1 also lost three things the revision states harder: the boat came back with two oars in the water instead of six folded up, the man came back standing and obvious instead of folded down and low-contrast, and the sky came back with painted clouds instead of bare paper.

**THE TEST STANDS: A MAN WHO IS HARD TO SEE, ON PURPOSE.** Everything else in the system is about making a figure resolve. If this renders, the technique is worth more than the picture.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy — take his hair and colouring
from it only. His face is barely visible in this picture.
Image 2: portrait reference of the man — take his hair and colouring
from it only. His face is not visible in this picture.

THE VIEWER IS HIGH ON A MOUNTAIN, LOOKING OUT AND STEEPLY DOWN. This
is the first thing to establish and everything else depends on it.
The yard is a narrow shelf of ground near the top of a mountain; the
sea is very far below it and a long way off. We are looking down at
the water from a great height, the way you look down from a clifftop,
not across it from a shore. THERE IS NO BEACH, NO SHORELINE AND NO
WATER'S EDGE ANYWHERE NEAR THE FIGURES.

THE PICTURE IS STACKED VERTICALLY, not arranged left to right:
— the bottom third is the yard itself: thin grass, bare rock, a
  post-and-rail pen, a woodpile against a low stone house wall;
— the middle third is the mountainside falling away below the yard,
  steep and empty, dropping and dropping in loose dry-brush;
— the top third is the sea, seen from far above as a flat pale
  sheet, with the horizon high in the frame.

Setting: a high mountain smallholding on a WHITE-SKY DAY at the end
of summer — flat overcast, no sun, no cast shadows anywhere.
Everything held at close middle values, no strong dark and no strong
light. THE SKY IS FLAT BARE UNPAINTED PAPER — no clouds are drawn at
all, no cloud shapes, no cloud edges, nothing rendered in it.

SCALE, and it carries the whole picture:
The boy is folded at the foot of a fence post and the top of his head
reaches roughly a third of that post's height. His head is large for
his body. He is a child — do not draw a teenager and do not draw a
toddler.
THE BOAT IS TINY. On the water far below, the entire boat is SMALLER
THAN THE BOY'S HEAD in this frame. If the boat is bigger than that,
the viewpoint is wrong and the picture has failed.

THE BOY IS BEING PART OF THE MOUNTAIN. Folded down at the foot of the
post, knees drawn up to his chest, both hands quiet and still on his
shins, his back against the post, his head down. He is not hiding
behind anything and not crouched ready to move — he has arranged
himself into a shape and stopped. HIS FACE IS DOWN AND HIS EYES ARE
NOT VISIBLE; the top of his head and one cheekbone in shadow is all
that is drawn of it.

THE MAN IS THE HARDEST THING IN THIS PICTURE AND HE IS DELIBERATELY
HARD TO SEE. HE IS NOT STANDING. He is folded down in the shadow at
the foot of the woodpile in exactly the same shape as the boy — knees
up, hands quiet, head down, back against the stacked wood — and HIS
HEAD IS BELOW THE TOP OF THE STACKED LOGS. He must be present and
findable, not omitted. But he is rendered at almost the same value as
the wood behind him, his outline broken by the ends of the logs, his
cloth the same dull grey-brown as the timber, with no line around him
stronger than the lines in the woodpile itself. A viewer should have
to look twice to find him. Do not stand him up, do not silhouette
him, do not light him, do not give him a clean edge, and do not move
him out in front of the wood.

THE BOAT: far below on the flat water, seen from high above, tiny. A
wide open boat with SIX OARS, AND EVERY OAR IS UP OUT OF THE WATER,
lifted and folded back along the hull all together like a beetle
folding its legs. NO OAR IS IN THE WATER AND NO OAR STICKS OUT
SIDEWAYS. The boat is not moving and leaves no wake. One small figure
stands at its bow holding a short dark stick up to his face, pointed
up at the mountain. No sail, no second boat, no bird anywhere on the
water.

NOTHING IS HAPPENING, AND THE STILLNESS IS THE SUBJECT. Nobody is
running, nobody is hiding in fear, nobody is calling out, nothing is
approaching. Three things in a landscape are holding absolutely
still and one of them is a boat.

Do not mix their cloth: both wear coarse undyed mended village cloth
in dull grey-brown and dead olive, the man's heavy and sized to him,
the boy's thin and a little too large. No ornament, no buckle, no
badge, no metal on either of them.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No woman, no animals, no dog,
no goat, no birds, no houses other than the one wall behind the
woodpile.
```

### PLATE: the-doorframe

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 2 canon: *One of her hands was up on the doorframe, the knuckles pale, and Kael thought she was holding the door up, and thought no more about it.*

**THE SUBJECT IS A HAND, SO THE CROP IS CLOSE.** Her sheet's standing complaint is that a head-and-shoulders portrait cuts off the only part of her the book ever spends. Here the hand *is* the picture, and it has to be near enough to read the knuckles — so this is waist-up, not a full figure, and the hand is at head height where nothing overlaps it.

She has been standing there some part of the afternoon. Nothing in her face says so.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the woman.

Setting: the doorway of a small stone-and-turf mountain house on a
white-sky day, as loose wash suggestion. She stands INSIDE the
doorway looking out; the room behind her falls away into deep cool
shadow, so she reads against a dark rectangle. Flat overcast daylight
on her from outside, no sun and no cast shadows.

Composition: waist-up, close, the figure filling most of the frame,
the dark doorway around her and a little of the stone wall at the
edges.

HER RIGHT HAND IS UP ON THE DOORFRAME AT ABOUT THE HEIGHT OF HER OWN
HEAD, and it is the subject of the picture. The hand is gripping the
frame — not resting on it, not leaning against it — the fingers
curled over the edge of the timber and THE KNUCKLES DRAWN PALE AND
HARD, the palest and most precisely finished thing in the whole
image. The arm is raised clear of her body with nothing overlapping
it, the whole line of it visible from shoulder to fingertips against
the dark of the room. Her left hand hangs at her side, empty and
open. Nothing is held in either hand.

HER FACE AND HER HAND DISAGREE, AND BOTH MUST SURVIVE. She is looking
out and away past the viewer, down the slope, watching something a
long way off. Expression entirely still and giving nothing back —
level, mouth closed and relaxed, brows unmoved, composed. NOT
worried, NOT afraid, NOT anguished, NOT tender, NOT sad, not tearful,
not straining. The only thing in this picture that is frightened is
the hand. Do not resolve the contradiction by putting the fear in her
face.

THE WOMAN: a grown woman of a mountain village. Fine bones under
skin with weather and sun on it but not coarsened. Long dark brown
hair falling loose past the shoulders, warm rather than cool, plainly
brown and not black, unstyled and naturally settled. Plain coarse
village working cloth — an overdress over a plain undershirt, the
neck closed, in dull grey-brown and undyed oatmeal, worn soft and
much mended. No ornament of any kind. She is not pregnant.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is calling out, nobody is arriving,
nothing is falling. It is an ordinary overcast afternoon and a woman
is standing in her own doorway.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No child, no man, no animals.
```

### PLATE: done

*Candidate 3.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-4.png` (hair and colouring only), `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 2 canon: *He got up the way the newborn goats got up, in pieces, and went down, and his father stood near him — close enough to catch him, and did not catch him — while the legs remembered.*

**THE CONTRADICTION IS THE WHOLE PICTURE**, and the system's finding is that a contradiction has to be stated as one or the run resolves it into an ordinary kindness: a father would be reaching. This one's hands stay down.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy — take his hair and colouring
from it only.
Image 2: portrait reference of the man — take his hair and colouring
from it only.

Setting: the same mountain smallholding at DUSK — the sky going grey,
the light nearly gone, the pens and the woodpile reduced to flat dark
shapes, the sea below a cold pale sheet. Loose wash suggestion, no
detail. ONE BAT in the upper air over the pens, tiny and far off,
drawn as a single small dark mark, no larger than a thumbprint at
this scale. One bat only — no birds, no flock, nothing else in the
sky.

Composition: both figures full length, mid-distance, grounded on the
same ground plane, close together near the corner of the pen.

SCALE: the boy is a child and his height is given by the man, not by
his age — the top of his head reaches about the man's lower ribs. His
head is large for his body and his limbs are short. Do not draw a
teenager and do not draw a toddler.

THE BOY IS GETTING UP OUT OF THE GROUND AND HIS LEGS DO NOT WORK. He
is caught partway up, unbalanced and coming apart — weight all on one
bent leg, the other knee still down, one hand flat on the ground
taking his weight, his back rounded, his head hanging down. Assembled
wrong, like a newborn animal standing for the first time. HIS FACE IS
DOWN TOWARD THE GROUND AND HIS EYES ARE NOT VISIBLE. Not crying, not
grimacing, not appealing to anyone — just working.

THE MAN IS STANDING NEAR HIM AND IS NOT HELPING, AND THIS IS THE
SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. He stands within arm's reach, square and still,
weight even, turned toward the boy and watching him. BOTH HIS HANDS
ARE DOWN AT HIS SIDES, OPEN AND EMPTY AND UNMOVING. He is not
reaching, not crouching, not bending, not offering a hand, not
steadying him, not stepping in. Nothing in either hand and no object
anywhere in the frame. HE IS CLOSE ENOUGH TO CATCH THE BOY AND HE IS
NOT CATCHING HIM. Do not resolve this into a father helping his son
up.

His face is level, mouth closed, gaze direct and completely
unreadable — nothing in it that is angry and nothing in it that is
anything at all. NOT concerned, NOT proud, NOT stern, NOT sad, NOT
kind, not smiling.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is in danger, nobody is calling out,
nothing is approaching. It is the end of an ordinary day.

Do not mix their cloth: both wear coarse undyed mended village cloth
in dull grey-brown and dead olive, the man's heavy and sized to him,
the boy's thin and a little too large. No ornament, no buckle, no
badge, no metal on either of them.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No woman, no animals.
```

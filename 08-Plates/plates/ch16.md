# Ch. 16 — The Terms · plate sheet

**Both composed at s45. `nine-strides` is adopted on its first run; `the-ear-on-the-brick` has not run.** Kael twelve, Aurelian twelve and built a year ahead, Elarine twelve, Valeria twelve. Anchors: `images/portraits/aurelian-14.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`, `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, and `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png` on the second plate.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S TWO COURTS.** The cistern court, where three children solve a problem on their hands and knees; and the old sea court, where a girl who has not turned around says a number out loud. **One is the only comic plate the book has and the other is the coldest arrival in it.**

**AND THE FIRST ONE BREAKS THE DEFAULT CROP ON PURPOSE, WHICH IS WHY IT IS ARGUED HERE.** `../README.md` s45 makes a chest-up with a face the default. **A boy lying on brick, a boy walking a circle and a girl up on a wall cannot be got into a chest-up**, and the height difference between them *is* the joke. So the frame is wide and **the ruling is honoured a different way: one face is resolved and large and near, and the other two are demoted** — which is `ch12/furniture`'s solve, and the point of §Name the winner.

**BOTH BLOCKS CARRY THE s45 CROP LESSON.** The cut is named as a landmark and the ground is named as a plane, after *chest-up* returned three-quarter length and two boys floated (§The crop rule, as amended).

**NO AGE INSTRUMENT ON ANY FACE.** Nine runs. On the second plate the small distant figure gets **scale by measurement instead**, which is a different instrument for a different job and the one that has never failed: a figure whose size in frame is load-bearing gets a stated fraction of something else in the frame.

**THE DOORWAY IS HELD, AND IT IS HELD FOR CONSISTENCY.** *Valeria Vel was a foot away, looking straight at him, with an expression he had never seen on her.* It is the chapter's climax and a close two-shot, and **the page calls the expression unprecedented and then declines to say what it is** — which is exactly the test that ruled Ch. 15's father undrawable one chapter ago. **The difference is real and does not settle it:** her state is spoken aloud four lines later — *there is nobody on my floor, I have been holding it since the bell, and you are standing in it* — so a face caught mid-question is arguably legible rather than invented. **Available on request; not taken unasked**, because a folder that applies THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE to one man's face and not to one girl's is not applying it.

**`candidates.md`'s third is held under the s45 ruling.** The hand flat on the door with nothing coming through is eleven weeks in one object — and it is a hand and an object, which is the crop the author ruled oversubscribed.

### PLATE: the-ear-on-the-brick

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchors: `images/portraits/aurelian-14.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`. Ch. 16 canon: *the heir of House Corvane, who would one day hold a fleet, lay face down on the wet brick of a disused cistern court with his cheek in a puddle while a herder's son walked circles around him, and after the fourth circuit said, in an entirely different voice, "Oh."* Then: *He got up on his elbows with his hair full of moss. "I can feel where you are with my face. That is revolting. Do it again."* And, at the wall: *Elarine... did not look up from the flame standing on her palm, which had not moved in four minutes and was the only tidy thing in the court.*

**IT IS THE ONLY COMIC PLATE IN THE BOOK AND THE COMEDY IS ENTIRELY IN ONE FACE.** Not in the situation, which a reader has to be told, but in the expression of a great-house child who has just discovered something with his cheek in a puddle and wants it done again. **So his face is resolved, near and large, and the other two are held back** — the boy walking the circuit is what the picture is about and is not what the picture looks at.

**THE THREE HEIGHTS ARE THE STAGING AND THEY ARE NAMED AS THREE DEPTHS.** One on the brick, one on his feet mid-stride, one up on the wall — near, middle, far. §Four figures found that **depth is what makes a group work and an arc makes a lineup**, and this is that finding with three.

**EVERY GAZE IS NAMED AND NOT ONE OF THEM MEETS.** He is looking at nothing, delighted, at ground level; Kael is looking down at his own feet, which is the pose that has solved that reference four times; Elarine is looking at the flame and **not up**, which is what the page says twice. Three faces, three directions, nobody at the viewer.

**THE FLAME THROWS NOTHING AND THE BLOCK SAYS SO.** A coin of fire on a palm in flat daylight lights nothing (§What a warm source will and will not light, and `ch01/fallen-stars` where a whole town threw nothing). It is the only warm colour in the picture and it is not a light source in it.

**RUN 1 (s45), NOT ADOPTED — re-run: *this one didn't land. Aurelian's body looks like it's coming off the ground.***

**THE FLOOR BECAME A MIRROR AND NOTHING COULD LIE ON IT.** The block asked for *wet dark brick with shallow standing water in the hollows* and then, at the bottom, for **the standing water and the wet sheen on the brick to be reserved as bare unpainted paper.** Together those made one continuous bright reflective sheet with no bricks and no joints in it — **so the near boy reads as rising out of water and the walking boy's boot hangs above it.** The reserve is a good instrument aimed at the wrong surface: **pointing it at the floor itself dissolved the one thing three figures needed to stand on.**

**AND THE BODY WAS NEVER GIVEN A CONTACT INVENTORY.** *Lying face down and propped up on both elbows* with *two hands flat on the brick under his shoulders* is a pose, not a set of contacts, and the hands arrived splayed out ahead of him with the elbows nowhere. §Describe a limb as a chain says two instructions about one limb get resolved independently unless the order along it is given; **a body on a floor is that problem one size up.** The block now names every part that touches — chest, both forearms elbow to hand, both palms — and does the same for the walking boy's leading boot.

**THE COMEDY DID NOT ARRIVE EITHER, AND THAT IS A SEPARATE FAULT.** *Delighted and disgusted at once: the eyes wide open, the brows up, the mouth open on a word* returned alarm — a wet face with a wide-open mouth low over water reads as difficulty, and there was no smile written anywhere in the block. **Delight has to be in the mouth shape, not in the openness**: grinning, corners up, teeth showing, eyes creased at the outer corners. The bans now point at the neighbourhood the run actually landed in rather than the one this folder expected.

**WHAT LANDED AND IS UNTOUCHED:** the girl on the wall with the coin of flame on her open palm, looking at it and not up; the walking boy looking down at his own feet; the three depths; the moss in his hair; nobody looking at the viewer. The flame threw a faint glow it was told not to throw, which is small and not worth a clause.

### PLATE: nine-strides

*Candidate 2.* Anchors: `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 16 canon: *She set her blades against the base of a column and walked out to the middle of the stones and stood there.* Then, with her back to him: *"Nine strides," Valeria Vel said. / She had not turned around. / "You are too light to be one of mine," she said, to the empty court in front of her. "They come in at forty and they land on their heels. Stand up."* And what he does: *Kael stood up out of the gorse with his hands where she could see them.*

**HER BACK IS THE PICTURE AND IT IS PAGE-EXACT RATHER THAN A DODGE.** *She had not turned around* is stated twice in four lines. **So the plate answers with framing and invents nothing** — no part of her face is in frame, and the reason is the page's, not the folder's. It is the one composition in the book where THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE and the best staging are the same instruction.

**AND IT IS AN OVER-THE-SHOULDER, WHICH THIS FOLDER HAS NEVER BUILT.** Her back and shoulder near and large on one side, the court running away, and a boy standing up out of the scrub forty feet off. **The distance is the subject**, so it gets §Scale between figures rather than an adjective: the whole standing boy is stated as a fraction of something in the near frame, which is the instrument that fixed a boat that kept arriving too close.

**THE GROUND IS ONE CONTINUOUS PLANE AND THE BLOCK SAYS SO** (§Settings, as amended at s45) — the flagstones run unbroken from her feet to the scrub at the far edge, which is what stops a distant figure floating on bare paper. **It is also the chapter's own physics:** *it is one piece of ground with joints in it, and you are hitting one end of it.*

**HIS HANDS ARE UP BECAUSE HIS FATHER TAUGHT HIM THAT**, and the page says so in the same breath — open, empty, where she can see them. It is the one gesture in the picture and it reads at that distance.

**THE FULL-FIGURE SHEET IS ATTACHED AGAIN.** It cost a wide crop at `ch14/both-hands-full`, and **this plate wants a wide crop**, so the trade that hurt there is free here. Her grey should repeat from that plate rather than being authored a third time.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch16-nine-strides.png`, first run (author, s45: *adopt*).**

**THE OVER-THE-SHOULDER WORKED ON A FIRST RUN AND THE FOLDER HAD NEVER BUILT ONE.** Her back and the back of her head near and large, no part of her face in frame, and the whole court and its far edge legible past her. **The composition that THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE forced turned out to be the best staging available** — which is worth writing down, because the rule is usually described as a constraint and here it did the design.

**AND THE DISTANCE LANDED WITHOUT AN ADJECTIVE ANYWHERE NEAR IT.** *The whole standing boy is about as tall in the frame as the back of the girl's head is wide* — he came back smaller than that, so the court reads even deeper than specified. **§Scale between figures again: state the fraction, get the depth.** The same instrument that fixed a boat which kept arriving too close.

**THE GROUNDING CLAUSE IS NOW THREE FOR THREE.** *The flagstones run away unbroken from her feet to the far edge of the court* — and the plane came back continuous, with a small figure standing in it rather than floating on it. Written into §The crop rule at s45 off the two boys who floated; it has held every time it has been asked since.

**AND THE SMALL THINGS ALL ARRIVED.** Two wooden practice swords leaning against the base of a column, held by nobody. Both his hands out and open and forward, holding nothing — his father's lesson, which the page names in the same breath. The colonnade on three sides, the scrub crowding the fourth, the sea beyond in pale wash, the low sun the only warm thing and the lit tops of the flagstones reserved as bare paper. Nobody else anywhere in the court.

**ONE DRIFT, NOT WORTH A RUN.** Her hair is half drawn back and tied rather than fully loose, where `../canon-brief.md` has it *worn loose, well past the shoulders, no ornament.* It is hair holding hair rather than a band, so it sits on the line; recorded rather than corrected, and the plate stands.

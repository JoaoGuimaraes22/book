# Ch. 25 — The Door · plate sheet

**Composed at s49 from the scene, and written under the register** (`../prompt-system.md` §Plate template). Kael is thirteen, on the night after the first bout and the dawn before the second. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/neris.png` on both, `images/portraits/vask.png` on the first.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S OWN SPLIT.** A night table with a lamp and a pot of mint on it, and a stone step at first light. The argument runs through both halves and is never said out loud: **the two people who have run this boy as an operation for eleven years have stopped running him.** Every signal of it is postural — a man who does not sit is sitting, a woman who asks thirty questions asks three, a pot comes off the fire, a hand goes somewhere it has never gone in front of him, and at dawn they are both at the door.

**SO NEITHER BLOCK ARGUES THE ARGUMENT.** A picture cannot say *never*. The first one gets the father into a chair with a cup in front of him, which is the only picture in the set of that man at rest, and the second puts the mother's hands somewhere and lets how long they stay be the content.

**THE ONE-LAMP INTERIOR IS THE SET'S FOURTH** — `ch12/the-law-on-the-table`, both `ch19` blocks and `ch21/the-nine-things` are all a plank table at night with one light on it. **What makes this one different is what is not happening at it:** those four are a refusal, a confession, an asking and a measurement. Nobody at this table is being taught, tested, refused or debriefed. It is the only happy picture in the folder, and it is worth the repeat of the furniture.

**THE DEMOTION IS SPENT ONCE, ON THE SECOND BLOCK ONLY.** Post-register evidence for it is `ch18/caught-it-halfway` run 1 (s48), where pale pinned sheets beside a face were held at quiet middle values and the sharpest step sited on the face — *the rival demoted on both channels exactly as written*. That is what a rival is: something pale, large or bright that will eat the subject. **Two hands against two faces at close range is that. Three faces alone in a dark room is not**, so the first block does not carry the clause and the crop does the work instead, which is what `ch16/the-ear-on-the-brick` found when the value-structure paragraph came out of it under the s47 rewrite and the near face stayed the finished one anyway.

**AND THE DOOR REPEATS A PICTURE THE BOOK ALREADY HAS.** `ch02/the-doorframe` is this woman inside this doorway with a hand up on the frame, and the page hands Ch. 25 the same posture — *one hand on the frame, and the whole weight of her carried forward.* The block declines it: the hands come off the timber and onto the boy's face, the door goes behind her as a dark rectangle instead of around her, and the light is cold pre-dawn instead of flat overcast. Two adopted plates of one woman in one doorway is the thing being avoided, and it is avoided by composition.

**NEITHER BLOCK STATES AN AGE**, as `ch24.md`'s pair did not: the anchor carries it, and §Injury has one known refusal shape — a stated age beside an injury — and the boy's arm is bound in both pictures.

**THE ARM IS CONTINUOUS WITH `ch24/turn-him-toward-the-lamp`:** four turns of plain cloth above the left elbow, stained where it has dried, and the older healed burn running up out of the cuff on the same forearm. The page re-tightens it on the step, so the second block draws it fresher.

**AND THE PREGNANCY IS FRAMED OUT OF BOTH BLOCKS** (author, s49, on run 1 of the first: *probably should have avoided illustrations with Neris' body showing — it is very weird with the guidelines and pregnancy*). The ruling is on `../README.md` and the fence is on `../canon-brief.md`. It is not a deletion of canon: the page's *"How close?" / "Days. A week."* stays true and the pictures simply do not answer it, which is what the hard rule prescribes — **a plate answers with framing where it cannot show.** So the term, the carry and the shape are named nowhere, nothing below her collar is authored, and what carries her state instead is posture and cloth: side-on in the chair with her weight back at the table, the shawl and the forward-carried weight at the door. `ch21/the-nine-things` predates the ruling and stands.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: four-seconds

**THE SUBJECT IS THREE FACES DOING THREE DIFFERENT AMOUNTS OF THE SAME THING**, and the amounts are the three people. The boy laughs with the whole of his face. The mother has just made the joke and has not moved her mouth. The father breathes out through his nose, and that is his entire contribution to the happiest four seconds in the book so far.

**IT IS THE MATCHED OPPOSITE OF `ch24/the-smile`, ONE CHAPTER LATER.** There a mouth moved and nothing else in the face went with it, and the cheek staying down was the whole point. Here everything moves at once, because the page says *properly, once, out of the middle of him*, and this face has not done that anywhere in twenty-five chapters.

**VASK GETS A WORKING PART**, per §Gaze as amended at s48. *The small sound in his nose* is a gift — it is an instruction to a specific piece of anatomy, so the block names the breath and leaves the rest of the face where it was. ### PLATE: both-hands

*Candidate 5, and the chapter's last beat.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 25 canon: *They were both at the door.* … *His mother was at the door too, with a shawl round her and one hand on the frame and the whole weight of her carried forward, at an hour she had no business being out of a chair, on a stone step in a spring wind.* … *Kael stood on the step in the cold with the mountain going grey behind the house* … *And Neris took Kael's face in both hands, which she had done perhaps eleven times in his life. She looked at it. She said nothing. Then she let go. / "Evening," said Vask. / "Evening," said Kael, and went down the hill.*

**THE PAGE GIVES HER NO EXPRESSION, SO THE BLOCK DOES NOT INVENT ONE.** *She looked at it. She said nothing.* Three sentences, all of them verbs of duration — and duration is the one thing a still picture can carry. The content is a pair of hands that have been somewhere longer than they need to be, on a face holding still for it, and neither face is doing anything a viewer could name.

**THE FATHER IS OUT OF FRAME AND THE SHEET SAYS WHY.** *They were both at the door* is the title beat and this block takes half of it, because the hands have to win and a third face is a rival. His half is candidate 6 and stays on the shortlist unspent.

**THIS IS THE PAIR'S ONE DEMOTION AND IT IS THE `ch18` RECIPE.** Two hands against two faces at close range: faces win that by default, so both are held at quiet middle values in the same breath as the hands are promoted, and the sharpest step is sited on her knuckles.

**RUN 1 (s49) — author: *i like it. their expressions are kind of a bit dead tho.* NOT ADOPTED; RUN 2 CHANGES THE TWO EXPRESSION PARAGRAPHS AND ONE CLAUSE.**

**WHAT LANDED, AND TWO OF THEM ARE EVIDENCE.** The doorway a dark rectangle behind her, the slope behind him going light, cold flat pre-dawn with no hard-edged shadow; both in profile at the same height, the eye contact the subject and neither of them at the viewer; the shawl slipped, her hair loose in a cold wind; no embrace, nobody weeping, nobody else in the frame. **The hand won its demotion** — knuckles and tendons the most finished drawing in the picture, both faces held softer. **And Kael is his reference for the first time this session**: straight dark hair hacked short and uneven, the long neck, the jaw with an edge on it, the grey reading clear. The authored hair clause is why, and `four-seconds` run 1 is why it was there — **a reference that loses a feature once should be authored in every block that follows.** The binding is Ch. 24's, four turns above the left elbow, stained where it dried.

**THE FIRST FAULT IS MINE AND IT IS A NEW SHAPE OF THE s48 ONE.** §Gaze says never tell a face to do nothing, and both paragraphs obeyed it in letter: each named a working part. **But hers was a movement** — *the eyes go over his face from one part of it to another and do not leave it* — **and a still picture cannot draw a movement.** Strip it and what she was actually given is a closed mouth, unmoved brows and four negations. His is worse: *the head steady, the mouth closed, the jaw easy, the eyes up on hers* are four absences of motion and not one visible state. **So the amended §Gaze needs a second half: a working part must be a STATE THE PICTURE CAN HOLD, not an action the face is performing.** Vacancy came back by a different door.

**RUN 2 GIVES EACH FACE ONE THING THAT CAN BE DRAWN STILL.** Hers: the lids fully raised and the eyes wide and fixed, the brows drawn a fraction together, the jaw set, the lips just perceptibly pressed — **a face working, not a face at rest** — with one negation instead of four. His: the eyes fully open and up on hers with the grey clear, the brows lifted a fraction, the mouth closed but unpressed — caught rather than composed, which is `../prompt-system.md` §Gaze's *not smiling — just not braced.* Neither invents a feeling the page does not give; both replace an unrenderable instruction with a renderable one.

**THE SECOND FAULT IS THE PICTURE'S TITLE.** Only one hand arrived. *She took Kael's face in both hands, which she had done perhaps eleven times in his life* — **the count is the beat**, and two hands in a profile is geometrically awkward enough that the tool resolved it by dropping one. Run 2 sites them relative to the viewer rather than to each other: the far hand on the cheek away from the viewer, the near hand on the cheek toward it, both visible.

**AND THE CROP CAME BACK LOOSE FOR THE FIFTH RECORDED TIME**, at the hip against *the lower ribs*, after `ch17` twice and `ch20` twice. Not corrected — the author likes the picture — and it is now well past `../prompt-system.md` §Findings' bar on recurrence, held back only by the correction not having been wanted every time. It has still never been wanted.

**THE ONE REAL RISK IS SENTIMENT** — a mother holding a boy's face at dawn pulls hard toward an embrace and toward tears, and `../canon-brief.md` has this woman *not warm, not maternal, not soft.* §Absence takes that pull and nothing else.

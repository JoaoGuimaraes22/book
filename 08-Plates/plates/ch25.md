# Ch. 25 — The Door · plate sheet

**Composed at s49 from the scene.** Kael is thirteen, on the night after the first bout and the dawn before the second. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/neris.png` on both, `images/portraits/vask.png` on the first.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S OWN SPLIT, AND THE CHAPTER SPLITS AT A LINE OF RULE.** A night table with a lamp and a pot of mint on it, and a stone step at first light. The chapter's argument runs through both halves and is never once said out loud: **the two people who have run this boy as an operation for eleven years have stopped running him.** Every signal of it is postural — a man who does not sit is sitting, a woman who asks thirty questions asks three, a pot comes off the fire, a hand goes somewhere it has never gone in front of him, and at dawn they are both at the door.

**SO NEITHER BLOCK ARGUES THE ARGUMENT.** A picture cannot say *never*, and both blocks stop trying: the first one simply gets the father into a chair with a cup in front of him, which is the only picture in the whole set of that man at rest, and the second puts the mother's hands somewhere and lets the length of time she takes be the content.

**THE ONE-LAMP INTERIOR IS THE SET'S FOURTH** — `ch12/the-law-on-the-table`, `ch19/we-were-owned`, `ch19/will-i-be-allowed-to-hold-it` and `ch21/the-nine-things` are all a plank table at night with one light on it. **What makes this one different is what is not happening at it:** the other four are a refusal, a confession, an asking and a measurement. Nobody at this table is being taught, tested, refused or debriefed. It is the only happy picture in the folder, and it is worth the repeat of the furniture.

**AND THE DOOR COLLIDES WITH `ch02/the-doorframe`, WHICH IS PRICED AND ANSWERED.** That plate is this woman inside this doorway on a white-sky day with a hand up on the frame, and the hand is the subject. The page hands Ch. 25 the same posture — *one hand on the frame, and the whole weight of her carried forward* — and the block declines it: the hands come off the timber and onto the boy's face, the door goes behind her as a dark rectangle instead of around her, and the light is cold pre-dawn instead of flat overcast. **The mechanism is deliberately the same one twice, and that is the sheet's finding:** the folder has now drawn this woman's feeling twice and neither time is it in her face. Ch. 2 put the fear in the knuckles. Ch. 25 puts the love in how long two hands stay where they are.

**NEITHER BLOCK STATES AN AGE**, as `ch24.md` did not: the fourteen-year-old anchor carries it, and `../prompt-system.md` §Injury has one known refusal shape — a stated age beside an injury — and the boy's arm is bound in both pictures.

**THE ARM IS CONTINUOUS WITH `ch24/turn-him-toward-the-lamp`:** four turns of plain cloth above the left elbow, stained dark where it has dried, and the older healed burn running up out of the cuff on the same forearm. The page re-tightens it on the step — *thumb well below the cut, one turn of the cloth tightened* — so the second block draws it fresher than the first.

**AND THE PREGNANCY IS DECIDED, NOT DEFAULTED** (`../canon-brief.md`): *"How close?" / "Days. A week."* Full term in both blocks, carried high and in front, and in the first one she is where the page puts her — *side-on in the chair, reaching with the one arm*, because she cannot get to the table properly any more (`ch21/the-nine-things` established the posture; this is the same body a week further on).

### PLATE: four-seconds

*Candidate 3.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`, `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 25 canon: *there was one lamp lit because the second one was for work.* … *His father got up, went to the fire, came back with the pot, and poured. The smell of it came up between them. It was the mint she grew by the step, and he had drunk it after every hurt of his entire life.* … *"Your sibling is very close now," Neris said. / "How close?" / "Days. A week." She put her hand on the top of it, which she had never done in front of him, and left it there.* … *"You keep saying he." / "I do," his mother agreed. "I have no evidence whatever. Do not write it down." / Kael laughed — properly, once, out of the middle of him — and his father made the small sound in his nose that was as far as his father went, and for about four seconds the three of them sat in a one-room house on a mountain with a lamp and a pot of mint between them, and that was all that was happening anywhere.*

**THE SUBJECT IS THREE FACES DOING THREE DIFFERENT AMOUNTS OF THE SAME THING**, and the amounts are the three people. The boy laughs with the whole of his face. The mother has just made the joke and has not moved her mouth. The father breathes out through his nose, and that is his entire contribution to the happiest four seconds of the book so far.

**IT IS THE MATCHED OPPOSITE OF `ch24/the-smile`, ONE CHAPTER LATER.** There a mouth moved and nothing else in the face went with it, and the cheek staying down was the whole point. Here everything moves at once — mouth, cheeks, eyes, the head gone back — because the page says *properly, once, out of the middle of him*, and this face has not done that anywhere in twenty-five chapters.

**VASK GETS A WORKING PART AND NOT A BLANK**, per `../prompt-system.md` §Gaze as amended at s48. *The small sound in his nose* is a gift: it is an instruction to a specific piece of anatomy, so the block names the breath and the nostril and the upper lip and leaves the rest of the face exactly where it was. **A face given nothing renders as vacancy** — and this is the one picture in the set where vacancy would be a lie about him.

**HER HAND IS IN FRAME AND OCCUPIED WITHOUT A FRAMING DODGE**, which `03/neris` requires of any scene and which `ch19.md` had to rule the other way. The page puts it on top of the pregnancy and says it has never been there in front of him, so the block puts it there flat and leaves it, and nothing in the picture points at it.

**THE TABLE HOLDS FOUR THINGS AND THAT IS THE POINT.** One lamp, a clay pot, three cups with green leaves in them. The sheet of paper her hand was flat on when he came in is deliberately not on it: by this beat *that was all that was happening anywhere*, and a written thing on the boards would be the other half of the chapter leaking into this one.

### PLATE: both-hands

*Candidate 5, and the chapter's last beat.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 25 canon: *They were both at the door.* … *His mother was at the door too, with a shawl round her and one hand on the frame and the whole weight of her carried forward, at an hour she had no business being out of a chair, on a stone step in a spring wind.* … *Kael stood on the step in the cold with the mountain going grey behind the house* … *And Neris took Kael's face in both hands, which she had done perhaps eleven times in his life. She looked at it. She said nothing. Then she let go. / "Evening," said Vask. / "Evening," said Kael, and went down the hill.*

**THE PAGE GIVES HER NO EXPRESSION, SO THE BLOCK DOES NOT INVENT ONE.** *She looked at it. She said nothing.* Three sentences, all of them verbs of duration — and duration is the one thing a still picture can actually carry. So the content of this plate is a pair of hands that have been somewhere longer than they need to be, on a face that is holding still for it, and neither of those two faces is doing anything a viewer could name.

**THE FATHER IS OUT OF FRAME AND THE SHEET SAYS WHY.** *They were both at the door* is the chapter's title beat and this block only takes half of it, because the hands have to win the picture and a third face is a rival for finish (§The winner and the rival). His half is candidate 6 and it stays on the shortlist unspent.

**AND THE HANDS BEAT THE FACE BY THE `ch02/the-doorframe` MECHANISM, WHICH FAILED THERE AND IS WRITTEN PROPERLY HERE.** That block named the winner and lost anyway, because naming the winner is half an instruction and the face was never demoted. This one demotes both faces in the same breath as it promotes the hands — quiet middle values, understated features, no hard dark anywhere in them — and sites the sharpest step from light to dark at her knuckles and the edge of her fingers against his cheek.

**THE ONE REAL RISK IS SENTIMENT**, since a mother holding a boy's face at dawn pulls hard toward an embrace and toward tears, and `../canon-brief.md` is explicit that this woman is *not warm, not maternal, not soft.* §Absence takes exactly that pull and nothing else: they are not embracing, her hands are the only contact between them, and nobody is weeping.

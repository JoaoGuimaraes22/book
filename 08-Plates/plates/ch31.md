# Ch. 31 — The Line · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Elarine is thirteen; the two adults are unpinned and unreferenced. Anchor: `images/portraits/elarine-14.png` on the first. **The second plate has no reference attached and no face in it.**

**THE CHAPTER IS VALERIA'S AND NEITHER PLATE IS OF HER.** That is deliberate: `ch28/rearranging-the-road` is a solo Valeria and `ch27/i-need-you` is her face at a hand's width, and what this chapter gives that the folder has nothing of is **a room deciding, and a yard saying goodbye.** She is the eye in both and appears in neither, which is what she does for the whole chapter.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S ONE ARGUMENT, PUT TWICE.** *A house stands for its own, and the sums are done in houses, and it is not possible to be cruel to a quantity you have never once had occasion to write down.* Inside: a hand closes a shutter and a sentence is finished. Outside: a woman who has just put her own name to a wall gives her daughter instructions and then puts a hand flat against the side of her head. **The same love makes both pictures**, and that is the thing the chapter will not let anybody off.

**THE FACELESS PLATE IS PRICED, NOT SMUGGLED** (s43). `ch27/the-sky` and `ch22/kael-of-the-island` are the precedents and both records say the same thing — the figure came back smaller than the block asked for and that was the power. This one goes further: **no face is resolved anywhere in it at all**, which §Plates says is written as two bans, and both are in the block. What buys it is that the subject is a hand on a shutter and the beat is what stops being audible when it closes.

**AND THE SHUTTER IS THE CHAPTER'S OWN INSTRUMENT, NOT AN INVENTED ONE.** *A man had begun a sentence about the north bastion when he stopped, went to the window himself rather than sending anybody, closed the shutter, came back, and finished the sentence. / Nobody remarked on it.* Four hours later: *That was what the shutter was for, whether the man who closed it had known it or not.* **The plate is composed on the closing**, with the room lit behind him and the last of the daylight going out of the gap — so the picture is a man doing an ordinary courteous thing, which is exactly how the page plays it.

**THE HAND-AND-OBJECT CROP IS THE EXCEPTION AND IT EARNS ITSELF HERE** (s42, s45): the object is a shutter, the hand is a house's, and the default chest-up would have to resolve a face the folder does not own. `ch13.md`'s rule reaches every unnamed head in that room.

**TWO LIGHTS, AND BOTH ARE ON THE PAGE.** *The light had gone orange along the top of the eastern wall* — so the first plate is lit by a low sun that touches nothing but the top of a wall, with the two of them in the shade under it. The second is the only lamplit interior in Part IV. §Light: a named source says where it is, that it is the only one, what it leaves dark, and where the warmth lands, and both blocks do.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **Drusane's two hands on his son's face** and **Oss's father** — *"You were always enough," the man told him. "You were always plenty."* — are the same beat as the plate that was taken, one yard over, and neither boy has a portrait (`ch13.md` deferred Oss for exactly that and named the portrait as the work that unblocks it). **Severin agreed with by the wrong man** — *there was nothing on his face at all, and she thought that a man who has just been agreed with does not usually look like that* — is the chapter's other great face and it asks the block for a face doing nothing, which §Gaze forbids and which renders as vacancy. It stays on `candidates.md` for a session willing to solve it.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-hand-flat

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch31-the-hand-flat.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53). The closest match to its block of anything run this session.**

**THE NAMED LIGHT LANDED EXACTLY AND IT IS THE PICTURE'S WHOLE MOOD.** *The last of the sun is on the top courses of that wall and nowhere else: a narrow band of warm orange along the top of the stone, with the whole of the yard below it in cool blue shade.* That is what came back, to the course — one warm stripe at the top of the frame and everything under it blue — and §Light's four requirements (where it is, that it is the only one, what it leaves dark, where the warmth lands) are what bought it.

**THE HAND IS THE PICTURE.** Flat against the side of the head, over the temple and the ear, the fingers spread and still, the wrist relaxed, resting and not gripping — and it is drawn at the same finish as the girl's face, which is what the closing paragraph asked for.

**AND THE FACELESS NEAR FIGURE HELD FOR THE THIRD TIME THIS SESSION.** The woman is seen from behind and to one side with nothing of her face resolved — after the man in the lit slot at `ch29` and the six backlit men at `ch30`. **Three different methods, three first-run successes:** an aperture with light behind it, a silhouette against a bright sky, and a head simply turned away in ordinary shade.

**THE REST AS WRITTEN**: both heads bound close and severe with nothing loose, the girl's near-black closed to the throat and the woman's deep blue of the same quality with no ornament on either, the cistern head behind them, the trestle tables further off, the girl's eyes up on a face the viewer cannot see, her mouth closed and her jaw tight, and nothing in her crying.

**WHAT DRIFTED — ONE THING, AND IT CORRECTS A CLAIM THIS SESSION MADE THREE PLATES AGO.** The reddened burn along her jaw is not in the picture. `ch29.md` recorded that a shoulder slash rendered where a jaw slash and a graze under an ear did not, and concluded that **the variable is size in frame** — this run contradicts it. Her face is nearly a portrait here, the mark had all the room it could want, and it still did not arrive. **Three small facial marks asked for this session and three missed, at three different scales.** What the three have in common is not size: it is that each was named as a *state of skin* — reddened, grazed, a slash — on a face that the same block was also instructing to do something. The shoulder slash had cloth to tear and nothing else competing for that square inch. **Recorded, not corrected, and the next block that wants a mark on a face should give it a torn or dressed thing to sit against.**

**THE SUBJECT IS ONE HAND ON A HEAD, AND THE FACE UNDER IT IS THE OTHER SUBJECT.** The mother is in the frame and her face is not: she is the near figure, turned away, cropped and unresolved, and everything the picture spends is spent on the daughter. **That is not a dodge — it is the chapter**, where the woman gives instructions, does not mention the wall, and puts her hand out at the end instead of saying anything.

**AND ELARINE'S FACE IS HELD, NOT BLANK.** `../canon-brief.md` puts her at assessment in progress, giving nothing back, and the page says something moved and did not get anywhere — so the block writes the holding as anatomy: lids raised, eyes steady on her mother's, mouth closed, jaw tight. **Her one tell, the spots of colour high on the cheeks, belongs to Ch. 13 and Ch. 17 and is not available here**, and the block says so by not naming it.

### PLATE: the-shutter

*Candidate 5, ★.* No reference attached. Ch. 31 canon: *The room had been somebody's dining hall and there were nineteen people in it.* … *a long table down the middle with nothing on it, chairs pushed back, everybody standing.* … *Outside, past the shutters, the sound off the hill was still going. / It had been going since before she came in. It was not loud. It came in under the talking the way rain comes in under talking, and every so often a piece of it stood up out of the rest and had one person inside it, and then went back down again. / Nobody in that room had mentioned it once.* … *A man had begun a sentence about the north bastion when he stopped, went to the window himself rather than sending anybody, closed the shutter, came back, and finished the sentence. / Nobody remarked on it.*

**NO FACE IS RESOLVED ANYWHERE IN IT, AND THAT IS TWO BANS, WRITTEN OUT** (§Plates). The man at the window has his back to the room and to the viewer; the nineteen behind him are a wash of standing shapes in lamplight. **What the picture is about cannot be put on a face**: it is a courtesy performed by somebody who is not being cruel.

**THE TWO LIGHTS DO THE ARGUMENT.** Warm lamplight fills the room and stops at the window; the gap in the shutter is cold, and the cold half is going out as the leaf comes across. **The warmth is inside and the noise is outside**, which is the district in one frame.

**AND THE ROOM IS DRAWN AS IT WAS READ.** Valeria's own sequence — exits, weapons, ground, then the people — gives the block its furniture: the long bare table, the chairs pushed back, nobody sitting. **One chair at the far end is occupied and its occupant is not resolved either**, which is all the page needs from the princess here.

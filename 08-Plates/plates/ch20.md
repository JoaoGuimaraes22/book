# Ch. 20 — The Word · plate sheet

**Composed at s48 from the scene.** Kael is twelve; Elarine twelve; Valeria twelve, and reading older off her anchor as she always does. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/elarine-14.png` on the first, `images/portraits/valeria-14.png` and `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S TWO COURTS AND ITS TWO REFUSALS OF THE SAME WORD.** A girl hands him the word and he cannot say it back; a girl tells him not to look for a word at all, and he uses a name instead and it is not answered. First light and last light, brick and water, and the same boy failing to price the same thing twice.

**THE CISTERN COURT IS ON ITS THIRD CHAPTER AND THE SECOND PLATE HAS TO PAY FOR IT.** `ch16/the-ear-on-the-brick` and `ch17/who-lives-in-it` are both that court; this one is the same brick in the opposite light — twenty minutes before anybody has any reason to be there, the rain stopped in the night, the walls wet and cold. **The pair's other half is the antidote:** open water, dusk, and two people side by side who never once look at each other.

**AND THE SECOND BLOCK DELIBERATELY BREAKS THE REGISTER'S ONE CONSTANT.** *Faces near and engaged with each other* is the scene-test shape and every adopted plate in the folder obeys it. Ch. 20 ends with a word going out and not coming back, and **the picture of that is two profiles and no eye contact** — the page states it twice (*She was looking straight out at the water*). It is the folder's first two-shot where nobody looks at anybody, and it is written that way on purpose, not for variety.

**ONE DETAIL LEFT OUT ON PURPOSE.** *She had blood on one knuckle from something earlier and was not attending to it* — `ch17`'s candidate 5 is held because fresh blood on a girl who reads sixteen is the refusal pattern, and a knuckle is below a hand's size in frame and could not carry finish anyway. Nothing is lost; the risk is a run that produces no picture at all.

### PLATE: you-are-my-friend

*Candidate 1.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`. Ch. 20 canon: *She was in the cistern court twenty minutes before anybody had any reason to be, sitting on the bench nobody sat on, with her satchel on her knees and her hands on the satchel.* … *Elarine Sedrane turned round on the bench and looked at him properly for the first time that morning.* … *"I am going to say something now, and you are going to do something strange with it, and I have decided in advance not to hold that against you."* … *"You are my friend," Elarine said. "It is not a complicated word. I have been using it about you privately since the summer, and I did not say it, because I could see exactly what you would do with it." Her chin came up a fraction.*

**HER FACE IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS DOING SOMETHING NO OTHER PLATE OF HER HAS DRAWN.** `ch13/the-spots-of-colour` is her being spent in public without choosing it and `ch17/who-lives-in-it` is her attention with nothing given back. This is Elarine Sedrane choosing to spend herself, in an empty court, with the sentence already out of her mouth and her chin up a fraction — **and watching what he does with it, which is the assessment her sheet describes turned on the one person it has ever cost her anything to run.**

**THE CHEEK-TELL IS BANNED BY NAME, WHICH IT HAS EARNED HERE.** `../canon-brief.md` reserves the spots of colour to Ch. 13 and Ch. 17 and nowhere else, and a girl of twelve saying this to a boy is the hardest pull toward a blush in the book. §Absence's test is exactly that: ban what the picture pulls toward.

**AND HIS FACE IS THE CHAPTER'S ARGUMENT.** *He did not have the word* — so the block asks for nothing arriving: not moved, not pleased, not embarrassed, the attention full on her and the face doing nothing at all with what it has been handed. **The picture is a girl who has said it and a boy who has no word.**

```
Image 1: elarine-14.png — portrait reference of the small girl with bound hair.
Image 2: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Setting: a disused cistern court very early on a winter morning, as
loose wash suggestion — old brick walls close behind them, cold
grey-brown, wet from rain that stopped in the night, moss in the
joints, the corners going into shade. Flat cold daylight from the
open side, no sun.

Composition: waist-up, two figures on a plank bench against the wall,
close together and near the viewer. The girl sits at the right and
has turned round on the bench to face the boy squarely, her satchel
set down on the bench beside her, her hands loose in her lap. The boy
sits at the left, facing along the bench, his hands on his knees. The
bottom edge crosses them both at the lap. They are twelve years old.
Nobody else is in the court.

THE GIRL HAS JUST SAID SOMETHING SHE DECIDED IN ADVANCE TO SAY AND IS
WATCHING WHAT HE DOES WITH IT. Her chin is up a fraction, the mouth
closed again after the sentence, the brows level, the eyes steady on
his face and giving nothing back. Not shy, not pleading, not smiling,
and no colour anywhere in her cheeks. Small and neat and finely
built; ink-dark hair bound severe with nothing loose; plain
close-fitting dark clothing of very good quality, a high buttoned
collar, ink-black and dark olive, no ornament of any kind.

THE BOY HAS BEEN HANDED SOMETHING HE CANNOT PRICE AND NOTHING IS
ARRIVING IN HIS FACE. The eyes on hers, the mouth closed, the brows
unmoved — attentive and entirely still. Not moved, not pleased, not
embarrassed, not blank. Undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dull
oatmeal and dead olive, no ornament and no metal.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER and neither is looking at
the viewer. This eye contact is the subject of the image.

Their faces are the most finished things in the picture; the brick,
the bench and the satchel stay at quiet middle values with soft
edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. Do not mix their
clothing: the fine dark cloth and the high collar belong only to the
girl, the mended undyed wool only to the boy. Her eyes very dark
brown, his pale grey.
```

### PLATE: val

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's last beat.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`. Ch. 20 canon: *She sat down on the stones beside him with her forearms across her knees.* … *"Then do not use a word," said Valeria Vel.* … *"Val," Kael said. / She did not answer. / It went on not being answered for long enough that he heard it going, the way you hear a stone going down a shaft and keep waiting after the sound should have come back.* … *the lamps came up along the water one at a time.*

**NO EYE CONTACT, AND THAT IS THE PICTURE.** The one word he had is out in the air between two people who are both looking at the sea. Every other adopted plate puts the faces at each other; this one may not, because the chapter's last page is a thing said and not answered.

**THE FAR LAMPS ARE PROVEN AND DO NOT LIGHT ANYBODY.** `ch01/fallen-stars` established it in this folder's first plate: an in-frame warm source at distance renders as a source and throws nothing, so the two of them are lit by the last of the sky and the lamps are the one warm thing in the frame. That finding is the reason this composition is safe to attempt at dusk.

**HIS HAND IS ON THE PAGE AND THE SHAKE IS NOT DRAWABLE.** *He sat with his hand shaking on his knee and did not take it back.* A tremor cannot be rendered, so the block asks for the hand open and slack on the knee rather than closed — the emptying written as a hand that is not gripping anything.

```
Image 1: valeria-14.png — portrait reference of the girl with long loose hair.
Image 2: valeria-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the same girl, for her clothing.
Image 3: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Setting: an old open flagstone court above the sea at the very end of
a winter afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — worn flagstones, a low
broken wall at the edge, and beyond it open water going dark. The
last flat grey light is off the water and the sky is nearly out.
Along the far waterline, small warm lamps have come up one at a time;

Composition: the viewer is at one side of them, level and close, so
both faces are seen in profile. Two figures sit side by side on the
cold flagstones with their backs to the low wall, the boy nearer the
viewer, the girl beyond him, both facing out to the water. She sits
with her forearms across her knees. His near hand lies open and slack

the shin. They are twelve years old. Nobody else is anywhere in the
picture.

NEITHER OF THEM IS LOOKING AT THE OTHER AND NEITHER IS LOOKING AT THE

sightlines run out of the frame on the same side. This is the subject
of the image: two people side by side and no eye contact anywhere in
it.

He has just said one word and it has not been answered: his mouth
closed, his face still, nothing asking and nothing withdrawn.

Her face gives nothing away — level, the mouth closed, the brows
unmoved, tired around the eyes, entirely unhurried. Not sad, not

sleeveless coat over the grey-blue underlayer, the high collar, the
steel vambraces.

He wears undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dull oatmeal and
dead olive, no ornament and no metal on him anywhere.

Their two profiles carry the most finished drawing in the picture;
the flagstones, the wall and the water stay at quiet middle values
with soft edges.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring; the two images of the girl are the same girl and
there are two figures in the picture. Do not blend their features. Do
```

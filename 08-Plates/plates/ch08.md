### PLATE: the-hand-over-the-face

*Candidate 3.* Anchors: `images/portraits/vask.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 8 canon: *his father made a sound... It had been short, low, one note — and his father's hand came up and covered the lower half of his own face, thumb and knuckle, a man rubbing his jaw at the end of a day, and above the hand his eyes had gone bright and creased at the corners, and his mother was not looking at either of them, she was looking at the bread, cutting it, and her mouth was pressed the way she pressed it when the bird got the song wrong on purpose, and the lamplight was moving on both their faces.*

**THE ONE PLATE IN THE BOOK WHERE VASK'S FACE IS ALLOWED TO DO SOMETHING.** `03` and the brief are absolute — *nothing in them that is angry, nothing that is anything* — and every plate so far has enforced it. **Here the page itself spends the exception**, in words, and this is the only time it does. So the eyes go bright and creased, and the rule is not touched: it is the page's one withdrawal from an account the book otherwise never draws on.

**AND BOTH OF THEM ARE HIDING IT FROM HIM, WHICH IS THE PICTURE.** He covers his face with his own hand; she will not look up from the bread. **The viewer is Kael**, so he is not in frame — the plate is the thing he was not supposed to see, seen.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch08-the-hand-over-the-face.png`, run 2.**

**RUN 2 CONFIRMS §NAME BOTH TEMPERATURES.** One clause naming the dark as cool grey-blue, two objects reserved as bare paper, and the ink told to stay the strongest element — **and the same composition stopped being brown.** The lamp is now a warm island in a cold room, the flame and the cut face of the loaf are the two lightest things in the picture, and the tool wall reads as cool iron instead of dissolving. **Nothing about the staging changed between the two runs.**

**RUN 1 (s42) — THE BEAT LANDED AND THE COLOUR DID NOT.** His own hand across his own mouth with the eyes bright and creased above it, her head down over the loaf and both hands working, neither looking at the other or at the viewer: all of it, first run, including the exception the page spends on his face.

**THE AUTHOR'S NOTE WAS MUDDY AND WATERY, AND THE CAUSE IS A CLAUSE THAT WAS NOT WRITTEN.** The block named a warm source and then held everything else *dark and loose with no hard edges* — **it never said what colour the dark was.** With only a lamp's hue in play the whole frame went one warm brown, no bare paper anywhere, and the ink softened into the wash.

**AND IT IS THE EXACT MIRROR OF `ch05/the-two-pails`**, which banned warm colour and came back greyscale. **Whichever temperature a plate does not name is the one that disappears.** Both plates in this folder that failed on colour failed the same way, from opposite ends. Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name both temperatures.

**Amended below**: the warm light is told to stop, everything beyond it is cool grey-blue with no brown in it, the flame and the cut face of the loaf are reserved as bare paper, and the ink is told to stay the strongest element instead of being softened along with everything else.

**HER HANDS ARE IN FRAME AND OCCUPIED**, cutting, which `03` requires of any figure of her: *the hands that undid knots... her hands are where the book keeps her.*

**A lamp on the table is a near warm source** — the configuration that lights a face (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §What a warm source will and will not light).

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the man.
Image 2: portrait reference of the woman.

Setting: the single room of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at
night, as loose wash suggestion — a scrubbed plank table across the
foreground, a wall of hand tools on pegs behind, everything past the
two figures falling into deep cool shadow.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS ONE SMALL OIL LAMP STANDING ON THE TABLE BETWEEN
THEM, close to both faces. It lights them from below and to one side,
catching the undersides of the jaws, the cheekbones and the hands.

THE WARM LIGHT STOPS WHERE IT STOPS, AND EVERYTHING BEYOND IT IS
COOL. The room, the roof, the tool wall and every shadow are cool
grey and grey-blue, with NO BROWN AND NO
WARM COLOUR ANYWHERE OUTSIDE THE LAMP'S REACH. The picture is warm in
one small place and cold everywhere else.

The lamp flame and the cut face of the loaf are reserved as bare
unpainted paper — the two lightest things in the picture and the only
places the paper is left bare.

Composition: the two of them seated at the far side of the table,
close, cropped at the chest, seen straight on from across the table
at a seated person's height. There is nobody else in the frame and
no third place set.

THE MAN'S OWN HAND COVERS THE LOWER HALF OF HIS OWN FACE — the heel
of the hand under the chin, the thumb along one side of the jaw and
the bent knuckle of the forefinger across the mouth, so that the
mouth is completely hidden. The posture is that of a man rubbing his
jaw at the end of a day. ABOVE THE HAND HIS EYES HAVE GONE BRIGHT AND THE SKIN
AT THEIR OUTER CORNERS IS CREASED INTO DEEP FOLDS. He is looking down
and slightly away, not at the viewer and not at the woman. He is
laughing, silently, and hiding it. Not grinning, not grimacing, not
weeping — the whole of it is in the eyes and the hand.

THE WOMAN IS NOT LOOKING AT ANYTHING BUT THE BREAD. Her head is
lowered over a round loaf on a board, both hands working — one flat
on the loaf, the other drawing a knife through it — and her eyes stay
on the cut. Her mouth is pressed flat and held, the lips pushed
together, the corners tight. She is holding the same thing in and it
is coming out at the mouth instead. Not smiling, not frowning.

Neither of them is looking at the other, and neither is looking at
the viewer.

Their two faces are the most finished things in the picture and carry
the sharpest step from light to dark. The table, the tools, the room
and the clothing are held at quiet dark values with soft wash edges,
but the ink linework stays the strongest element in all of them and
does not blur away.

THE MAN: broad across the top of the back and nothing wasted below
it. Iron-dark hair to the jaw. A face weathered far past its years,
deep-lined. Coarse undyed working cloth, closed at the throat.
THE WOMAN: long dark hair loose past the shoulders, fine bones under
worked hands. Plain coarse mended village cloth.
The ink carries the cloth on both, at the seams and the mended
places. Neither wears any ornament or metal.
```

### PLATE: the-one-who-did-not-laugh

*From `candidates.md`'s Ch. 8 alternate, corrected to the page.* Anchor: `images/portraits/elarine-14.png` — her face and colouring; she is younger here. Ch. 8 canon: *it made the benches laugh — a short crop of laughter, many throats at once*... *the way the girl at the end of the second bench — dark-haired, straight-backed, with a face that gave out nothing it hadn't priced first — had not laughed. Her stillness had changed when he spoke; that was all. Everyone else in the room he had learned nothing about. About her he had learned one thing, and it was the only thing all day he knew how to read.* And, on the slate: *"The minima," Priscian said, and wrote it on the great slate in letters a hand high.*

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch08-the-one-who-did-not-laugh.png`, first run.**

**AND A SECOND TREATMENT OF THE SAME MOMENT IS ALSO ADOPTED (author, s42):** `images/plates/ch08-the-one-who-did-not-laugh-cold.png`, from `### PLATE: the-one-who-did-not-laugh-cold` below. **Same staging, same four children, same beat — different colour and different edge.** The author kept both: *this one is best... less watery, but the one has its own appeal too.*

**RUN 1 (s42) — THE TWO-CLAUSE MECHANISM DID CHARACTERISATION, and that is the finding.** She is the smallest and quietest figure in the frame and unmistakably the most finished thing in it: the three laughing children came back broadly washed with summary features and soft edges, and her face carries every hard edge in the picture. **`03`'s *forgettable at a glance and unforgettable the third time you notice she hasn't moved* is not a note about her personality — it is a value instruction, and it renders.** Also held: four figures and no crowd, the bound hair against a laughing girl's loose hair (silhouette separation arriving for free), the high collar, no ornament, no warm colour, no master and no lectern.

**THE LETTERING RENDERED CLEAN, WHICH WAS THE GAMBLE.** `MINIMA` came back correctly spelled, in even chalk capitals, at the stated size. **The recipe worth keeping: one short word, all capitals, named exactly, stated as the only thing written on the surface.** The folder had no evidence text could be trusted at all; it now has one clean instance and the conditions that produced it.

**MISS: SHE READS OLDER THAN THE OTHERS.** The three laughing children come in at about eleven and she reads adolescent — a gap inside one frame, on a bench where every child is the same age. The block put her age in the reference line (*she is younger here, about eleven*) and nowhere else, which is exactly the placement `../portrait-prompt-system.md` §AGE block says gets ignored. **This is the second run to point at §A close-up plate and the age line, which is logged there as raised-and-unconfirmed** — the first datum was overturned by the author, so this one is recorded and the section is left open rather than promoted on two soft readings.

**ELARINE'S FIRST APPEARANCE, AND THE COMPOSITION IS THE CHARACTER.** `03`: *forgettable at a glance and unforgettable the third time you notice she hasn't moved.* **So she is not the biggest thing in the frame and she is the most finished thing in it** — the two-clause mechanism doing characterisation rather than decoration. The laughing faces are animated and loose; hers is quiet and exact.

**THE VIEWER IS KAEL**, at his own bench, so the room turns toward the camera. He is not in frame and the laughter is aimed at where he is.

**FOUR FIGURES, WHICH IS THE TESTED CEILING** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Four figures). *A short crop of laughter, many throats at once* is written as three, not as a crowd.

**HER ONE TELL IS DELIBERATELY ABSENT.** `03` keeps the spots of colour high on her cheeks for Ch. 13 and Ch. 17 — *when something is about to be said properly or when she is being spent in public* — and this is neither.

**THE LETTERING IS A GAMBLE AND IS WORTH ONE RUN.** Text renders unreliably; if it comes back as chalk marks rather than a word, the picture still works and the slate is still a slate.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the girl — her face and colouring; she
is younger here, about eleven.

Setting: a stone lecture room in a temple, as loose wash suggestion —
long plank benches, a great dark slate on the wall behind, the far
corners falling into shadow. Flat cool daylight from a high window on
one side. No warm colour anywhere.

On the slate, chalked in white letters a hand high: MINIMA. Nothing
else is written on it.

Composition: looking along a bench from one end, at seated height,
cropped at the chest. FOUR CHILDREN OF ABOUT ELEVEN, and no more than
four. THREE OF THEM ARE TURNED TOWARD THE VIEWER AND LAUGHING —
heads back or tipped together, mouths open, eyes creased, one with a
hand half up. THE FOURTH, AT THE FAR END OF THE BENCH, IS NOT
LAUGHING.

THE GIRL AT THE END: sitting straight-backed and completely still,
her head turned the same way as the others, her attention on the same
point out of frame — but her face gives nothing back. Mouth closed
and unmoved, brows level, no reaction anywhere in it. She is not
frowning, not disapproving, not bored, not cold and not tired. She is
looking at something and pricing it, and the pricing is still
running.

She is small and finely built, narrow through the shoulders, with
nothing on her that does not work. Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral
black with no blue in it, drawn back severely and bound tight at the
nape — nothing loose anywhere. Pale indoor skin, no weathering and no
colour in the cheeks. Eyes very dark, near black. Plain dark
close-fitting clothing of very good quality, exactly made and
buttoned high at the throat, in ink-black and dark olive. No
ornament, no metal and no white on her anywhere.

HER FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING IN THE PICTURE and carries the
sharpest step from light to dark, and she is the smallest and
quietest figure in it. The three laughing children are loose and
broadly washed with soft edges, their features summary — no hard
edges and no detail in any of their faces. The benches, the slate and
the room are held at quiet middle values.

The three laughing children wear plainer, looser clothes than hers,
and none of them is dressed like her. Nobody else is in the frame:
no master, no lectern and no adult.
```

### PLATE: seeing-about-the-goat

*Author-requested (s40); built then, never run, unchanged.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png` — though his face is not in this picture. Ch. 8 canon: *his father stood up and went to see about the goat, who did not need seeing about, and was some time in coming back.*

The other half of `the-hand-over-the-face`, and it is the same beat: the man leaves a lit room in order to have a feeling about his son where nobody can see it.

```
No reference image.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house at night,
wet after rain, as loose wash suggestion — a fenced pen, a woodpile,
the mountain behind lost in deep cool wash.

Composition: the man standing full length in the yard with his BACK
TO THE VIEWER, facing away toward a goat in the pen. He is between
the viewer and the animal. No part of his face is visible.

Behind him and to one side, an open doorway stands empty — a plain
plank door open on a lit room, with NOBODY IN IT and nobody in the
doorway. The lamplight from it lies across the wet ground in a long
bright rectangle, and his own shadow is cut into it, long and black,
reaching away from the door toward him.

THE LIGHT ON THE GROUND IS THE BRIGHTEST THING IN THE PICTURE and
carries the sharpest step from light to dark, wet ground taking the
light in broken reflections. The man is a dark shape against it. The
yard, the pen, the woodpile and the sky are held dark and loose.

He is doing nothing. Standing square and still with his arms at his
sides, both hands empty, not leaning on the rail, not reaching for
the animal, not working. ONE GOAT, side-on in the pen, entirely
indifferent — not looking at him, not curious, not alarmed.

Broad across the top of the back and nothing wasted below it. Coarse
undyed mended working cloth, the ink carrying the cloth at the seams
and the mended places.
```

### PLATE: the-one-who-did-not-laugh-cold

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch08-the-one-who-did-not-laugh-cold.png`, first run.** The second treatment of the plate above, kept as its own block because **one image, one prompt**: this text produced that picture and the block above produced the other.

**WHAT DIFFERS IS COLOUR AND EDGE, AND NOTHING ELSE.** The staging, the four children, the slate, the crop and the two-clause mechanism are identical. Three changes: the cold hues named and sited on the slate, the walls, the benches and the clothing with skin as the only warm thing; three specific objects reserved as bare paper; and the ink protected against the general softening — **with that softening scoped to the three laughing FACES only**, which is what stopped the benches and their clothes going soupy with them.

**IT IS THE COLDEST AND MOST DRAWN PICTURE IN THE FOLDER**, and it sits beside a warm, washed version of the same frame. **That pair is the most useful thing in the set for the finish question the `README` leaves open** — not an argument about which is right, but the same picture rendered both ways with the diff written down.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the girl — her face and colouring; she
is younger here, about eleven.

Setting: a stone lecture room in a temple, as loose wash suggestion —
long plank benches, a great dark slate on the wall behind, the far
corners falling into shadow. Flat cool daylight from a high window on
one side.

THE COLOURS ARE NAMED AND SITED, AND THEY ARE ALL COLD: the slate a
blue-black, the stone walls a cool pale grey, the benches a grey
timber with no warmth in it, all the clothing in blue-greys and
ink-black. THE ONLY WARM THING IN THE PICTURE IS SKIN, and there is
no brown anywhere else in it.

Bare unpainted paper is reserved in exactly three places and nowhere
else: the window, blown out to white; the chalked letters; and the
pale edge of light along the front of the nearest bench.

On the slate, chalked in white letters a hand high: MINIMA. Nothing
else is written on it.

Composition: looking along a bench from one end, at seated height,
cropped at the chest. FOUR CHILDREN OF ABOUT ELEVEN, and no more than
four. THREE OF THEM ARE TURNED TOWARD THE VIEWER AND LAUGHING —
heads back or tipped together, mouths open, eyes creased, one with a
hand half up. THE FOURTH, AT THE FAR END OF THE BENCH, IS NOT
LAUGHING.

THE GIRL AT THE END: sitting straight-backed and completely still,
her head turned the same way as the others, her attention on the same
point out of frame — but her face gives nothing back. Mouth closed
and unmoved, brows level, no reaction anywhere in it. She is not
frowning, not disapproving, not bored, not cold and not tired. She is
looking at something and pricing it, and the pricing is still

She is small and finely built, narrow through the shoulders, with
nothing on her that does not work. Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral
black with no blue in it, drawn back severely and bound tight at the
nape — nothing loose anywhere. Pale indoor skin, no weathering and no

HER FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING IN THE PICTURE and carries the
```

# Ch. 29 — The Gate · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, with `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the first for the kit.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S ARGUMENT AND THE ORDER MATTERS:** an institution that says no to him at the width of one person, and a stranger with a third of a pint who says nothing at all and spends some of it on him. **The wall is the one with the light behind it.**

**HALDEN IS IN THE FIRST PICTURE AND HIS FACE IS NOT.** `ch13.md` ruled it and named him in the ruling: *a plate is not where a person's face gets decided; whatever it drew would become his reference by default*, and there is no `images/portraits/halden.png`. **The block does not work around that — it uses it.** The judge is a broad dark shape filling a lit slot with no features on him, which is what a boy outside a gate can see of the man who is deciding about him, and it costs the beat nothing because the beat is the eighteen inches and not the face. **The work that unblocks a resolved Halden is a portrait, not a plate.**

**THE WOMAN WITH THE SHAWL IS AUTHORED WHOLE, AND THAT IS LEGAL WHERE HALDEN IS NOT.** She has no name, no sheet and no second scene — nothing for a drawn face to become. `ch18/the-man-at-the-second-bend` is the precedent: an unnamed islander built out of the block alone.

**THE TWO LIGHTS ARE THE SET'S FIRST DELIBERATE PAIR.** Everything outside that wall is flat cold fogged daylight with smoke in it; the only warm thing in either picture is *light on stone and the ordinary sound of an ordinary street*, and it is behind the man in the gap and it does not reach the boy's face. §Light is explicit that a warm source lights a face only when it is close, so the warmth stays inside the wall — **which is the chapter, drawn.**

**HE IS COVERED AND THE PAGE SAYS SO TWICE.** *He was a boy of thirteen with blood to the elbow and two short blades across his back* … *"You're bleeding, love."* … *"It's not mine," Kael said, which was true of most of him and not of that.* So the red is old and dry everywhere except the shoulder, and the shoulder is the one thing on him that is his own. **Both blocks site it there and nowhere else**, which is what makes the second picture legible without a word of dialogue.

**AND THE SECOND PICTURE'S SUBJECT IS A LINE OF SIGHT THAT DOES NOT MEET.** She is working and looking at the shoulder; he is looking at her face. *She did not know who he was. That was the thing he could not put anywhere.* §Gaze names eye contact as a subject when it happens; here the block names the miss, on purpose, and gives the working hands the finish instead.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** *she let herself be seen* — Valeria alone in the strip of open ground in front of the spear line — is the chapter's other great picture and it is a solo Valeria one chapter after `ch28/rearranging-the-road`. **The mountain is not empty** — eleven fires read as houses across four miles of ground, men going along the field walls like water in a gutter, boats on the eastern water — is the widest thing in Part IV and it is the same shape as `ch27/the-sky`. Both stay on `candidates.md` with their framing.

### PLATE: the-eighteen-inches

*Candidate 1, ★.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 29 canon: *It opened the width of a person. Not a gate opening — a bar coming off and a leaf coming back about eighteen inches and stopping, with men behind it and their shoulders in it, and the whole weight of that hillside arriving against the front of the spear line in the same second.* … *Behind him, inside, there was light on stone and the ordinary sound of an ordinary street.* … *"Sir." He had never once called anybody sir in his life. It came out anyway. "Sir. Please. My mother is on the mountain."* … *the judge stood in the gap and gave Kael the whole of his attention, which is what he had always done.*

**THE SUBJECT IS A MEASUREMENT.** Eighteen inches is on the page as a number, so the block gives it a ratio the frame can check — the slot is a little wider than the boy's shoulders and no more — per §Scale, which says a ratio to something in the frame and never an adjective. Everything else in the picture is built to make that gap read: the leaf is heavy and dark and takes the left of the frame, the wall goes up out of the top of it, and the warm light is only ever in the slot.

**HIS HANDS ARE ON THE WOOD AND THE PAGE PUT THEM THERE TWICE** — *He got a hand on the edge of the leaf* and then *both hands on the wood now*. It is the only thing in the chapter he does with his body, and it is the thing a boy does to a door that is closing.

**HIS FACE IS ASKING AND IT IS NOT CRYING.** The chapter is explicit that he heard himself climb — *Kael heard his own voice climb and could not get underneath it* — so the block gives an open mouth mid-word, the brows up and drawn in, the chin lifted, and bans the tears; **what is happening to him is entirely in the hands and the chin.**

```

2: kael-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the same boy, for his
build and clothing.

Setting: the outside of a high cut-stone wall, close to, as loose wash
suggestion — great dressed blocks rising past the top edge of the
picture, a heavy dark timber gate leaf standing open a hand's width at
the left, and behind the boy a packed mass of people falling away into
smoke and cold grey. Flat cold fogged daylight outside, no sun and no
hard-edged shadow.

Composition: the bottom edge of the picture crosses the boy just below
the waist. He stands at the left of centre, close to the viewer and
turned three-quarters away from it toward the gate, his head lifted, his
face in profile-to-three-quarter view and fully visible. The opening
between the leaf and the jamb is a tall narrow vertical slot beside him,
a little wider than his own shoulders and no wider, running from the
bottom of the frame to the top of it.

BOTH HIS HANDS ARE FLAT ON THE EDGE OF THE OPEN LEAF at about the height
of his own chest, the fingers hooked over the timber, the arms half
straight. Nothing else is in either hand and no blade is drawn anywhere
in the picture.

INSIDE THE SLOT, AND ONLY INSIDE IT, THE LIGHT IS WARM: pale gold on
dressed stone a little way in, the one warm thing in the whole picture.
It does not reach the boy, and everything on his side of the gate stays
cold grey.

A BROAD MAN STANDS IN THE SLOT, filling it, seen against the light
behind him as a dark shape with no features on him — no eyes and no face
drawn anywhere — and his head is not turned toward the viewer. He is not
hurrying and nothing about him is raised. He is the only person drawn
inside the gate.

HIS FACE IS TURNED UP TO THE MAN IN THE SLOT: the mouth open mid-word,
the brows raised and drawn together, the eyes wide with the pale grey
clear and steady on him. He is not looking at the viewer. There are no
tears on his face.

Dried dark red up both forearms and across the backs of both hands. One
fresh red slash through the cloth at his right shoulder, its edges
clean, and red at the cloth around it. Grey dust and soot over the front
of him. A cloth-wrapped bundle slung across his back with the shape of
two hilts in it, the hilts covered.

The crowd behind him is a wash of small shapes with no face resolved
anywhere in it, at quiet middle values with soft edges. His face, his
two hands and the lit slot carry the sharpest steps from light to dark
in the picture.

Preserve his face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as shown in
the reference images. He is the only figure with a face in the picture.
Pale grey eyes. He wears undyed coarse working wool, much mended, in
dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no metal anywhere on him.
```

### PLATE: the-shawl

*Candidate 4, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 29 canon: *"You're bleeding, love."* … *She was somewhere past fifty. She had a shawl, one bundle, nothing else at all, and she was pointing at his shoulder with two fingers, at a cut he had taken at the wells and not looked at since.* … *She got down beside him with the noise people that age make getting down, and she took the corner of the shawl off her own shoulders, and she wet it out of a stoppered bottle that was the only thing in the bundle worth anything, and she cleaned it. She did it briskly and not very well. She talked the whole time about somebody called Ilva who was not there and who she was quite sure had gone to her sister's.* … *She did not know who he was.*

**IT IS THE ONLY WARM PICTURE IN PART IV AND IT IS NOT LIT WARM.** Nothing on that hillside has a warm source in it; the warmth is entirely in the staging — two people close, one of them working on the other — which is the instrument `ch01/talking-the-goat-down` was built on. **The light stays exactly as cold as it is in the picture beside it.**

**SHE IS BRISK AND SHE IS NOT TENDER**, and the block says so in anatomy: the hand doing the work is quick and not careful, and her mouth is open mid-word on a subject that has nothing to do with him. *She did it briskly and not very well.* **Warmth written as competence, never as sentiment** — the same fence `ch01` put on the father.

**THE BOTTLE IS THE POINT AND IT IS SMALL.** *A stoppered bottle that was the only thing in the bundle worth anything.* It gets its own hand and its own line so that the frame can price it, per §Hands.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the dark-haired boy.

Setting: an open hillside outside a wall, as loose wash suggestion — dry
grass, a low field wall of loose stone close behind them, and past that
a packed mass of standing people falling away into smoke and cold grey.
Flat cold fogged daylight, no sun and no hard-edged shadow anywhere.

Composition: two figures, close together and near the viewer, the bottom
edge of the picture crossing both at the lower chest. The boy sits on
the ground at the right with his back against the low stone wall, turned
three-quarters toward the viewer. The woman is beside and above him at
the left, crouched down on her heels and leaning in, her body turned
toward his right shoulder and away from the viewer, her face in three-
quarter view and fully visible. Both faces are in the upper half of the
frame. Nobody else is drawn near them.

A WOMAN PAST FIFTY, weathered, broad in the face, the skin loose at the
jaw and the eyes small and quick. Grey hair pulled back untidily. A
heavy knitted shawl in undyed grey wool over her shoulders, its near
corner taken up off her shoulder and bunched in her working hand. Coarse
village clothing, mended, in dull brown and dead green. There is nothing
ornamental anywhere on her.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: her right hand holds the bunched corner of her own
shawl and is pressing it against the boy's right shoulder, quick and not
careful. Her left hand holds a small stoppered clay bottle down at her
knee and nothing else. His right hand rests palm down on his own raised
knee, open and empty. His left hand is flat on the ground beside him.
Four hands, two objects, nothing else in any hand.

HER MOUTH IS OPEN MID-WORD and her eyes are down on the shoulder she is
working on. She is not looking at the viewer.

HE IS LOOKING AT HER FACE: the head turned up to her, the lids fully
raised, the pale grey clear, the mouth closed, the brows level. He is
not looking at the viewer. THE TWO LINES OF SIGHT DO NOT MEET, AND THAT
IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE.

A fresh red slash through the cloth at his right shoulder, its edges
clean, with the shawl corner against it. Dried dark red up both his
forearms and across the backs of both his hands, older and browner than
the shoulder. Grey dust and soot over the front of him. A cloth-wrapped
bundle is on the ground beside him with the shape of two hilts in it,
the hilts covered.

Both faces are the most finished things in the picture and her two
working hands carry the sharpest step from light to dark after them; the
field wall, the crowd and the smoke stay at quiet middle values with
soft edges.

Preserve the boy's face, hair, colouring and apparent age exactly as
shown in the reference image. Pale grey eyes. He wears undyed coarse
working wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive. Do not blend
their features and do not mix their clothing: the knitted shawl belongs
only to her.
```

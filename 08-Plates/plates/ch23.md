# Ch. 23 — The Ghost · plate sheet

**Composed at s48 from the scene.** Kael is thirteen. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the first for the body.

**THE PAIR IS THE ARGUMENT AND WHAT IT COST HIM.** A boy declining to draw in front of the empire's own grammar, and the same boy four minutes later at the seaward rail with his arm open, looking at a horizon that has never had anything on it and now has banners.

**LUCAN HAS NO PORTRAIT FILE, AND THE COMPOSITION ANSWERS IT.** `ch13.md`'s ruling on Oss: a plate is not where a person's face gets decided, and whatever it drew would become his reference by default. So he is in the near foreground **with his back to the viewer** — the `ch16/nine-strides` device — which costs nothing and buys something: the reader stands behind the taught system, looking across forty feet at the boy who will not answer it. `03/lucan` gives *tall, narrow, all reach; a careful dresser even in yard clothes*, and that is all the block spends on him.

**NEITHER BLOCK STATES AN AGE.** §Damage's known refusal pair is a stated age beside harm, and `ch21/dead` was refused with a weapon in the frame. The first block has a drawn sword in it and the second has an open cut, so both leave the number out — the anchor governs the face anyway, and every plate this session has come back mid-teens whatever the rung said.

**THE CUT IS THIS CHAPTER'S PROPERTY AND IT IS DRAWN HERE FIRST.** `../canon-brief.md`: the long shallow scar on the outside of the **left** arm above the elbow is Ch. 23's, drawable only where the chapter has shown it. This is the chapter, and in the second block it is still open — *he felt the cold of it before he felt anything else, and then it was warm all the way to his wrist.* It is written the way §Damage prescribes: name the cut and where it is, never the tissue, and `aftermath-kael` proves the register renders blood, bruising and torn cloth without difficulty.

### PLATE: empty

*Candidate 1.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 23 canon: *Lucan Drusane came to the measure the way he had been taught to. The front foot down, the back foot turned out, the sword up into the guard a paid master had spent four years putting into his shoulder. The off-hand open, empty, out to the side, because that was doctrine.* … *Then he came to his own mark, and he stopped. / He did not draw.* … *The two short blades were across his back in their wrap … they were not going to be near one today.* … *He put his hands at his sides and left them empty.* … *"You have to draw," Lucan said, from the measure.*

**THE SUBJECT IS TWO POSTURES AND THE FORTY FEET BETWEEN THEM.** Everything the chapter argues is in the bodies: one built by four years of a paid master into a shape that only works at a distance, the other standing with nothing in either hand and a bundle on his back he has decided not to open. **This is the silhouette-separation table doing the work it was written for** (`../README.md`) — one figure all reach and grammar, the other invisible by stillness.

**AND THE WRAP IS THE POINT OF THE PICTURE, SO IT IS WRITTEN IN BY HAND.** `../canon-brief.md` says his full-figure sheet carries no blades and that the wrap goes in when the moment carries it. This moment is entirely about the fact that it stays shut.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: kael-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the same boy, for his build and his clothing.

Setting: the floor of a great open arena on a bright cold morning, as
loose wash suggestion — raked white sand underfoot, banked seating
rising behind and above, packed with people drawn only as a dense
wash of small shapes with no faces, and a cold blue sky at the top of
the frame. Bright flat winter daylight from above.

Composition: a wide view along the sand, at head height. In the near
foreground at one side, seen from behind and slightly to one side, a
tall narrow boy stands squared at the measure: his back to the
viewer, no part of his face visible anywhere, the front foot planted
and the back foot turned out, a long straight sword raised into a
formal guard and his other hand open, empty, held out to the side.
He is in frame from the thigh up and is cut off by the edge of the
frame. Across the open sand from him, at a distance of many times his
own height, the dark-haired boy stands alone at his mark, facing him
and facing the viewer, in frame from the knees up and small in the
picture but clearly readable.

THE DARK-HAIRED BOY HAS NOT DRAWN AND HIS HANDS ARE THE SUBJECT: both
arms hang at his sides, the hands open and completely empty, nothing
held in either of them and nothing raised. Across his back, over the
shoulder, rides a long bundle wrapped in plain cloth and tied — no
hilt, no blade and no metal shows anywhere on it, and it has not been
touched.

He is standing still and giving nothing: the head level, the face
turned to the boy across the sand, the mouth closed, the whole body
easy and unarranged. Not braced, not defiant, not afraid. He is
looking at the other boy and not at the viewer.

Undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dull oatmeal and dead olive,
with no ornament, badge, colour or metal on him anywhere. The other
boy's clothing is well made and carefully worn, in darker cloth.

Nothing is happening yet: nobody has moved, nothing has been thrown,
and no blood, wound or mark is anywhere in the picture.

The raked sand is reserved as bare unpainted paper and is the
brightest thing in the frame; the two figures are the darkest marks
on it and carry the hardest edges. The crowd, the seating and the sky
stay at quiet middle values with soft edges, and nothing in the crowd
is drawn sharply enough to read as a person.

Preserve the dark-haired boy exactly as shown in his reference images
— face, hair, colouring; the two images are the same boy. Pale grey
eyes.
```

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-horizon

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's peak.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 23 canon: *He looked out over the seaward rail while the noise came down on him. / From the middle of the floor you could see over the top of the far seating, and past it there was the sea, and on the sea there were more ships than he had known existed, and behind them a horizon that went on being a horizon. He had been looking at that line since he was four years old from a rock on a mountain, and it had never once had anything on it. Now it had banners on it* … *He was thirteen, and he was bleeding, and he had never in his life been so happy. / He did not notice that thought arrive.*

**THE FACE IS HAPPY AND DOES NOT KNOW IT**, which is the whole instruction. Not a smile: the page is explicit that the thought went into him *the way water goes into cloth* and that he did not notice it arrive. So the block asks for an open, unguarded face with nothing arranged on it — the same rarity as `ch19/will-i-be-allowed-to-hold-it`, and the second time in the book the coat is off him.

**THE HORIZON IS THE BOOK'S OLDEST OBJECT.** He has watched that line from a rock since he was four and it has never had anything on it. Putting ships and banners on it is the payoff of every sea-watching beat in Part I, and it costs one clause.

**THE ARM IS OPEN AND UNBOUND, WHICH DATES THE PICTURE EXACTLY.** Ruthen wraps it in four turns at the mouth of the gate a few minutes later; this is before that, on the floor, with the blood already down to the fingers.


**RUN 1 (s48) — REFUSED AS WRITTEN, THEN RUN BY THE AUTHOR ON A TWO-WORD CHANGE.** *had to change prompt to pass guidelines.* Everything else in the block identical: **`A long open cut` → `A long slash`**, and **`there is blood down the outside of the forearm` → `there is red down the outside of the forearm`.** The heading *HIS LEFT ARM IS CUT* and the later *torn where the cut is* both stayed and neither was a problem.

**THIS IS THE CLEANEST ISOLATION THE FOLDER HAS.** `ch21/dead` moved four variables at once and proved nothing about which one mattered; this moved two words in one sentence. **`../prompt-system.md` §Damage is amended on both counts**: a refusal is often a fault in the block rather than a limit on the moment, and the two nouns that bought this picture are named there. **Harm is not the barrier** — the same block draws a slash, torn cloth and red down a hand, and `aftermath-kael` has been rendering bruising and soot since the scene system was built.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch23-the-horizon.png` (author, s48: *adopt*). THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S AS-RUN TEXT**, not the composed one: the folder's rule is that a plate's block is the text that made the picture, and the two changed words are `slash` and `red`.

**WHAT LANDED.** The turn-away with the head out to the sea; the far seating as a wash with no faces; the ships with banners at the mastheads as the only saturated colour in the frame; the horizon under cold blue sky; the sand as bare paper. The slash along the outside of the left arm above the elbow with red down the forearm, the arm bare and unbound — **which dates the picture before Ruthen's four turns of cloth.** Nothing in either hand and no weapon anywhere.

**WHAT DRIFTED.** The face reads a shade closer to stunned than to *giving everything away*; *not dazed* was banned by name and did not quite win. The tear in the cloth is larger than *torn where the cut is* — the whole shoulder is out. He reads fifteen or sixteen; the anchor.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the middle of the floor of a great open arena on a bright
cold morning, as loose wash suggestion — raked white sand, and beyond
the far side of the arena the top of the banked seating, and past
that open sea with many ships standing on it, small with the
distance, banners at their mastheads. A flat sea horizon runs across
the upper part of the frame under a cold blue sky. Bright flat
winter daylight from above.

Composition: chest-up, one figure, near the viewer and turned
three-quarters away, his head turned out toward the sea so that his

the chest. Behind and past him, small and low in the frame, the far
seating with a wash of standing people on it, no faces drawn, and
then the sea, the ships and the horizon line.

HIS LEFT ARM IS CUT. A long slash runs along the outside of the
left arm above the elbow, its edges clean, and there is red down
the outside of the forearm and across the back of the hand, dark and
wet. The arm is bare and unbound and no cloth or wrapping is on it.
His clothing is torn where the cut is and grubby with sand.

HIS FACE IS OPEN AND HE DOES NOT KNOW IT. The eyes wide and out on
the far horizon, the mouth closed, the brows lifted a little, the
whole face unguarded and giving everything away — and no smile
anywhere on it. Not laughing, not grinning, not triumphant, not
dazed, not in pain.

He is looking out at the ships on the horizon and nowhere else, not
at the viewer.

Undyed coarse working wool, much mended, dull oatmeal and dead olive,
with no ornament, badge, colour or metal on him anywhere. Nothing is
in either hand and no weapon is anywhere in the picture.

His face carries the sharpest step from light to dark and is the most
finished thing in the frame; the sand, the seating, the ships and the
sky stay at quiet middle values with soft edges. The banners on the
far masts are the only saturated colour anywhere in the picture.

Preserve him exactly as shown in his reference image — face, hair,
colouring. Pale grey eyes, the lightest value in his face. Dark hair
cut short and uneven.
```

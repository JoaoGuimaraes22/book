# Ch. 19 — The Winter · plate sheet

**Composed at s48, on the author's delegation (*you choose*), from the scene.** Kael is twelve; Neris is three months along and it is the night she says so. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/neris.png` on the second. **Vask's reference is deliberately not attached to the first** — his face is out of that frame by the page's own staging, so there is nothing for it to carry.

**THE PAIR IS THE HOUSE'S SECRET AND THE HOUSE'S ONE WARM MINUTE.** A boy who has counted around a floorboard since he was nine watching it move, and a boy who asks whether he will be allowed to hold the baby. Neither is at the council table.

**AND THE COUNCIL TABLE IS WHY THE CHAPTER'S LARGEST MOMENT IS NOT HERE.** The parents disagreeing in front of him for the first time in his life — *"It may not be them." / "It is them."* — is the biggest fact in the chapter and it is three people at that table by that light, which is `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` exactly: same room, same boards, same three, hands flat. **So the pair is built to go nowhere near it**, and the two blocks below share neither the light nor the crop nor the cast of that plate: fire rather than a lamp, two figures rather than three, a floor-level camera and a chest-up close rather than a waist-up across a table.

**A REAL COLLISION, AND IT IS RULED IN THE PLATE RULE'S FAVOUR.** `../canon-brief.md` says Neris's hands go in frame and occupied in any scene — *her hands are where the book keeps her* — and the chapter gives her exactly one occupied gesture, *her palm flat on a bar that was already down*, which belongs to a different beat and is in any case the composition `ch10` rejected as a near-duplicate of `ch02-the-doorframe` (same woman, same door, same hand). At the moment this plate draws, the page puts nothing in her hands. **THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE says a silent page is answered with framing and never with invention**, so her hands are out of the frame and the block says they are, rather than inventing a gesture to satisfy a note about how she is drawn. The boy's hands stay in, flat on the boards, because this chapter puts them there in its own words. **The fence is not weakened**: it governs a full figure, which this is not.

**THE PREGNANCY IS DECIDED AND NOT DEFAULTED** (`../canon-brief.md` requires the choice): three months, nothing showing, and the crop has no waist in it — so no clause is spent on it at all.

### PLATE: the-third-board

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 19 canon: *His father crossed the room in the middle of it, thinking, his thumb moving along his fingers the way it did when he was counting a thing that was not in the room, and stopped at the foot of the loft ladder and put his boot flat down on the third board and stood on it.* … *It went down about the thickness of a fingernail, all of it at once, end to end, and when the weight came off it came back up all at once as well, and in the black seam along its edge, for about as long as it takes to blink, there was a line of iron.* And the boy: *Kael sat with his hands flat on the table and did not ask.*

**THE PICTURE IS THE BLINK.** One line of iron in a seam, for the length of time it takes a board to come back up — and the whole plate is built to make a thing the width of a finger win a frame. §The winner and the rival, both channels: the iron takes the hardest edge and the sharpest step in the picture, and everything else in the room — the boot, the ladder, the boy, the boards — is held at quiet middle values with soft edges. **The instrument's own limit says a tell below a hand's size in frame cannot carry finish and the fix is the crop**, so the camera goes down onto the floor and the seam runs a hand's width across the near frame.

**THE FATHER'S FACE IS OUT OF FRAME BECAUSE THE PAGE PUT IT THERE.** *Neither of them looked at the floor, and there was no reason on earth why either of them should have.* A man whose face is in the picture is a man who knows what is under his boot and is thinking about it; the leg and the boot alone are a man counting a goat. That is also why no reference is attached for him — nothing above the knee is in the picture to preserve.

**AND THE BOY IS IN IT, SMALL, WHICH IS THE ARGUMENT.** A faceless candidate is priced, not disqualified (s43), and this one is not faceless: the only face in the frame belongs to the person who has just understood the floor and will not say so for years. He is at middle distance and softly finished, and he is what makes the picture a scene rather than a still life.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house late at night, as loose
wash suggestion — bare plank floorboards, the foot of a loft ladder,
the walls and corners going into deep dark. A low fire out of frame
at one side is the only light in the picture: it lands along the
floorboards and leaves the ladder and the far wall dark.

Composition: a low viewpoint, the viewer's eye a hand's height above
the floor, looking along the boards toward the foot of the ladder.
Nearest and largest, running across the frame, one floorboard with a
man's heavy worn boot set flat on it and the whole of his weight on
that foot; his leg is in frame to above the knee in coarse dark
working wool and the rest of him is out of the frame above. The board
has sunk a fingernail's thickness along its whole length. Beyond the
ladder, at the far side of the room, a boy of twelve sits at a plank
table with both hands flat on the boards, his face turned down toward
the floor and his mouth closed.

THE SUBJECT IS A LINE OF IRON IN THE SEAM BESIDE THE BOOT. Along the
open edge of the sunken board, a single thin line of iron shows in
the black gap, about a hand's length in the frame — the hardest edge
and the sharpest step from light to dark anywhere in the picture, and
the only metal in it. No nail heads and no other metal appear
anywhere in the floor.

The boot, the ladder, the table, the boy and the boards all stay at
quiet middle values with soft edges and no competing detail. Warm
firelight low across the floor, cool dark everywhere it does not
reach.

Nothing is happening: the man is standing still on the board, not
looking down, and the boy is sitting still, not moving and not
speaking. Nobody else is in the room, and no face other than the
boy's appears anywhere in the picture.

Preserve the boy exactly as shown in his reference image — face,
hair, colouring. Pale grey eyes.
```

### PLATE: will-i-be-allowed-to-hold-it

*Candidate 2.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 19 canon: *"I'm going to have a brother. Or a sister." / "One or the other," his mother agreed.* … *"Will I be allowed to hold it." / He heard it come out of his own mouth and could not think where it had come from. It was not a count and it priced nothing and nobody in that room needed it, and he was twelve years old … and he had asked it in the voice he had asked things in when he was five.* … *His mother looked at him for some time. / "Yes."* … *Something came up into her face that had not been anywhere in it since he came through the door, and stood there a moment, and went.*

**THE PAGE HANDS OVER THE EXPRESSION, SO THE PLATE MAY DRAW IT** — the same licence as `ch17/her-own-hands`. What it may not do is let it arrive as warmth in general: `../canon-brief.md` says *not warm, not maternal, not soft*, and the page says something came up **and went**. So it is written as anatomy and as an easing rather than a smile, on a face that is otherwise still.

**THE EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT** (§Gaze), and it is also this plate's insurance: two people looking at each other cannot send a gaze to the viewer, which is the fault that took two runs off `ch18/caught-it-halfway` and one off `ch18/the-man-at-the-second-bend`.

**IT IS THE ONLY PLATE IN THE BOOK WHERE KAEL'S FACE IS OPEN.** Every other one gives nothing away by design — the coat. Here the page takes it off him for one line, in his own house, and the block asks for the face of a much younger boy on a twelve-year-old.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: neris.png — portrait reference of the woman.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house late at night, as loose
wash suggestion — a plank wall behind them, the room falling into
deep dark. A low fire out of frame at one side is the only light: it
comes in low and warm across the near side of both faces and leaves
the far side of the room black.

Composition: chest-up, two figures, close. The boy of twelve sits at
the plank table at the lower part of the frame, his head tipped back
a little and turned up. The woman stands beside the table, close to
him, in frame from the chest up, her head tipped down to him. Their
faces are near each other and fill the upper half of the frame. The
bottom edge crosses the boy at the chest, and the woman's hands are
not in the frame.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. His eyes up, hers down,
the lines of sight meeting. This eye contact is the subject of the
image. Neither of them is looking at the viewer.

THE BOY HAS JUST ASKED FOR SOMETHING, AND HIS FACE IS OPEN, which is
rare in him: the mouth closed again after the question, the brows
lifted a little, the eyes wide and waiting, nothing held back and
nothing guarded anywhere in the face. Not anxious, not pleading, not
smiling.

THE WOMAN HAS BEEN LOOKING AT HIM FOR SOME TIME AND SOMETHING HAS
JUST EASED IN HER FACE. The mouth stays closed and the face stays
still; the easing is at the outer corners of the eyes and along the
jaw only. It is not a smile and it is not tenderness on display — a
hard, watchful face with one thing moving in it.

Nothing else is happening. Nobody else is in the room. Both wear
plain undyed village working wool, coarse and mended, closed at the
throat, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no ornament and no metal
on either of them.

Warm firelight on the near side of both faces, the plank wall behind
them cool and dark. Their faces are the two most finished things in
the picture and carry the sharpest step from light to dark.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. His eyes pale grey,
hers dark.
```

# Ch. 19 — The Winter · plate sheet

**Composed at s48, on the author's delegation (*you choose*), from the scene; the pair changed shape mid-session when the first plate was rejected at design.** Kael is twelve; Neris is three months along and it is the night she says so. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` and `images/portraits/neris.png` on the adopted plate, `images/portraits/vask.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE HOUSE'S ONE WARM MINUTE AND THE FORTY YEARS MISSING OUT OF THE MIDDLE OF IT.** A boy who asks whether he will be allowed to hold the baby, and a man who lays his forearms on a table and spends three sentences on a life. Neither is at the council table.

**AND THE COUNCIL TABLE IS WHY THE CHAPTER'S LARGEST MOMENT IS NOT HERE.** The parents disagreeing in front of him for the first time in his life — *"It may not be them." / "It is them."* — is the biggest fact in the chapter and it is three people at that table by that light, which is `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` exactly: same room, same boards, same three, hands flat. **So the pair is built to go nowhere near it**, and neither block below shares the light, the crop or the cast of that plate: fire rather than a lamp, and two faces close or one face across a table rather than three at a waist-up.

**A REAL COLLISION, AND IT IS RULED IN THE PLATE RULE'S FAVOUR.** `../canon-brief.md` says Neris's hands go in frame and occupied in any scene — *her hands are where the book keeps her* — and the chapter gives her exactly one occupied gesture, *her palm flat on a bar that was already down*, which belongs to a different beat and is in any case the composition `ch10` rejected as a near-duplicate of `ch02-the-doorframe` (same woman, same door, same hand). At the moment the adopted plate draws, the page puts nothing in her hands. **THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE says a silent page is answered with framing and never with invention**, so her hands are out of the frame and the block says they are, rather than inventing a gesture to satisfy a note about how she is drawn. The boy's hands stay in, flat on the boards, because this chapter puts them there in its own words. **The fence is not weakened**: it governs a full figure, which this is not.

**THE PREGNANCY IS DECIDED AND NOT DEFAULTED** (`../canon-brief.md` requires the choice): three months, nothing showing, and the crop has no waist in it — so no clause is spent on it at all.

### REJECTED AT DESIGN: the-third-board

**REJECTED BEFORE IT RAN (author, s48: *the third board is not good, i dont like just a boot and kael at the end, does not move me*).** Not an execution note and not a fence — the moment is legal, the instruments were right, and it does not move him. **The block is deleted rather than kept as a variant.** `ch12/the-hole-through-the-tree` was kept because it had run and its picture had to stay explainable; this one has no picture to explain, and `git log -p` has the text. **Do not re-propose it.**

**WHAT THE REJECTION SAYS ABOUT THE SET IS WORTH MORE THAN THE PLATE WAS.** The composition put the object near and the only person far — a boot and a line of iron in the near field, the face at the other side of the room. **Every plate this folder has adopted does the reverse**: the face is near, and the object is the thing that face is doing something about. §The winner and the rival can make a small thing win a frame; winning the frame is not the same as being worth it. *An object plate needs a face at the object's own distance* — a finding, not a rule, and the candidate to test it against next is `ch16/the-hand-on-the-door`, held since s45 for the same reason.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: we-were-owned

*Candidate 4, taken at s48 when candidate 1 was rejected.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 19 canon: *Now he put both forearms on the table, the way he did in the yard when he was going to explain a thing once, and the tally-marks came up out of the sleeve and lay there where they had lain in front of Kael for twelve years.* … *"Three sentences," Vask said. "They are not the history. You will not have the history from me tonight or on any night I can see from here. You are going to hate me for how small they are. Listen anyway."* … *"We were owned. We stopped being owned. People who own things do not forget."*

**THE VIEWER SITS WHERE KAEL SITS AND THE BOY IS NOT IN THE PICTURE** — `ch10/the-waterer`'s device, and the reason it is right here is that the page says these marks *had lain in front of Kael for twelve years*: the plate puts them in front of the reader on the same terms. **It also means the man is looking straight out of the picture, which is the one composition where a gaze at the viewer is the design and not the drift** that cost three runs on Ch. 18.

**THE MARKS ARE THE SECOND SHOWING AND THE FIRST DELIBERATE ONE.** `ch03-the-back` spent them as something a son reads off a father's body without permission; here the father lays them on a table on purpose, in the same gesture he uses in the yard when a thing gets explained once. Same marks, opposite transaction. `ch03.md` called itself the only moment that needed them *so far*, and this is the one it was leaving room for.

**AND IT KEEPS CLEAR OF `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` ON EVERY AXIS THAT PLATE OWNS:** one figure rather than three, firelight rather than the oil lamp, forearms rather than four flat hands, and no boy's hands in the frame at all — which is the exact echo `ch12` was built on.

**PRICED: BOTH PLATES ARE THE SAME ROOM ON THE SAME NIGHT**, which `ch10.md` would have split if the chapter gave two settings. This one does not — it is one room from the first line to the last — so the distance is bought on cast, crop and camera instead: two faces close by the fire, and one face across a table with the forearms in the near field.


**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch19-we-were-owned.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s48: *adopt*).**

**WHAT LANDED.** The whole composition on the first run: both forearms flat on the boards with the sleeves pushed back, the hands loose and open and holding nothing, the arms running across the lower half nearest the viewer and the face above them. **The marks read as a tally** — a dozen and more, one length, one slant, on both forearms — and they hold the near field without being shouted at. Mouth open mid-word, the eyes steady straight out of the picture at the son who is not in it. Fire low and warm from one side, the wall black, mended coarse wool, no metal anywhere. **And the gaze at the viewer is the design here and behaved like one**: the fault that cost three runs on Ch. 18 is an asset the moment the viewer is standing in for somebody.

**WHAT DRIFTED, AND BOTH ARE THE REFERENCE BEATING THE BLOCK.** No grey at the temples — `images/portraits/vask.png` carries none, which was ruled correct at s43 and not re-cut, so *scattered thinly at the temples* was never going to win. And the face reads less weathered than *a face like used tools*: handsome, mid-thirties, lightly worn. **Same pattern as `ch17/her-own-hands` returning its costume off the full-figure sheet — in a close crop the reference wins, and a clause that argues with it is spent breath.** The next block that wants weather on this face has to get it from a new reference, not from words.

```
Image 1: vask.png — portrait reference of the man.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house late at night, as loose
wash suggestion — a plank wall behind him, the room falling into deep
dark. A low fire out of frame at one side is the only light in the
picture: it comes in low and warm across the tabletop and the near
side of his face, and leaves the wall and the corners black.

Composition: the viewer sits at the near side of a plank table, close
to it. Across the table the man has laid both forearms flat on the
boards, sleeves pushed back to the elbow, the hands loose and open and
holding nothing. The forearms run across the lower half of the frame,
nearest the viewer; his face is above them, chest-up. Nobody else is
in the picture and no part of anybody else is in the frame.

THE TALLY-MARKS ON HIS FOREARMS ARE THE SUBJECT AND THEY LIE IN THE
OPEN. Short pale scars on the inside of both forearms, a dozen or
more, every one the same length and every one at the same slant, old
and flat and lighter than the weathered skin around them. They carry
the sharpest step from light to dark in the picture and are the most
finished thing in it; the table, the wall and the room stay at quiet
middle values with soft edges and no detail.

He is saying something short that he knows is too small for what it
is standing in for: mouth open mid-word, and nothing else in the face
moving. Level, unhurried, the brows unmoved, the eyes empty and
steady — not angry, not cold, not kind, not sad, not ashamed.

HE IS LOOKING DIRECTLY OUT OF THE PICTURE AT THE VIEWER, at his son,
and his are the only eyes in the frame.

Iron-dark hair of medium length with grey scattered thinly through it
at the temples — scattered, with no streak, seam or band of white
anywhere. A heavily weathered face. Coarse undyed working wool, much
mended, closed at the throat, the sleeves pushed back; no ornament and
no metal anywhere on him.

Preserve him exactly as shown in his reference image — face, hair,
colouring. Dark eyes.
```

### PLATE-VERBATIM: will-i-be-allowed-to-hold-it

*Candidate 2.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`. Ch. 19 canon: *"I'm going to have a brother. Or a sister." / "One or the other," his mother agreed.* … *"Will I be allowed to hold it." / He heard it come out of his own mouth and could not think where it had come from. It was not a count and it priced nothing and nobody in that room needed it, and he was twelve years old … and he had asked it in the voice he had asked things in when he was five.* … *His mother looked at him for some time. / "Yes."* … *Something came up into her face that had not been anywhere in it since he came through the door, and stood there a moment, and went.*

**THE PAGE HANDS OVER THE EXPRESSION, SO THE PLATE MAY DRAW IT** — the same licence as `ch17/her-own-hands`. What it may not do is let it arrive as warmth in general: `../canon-brief.md` says *not warm, not maternal, not soft*, and the page says something came up **and went**. So it is written as anatomy and as an easing rather than a smile, on a face that is otherwise still.

**THE EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT** (§Gaze), and it is also this plate's insurance: two people looking at each other cannot send a gaze to the viewer, which is the fault that took two runs off `ch18/caught-it-halfway` and one off `ch18/the-man-at-the-second-bend`.

**IT IS THE ONLY PLATE IN THE BOOK WHERE KAEL'S FACE IS OPEN.** Every other one gives nothing away by design — the coat. Here the page takes it off him for one line, in his own house, and the block asks for the face of a much younger boy on a twelve-year-old.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch19-will-i-be-allowed-to-hold-it.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s48: *great!*).**

**WHAT LANDED.** The eye contact, named as the subject and delivered on the first run — his eyes up, hers down, the lines meeting, and **nobody at the viewer**, which is the first plate this session to get a gaze right the first time. Two faces close and warm-lit off a fire out of frame, the plank wall black behind them, the table edge across the bottom and her hands out of it. **The boy's face open** — brows up, eyes wide, the mouth closed again after the question — which no other Kael plate in the book has. Both in mended undyed wool with the stitching drawn, no ornament and no metal on either.

**WHAT DRIFTED, AND IT IS ONE NOTCH.** The easing in her face is fainter than the block asked for; she reads grave and attentive rather than a hard face with one thing moving in it. **Nothing was over-drawn, which was the risk** — no maternal softness arrived, and `../canon-brief.md`'s *not warm, not maternal, not soft* held. She reads early thirties, which is the reference and is ruled correct (s44).

**AND THE GAZE INSURANCE WORKED.** Two people looking at each other cannot look at the viewer — after three runs lost to that fault on Ch. 18, this is the cheapest fix the folder has: where the moment allows it, make the eye contact the subject.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: neris.png — portrait reference of the woman.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms,
bare paper in the lights.

Setting: the single room of a mountain house late at night, as loose
wash suggestion — a plank wall behind them, the room falling into
deep dark. A low fire out of frame at one side is the only light: it
comes in low and warm across the near side of both faces and leaves
the far side of the room black.

Composition: chest-up, two figures, close. The boy of twelve sits at
the plank table at the lower part of the frame, his head tipped back
a little and turned up. The woman stands beside the table, close to
him, in frame from the chest up, her head tipped down to him. Their
faces are near each other and fill the upper half of the frame. The
bottom edge crosses the boy at the chest, and the woman's hands are
not in the frame.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER. His eyes up, hers down,
the lines of sight meeting. This eye contact is the subject of the
image. Neither of them is looking at the viewer.

THE BOY HAS JUST ASKED FOR SOMETHING, AND HIS FACE IS OPEN, which is
rare in him: the mouth closed again after the question, the brows
lifted a little, the eyes wide and waiting, nothing held back and
nothing guarded anywhere in the face. Not anxious, not pleading, not
smiling.

THE WOMAN HAS BEEN LOOKING AT HIM FOR SOME TIME AND SOMETHING HAS
JUST EASED IN HER FACE. The mouth stays closed and the face stays
still; the easing is at the outer corners of the eyes and along the
jaw only. It is not a smile and it is not tenderness on display — a
hard, watchful face with one thing moving in it.

Nothing else is happening. Nobody else is in the room. Both wear
plain undyed village working wool, coarse and mended, closed at the
throat, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, with no ornament and no metal
on either of them.

Warm firelight on the near side of both faces, the plank wall behind
them cool and dark. Their faces are the two most finished things in
the picture and carry the sharpest step from light to dark.

Preserve each exactly as shown in their reference image — faces,
hair, colouring. Do not blend their features. His eyes pale grey,
hers dark.
```

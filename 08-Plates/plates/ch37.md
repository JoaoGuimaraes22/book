# Ch. 37 — The Still · plate sheet

**Composed at s58 from the scene, under the register. The first two plates of Book Two.** Ch. 37 stands thirty days after Ch. 36, so nothing has aged: Kael is thirteen, Wren is a month old, and the anchors do not move — `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on the first, `images/portraits/severin.png` on the second. `kael-17`, `valeria-17` and `aeliana-18` stay in the library for whatever later Book Two does with them.

**THE PAIR IS A COUNTRY AND A MINUTE.** The chapter is a month of thirst on an open hull and then, in its last third, a continent — and the two plates take one of each: **water running loose in a stone channel with nobody counting it**, and **an old man going down on his knees in the wet sand with a newborn while everybody else gets ready to fight.** Book One's set is island and capital and has no south anywhere in it; the first of these is the folder's first picture of the other side of that sea.

**AND THEY SHARE THE CHAPTER'S ONE NEW FACT ABOUT THE WORLD, WHICH IS THE LIGHT.** *The ground was brown and hard, and the light came up off it as well as down, so that there was no shade anywhere, not even your own.* The register's default is soft daylight from one side and neither of these takes it: both are written under a high white southern glare that comes down out of a bleached sky and back up off the ground, with the shadows short and weak and no deep dark anywhere. **It is one named light, used twice, because it is the thing the chapter discovered** — and it gives the pair a family look that says *this is not the island* before anything in either frame does.

**THE FIRST PLATE IS THE EPIGRAPH.** *I have been thirsty since … even now I cannot hear water going over stone without stopping where I stand, and people who did not know me have taken it for prayer.* The chapter opens on a man who will be stopping in roads for the rest of his life and closes a mile from the water that starts it: *He stood and looked at water going past in the open, with nobody counting it, in a quantity that would have kept eight people alive for a year, going under a road.* **The plate is the sentence the book has already promised to come back to**, and it wants nothing in it but a wall, a gutter and a boy who has stopped walking.

**AND ITS ONE BAN IS THE PICTURE.** §Absence says ban what the frame pulls toward and nothing else. A thirsty boy beside running water pulls toward kneeling, cupping, drinking harder than anything else in this folder, so the block spends its single absence there — *he is not touching the water and he is not drinking* — set against the positive state that does the real work: both feet flat, both hands empty and hanging at his sides.

**THE SECOND PLATE IS THE FOLDER'S FIRST SEVERIN, AND IT IS ABOUT HIS HANDS.** `candidates.md` has flagged the absence twice — *the folder still has no plate of Severin at all* — and the moment that finally earns one is not a teaching beat but this: *Behind him his mother took the child out of her coat and put him into the old man's arm, and Severin, on his knees, took him without looking up and put his other hand flat on the wet sand and left it there.* Seven people got ready in the order their bodies took it and the eighth sat down on the ground with the baby and put a palm on a continent.

**AND THE HAND IS THE WINNER, NOT THE CHILD.** §The winner and the rival, on the `ch28/the-hand-back` specimen: the hand gets a ratio to something beside it — *wider in the frame than the child's whole head* — and the rival demoted by name is the face, on both channels at once. A newborn is the thing anybody sees; **the open hand flat on wet sand is the thing only a reader sees**, and the picture is built so that it wins.

**THE LINEN IS IN ITS FOURTH PLATE.** `ch34`, `ch35`, `ch36` and now this: a strip of pale cloth across a newborn's eyes, level, clear of the nose and mouth, with no eye drawn anywhere. **Ch. 37 is the first chapter in which the strip is explained out loud** — to a governor, in the trial-voice, as damage from a bad birth — and the plate does exactly what the other three did and explains nothing.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **the cloth** — the princess on her knees wringing over the side with her palms bleeding through the strips of her own hem, corrected once and right the second time — is the chapter's emotional centre and it is her third hands-doing-a-job picture in five chapters, after `ch33/the-lamp` and `ch34/the-elbow`; it stays first on `candidates.md` for the session that wants it. **The first drop** — a fire the size of a fist and the colour of a forge standing off a boy's palm with a stone jar sitting above it on nothing — is the chapter's only light source and the strongest thing left unbuilt in it. **The street in stripes** is Book Two's establishing shot and it wants no face at all, which makes it a cheap plate to hold for a chapter with a thinner list.

**ONE FRAMING CHOICE IN EACH, BOTH FORCED BY SILENCE.** The page does not say which of Severin's arms took the child, so the block picks the left and puts the right on the sand. And **Valeria stays out of both plates**, because Ch. 36 gives *half her face was burned red and shining down one side* and never says which side: a plate with her face in it either invents a side or turns it away, and neither of these moments needs her.

### PLATE: the-water-at-the-gate

*Candidate 1, ★.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 37 canon: *There was water at the gate. / He heard it before he saw it. It ran in a channel of cut stone along the foot of the wall and went in under the road through an arch the height of a dog, and it was running, loud and quick where it went over a lip in the stone. Kael stopped in the road.* … *walls the colour of the ground with the ground's own grain in them, as if the ground had been made to stand up; and over the top of the walls, green: the heads of trees he had never seen, bare-stemmed and tall with all the green at the top of them, going over in the wind like people leaning.*

**THE WATER IS GIVEN AREA RATHER THAN A DEMOTION.** §The winner and the rival is for a subject an inch across; this one can have the bottom third of the frame, so the instrument is the crop and not the two-channel demotion. What the block does site by name is the one true dark available under a shadowless light — **the black mouth of the arch against the moving water** — and it holds his face at quieter values behind it.

**AND THE STATE, NOT THE ACTION.** Running water is the hardest thing in the folder to ask for as a verb. The block never uses one: **a stone lip a hand high, the water standing over it in one unbroken curved sheet, white broken water below it.** That is a shape a still picture can hold.

**THE BLADES ARE THE JOKE AND THEY COST ONE CLAUSE.** A boy hung with three blades, stopped dead by a gutter. His father's sword rides on the length of cord where the page put it, and the two shed blades are through his belt, not bundled on his back — Ch. 37 moved them there and the plate follows.

**THE HAND WON, AGAINST A FACE AND A NEWBORN.** It is the nearest thing to the viewer, palm down with all five fingers spread and every one of them on the ground, sitting dark on a film of water that is throwing the bleached sky back up — and **the sharpest step from light to dark in the picture is its edge against the wet sand**, exactly where the block sited it. **THE FOUR BACKS AND THE SIX SPEARS BOTH RENDERED UNRESOLVED**, which is what makes it the minute rather than a picture of an old man holding a baby: shapes in loose wash with a blade in one hand each, and beyond them, small, the spears coming down.

**WHAT DRIFTED, three, and none of them is a correction owed:**

- **The six on the brown came back MOUNTED.** Upright spears as written, but they sit on horses; the page walks six men down a beach. At that size it is a silhouette decision the block never made either way.
- **The ground is a shallow tidal flat and he is kneeling in water**, not on wet sand — the four backs are standing in it to the calf. Not what the block said, **and it is what makes the hand work**: the film of water is everywhere instead of only around the hand, so the mirror the instrument needed is the whole floor of the picture.
- **The crop is loose again.** The block cut the bottom edge across the sand just in front of the planted hand with the lower legs out of frame behind him; the picture keeps his folded knee and lower leg. **Twelfth instance, and still never once corrected.**

**AND A FINISH NOTE, since the folder has not settled finish:** the coat and the skin came back under a dense granular stipple — the most worked surface in the set, well past `ch08`'s controlled pair. Adopted as drawn; recorded here because the next block that wants this hand-level of resolution has a specimen for it now.

**THE OPTIMISATION IS A LESSON ABOUT MY BLOCK, NOT ABOUT THE PICTURE.** Five changes, and the first is the one that mattered:

1. **§Damage was applied to his coat and not to his body.** Mine wrote the month at sea into him as pure subtraction — *gone thin, with salt dried pale in his hair and his beard* — with nothing named that survived it. The author's writes it on §AGE's own formula for a grown face: *a month at sea is on him and it is all on the surface: the skin scoured by wind and sun and salt, and the frame under it sound — the strength still there, nothing taken out of him, not frail and not diminished.* **That is the same rule my own next paragraph applied to the coat** (*well made and sound … soaked black*) and missed on the man. `03` has him *not frail, not kindly-grandfatherly*, and one clause is the difference.
2. **The camera is written as what it exposes, not as where the viewer stands.** Mine: *low, at about the height of a kneeling man's shoulder.* Theirs: *the viewer is out on the water side and low: the horizon sits level with his shoulders* — §Light's rule, pinned to a landmark inside the frame, and it rendered at that height exactly.
3. **The cut is named against a thing in the picture rather than a landmark on the body.** *The bottom edge of the picture runs across the wet sand just in front of his planted right hand … his lower legs and feet are out of it behind him*, where mine said *crosses him at the knees*.
4. **Two of my clauses were bans and became states.** *There is no deep shadow anywhere in the picture* → *the shadows short and weak and pale everywhere in the picture*; *no eye is drawn on the child anywhere* → *no eyes are visible on the child anywhere in the picture*. §Absence's own warning, turned on clauses I had written as prohibitions.
5. **The salt in the grey got a quantity and a forbidden shape instead of a bare ban** — *dried pale through them as scattered light strands, never as a streak, seam or patch* — which is §Ban the shape, count the thing, and the Vask formula.

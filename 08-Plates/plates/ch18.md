# Ch. 18 — The Entry · plate sheet

**Composed at s48, on the author's delegation (*you choose*), from the scene: the chapter had no `candidates.md` entry before this session.** Kael is twelve on this page and the page says so — *"You are twelve years old," Severin said.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, and `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the second for the body below the collar.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S TWO HALVES.** The name arriving on a wall in a public hand, and what the name puts on the road behind him the same evening. One is a face in a gap in the rain; the other is the only picture in the set with real depth stacked vertically, and the two share nothing — not the weather, not the crop, not the number of figures, not the light.

**THE JUDGE IS BLOCKED, AND IT IS THE SAME BLOCK AS OSS.** The rail above the yard is the chapter's best two-hander — *"Congratulations," the judge said, and put his own hand out. / Kael looked at it.* — and there is no `images/portraits/halden.png`. A plate would author his face and it would become his reference by default (`ch13.md`, s45). **The work that unblocks it is a portrait, not a plate.** The one legal version — Kael near, his face down on the offered hand, the judge a wool shoulder and forearm at the frame edge with his head above the top of it — is a picture built around a missing file, and the chapter has two better moments that are not. Recorded on `candidates.md` so the next session does not re-propose the plate first.

**THE STUDY IS PASSED OVER AND THE REASON IS THE SET, NOT THE MOMENT.** *"Nothing," said Severin* is the chapter's argument, both faces are anchored, and the room's furniture is already on a page — `ch10/the-honey-and-the-bread` put the fern and the jar there specifically to establish the room Ch. 18 comes back to. **A second study interior would be the set's fourth close two-figure room in nine chapters** (honey, the law on the table, who lives in it), and the pair above buys more ground. It stays candidate 3 and it is fully composable the day it is wanted.

**A DOCUMENT IS THE FIRST PLATE'S OBJECT, AND §LETTERING FORBIDS DRAWING IT.** *One short word, capitals, named exactly … Not a page.* Two ruled sheets of four hundred names is exactly a page, so the list can only be texture: pinned paper at a raking angle, ruled columns, no legible word anywhere. **The chapter loses nothing by it** — the reader knows what is on the sheet, and the picture is the face reading it, which is what the chapter is about. The one real cost is that the second column cannot be shown, so the plate takes the breath before it instead.

**BOTH BLOCKS WRITE THE AGE IN AND NEITHER SAYS *apparent age*.** The standing anchor ruling (s44) is that age is written into the block or the picture will be fourteen, so the closing preserve clause carries face, hair and colouring only; carrying *apparent age* as well, as the Ch. 17 blocks did, tells the tool two things at once. **Neither block stages a ratio**: nothing in the first crop can perform one — his hands are down and there is no second body in frame — and the second one's ratio is spent on distance, which is that picture's subject. The rung is doing this alone on plate 1, and that is the test.

### PLATE: caught-it-halfway

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 18 canon: *Kael came in off the road with his boots wet to the ankle and the rain still in his hair, and the first court was solid at the colonnade, three deep* … *the pillar stood off the wall by the width of a hand and a small boy could get his shoulder into the gap and read sideways, and he read the list sideways, in the grey light, over the top of somebody's collar.* And the subject: *And for about the space of one breath, before anything else got there, he was happy.* … *He felt his own face start to do something. He caught it about halfway and put it back.*

**THE SUBJECT IS AN EXPRESSION IN THE ACT OF BEING STOPPED**, which is the one thing this book has that no other picture in the folder has drawn: every other Kael plate is a face already closed. The page hands over both halves — the thing that started and the catching of it — so the plate draws a beginning, not a state. **§Gaze's rule does the work: point at the mouth, not the abstraction.** *Halfway* is not renderable and *the lips a little apart, one corner begun, the brows up, and the rest of the face not moved* is. The risk is a plain smile coming back, which would be the wrong picture entirely — a happy boy at a list is not this chapter — so the arrival is banned by name in the same paragraph, on §Absence's pull test.

**THE CROWD IS ONE SHOULDER AND THE PAGE PUT IT THERE.** *over the top of somebody's collar* — a stranger's dark collar at the near edge, cut by the frame above it, no head and no face. That is the whole of the three-deep court, and it does the job a crowd of drawn faces would fight the subject for.

**THE PAPER IS THE RIVAL AND IT IS DEMOTED ON BOTH CHANNELS.** §The winner and the rival: pale paper beside a face is a real competitor for the eye, so the sheets are held at quiet middle values with soft edges and the sharpest step from light to dark is sited on the face. This is the first block in the folder where the rival is the thing the scene is nominally about.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the colonnade of a temple school on a wet grey morning, as
loose wash suggestion — a stone pillar standing a hand's width off a
stone wall, close behind him and beside him, the depth of the
colonnade falling away into shadow. Behind him, rain comes off the
roof in a straight falling line. Flat grey daylight from the open
side, no sun and no hard shadow.

Composition: chest-up, one figure. The boy is twelve years old. He
stands sideways in the narrow gap between the pillar and the wall
with his near shoulder wedged into it and his cheek close to the
stone, his body turned away to the viewer's left and his head turned
back to the right. The bottom edge crosses him at the chest, below
the collarbone, so no hands and no arms are in the frame at all. At
the right edge of the frame, pinned flat to the wall, two sheets of
pale paper seen at a steep raking angle, ruled into faint vertical
columns; the writing on them is a grey suggestion of marks and no
word anywhere on them is legible. There is no lettering of any kind
anywhere in the picture. At the near left edge, the dark shoulder and
collar of a stranger standing in front of him, blurred and out of
focus and cut off by the edge of the frame above the collar, so that
no head and no second face appears anywhere.

THE FACE IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS AN EXPRESSION CAUGHT HALFWAY.
Something has just started in it and been stopped: the lips have come
a little apart, one corner of the mouth has begun to lift, the brows
are up, the eyes are wide and bright and fixed. The rest of the face
has not moved and is already going still again. The smile has not
arrived — he is not smiling, not grinning, not laughing. Not
frightened, not sad, not blank.

His hair is wet and pushed off his forehead, the rain still on his
face, his shoulders dark with it.

He is looking at the pinned sheets at the right edge of the frame and
nowhere else — not at the viewer, and his head does not turn toward
the viewer.

Undyed coarse working wool, much mended, the collar loose at the
throat, in dull oatmeal and dead olive, wet through and darker at the
shoulders. No ornament, no metal, no badge and nothing carried.

The stone is cool grey and the falling rain behind him is reserved as
bare unpainted paper. HIS FACE CARRIES THE SHARPEST STEP FROM LIGHT
TO DARK IN THE PICTURE and is the most finished thing in it; the
pinned sheets, the stranger's shoulder and the colonnade behind stay
at quiet middle values with soft edges and no detail, and the paper
does not compete with the face for finish. The ink linework stays the
strongest element throughout.

Preserve him exactly as shown in his reference image — face, hair,
colouring. Pale grey eyes, the lightest value in his face. Dark hair
cut short and uneven. One figure in the picture.
```

### PLATE: the-man-at-the-second-bend

*Candidate 2, and the pair's second half.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 18 canon: *At the bend above the village the road gave you two hundred strides of the way you had come.* … *There was a man on it at about a hundred and fifty, walking.* … *the bank gave out into scree, a long tilted field of loose grey stone* … *He went up eleven feet of bank on the outsides of his feet and lay down in the stones.* … *The man came along at the same unhurried pace he had kept for two miles and went by twenty feet under him: wool cap, wool coat, a man about his father's build and nothing whatever like his father's carriage, no badge, no colour, nothing on him anywhere that meant a single thing. Kael never saw the face and did not need it.*

**THE FACELESS FIGURE IS THE PAGE'S OWN AND NOT A WORKAROUND.** *Kael never saw the face* — this is the one moment in the book where the two bans §Plates prescribes are not the plate hiding something but the chapter's plainest fact. It is also why this is the second plate and the rail is not: the same absence that blocks Halden is, here, the subject.

**IT IS THE SET'S FIRST VERTICAL DEPTH STAGING**, and it is built on §Light's recipe for depth exactly — where the viewer stands, said first; the frame stacked bottom to top; the distance pinned with a ratio. The ratio is the picture's whole content: **a hand's length of man, twenty feet under a boy's face that fills the near corner.** Every other plate in the folder is one plane or two.

**NOTHING IS HAPPENING, WHICH HAS TO BE SAID.** A hillside, loose stone and a man walking below a hidden boy pulls hard toward a chase or an ambush, and the page is the opposite: *The man did not slow, and did not look up, and did not come back.* The stillness is the achievement, so the block states the absence of action outright, as the template prescribes where the subject implies violence.

**THE FACE CARRIES THE SPLIT AND IT IS WRITTEN AS A CONTRADICTION** (§Gaze: state a contradiction as one). *The first half arrived at once and it was very nearly joy* … *it was enormous, and it was fun.* … *The other half came in underneath it about ten strides later and was the size of the mountain.* Bright steady eyes and a mouth closed hard: good at this, glad of it, and carrying something heavy underneath — and no grin, because the chapter says he was ashamed of about half of it for years afterward.

**NO WRAP AND NO BLADES.** The page does not put them on him anywhere in this chapter and `../canon-brief.md` says his full-figure sheet carries neither; where the page is silent the plate answers with framing, so the block says he carries nothing rather than deciding it.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: kael-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the same boy, for his build and his clothing.

Setting: a bare hillside above a fishing village at the end of a wet
afternoon, as loose wash suggestion — a long tilted field of loose
grey stone, and below it a narrow wet mud road cutting across the
slope. Flat grey overcast light, no sun and no hard shadow anywhere,
the sky low and coming on to rain.

Composition: real depth, seen from up the slope. The viewer stands on
the loose stone above the boy and a little behind him, looking down
across the field of stone to the road. The frame stacks from bottom
to top: nearest and largest, filling the bottom third, the boy lying
flat on his front along the slope with his head to the viewer's left,
his cheek almost on the stones, his face in three-quarter view seen
from above, one hand flat on the stone near his chin; then the tilted
stone field falling away; then, across the upper third, the road, and
on it one grown man walking away to the viewer's left; then the far
bank and a strip of low grey sky.

SCALE — the distance is the picture. The walking man is no taller in
the frame than the boy's hand is long. The boy's head alone is wider
in the frame than the man is tall.

Nothing is happening. The man walks on at an even unhurried pace and
the boy lies still and watches him go by below. Nobody is running,
nothing is drawn or raised, and nothing in either body says a chase.

THE MAN IS SEEN FROM ABOVE AND BEHIND. A brown-grey wool cap and a
heavy brown-grey wool coat, ordinary and worn, the build of a working
man; no badge, no colour, no insignia, no pattern, and nothing
carried in either hand. His face is not visible anywhere in the
picture, no eyes appear anywhere in the picture except the boy's, and
he does not turn his head.

THE BOY'S FACE IS THE SUBJECT AND IT IS DOING TWO THINGS AT ONCE. The
eyes are wide open, bright and completely steady, tracking the man
down on the road; the mouth is closed and firm; the face is still and
the body is stiller. He is very good at this and glad of it, and
underneath that he has just understood something heavy. Not
frightened, not grinning, not blank.

He is looking down at the walking man and nowhere else — not at the
viewer, and his head does not turn toward the viewer. The boy is
twelve years old.

His clothing as in his full-figure reference: undyed coarse working
wool, much mended, in dull oatmeal and dead olive. He carries nothing
and nothing is strapped to his back.

Colour: the stone field cool grey with green-grey lichen in it, the
road pale wet mud, the far bank dull olive and dead bracken brown,
the man's cap and coat the same brown-grey as the rest of the
hillside. The boy's skin is the one warm thing in the picture.

THE BOY'S FACE AND THE HAND BESIDE IT CARRY THE SHARPEST STEP FROM
LIGHT TO DARK and are the two most finished things in the picture.
The road, the man and the far bank stay at quiet middle values with
soft edges and no detail. The ink linework stays the strongest
element throughout and does not blur into the wash.

There is nobody else on the road and nobody else on the hillside: two
figures only, no other walker, no house and no animal anywhere in
the frame.

Preserve him exactly as shown in his reference images — face, hair,
colouring; the two images are the same boy and there is one boy in
the picture. Pale grey eyes. Dark hair cut short and uneven.
```

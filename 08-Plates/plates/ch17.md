Composition: waist-up. Four figures at two depths, all on the one brick

the right, nearest, in frame from the chest up, his face turned up and

long-haired girl stands at the left with a blade in one hand, point
down, and a cloth in the other held against the flat of it. The bottom
edge crosses the three standing figures at the belt. Nobody is looking

The dark-haired boy has just asked a question and the three have
turned to look at him. Nothing else is happening; no blade is raised.

THE FAIR-HAIRED BOY'S FACE IS THE SUBJECT: he does not understand the
question and there is no contempt in him anywhere — lips parted on the
first word of an answer, brows drawn a little together, the eyes open

smug. Good dark wool, well cut, in deep umber and warm charcoal, an
open collar over a clean white undershirt, no ornament.

The dark-haired boy waits with his mouth closed, his face plain and
giving nothing away, a healing split in his lower lip and a green
bruise under the jaw. Undyed working wool, coarse and much mended, in

The small girl's head has come up; her attention is entirely on the

buttoned collar, in ink-black and dark olive, no ornament.

The long-haired girl has stopped cleaning the blade and looks at the
boy on the bench without hurry, her face flat. Her clothing as in her
full-figure reference: the charcoal sleeveless coat over the grey-blue
underlayer, the steel vambraces.

Preserve all four exactly as shown in their reference images — faces,
hair, colouring, apparent age. Do not blend their features. Do not mix
their clothing: the steel belongs only to the long-haired girl and no

### PLATE: her-own-hands

*New at s47.* Anchors: `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`. Ch. 17 canon: *Valeria Vel stood in her doorway with a blade in each hand and looked at him for what felt like a considerable portion of his life.* … *She looked at the blades in her hands. / "Yes," Valeria said. "All right." / And her face, for about a quarter of a second, did something he could not read at all, and if he had been able to read it he would have seen a girl who had just surprised herself so badly that she had to look at her own hands to make sure they were still hers.*

**THE PAGE HANDS OVER THE EXPRESSION HE COULD NOT READ**, so the plate may draw it: the narrator's description, not invention. A chest-up in a doorway is the register's shape exactly, with both hands full at the bottom edge — her trait, and not a hand crop: the face is the subject and the hands are what it is looking at. Her scars stay out: below a hand's size, and under the vambraces.

```
Image 1: valeria-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.
Image 2: valeria-14-full.png — full-figure reference of the same girl, for her clothing and her blades.

Setting: a plain stone doorway, as loose wash suggestion — the jamb
and the lintel implied close around her, the room behind her falling

Composition: chest-up, one figure, three-quarter view, standing in the

She is looking down at her own hands, and her face is the subject of
the picture: she has just surprised herself and is looking at her
hands to make sure they are still hers — the eyes down and a little
wide, the brows lifted a fraction, the lips barely parted, the whole
face still. Not frightened, not sad, not smiling; the surprise is at
herself.

Her black hair is loose, well past the shoulders, no ornament. Her
clothing and her two swords as in her full-figure reference: the
charcoal sleeveless coat over the grey-blue underlayer, the high
collar, the steel vambraces on both forearms; the two swords a matched
plain pair, one in each hand.

The stone of the doorway is cool grey; the coat charcoal, the
underlayer grey-blue, the vambraces dull steel; her skin is the warm
thing in the picture. Nobody else is in the doorway or the room
behind.

Preserve her exactly as shown in her reference images — face, hair,
colouring, apparent age; the two images are the same girl and there
is one figure in the picture. Green eyes.
```

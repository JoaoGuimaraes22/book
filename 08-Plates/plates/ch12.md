# Ch. 12 — The Telling · plate sheet

**One plate adopted, one built and unrun (s44).** Kael is eleven; anchor `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Vask: `images/portraits/vask.png`.

**THE PAIR SPLITS THE CHAPTER'S TWO WEATHERS.** A crowded hall at midday with four faces in it, and a cold empty dawn with one forearm and a tree. `candidates.md`'s third — the law on the table, *"You will not enter it"* — is the chapter's largest story beat and is passed over for now on set grounds: it would be the third lamp-lit table in the book after `ch08/the-hand-over-the-face` and `ch10/the-honey-and-the-bread`.

**BOTH BLOCKS CARRY THE TWO FINDINGS THIS FOLDER LEARNED LAST RUN.** The age instrument is the eye-line rung **plus a ratio against something else in the frame** (§A close-up plate and the age line, answered s44) — here a hand against another hand, which §Size contrast must be stated per hand already prescribes. And **every visible face gets its gaze named**, because `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` emptied its frame and gave its subject's eyes nowhere to go, and the eyes went to the viewer.

**CUT BACK AFTER THE AUTHOR FLAGGED THE NEGATION HABIT (s44), before either block ran.** *This is so inefficient and redundant for image gen.* **The rule was already there and was the author's own** — §Negative inventory, s40: *ban what could plausibly arrive, not everything that could theoretically arrive*, written up after the author cut precisely these lines from every run of that session and no strays appeared. Both blocks were carrying them again.

**What went:** the absence lines in both blocks, where the positive inventory already names every figure in the frame and says what the rest are; four gaze negatives down to one; a ten-term expression list down to six; nine burning terms down to four; *no ornament, no metal, no ring, no band* on a boy already dressed in mended undyed wool; and six ways of saying nothing is glowing.

**What survived, by the channel test** (§Over-writing contradicts — *count the channel, not the sentence*, and the re-cut that lost two jobs by trimming on word-count instead): **not at the viewer**, because `kael-14`'s reference points its gaze there and `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` proved that pull is real; **NO CHAR, no scorch, no smoke**, because a hole in wood pulls hard toward burning; **not blank**, the opposite-side risk of asking for a face with nothing in it; and **no glow, no spark**, because the thing that made the hole was lightning.

**ANCHORING COST.** Both references are head-and-shoulders. The hall plate authors everything below Kael's collar; the tree plate is a forearm and a hand, so it is anchored on skin and cloth alone and its anchor is doing almost nothing.

### PLATE: furniture

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 11 canon for the hall's shape and Ch. 12 for the moment: *A kitchen boy brought it to the middle tables, hot from the source and proud of the carrying... "Two winters gone," the boy said, the tray still on his hip.* And the line the picture is built on: *Kael was at the bench-end, being furniture, and furniture hears everything.* Then: *Kael went on eating.* And, at the end of the same telling: *Kael's hands stayed quiet on the bread, and none of it showed.*

**THIS IS THE ONE LEGAL WAY TO DRAW THE COAT.** `../canon-brief.md` forbids designing the coat, because it is the book's metaphor for concealment and not a garment — *Literalising it flattens the image.* **A room telling a boy's own night back to him while his face stays at the room's temperature is the metaphor without the garment**, and it is the only picture in the book where the reader knows something that no face in the frame does.

**THE COMPOSITION PUTS THE STORY BEHIND HIM AND HIM IN FRONT.** He is nearest the viewer and the largest figure; the telling is further off and smaller. That is the two-clause mechanism doing the chapter's work rather than decorating it: **the teller is the animated thing and the boy is the finished thing**, and the eye lands on the one nobody in the picture is looking at.

**FOUR RESOLVED FIGURES, WHICH IS THE TESTED CEILING** (§Four figures) — the boy with the tray, two children turned round to him, and Kael. The rest of the hall is summary shapes with no features, the same solve as `ch08/the-one-who-did-not-laugh` and `ch09/the-roller-alone`.

**AND HERE THE REFERENCE'S OWN FACE IS FINALLY THE RIGHT ONE.** `kael-14`'s block is *still and level, mouth closed and relaxed, gaze flat and direct* — the coat, exactly. **The only thing that must move is the gaze**, which the reference points at the viewer and this picture points down at the bread, and a downcast gaze is the change a reference holds no version of and therefore cannot fight (s43, twice proven).

**NO ADULT IS IN THE FRAME, AND THE BLOCK DOES NOT SAY SO.** The masters are not named as present in this scene, and an authority in a picture whose whole subject is that nobody is watching him would be wrong — but the composition already names every figure in the frame and says what the rest are, which is what makes the ban unnecessary rather than merely short.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch12-furniture.png`, first run.** *Adopted on the standing pattern below rather than on a fresh ruling; one word and it comes back out.*

**THE STAGING CAME THROUGH ENTIRELY.** He is nearest, largest and turned away; the telling is behind him and smaller; four figures resolved and the rest of the hall summary. The high window is the only light and reserves as bare paper with the torn face of the bread. **Skin and bread are the only warm colour in the picture** and everything else holds cool. His face and his two hands are the most finished things in it, the three children behind are looser, and **the ink carries the cloth** so hard that the mending is legible at every seam. His eyes are down on the bread and not on the viewer, and nobody in the picture is looking at him.

**AND THE CUTS COST NOTHING, WHICH IS THE FIRST DIRECT EVIDENCE THIS SESSION.** The absence lines came out of this block before it ran — *there is no adult in the picture and no master anywhere in it* — and **no adult arrived**, no stray figure, nothing extra on the table. §Negative inventory's ruling holds exactly as the author wrote it: the positive inventory was already complete, so the ban was buying nothing.

**MISS: HE READS FOURTEEN OR FIFTEEN, AND IT COMPLICATES LAST RUN'S FINDING.** This block carried both instruments — the eye-line rung **and** a ratio — and the face still came back years above eleven. That is a third data point and it does not agree with the second, so §A close-up plate and the age line has been walked back from *answered* to part-answered.

**THE DIAGNOSIS IS SPECIFIC AND IT IS THE BLOCK'S FAULT.** The ratio written here was *his hand is plainly smaller and shorter-fingered than the standing boy's hand on the tray* — **and the picture cannot make that comparison.** The two hands are at opposite ends of the hall, one large in the foreground and one small in the far middle distance, so there is nothing for a renderer to measure against. `ch11/the-white-stitch` put the hand and the copper bar **side by side, at the same depth, inside the same crop**, and that face came back eleven. **A ratio is only an instrument where the framing can actually perform the comparison** — which is the occlusion lesson from s43 in a new coat: a fact named but staged out of reach.

**The second measurement was no better staged.** *Seated at a grown man's table the top of the boards comes to the height of his lower ribs* is a landmark the crop cuts off, since the tabletop enters the frame only in front of him.

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the long hall of a temple at midday in winter, as loose wash
suggestion — a stone wall and one high window behind, long plank
tables and benches running away into quiet wash, lamp-smoke in the
upper air. Flat cold daylight from the high window, and it is the
only light in the picture.

Composition: waist-up. The boy sits at the very end of a bench in the
near foreground, closest to the viewer and the largest figure in the
frame, turned three-quarters away from the others. Behind him and
further off, at the middle tables, three other children: one standing
with a serving tray and his mouth open mid-word, and two seated
and turned right round toward him.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: the boy's two hands rest on the table in
front of him, the right one holding a torn piece of bread. The
standing child's left hand holds the tray against his hip and his
right hand is open in the air in front of him. Nothing else is held
by anybody.

WHERE EVERY FACE IS LOOKING. The
two seated children are turned right round and are looking at the boy
with the tray. He is looking at them. The boy in
the foreground is looking down at the bread in his own hands and not
at the viewer.

NOTHING IS SHOWING IN HIS FACE, AND THAT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
IMAGE. Still, mouth closed, the eyes lowered
and easy, the brows unmoved, both hands quiet on the table. NOT
shocked, NOT grieving, NOT frightened — and equally NOT amused, NOT
curious, and not blank. He is eating.

HE IS ELEVEN AND TWO THINGS CARRY IT: the eyes sit a little below the
vertical midpoint of the head, nearer that midpoint than a young
child's and still clearly below it, the jaw not come in and the
cheeks still full under the bone. AND THE MEASUREMENT: his hand on
the table is a child's and is plainly smaller and shorter-fingered
than the standing boy's hand on the tray, and seated at a grown
man's table the top of the boards comes to the height of his lower
ribs.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest value
in his face. Dark hair cut short and uneven at home. Plain undyed
working wool, coarse and much mended, the collar loose at the throat
— the ink carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the seams,
the patches and the mended places.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: skin and the bread
carry the only warm colour, and the stone, the tables, the benches,
the air and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue with no
brown in them. The light from the high window and the cut face of the
bread are reserved as bare unpainted paper.

HIS FACE AND HIS TWO HANDS ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN THE
PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The three
children behind him are looser and softer-edged, and the rest of the
hall is dark summary shapes at the edges with no features drawn at
all. The ink linework stays the strongest element throughout and does
not blur into the wash.
```

### PLATE: the-hole-through-the-tree

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 12 canon: *His father began. He threaded the lightning through his own hand, and put the hand through the trunk of a tree, wrist-deep, as if the wood were wool, and drew it out. The tree stood there with a hole through it.* And what the boy took from it: *Kael read the holding, not the hole.*

**THE HOLE IS BARE PAPER, THE SAME INSTRUMENT AS THE STITCH.** The pale dawn sky on the far side of the trunk is seen through it, reserved rather than painted, with the ink drawing stopping at the hole's edge. It is the second plate in two chapters where the subject *is* the reserve, and that is not a habit — it is what these two moments happen to be.

**A HOLE IN WOOD PULLS HARD TOWARD BURNING, so the negation earns its place** (§Negative inventory). Nothing on the page is scorched: *as if the wood were wool.* Char, smoke, embers, thrown splinters and broken bark are all banned by name, and the construction is stated first with a measurement so the ban trims rather than replaces (§Name a thing by construction, the order rule, and `ch07.md`'s waster as the warning on the far side).

**THE FOREARM IS BARE AND THE TALLY-MARKS ARE THEREFORE DRAWN.** `../canon-brief.md` keeps Vask's scars out of frame unless the moment needs them — but this crop is a forearm, an image cannot decline to answer, and **the marks are page canon in Ch. 3**, described exactly: many, all the same length, all the same slant. So they are written in rather than left to invention, and **the cloth is stated by where it stops on the arm, not by where the sleeve ends**, which is the fault that lost the wrists on `ch10/the-honey-and-the-bread`. It also rhymes on purpose with the adopted `ch03-the-back`: the marks a bad employer left, on the arm that has just gone through a tree.

**NO FACE, AND THE BOY IS OUT OF FRAME.** The page has Kael watching, and the viewer stands about where he stands — the same solve as `ch10/the-waterer`, used a second time deliberately, because what the page says he took from it was *the holding, not the hole*, and the holding is an arm.

**NOTHING GLOWS.** No thread, no light, no spark and no residue: the working is finished and the hand is already out. The plate is the second after, and the only evidence left in the world is a hole.

```
Image 1: vask.png — portrait reference of the man.

Setting: a mountain yard at first light on a winter morning, as loose
wash suggestion — frost on the ground, a stand of pines going into
cool wash behind, and the sky beyond them bare unpainted paper. Cold
flat half-light before sunrise, no sun and no shadows with edges, and
it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: close on one pine trunk and a man's right arm, seen from
the side. The trunk stands upright through the middle of the frame,
about as thick as a man's thigh. The picture is cropped at his
shoulder and at his waist, so no head and no face is in the frame.

THE HOLE IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE AND IT IS DRAWN AS BARE
UNPAINTED PAPER. One clean round hole the width of his wrist goes
straight through the trunk, and the pale dawn sky on the far side is
seen through it as reserved paper, with the ink drawing stopping at
the hole's edge. Its edges are clean and sharp, as if cut. NO CHAR, no
scorch, no smoke and no broken bark.

WHAT THE HAND IS DOING: his right hand has just come out of the hole
and hangs open beside it, a hand's width clear of the trunk, the
fingers relaxed and half-curled as if around something set down, and
holding nothing. His left hand is out of frame.

No glow, no spark and no thread of light anywhere: the working is
over.

THE ARM IS BARE FROM THE ELBOW DOWN — the sleeve is pushed back above
the elbow and stays there, so the whole forearm, the wrist and the
back of the hand are uncovered. Across the outside of that forearm
run many short old white scars, all of them the same length and all
of them at the same slant, healed long ago and flat to the skin. The
forearm and the hand are heavy and worked, the tendons standing, the
knuckles broad.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: his skin is the only
warm colour anywhere. The trunk, the frost, the pines, the ground and
his clothing are cool grey and grey-blue with no brown in them. The
frost on the ground is reserved as bare unpainted paper with the sky.

HIS HAND AND THE EDGE OF THE HOLE ARE THE MOST FINISHED THINGS IN THE
PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The pines and
the ground behind stay at quiet middle values with soft edges and no
detail, and nothing back there competes for finish. The ink linework
stays the strongest element throughout and does not blur into the
wash.

His garment, by construction: coarse undyed working cloth, much
mended — the ink carries the cloth in dense scratchy linework at the
seams and the mended places.
```

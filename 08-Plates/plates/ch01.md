# Ch. 1 — The Stars That Fell · plate sheet

Three candidates from `candidates.md` built as prompts. **None has been run.** Nothing here is adopted; when one is, the adopted note goes above its block and the image goes in `images/`.

**WHY THESE THREE TOGETHER.** They are three different kinds of picture — a two-figure night exterior, a single-figure gesture, an interior with nobody in it — and **all three are lit from below.** That is an accident of the chapter and it is the reason to run them as one batch: the portrait system's fixed lighting is soft and directional and has never once been asked for a source under the subject. If low warm light works here, Aurelian's candle in Ch. 9 is unblocked.

**ANCHORING COST, and it is the real cost of Part I.** Kael at four and both parents exist as **head-and-shoulders portraits only**; there is no full figure for any of them at any age below fourteen. So in plates 1 and 2 everything below the collar is authored in the prompt and anchored to nothing, and **it will not repeat in the next Part I plate.** Plate 3 has no reference at all. Two of these three pictures share a face with the library and share a body with nothing.

**THE GREY: FRAMED OUT BY DESIGN, RULED ON BY THE AUTHOR IN THE RUN.** The plan was that no plate may show Kael's eyes while the grey is unspent, so this one puts both faces away and says so twice. The run turned his face toward his mother anyway, and **the author ruled the eyes not visible and adopted the image (s37).** The rule is unchanged and still binds the next plate; what moved is one picture, on the author's reading of it.

**THE INHERITED COLOUR PARAGRAPH HAD TO BE OVERRIDDEN IN TWO OF THE THREE.** The house style ends *the warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth* — written for portraits, where the only warm thing is a face. A plate lit by a fire or by a town has its warmth landing on timber, stone, wool and rock, and one of these three has no skin in it at all. Both blocks now say so in their own words. **This is a general plate problem, not a Ch. 1 problem**, and it is written up in `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Plates.

**DECIDED, so it is not defaulted:** Neris is **not pregnant** in this chapter. Kael is four; the pregnancy belongs to the tournament season nine years later.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: fallen-stars

**ADOPTED — the first plate in the book.** `images/plates/ch01-fallen-stars.png`, first run. Anchored on `images/portraits/kael-4.png` and `images/portraits/neris.png`.

**THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S REVISION AND IS VERBATIM AS RUN.** It is not the block this sheet first generated — that one was rewritten before it was ever run, and what it produced is not recoverable from it. `PLATE-VERBATIM` means the build script emits this block untouched, with no style header prepended, because the text already carries its own. **Do not reassemble it.**

**THE EYES: AUTHOR RULING (s37).** The boy's face came into frame turned toward his mother rather than down at the water, and the ruling is that **his eyes are not visible in the adopted image and the plate is legal.** The picture is adopted as drawn. What the ruling does not do is move `03` or the brief: Kael's grey stays unspent on the page, and the next plate still frames it out.

**WHAT THIS RUN PROVED, and the last one is the one that travels:**

1. **The house look survives into a plate.** Granulation, ink linework, wash blooms, bare paper, muted and earthy — no drift, on the first run, in a night exterior. This was the open question and it is answered.
2. **Costume ownership held between two figures in the same undyed wool.** Pale oatmeal blanket on the boy, mended grey-brown layers on the woman, no bleed either way. The rule holds outside the scene tests it was written in.
3. **The stars did not compete with the lamps.** The worry that two reserved-paper elements would claim the same value slot was wrong; the lamps won on warmth alone.
4. **AN IN-FRAME WARM SOURCE IS NOT LIGHT FROM BELOW.** The lamps render beautifully *as* a source and throw nothing: no warm rim on the shoulder, no lit edge on the blanket, the figures lit by ambient sky. **So Aurelian's candle in Ch. 9 is still unproven** — a face lit from below is a different and harder ask, and nothing here has tested it.
5. **A described absence loses to a described thing.** *Neither face is visible* did not survive contact with a renderer that knows what a carried child does; it turned his face to his mother. A positive instruction — what the viewer sees instead of a face — is the version to write next time.
6. **Word-described child proportion lost to an attached face reference.** The boy reads several years above four. When a picture hides the head, the reference is arguing for an age the prompt cannot outvote.

animals, no boats. No fire, lamp or torch anywhere near them — every
light in this picture is far below them.

Also a control on lighting: flat winter daylight, no source below anything.

### PLATE: talking-the-goat-down

*Author-requested (s37): the warm one.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png` for the man; **no reference for the boy**, as with `telling-the-goat`. Ch. 1 canon: *once, when a slate slid off the roof in a storm, his father's hand went up and the slate was in it, and his father kept talking to the goat the whole time, because the goat was upset about the storm.*

**HOW THE WARMTH IS CARRIED, and this is the whole design.** `03` and the brief both forbid warmth in Vask's face — *nothing in his eyes that is angry and nothing that is anything; not hostile, not cold, not sad, not kind.* A tender expression would be the wrong man, and the sheet does not move for a picture. So the warmth is staged instead: **he is down on the animal's level, his hand is flat on its neck, he is mid-word, and the boy is tucked in against his side** — and the animal has stopped being frightened, which is the only evidence in the frame that anything kind is happening. The face stays exactly as level as it is everywhere else.

**The slate is deliberately not in this picture.** `the-catch` owns that object and it would split the frame's attention; here the subject is the talking, not the hand.

**Third test of the same question:** no reference for the boy, and his face away for a staged reason — he is watching the goat.

# Ch. 1 — The Stars That Fell · plate sheet

Three candidates from `candidates.md` built as prompts. **None has been run.** Nothing here is adopted; when one is, the adopted note goes above its block and the image goes in `images/`.

**WHY THESE THREE TOGETHER.** They are three different kinds of picture — a two-figure night exterior, a single-figure gesture, an interior with nobody in it — and **all three are lit from below.** That is an accident of the chapter and it is the reason to run them as one batch: the portrait system's fixed lighting is soft and directional and has never once been asked for a source under the subject. If low warm light works here, Aurelian's candle in Ch. 9 is unblocked.

**ANCHORING COST, and it is the real cost of Part I.** Kael at four and both parents exist as **head-and-shoulders portraits only**; there is no full figure for any of them at any age below fourteen. So in plates 1 and 2 everything below the collar is authored in the prompt and anchored to nothing, and **it will not repeat in the next Part I plate.** Plate 3 has no reference at all. Two of these three pictures share a face with the library and share a body with nothing.

**THE GREY: FRAMED OUT BY DESIGN, RULED ON BY THE AUTHOR IN THE RUN.** The plan was that no plate may show Kael's eyes while the grey is unspent, so this one puts both faces away and says so twice. The run turned his face toward his mother anyway, and **the author ruled the eyes not visible and adopted the image (s37).** The rule is unchanged and still binds the next plate; what moved is one picture, on the author's reading of it.

**THE INHERITED COLOUR PARAGRAPH HAD TO BE OVERRIDDEN IN TWO OF THE THREE.** The house style ends *the warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth* — written for portraits, where the only warm thing is a face. A plate lit by a fire or by a town has its warmth landing on timber, stone, wool and rock, and one of these three has no skin in it at all. Both blocks now say so in their own words. **This is a general plate problem, not a Ch. 1 problem**, and it is written up in `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Plates.

**DECIDED, so it is not defaulted:** Neris is **not pregnant** in this chapter. Kael is four; the pregnancy belongs to the tournament season nine years later.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: fallen-stars

**ADOPTED — the first plate in the book.** `images/plates/ch01-fallen-stars.png`, first run. Anchored on `images/portraits/kael-4.png` and `images/portraits/neris.png`.

**THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S REVISION AND IS VERBATIM AS RUN.** It is not the block this sheet first generated — that one was rewritten before it was ever run, and what it produced is not recoverable from it. `PLATE-VERBATIM` means the build script emits this block untouched, with no style header prepended, because the text already carries its own. **Do not reassemble it.**

**THE EYES: AUTHOR RULING (s37).** The boy's face came into frame turned toward his mother rather than down at the water, and the ruling is that **his eyes are not visible in the adopted image and the plate is legal.** The picture is adopted as drawn. What the ruling does not do is move `03` or the brief: Kael's grey stays unspent on the page, and the next plate still frames it out.

**WHAT THIS RUN PROVED, and the last one is the one that travels:**

1. **The house look survives into a plate.** Granulation, ink linework, wash blooms, bare paper, muted and earthy — no drift, on the first run, in a night exterior. This was the open question and it is answered.
2. **Costume ownership held between two figures in the same undyed wool.** Pale oatmeal blanket on the boy, mended grey-brown layers on the woman, no bleed either way. The rule holds outside the scene tests it was written in.
3. **The stars did not compete with the lamps.** The worry that two reserved-paper elements would claim the same value slot was wrong; the lamps won on warmth alone.
4. **AN IN-FRAME WARM SOURCE IS NOT LIGHT FROM BELOW.** The lamps render beautifully *as* a source and throw nothing: no warm rim on the shoulder, no lit edge on the blanket, the figures lit by ambient sky. **So Aurelian's candle in Ch. 9 is still unproven** — a face lit from below is a different and harder ask, and nothing here has tested it.
5. **A described absence loses to a described thing.** *Neither face is visible* did not survive contact with a renderer that knows what a carried child does; it turned his face to his mother. A positive instruction — what the viewer sees instead of a face — is the version to write next time.
6. **Word-described child proportion lost to an attached face reference.** The boy reads several years above four. When a picture hides the head, the reference is arguing for an age the prompt cannot outvote.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy — take his hair colour and
colouring from it only. He is much younger here than in the
reference; ignore its age entirely.
Image 2: portrait reference of the woman — take her hair, colouring
and apparent age from it only.

Same medium and style as the reference images: loose watercolour and
ink on rough cold-press paper, visible paper tooth and pigment
granulation, scratchy ink linework, wash blooms with hard irregular
edges, bare unpainted paper reserved for the lights.

Setting: a bare shelf of rock high on a mountainside at night, open
sky above, the ground falling away steeply in front of them. Loose
wash suggestion only — the rock implied in a few dry-brush strokes,
no detail anywhere, everything past the two figures going down into
deep cool wash.

Composition: both figures FULL LENGTH, SEEN FROM DIRECTLY BEHIND at
about their own height, the view angled slightly downward past them
so the drop opens beyond the shelf edge. The two of them small in the
frame and set high in it, the drop and the water far beneath them
filling the lower two thirds of the picture. A woman stands at the
edge of the shelf with a small boy carried on her hip, wrapped to the
chin in a large coarse wool blanket. Both are facing away from the
viewer, out and down over the drop.

THE SILHOUETTE: woman and boy read as one dark upright shape against
the drop, his head clearly above her shoulder on the far side, her
carrying arm making the break between them. Do not merge them into a
single bulk and do not separate them into two standing figures.

NEITHER FACE IS VISIBLE, AND THAT IS DELIBERATE. The woman is seen
from directly behind — the back of her head is what the viewer sees.
The boy's face is turned down and away, mostly lost in the wool and
in shadow. NO EYES ARE VISIBLE ANYWHERE IN THE PICTURE. Do not turn
either head toward the viewer.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
Her left arm is under the boy, carrying his weight against her hip.
Her right hand is up at his jaw, fingers resting along it and turning
his face DOWNWARD — away from the sky, toward the water far below.
The touch is light; she is showing him something, not holding him
still. Both her hands are in the frame and both are occupied. The
boy's hands are inside the blanket and not visible. Nothing else is
in any hand. Neither of them is carrying a light.

THE LIGHT COMES FROM BELOW, AND IT IS THE SUBJECT. Far down at the
foot of the mountain, where the land meets the sea, a heap of small
warm orange lights lies scattered along the shore — a town's lamps at
night, gathered in one corner where the shore meets the water,
doubled and broken in the black water beneath them, the reflections
trembling and slightly displaced. The lamps and their reflections are
reserved as bare unpainted paper with a warm tint only at their
edges: they are the lightest value in the picture, the only warm
colour in it, and the one fully resolved thing in an image that is
loose everywhere else. Their light reaches the two figures only
faintly — a thin warm rim along the woman's shoulder and the near
edge of the blanket, and nothing more. Everything above them is cold:
rock, cloth, hair, sky. A few small cold stars in the upper sky,
picked out as tiny reserved flecks, clearly separate in colour and
character from the warm lights below.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is in danger, nobody is hurrying. It is
a still, mild night and the two of them are standing looking down.

Bare skin is nearly absent from this picture: her two hands are the
only skin anywhere in the frame, small and held in cool shadow, no
warm ochre in them. No other skin is visible.

THE WOMAN: a grown woman of a mountain village. Tall, straight and
completely still. Long dark hair falling loose past the shoulders,
brown rather than black, kept low and desaturated — the warmth is in
the hue only, never in the light. Plain coarse village working cloth,
much mended, in dull grey-brown and undyed oatmeal. No ornament of
any kind.

THE BOY: four years old and built like it — the head large in
proportion to the body, the limbs short and soft with no length in
them at all, the whole child small enough to sit on one hip. Wrapped
to the chin in coarse undyed wool in dull oatmeal, dark hair on the
crown of his head.

Do not mix their cloth: the blanket belongs only to the boy and
covers him chin to feet; the mended working clothes belong only to
the woman. They must read as two separate garments, not one
continuous wrap.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No buildings, no fences, no
animals, no boats. No fire, lamp or torch anywhere near them — every
light in this picture is far below them.
```

### PLATE: the-catch

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png` — face, hair and colouring; the body is authored here.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his face, hair and
colouring from it.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house in a
storm, as loose wash suggestion — a low roof-line behind him with
slates on it, a fence and a goat pen implied in a few strokes, the
mountain beyond lost in grey wash. Rain coming across at a steep
angle: dry-brush drag and flicked spatter through the whole
background.

Composition: the man waist-up, standing, his body and face turned
AWAY from his own raised hand, angled toward a goat in the pen at the
left of the frame. He is mid-sentence, talking to the animal.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
His right arm is raised straight up, above and slightly behind his
head, and his right hand has closed on ONE flat grey roof slate — the
slate held flat in his palm, caught and stopped, not falling and not
being thrown. His left hand is down at his side, empty and open. Two
hands, one slate, nothing else in either hand. No tool, no rope, no
stick, no second slate anywhere in the frame.

HE IS NOT LOOKING AT THE SLATE, AND THIS IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
PICTURE. His face is turned away from his own raised hand, toward the
goat, mouth open mid-word. His expression has not changed for the
catch at all — level, unhurried, entirely unbothered, giving nothing.
The hand has done this without him. Do not have him look up at it. Do
not make him alarmed, braced, straining, heroic or surprised. No
effort anywhere in the body.

THE MAN: a grown man of a mountain village, a herder by every
appearance. Broad through the shoulders and no further — heavy across
the top of the back and the neck, nothing wasted below it, an economy
rather than a bulk. Iron-dark hair of medium length, wet, pushed back
off the forehead. Weathered hard, wind and salt worked deep into the
skin. Coarse undyed working cloth, heavy weave, plain collar closed
to the base of the throat, much mended, soaked dark with rain. No
ornament, no buckle, no badge.

One goat in the pen, ordinary, unsettled by the weather, looking at
him.

Light: flat cold storm light from a low grey sky, with the warm value
in the picture confined to the skin of his face and hand.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No child, no woman, nobody
watching.
```

### PLATE: room-of-true-things

*Candidate 3.* No anchor at all — the test of whether the house look survives with nothing to hold it.

```
No reference image. Follow the medium and colour description above
exactly.

Subject: the single room of a small, poor, very well-kept mountain
house at night, seen FROM ABOVE — from the height of a sleeping loft,
looking down into the room over the edge of the loft floor, so that a
roof beam and the near edge of rough boards frame the top of the
picture. Interior only, the whole room in one view.

THE LIGHT COMES FROM ONE PLACE AND IT IS BELOW EVERYTHING. In the
middle of the floor is a square hole lined with stone, and in the
hole a small orange fire, low and contained. It is the only light in
the picture. It throws warm light up the walls and up the underside
of the table, the beams and the tools, and it leaves the corners and
the roof in deep cool shadow. The fire is SMALL and deliberately kept
small — a banked hearth fire, not a blaze, not a bonfire, no flames
reaching.

In the room, plainly arranged and readable at a glance:
a heavy table of plain hand-made wood;
two low beds against one wall with folded coarse wool on them;
a wooden chest, closed;
and one whole wall given over to TOOLS — a dozen or more hand tools
hung on pegs in a deliberate order, each with its own place, evenly
spaced, every one of them hanging straight and level and not one
crooked. After the fire, the tool wall is the most important thing in
the picture.

One heavy plank door, shut, with a wooden bar down across it in iron
brackets.

NO PEOPLE ANYWHERE IN THE FRAME. Nobody in the beds, no hands, no
figures, no faces, no silhouettes, nobody at the door. The room is
empty of people and that is deliberate.

Nothing is happening. Nothing is broken, knocked over, spilled or
burning that should not be. It is an ordinary night in a house where
everything has a place and is in it.

Colour: muted, earthy, desaturated — dull grey-brown timber, dead
olive wool, cold grey stone, and the single warm orange source down
in the floor. There is no skin in this picture at all: the warmth
belongs entirely to the firelight, and it falls on timber, stone and
wool.
```

### PLATE: telling-the-goat

*Author-requested (s37).* Anchor: `images/portraits/vask.png` for the man — **and deliberately NO reference for the boy.** Ch. 1 canon: *"Not yet," his father said, splitting wood. His father split wood in one stroke, always. Crack. "Not this winter." / "Next winter?" / "Perhaps." Crack. / Kael nodded and went to tell the goat.*

**IT TESTS THE TWO THINGS THE FALLEN STARS LEFT OPEN.** (1) The boy's face is away from the viewer for a **staged reason** — he is walking to the goat — rather than by a described absence, which is the failure mode that turned his face to his mother last time. (2) **No face reference is attached for him**, so word-described child proportion has nothing arguing against it. If he reads four here and read seven there, the reference was the cause and the finding is confirmed.

Also a control on lighting: flat winter daylight, no source below anything.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the man — take his face, hair and
colouring from it only.

Setting: the yard of a small stone-and-turf mountain house in
winter, as loose wash suggestion — a low roof-line behind, a
woodpile, a fenced pen to one side, snow on the ground and on the
roof, the mountain beyond going up into pale wash. Bare unpainted
paper carries the snow.

Composition: full length, all three subjects in one frame, grounded
on the same ground plane. THE MAN at the left at a chopping block,
mid-stroke. THE BOY small, in the middle distance at the centre,
walking away from the viewer and away from his father, toward the
pen. THE GOAT in the pen at the right, behind the fence. The three
of them read left to right as man, boy, goat.

THE BOY IS SEEN FROM DIRECTLY BEHIND AND HIS FACE IS NOT IN THE
PICTURE — he is walking away, and the back of his head, his back and
his heels are what the viewer sees. Do not turn him. Do not show his
face in profile. No feature of his face is drawn at all.

THE BOY IS FOUR YEARS OLD AND MUST READ AS FOUR. Very small — the
top of his head no higher than the man's hip. The head large in
proportion to the body, the shoulders narrow and soft, the arms and
legs short with no length in them, the walk slightly wide and
unsteady the way a small child walks in snow. He is a toddler, not a
boy of seven or eight. Dark hair, fine and soft, cut short and
uneven at home.

WHAT IS IN EACH HAND:
The man's two hands are together on the haft of one axe, raised
above and behind his head at the top of the stroke. One axe, held in
two hands, nothing else in either hand. The boy's hands are empty
and down at his sides. No tool, no stick, no rope, no bucket
anywhere else in the frame.

THE MAN: a grown man of a mountain village. Broad through the
shoulders and no further — heavy across the top of the back and the
neck, nothing wasted below it, an economy rather than a bulk. Iron-
dark hair of medium length, pushed back off the forehead. Weathered
hard. He is looking down at the block and the wood, not at the boy,
his face level and giving nothing, mouth closed, entirely unhurried.
He has already answered and gone back to work.

ONE GOAT, standing in the pen, ordinary and shaggy, side-on. THE
GOAT IS NOT LOOKING AT THE BOY — its head is turned away toward
something else entirely, wholly indifferent. This indifference is
the joke of the picture. Do not make the goat curious, friendly,
alarmed or attentive.

Nothing is happening. Nobody is in danger, nobody is hurrying,
nobody is calling out. It is an ordinary cold morning and each of
the three is doing his own thing.

Do not mix their cloth: the man wears coarse undyed working cloth,
heavy weave, plain collar closed to the throat, much mended, in dull
grey-brown and dead olive; the boy wears plain undyed wool, coarse
and much mended and a little too big for him, in dull oatmeal. No
ornament on either of them, no buckle, no badge.

Light: flat, cold, even winter daylight from an overcast sky. No
warm source anywhere in the picture, no fire, no lamp, no sun.

No other figures anywhere in the frame. No woman, nobody watching,
no other animals.
```

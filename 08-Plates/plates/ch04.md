### PLATE: blade-across-her-knees

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/neris.png` — hair and colouring; her face is not in this picture. Ch. 4 canon: *His mother sat at the window in the dark with her back straight and her face to the sea, and across her knees, unsheathed, lay a blade he had never been shown, long and thin and older than the tools on the wall, and her hands rested on it the way her hands rested on bread dough, at home.*

**ADOPTED.** `images/plates/ch04-blade-across-her-knees.png`, first run.

Her sheet says this plate is already composed and needs no design: back, window, sea, blade, hands. Kael is not in frame — the view is his, from the ladder.

**A POSITIVE PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION BEAT THE SCENE'S OWN PULL.** A woman alone at a window at night with a bare blade is an image that wants to be tense, and the block never forbids tension — it just says what the hands are doing: *palms down, fingers relaxed and spread, the hands of someone resting them on dough, not gripping a weapon.* That was enough. **Where the doorframe needed the wrong reading named and banned, this needed only the right one described precisely** — worth weighing before reaching for a negation stack.

**The two-clause demotion carried the picture again:** hands and blade the only finished things, catching the window light along their length, everything else dark and loose.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the woman — hair and colouring only.

Setting: the single room of a small mountain house at night, unlit.
One small window in a thick stone wall, open to a night sea far
below — the sea a faint pale sheet, the only light in the picture.
The room around her is deep cool wash going to black.

Composition: the woman seated on a low stool at the window, seen
from BEHIND AND SLIGHTLY ABOVE, from the height of a loft ladder.
She reads as a dark upright shape against the window. Her back is
straight, her face turned to the sea. No feature of her face is
drawn.

ACROSS HER KNEES, HORIZONTAL, LIES ONE UNSHEATHED BLADE — long,
thin, plain, old, with no ornament and no guard-work, older-looking
than any tool in the room. It lies flat across both thighs, not
held up, not pointed at anything. Nothing else is in the picture
with her: no scabbard, no cloth, no lamp, no second blade.

BOTH HER HANDS REST ON THE BLADE, palms down, fingers relaxed and
spread — the hands of someone resting them on dough, not gripping a
weapon. The hands and the blade are the subject of the image.

THE BLADE AND HER HANDS ARE THE ONLY THINGS FINISHED. They catch
the window light along their length and carry the sharpest step
from light to dark in the picture. The room, the stool, her hair
and her clothing are held dark and loose, no hard edges, nothing
competing for finish.

Long dark brown hair falling loose past the shoulders. Plain coarse
village working cloth, much mended, THE INK CARRYING THE CLOTH at
the seams and the mended places.
```

### PLATE: the-master-arrives

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/severin.png`. Ch. 4 canon: *old the way the split rock was old — worn to what mattered. Tall, gone lean, grey all the way through, in a coat too plain to be a villager's and too well-made to be a villager's, carrying a wrapped bundle on a strap · a scholar's stoop that sat on him the way a borrowed tool sits in the hand · the wrists coming out of the sleeves were too thick for a man whose work was paper.*

His first appearance, in the mountain register. The grey must stay ambiguous — age or drain, never resolved — so it is a true even neutral, no seam and no warm silver.

**RUN, NOT YET ADOPTED (s42).** The house look is the strongest in the folder — loose wash, ink linework, bare-paper sky, real granulation — and the body canon held: **not frail**, tall, still broad, walking up a real mountain with the sea in flat pale wash far below. The hand inventory held exactly (bundle on the strap, left hand on it, right hand empty and open, nothing else carried). **No seam, streak or patch anywhere in the hair**, which is the one prohibition that is load-bearing, since a seam would confirm the drain outright.

**THREE MISSES, and two of them generalise:**

1. **THE COAT CAME BACK NINETEENTH-CENTURY.** A frock coat over a waistcoat over a winged collar — Dickensian, and it is neither of `02/geography`'s two registers: not Latin-Byzantine, not island material culture. **The word did it.** *Coat* is page canon (Ch. 4: *a coat too plain to be a villager's and too well-made to be a villager's*) but in a prompt it is a costume-history term with a default. Same failure as *dagger* rendering a near-longsword. Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name a garment by construction.
2. **THE WRISTS — THE DECLARED SUBJECT — DID NOT RENDER.** The block said *the wrists are the most precisely drawn thing in the picture* and they arrived as ordinary wrists inside cuffs. **A tell that small cannot win a full-length frame however it is prioritised**, and this is the second time the folder has learned it: `the-knot` found hand size *moved it and did not solve it*. §Name the winner AND demote the rival works on things large enough to hold finish. **The fix is crop, not emphasis** — Severin's own portrait already takes the half-figure COMPOSITION override for exactly this reason.
3. **The borrowed stoop did not survive, and the face drifted slightly stern.** He walks upright, and the block's stated contradiction — *his posture and his body disagree, and both must survive* — resolved to the body's side. That is the better half to keep if only one survives, but the stoop is the tell that he is playing a scholar.

**AMENDED AFTER THE RUN (s42), and the register question is priced here.** The author asked for something *more temply — robes and such*. **Ch. 4 puts him on the mountain in a coat**, and the brief holds robes-and-the-chain as the *Temple and games* register: two registers, both page canon, and the difference carries a fact — he dresses down to come up here. **Spending that would cost the visual difference between Temple-Severin and mountain-Severin, which is the whole point of his first appearance being plain.**

**So the tightest version, and it is what the run's own failure argued for anyway:** keep the coat, describe it by construction instead of by name. A long straight undyed overgarment to mid-calf, closed edge to edge, standing collar, no lapel and no waistcoat, sleeves ending square at a bare wrist — **that reads monastic, and none of it is nineteenth-century.** The Dickensian result came from the word, not the garment. **The chain stays off**: it is Temple-and-games canon and it is the one ornament that would resolve the register outright.

**IF FULL ROBES ARE WANTED, THEY WANT A TEMPLE-SET PLATE** — `candidates.md` Ch. 7's *Temple from the road* is the natural home, and `images/portraits/severin.png` already carries the robe-and-chain register as a reference.

**TWO OTHER FIXES APPLIED FROM THE RUN.** The wrists lose their priority claim — proven not to render at full length (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name the winner, the limit) — and stay as plain anatomy, with the cuff removed so the sleeve stops hiding them. The stoop is restated mechanically (*head carried forward of the shoulders, upper back rounded, chin down*) rather than as a stated contradiction, and the stern drift gets its mechanism named: *the brows level and not lowered.*

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch04-the-master-arrives.png`, run 2.**

**RUN 2 (s42) — THE GARMENT FIX IS CONFIRMED.** A long straight dark overgarment to mid-calf, standing collar, no lapel, no waistcoat, sleeves ending at a bare wrist. **It reads monastic, and it is the same page-canon coat**; nothing about the garment changed except that the words stopped naming it. §Name a garment by construction is proven, not merely diagnosed.

**Three things moved with it, none of them asked for directly.** The hair came back plainly grey through the whole head, where run 1 carried dark strands — **the grey clause was untouched, so what fixed it was removing the Victorian tailoring that had been dragging the whole figure toward a younger, darker register.** The stoop is now present (head forward of the shoulders, the upper back mildly rounded) after being restated as mechanics rather than as a contradiction. And the face is closer to courteous-and-giving-nothing, though the deep lines still carry some severity.

**ONE INSTRUCTION HAS NOW FAILED TWICE: THE CAMERA HEIGHT.** *THE VIEWER IS LOW, at the eye height of a boy of nine, so the man is seen slightly from below* produced a level adult viewpoint in both runs. **A camera position is not drawable; its consequences are.** Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Altitude. The picture is good without it — but the shot was designed to be a nine-year-old's first sight of him, and that is not what it is.

**FOR THE AUTHOR:** the hair reads grey but carries visibly darker strands through the back and under the mass. Whether that clears Ch. 4's *grey all the way through* is a judgement on the image, not something the sheet can rule.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the old man.

Setting: a steep mountain path coming up to a smallholding yard,
midmorning, as loose wash suggestion — thin grass, bare rock, the
sea far below in pale flat wash, the sky bare unpainted paper.

Composition: the old man full length on the path, coming up toward
the viewer, most of the frame. THE VIEWER IS LOW, at the eye height
of a boy of nine, so the man is seen slightly from below.

HIS POSTURE AND HIS BODY DISAGREE, AND BOTH MUST SURVIVE. He walks
with a scholar's stoop — head carried forward, shoulders rounded —
and it sits on him like borrowed clothing rather than belonging to
him: the head carried forward of the shoulders, the upper back
rounded, the chin down. Underneath it he is tall, lean and still broad
across the shoulder, and the bare wrists coming out of the sleeves are
thick and heavy, too thick for a man whose work is paper. Do not
resolve him into a frail old scholar.

Hair fully grey, the whole head, a true even neutral grey — not
warm silver, not white. No streak, no seam, no patch, nothing
gathered anywhere in it.

Old and worn down to structure: deep lines from nose to mouth,
across the forehead and at the outer corners of the eyes, skin dry
and drawn tight over the bone. No hollow cheeks, no sunken temples,
no thin neck, no slackness, no frailty. Face courteous and unhurried,
giving nothing away, THE BROWS LEVEL AND NOT LOWERED — not warm, not
kindly, and not stern or grim.

HIS GARMENT, DESCRIBED BY CONSTRUCTION AND NOT BY NAME: one long
straight overgarment falling to mid-calf, dark and undyed, close at
the shoulder and hanging loose and unshaped below it, closed edge to
edge down the centre front. No lapel, no revers, no turned collar, no
button placket, no pockets, no belt, no braid, no trim, no metal
anywhere on it. A standing collar closed at the base of the throat,
with no shirt collar and no neckcloth showing above it. Plain full
sleeves ending square at the wrist with NO CUFF AND NO SLEEVE BENEATH,
so the wrist and the back of the hand are bare. It is exactly made and
well kept, and it has no ornament of any kind: too plain for a
gentleman and too well made for a villager.

NOTHING NINETEENTH-CENTURY. This is not a frock coat, not a tailcoat,
not a greatcoat and not a suit. There is no waistcoat under it and no
second garment visible at the chest.

One wrapped bundle carried on a strap over the shoulder, his left hand
resting on the strap. His right hand is empty and open at his side.
Nothing else is carried. THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH in dense
scratchy linework at the seams and the strap.
```

### PLATE: the-knot

**ADOPTED.** `images/plates/ch04-the-knot.png`, run 2 — run 1 had a fourth hand and adult-sized boy's hands; the block below is what produced the adopted image.

**THE CHAIN RULE IS CONFIRMED.** The cord came back in his fingers both times, both hands working it, nothing lying loose — the failure that put the Ch. 3 knife on the stone did not recur once the limb was described in order with the negative attached.

**HAND SIZE BY COMPARISON MOVED IT AND DID NOT SOLVE IT.** *Each of his hands is little more than half the size of the old man's hand in the same picture* got hands that read smaller, smoother and younger than the old man's — but not a nine-year-old's. **Hand size looks like a weak channel for age**, unlike whole-body proportion, which the pasture scene and the long game both carried outright. Worth knowing before a plate leans on it again.

**THREE HANDS FROM TWO PEOPLE STAYED DISTINGUISHABLE**, told apart by sleeve and skin alone — mended undyed wool against a plain dark coat, smooth skin against heavy worked knuckles. And `NO FOURTH HAND ANYWHERE IN THE FRAME` held, which is a negative with a real pull behind it: a table invites hands.

*Candidate 3.* Ch. 4 canon: *a cord of dark silk tied in a knot Kael had never met before. The master set the knot on the table between them like a plate of food* — and, while the boy works it, *the master had set his cup down without drinking* and *his mother's hand had come to rest on the table, flat.*

Four hands and an object, no faces. The knot is the chain: cord, fingers, pull.

```
Setting: the scrubbed plank table of a small mountain house,
daylight from one side, as loose wash — the room beyond falling
into shadow, no detail.

Composition: a CLOSE overhead view of the table top. No faces, no
heads, no shoulders — the frame holds the table, three hands, one
cup and one knotted cord. Everything above the forearm is out of
frame.

AT THE CENTRE, ONE CORD OF DARK SILK TIED IN A COMPLICATED KNOT,
about the size of a fist, sitting on the bare boards. A BOY'S TWO
HANDS ARE ON IT, working the cord: one hand holding the knot
steady, the fingers of the other pushed into a loop. The cord is IN
HIS HANDS and is not lying loose on the table.

THE BOY'S HANDS ARE A CHILD'S AND THE COMPARISON IS THE ONLY THING
CARRYING IT: each of his hands is little more than half the size of
the old man's hand in the same picture, and his whole hand would
sit inside the old man's palm. Short fingers, soft knuckles, no
tendon or vein standing out anywhere, small nails.

ONE POTTERY CUP stands on the table at the edge of the frame,
upright and full, untouched, with a large weathered old man's hand
resting on the boards beside it — not holding it, set down flat next
to it. Heavy knuckles, thick wrist, the skin dry and worked.

Three hands, one cup, one cord. Nothing else is on the table and
nothing else is held. NO FOURTH HAND ANYWHERE IN THE FRAME.

THE KNOT IS THE SUBJECT. It is the most precisely drawn thing in
the picture and carries the sharpest step from light to dark — the
silk dark, tight and legible, every turn of the cord readable. The
table, the cup, the sleeves and the hands are held at quiet middle
values, loosely washed, no hard edges.

The sleeves at the frame's edges are coarse undyed mended wool, THE
INK CARRYING THE CLOTH at the seams; the old man's sleeve is a
plain dark well-cut coat.
```

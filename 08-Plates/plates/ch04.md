### PLATE: blade-across-her-knees

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/neris.png` — hair and colouring; her face is not in this picture. Ch. 4 canon: *His mother sat at the window in the dark with her back straight and her face to the sea, and across her knees, unsheathed, lay a blade he had never been shown, long and thin and older than the tools on the wall, and her hands rested on it the way her hands rested on bread dough, at home.*

Her sheet says this plate is already composed and needs no design: back, window, sea, blade, hands. Kael is not in frame — the view is his, from the ladder.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the woman — hair and colouring only.

Setting: the single room of a small mountain house at night, unlit.
One small window in a thick stone wall, open to a night sea far
below — the sea a faint pale sheet, the only light in the picture.
The room around her is deep cool wash going to black.

Composition: the woman seated on a low stool at the window, seen
from BEHIND AND SLIGHTLY ABOVE, from the height of a loft ladder.
She reads as a dark upright shape against the window. Her back is
straight, her face turned to the sea. No feature of her face is
drawn.

ACROSS HER KNEES, HORIZONTAL, LIES ONE UNSHEATHED BLADE — long,
thin, plain, old, with no ornament and no guard-work, older-looking
than any tool in the room. It lies flat across both thighs, not
held up, not pointed at anything. Nothing else is in the picture
with her: no scabbard, no cloth, no lamp, no second blade.

BOTH HER HANDS REST ON THE BLADE, palms down, fingers relaxed and
spread — the hands of someone resting them on dough, not gripping a
weapon. The hands and the blade are the subject of the image.

THE BLADE AND HER HANDS ARE THE ONLY THINGS FINISHED. They catch
the window light along their length and carry the sharpest step
from light to dark in the picture. The room, the stool, her hair

Long dark brown hair falling loose past the shoulders. Plain coarse

the seams and the mended places.
```

### PLATE: the-master-arrives

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/severin.png`. Ch. 4 canon: *old the way the split rock was old — worn to what mattered. Tall, gone lean, grey all the way through, in a coat too plain to be a villager's and too well-made to be a villager's, carrying a wrapped bundle on a strap · a scholar's stoop that sat on him the way a borrowed tool sits in the hand · the wrists coming out of the sleeves were too thick for a man whose work was paper.*

His first appearance, in the mountain register. The grey must stay ambiguous — age or drain, never resolved — so it is a true even neutral, no seam and no warm silver.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the old man.

Setting: a steep mountain path coming up to a smallholding yard,
midmorning, as loose wash suggestion — thin grass, bare rock, the
sea far below in pale flat wash, the sky bare unpainted paper.

Composition: the old man full length on the path, coming up toward
the viewer, most of the frame. THE VIEWER IS LOW, at the eye height
of a boy of nine, so the man is seen slightly from below.

HIS POSTURE AND HIS BODY DISAGREE, AND BOTH MUST SURVIVE. He walks
with a scholar's stoop — head carried forward, shoulders rounded —
and it sits on him like borrowed clothing rather than belonging to
him. Underneath it he is tall, lean and still broad across the
shoulder, and HIS WRISTS COMING OUT OF THE SLEEVES ARE THICK AND
HEAVY, too thick for a man whose work is paper. The wrists are the
most precisely drawn thing in the picture. Do not resolve him into
a frail old scholar.

Hair fully grey, the whole head, a true even neutral grey — not
warm silver, not white. No streak, no seam, no patch, nothing
gathered anywhere in it.

Old and worn down to structure: deep lines from nose to mouth,
across the forehead and at the outer corners of the eyes, skin dry
and drawn tight over the bone. No hollow cheeks, no sunken temples,
no thin neck, no slackness, no frailty. Face courteous and
unhurried, giving nothing away — not warm, not kindly, not stern.

A plain dark coat, well cut and well kept, no ornament of any kind:
too plain for a gentleman and too well made for a villager. One
wrapped bundle carried on a strap over the shoulder, his left hand
resting on the strap. His right hand is empty and open at his side.
Nothing else is carried. THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH in dense
scratchy linework at the seams and the strap.
```

### PLATE: the-knot

*Candidate 3.* Ch. 4 canon: *a cord of dark silk tied in a knot Kael had never met before. The master set the knot on the table between them like a plate of food* — and, while the boy works it, *the master had set his cup down without drinking* and *his mother's hand had come to rest on the table, flat.*

Four hands and an object, no faces. The knot is the chain: cord, fingers, pull.

```
Setting: the scrubbed plank table of a small mountain house,
daylight from one side, as loose wash — the room beyond falling
into shadow, no detail.

Composition: a CLOSE overhead view of the table top. No faces, no
heads, no shoulders — the frame holds the table, three hands, one
cup and one knotted cord. Everything above the forearm is out of
frame.

AT THE CENTRE, ONE CORD OF DARK SILK TIED IN A COMPLICATED KNOT,
about the size of a fist, sitting on the bare boards. A BOY'S TWO
HANDS ARE ON IT, working the cord: one hand holding the knot
steady, the fingers of the other pushed into a loop. The cord is IN
HIS HANDS and is not lying loose on the table.

THE BOY'S HANDS ARE A CHILD'S AND THE COMPARISON IS THE ONLY THING
CARRYING IT: each of his hands is little more than half the size of
the old man's hand in the same picture, and his whole hand would
sit inside the old man's palm. Short fingers, soft knuckles, no
tendon or vein standing out anywhere, small nails.

ONE POTTERY CUP stands on the table at the edge of the frame,
upright and full, untouched, with a large weathered old man's hand
resting on the boards beside it — not holding it, set down flat next
to it. Heavy knuckles, thick wrist, the skin dry and worked.

Three hands, one cup, one cord. Nothing else is on the table and
nothing else is held. NO FOURTH HAND ANYWHERE IN THE FRAME.

THE KNOT IS THE SUBJECT. It is the most precisely drawn thing in
the picture and carries the sharpest step from light to dark — the
silk dark, tight and legible, every turn of the cord readable. The
table, the cup, the sleeves and the hands are held at quiet middle
values, loosely washed, no hard edges.

The sleeves at the frame's edges are coarse undyed mended wool, THE
INK CARRYING THE CLOTH at the seams; the old man's sleeve is a
plain dark well-cut coat.
```

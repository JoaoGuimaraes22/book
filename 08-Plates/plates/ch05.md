### PLATE: past-the-split-rock

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick, and the author's named target (s40).* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-9.png` — hair and colouring; his face is not in this picture. Ch. 5 canon: *The split rock stood up out of the dark where it had always stood, at the end of the path, at the end of the world. Kael put his hand on it, the way you put your hand on the goat, on the door, on a thing of the house... Below him, far down, in the corner where the land met the sea, the whole heap of fallen stars was burning — more of them than he had ever seen, doubled and trembling in the black water, the longest night of the year lit end to end... He stepped past the rock.* And, of the walk down: *Wool socks over the boots' voice.*

**THE DELIBERATE BOOKEND TO `ch01-fallen-stars`** — same lights, same child, same water, and this time nobody turns his chin down. The rhyme is carried at pixel level by reusing that image's proven lamp-and-reflection wording rather than re-inventing it: reserved bare paper, warm tint at the edges only, doubled and displaced in the black water, cold stars above kept separate in colour and character. **For a bookend, proven phrasing is the point.**

**THE STEP IS ALREADY TAKEN, and that is the staging call.** *A hand coming off the stone* is a motion, and a still image cannot carry it. What it can carry is the geometry: the leading foot down on the far side with the weight already on it, the trailing arm stretched back at full reach, one palm still flat on the rock. The law behind him and one point of contact left. It is written as `arm, hand, stone — one continuous limb, in that order`, per §Describe a limb as a chain.

**THE GREY COSTS NOTHING HERE, FOR A NEW REASON.** `fallen-stars` lost *neither face is visible* because there was a second person in the frame for the boy to turn toward, and a renderer that knows what a carried child does turned him. **He is alone in this one** — there is nothing a head could turn to — and the framing is written as a positive: what the viewer sees instead of a face, which is the version that sheet said to write next time.

**THE LIGHTS ARE INSTRUCTED TO THROW NOTHING, which reverses `fallen-stars` on purpose.** That block asked for a thin warm rim along the shoulder and the run gave none: an in-frame warm source renders *as* a source and does not light anything. Rather than ask again for what has already failed, this block states the behaviour as the intent — *he is a dark shape lit only by the night sky, no warm rim on his shoulder* — and the picture is better for it. **The light-from-below rematch is not this plate's job**; the Ch. 8 doorway owns it.

**THIS PLATE FIXES THE SPLIT ROCK'S SIZE FOR THE BOOK — a man-high upright stone with one cleft top to bottom.** Nothing on the page pins it, so whatever is drawn here becomes canon-by-precedent, and **the rock recurs**: Ch. 6 carries the boy home past it, Ch. 7 stands the master on its near side leaning on a stick he does not need. Man-high works in all three, and it earns the scale anchor this picture otherwise lacks — a lone figure has nothing to be measured against, so §Scale between figures gets its third subject.

**ANCHORING COST.** There is still no full figure of Kael below fourteen, so everything under the collar is authored here and anchored to nothing. It will not repeat in the next Part I plate. The portrait buys the medium, the palette and the hair — which is worth having, since `room-of-true-things` was written to test whether the house look survives with no reference at all and has never been run.

Image 1: portrait reference of the boy — hair and colouring only.

THE VIEWER IS HIGH ON A MOUNTAIN AT NIGHT, standing a few feet behind
a boy and slightly above him, looking out and steeply down. There is
no beach, no shoreline and no water's edge anywhere near him.
Everything below him is a long way below him.

Setting: the end of a footpath on a bare mountainside, open sky above,
the ground falling away steeply beyond. Loose wash suggestion only —
thin frozen grass and bare rock in a few dry-brush strokes, no detail
anywhere, the whole drop going down into deep cool wash. A clear
winter night, the air completely still, no wind in his hair or his
clothing.

Composition, stacked from the top of the frame down:
TOP THIRD — bare night sky with a few small cold stars picked out as
tiny reserved flecks; the boy's head and shoulders and the top of the
split rock stand up into it on the left.
MIDDLE THIRD — the boy and the rock at the left, close to the viewer;
beyond them the mountain falling away, dark and empty. His feet are at
the bottom of this third.
BOTTOM THIRD — far below and far off, a burning waterline and its
double in black water.

THE SPLIT ROCK stands at the left of the frame in the near foreground:
one upright standing stone, roughly the height of a grown man, split
top to bottom by a single clean cleft. It is the nearest thing in the
picture and the only solid object in it.

THE BOY IS NINE AND MUST READ NINE:
The top of his head reaches about three quarters of the way up the
split rock.
His head is roughly a sixth of his total height.
Slight, narrow through the shoulders, the limbs thin and with some
length in them — a boy, not a small child and not an adolescent.

THE STEP HAS ALREADY BEEN TAKEN AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE.
He stands past the rock, on the far side of it and downhill of it. His
leading foot is flat on the ground beyond the stone with his weight
already on it. His near arm is stretched back behind him at full reach
and HIS HAND IS STILL FLAT ON THE STONE, the whole palm on it — arm,
hand, stone, one continuous limb, in that order, and it is the last
point of contact. His other arm hangs at his side. Nothing is in
either hand. He carries no lamp, no torch and no light of any kind,
and there is no fire anywhere near him.

HE IS SEEN FROM DIRECTLY BEHIND. The viewer sees the back of his head
— the crown and the nape — the backs of both shoulders, his back and
his heels. No feature of his face is drawn and NO EYE APPEARS ANYWHERE
IN THE PICTURE. Do not turn his head toward the viewer.

He is standing still on level ground, shoulders down, breathing easy.
Not braced, not crouched, not reaching, not hurrying; his arms are not
out for balance and he is not in danger.

FAR DOWN AT THE FOOT OF THE MOUNTAIN, where the land meets the sea, a

THE LIGHTS BELOW ARE THE BRIGHTEST AND MOST FINISHED THING IN THE
PICTURE. The sharpest step from light to dark in the whole image
happens at the edge of the lights and their reflections. The boy, the
rock, the ground and the sky are held dark and loose at quiet
middle-to-low values, no hard edges anywhere in them, nothing
competing for finish. The stars above are cold and clearly separate in
colour and character from the warm lights below.

THE LIGHTS THROW NOTHING ON HIM. They do not reach him. He is a dark
shape lit only by the night sky — no warm rim on his shoulder, no lit
edge anywhere on him.

There is no skin in this picture except the one hand on the stone,
small and held in cool shadow with no warm ochre in it. All the warmth
in the image is far below, in the lamps and the water; none of it
lands on cloth, rock, ground or sky.

Dark hair, cut short and uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick and
layered for a winter night and a little too large for him, in dull
oatmeal and dead olive. Heavy boots WITH THICK WOOL SOCKS PULLED ON
OVER THEM, shapeless and pale, bunched over the boot and the ankle;
both feet are in frame and both are wrapped this way. THE INK CARRIES

mended places and the bunched wool at his feet, ink dominant over wash

No other figure anywhere in the frame, and no boat on the water.

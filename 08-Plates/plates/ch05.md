### PLATE-IMAGE-ONLY: hand-on-the-rock

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick, and the author's named target (s40).* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-9.png` — hair and colouring; his face is not in this picture. Ch. 5 canon: *The split rock stood up out of the dark where it had always stood, at the end of the path, at the end of the world. Kael put his hand on it, the way you put your hand on the goat, on the door, on a thing of the house... Below him, far down, in the corner where the land met the sea, the whole heap of fallen stars was burning — more of them than he had ever seen, doubled and trembling in the black water, the longest night of the year lit end to end... He stepped past the rock.* And, of the walk down: *Wool socks over the boots' voice.*

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch05-hand-on-the-rock.png`, first run.** Named for what it shows. It came back as the paragraph before the crossing — *Kael put his hand on it, the way you put your hand on the goat, on the door, on a thing of the house* — which is its own Ch. 5 beat, so it stands as its own plate rather than as a rejected version of the one below.

**WHAT THE RUN PROVED, and the second one is the one that travels:**

1. **THE WOOL SOCKS RENDERED, from description alone.** Pale wool bunched over dark boots at both ankles, unmistakable, with no reference anywhere in the library for improvised footwear. It was the block's riskiest single object and it came through first try. **Named clothing built out of ordinary materials is describable** — the folder had no evidence for that before.
2. **THE TWO CONTRADICTIONS ARE VISIBLE IN THE RESULT.** The block was audited before the run and found to contain `not reaching` alongside *an arm stretched back at full reach*, and `standing still on level ground` alongside *the ground falling away steeply* and *downhill of it*. **Both fought the picture and both left a mark:** the arm came back out to the side rather than extended rearward, and he stands on a broad level shelf with the drop starting well to his right. **An over-written prompt does not merely dilute — it contradicts, and the renderer splits the difference.** Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Over-writing contradicts.
3. **THE BEAT MOVED ONE PARAGRAPH BACK, AND IT IS STILL PAGE CANON.** What is on the page is *Kael put his hand on it* — the pause at the boundary — rather than *He stepped past the rock*. Both are Ch. 5, so the plate is legal as drawn; but the crossing, which the sheet designed as the subject, is not what a reader will see. See the re-cut below if the step is wanted.
4. **THE SPLIT ROCK CAME BACK AT THE INSTRUCTED HEIGHT.** His head reaches about four fifths of the way up it against the three quarters asked for — man-high, as the sheet fixed it. **It reads as a monolith because it is in the near foreground, not because it is large**, which is worth knowing for Ch. 6 and Ch. 7: the rock can be staged to loom without growing.
5. **THE FACE HELD COMPLETELY.** Crown and nape only, no eye anywhere, no head turned. The grey stays unspent. **A solitary figure is the cheap way to hide a face** — there is nothing in frame for a head to turn toward, which is exactly what `fallen-stars` lacked.
6. **`THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH` AT ITS FULL STRENGTH.** The garment is very nearly all linework — seams, patches and mends dominant over wash across the whole figure, further than any plate has taken it. **This is the finish lever at maximum**, and it is now the set's high-water mark to measure the glossier plates against.
7. **The frame stack was not obeyed literally.** The bottom third was specified as the waterline; what arrived is near-foreground rock and scrub, with the lights at about half height on the right. The picture is better for it — the near ground gives the drop somewhere to begin — but **a three-part stack is a suggestion, not a grid.**

**UNREQUESTED AND KEPT:** a scarf wrapped at the throat. Not in the block, not forbidden, consistent with *thick and layered for a winter night*.

**THE DESIGN NOTES BELOW WERE WRITTEN FOR THE FIRST RUN AND GOVERN BOTH IMAGES** — the moment, the bookend, the framing and the rock are unchanged between them.

**THE DELIBERATE BOOKEND TO `ch01-fallen-stars`** — same lights, same child, same water, and this time nobody turns his chin down. The rhyme is carried at pixel level by reusing that image's proven lamp-and-reflection wording rather than re-inventing it: reserved bare paper, warm tint at the edges only, doubled and displaced in the black water, cold stars above kept separate in colour and character. **For a bookend, proven phrasing is the point.**

**THE STEP IS ALREADY TAKEN, and that is the staging call.** *A hand coming off the stone* is a motion, and a still image cannot carry it. What it can carry is the geometry: the leading foot down on the far side with the weight already on it, the trailing arm stretched back at full reach, one palm still flat on the rock. The law behind him and one point of contact left. It is written as `arm, hand, stone — one continuous limb, in that order`, per §Describe a limb as a chain.

**THE GREY COSTS NOTHING HERE, FOR A NEW REASON.** `fallen-stars` lost *neither face is visible* because there was a second person in the frame for the boy to turn toward, and a renderer that knows what a carried child does turned him. **He is alone in this one** — there is nothing a head could turn to — and the framing is written as a positive: what the viewer sees instead of a face, which is the version that sheet said to write next time.

**THE LIGHTS ARE INSTRUCTED TO THROW NOTHING, which reverses `fallen-stars` on purpose.** That block asked for a thin warm rim along the shoulder and the run gave none: an in-frame warm source renders *as* a source and does not light anything. Rather than ask again for what has already failed, this block states the behaviour as the intent — *he is a dark shape lit only by the night sky, no warm rim on his shoulder* — and the picture is better for it. **The light-from-below rematch is not this plate's job**; the Ch. 8 doorway owns it.

**THIS PLATE FIXES THE SPLIT ROCK'S SIZE FOR THE BOOK — a man-high upright stone with one cleft top to bottom.** Nothing on the page pins it, so whatever is drawn here becomes canon-by-precedent, and **the rock recurs**: Ch. 6 carries the boy home past it, Ch. 7 stands the master on its near side leaning on a stick he does not need. Man-high works in all three, and it earns the scale anchor this picture otherwise lacks — a lone figure has nothing to be measured against, so §Scale between figures gets its third subject.

**ANCHORING COST.** There is still no full figure of Kael below fourteen, so everything under the collar is authored here and anchored to nothing. It will not repeat in the next Part I plate. The portrait buys the medium, the palette and the hair — which is worth having, since `room-of-true-things` was written to test whether the house look survives with no reference at all and has never been run.

**NO PROMPT IS CARRIED FOR THIS IMAGE — author instruction, s41.** `PLATE-IMAGE-ONLY` is what that heading means, and `scripts/build-prompts.sh` generates nothing from it. Only the compressed block below is kept. The 903-word block that produced `ch05-hand-on-the-rock.png` was deleted deliberately and lives in `git show d5b3a5a:08-Plates/plates/ch05.md`. **So this image and the block below did not make each other**, and nothing on this sheet should be read as their pairing.

### PLATE: past-the-split-rock

**RUN — `images/plates/ch05-past-the-split-rock.png`. One ruling outstanding, below.** The compression of the block above, kept as a separate prompt because **the adopted image's prompt of record may not be edited.**

**IT FIXED WHAT IT AIMED AT.** *The ground he stands on is a narrow lip — the drop begins within a stride of that foot* produced exactly that: he stands on a rock shelf that ends at his boots, and the broad level ground of the first run is gone. *The rock stands behind his shoulder, not beside him* moved his body clear of the stone, so the crossing reads. **Replacing a banned pose with a described one worked both times**, which is §Write for an image generator's rule 6 the right way round.

**AND IT BROKE TWO THINGS THE COMPRESSION HAD CALLED REDUNDANT.** This is the finding, and it is worth more than the compression was:

- **THE HEAD TURNED.** The as-run block forbade the face four ways — a positive list (*the crown and nape of his head, the backs of both shoulders, his back, his heels*), then *no feature of his face is drawn*, then *NO EYE APPEARS ANYWHERE IN THE PICTURE*, then *do not turn his head toward the viewer*. The re-cut dropped the second as restatement and **the head came back turned**, cheek and jaw in frame at rear three-quarter. Whether an eye is visible is the author's call; what is not in doubt is that a clause I removed was load-bearing.
- **THE FOREGROUND CAME BACK FINISHED.** The as-run block held the rivals down with two clauses — *no hard edges anywhere in them* AND *nothing competing for finish*. The re-cut kept the first and cut the second, and the rock, the scrub and the cloth all returned lighter, harder-edged and more rendered than the adopted plate. **§Name the winner AND demote the rival is a two-clause mechanism and I collapsed it to one.**

**THE REARWARD REACH IS A TOOL LIMIT, NOT A PROMPT BUG.** Both runs put the arm out to the side rather than stretched back at full extension, and the second had no contradiction left to blame. `not reaching` made it worse in the first run; removing it did not make it right. **A limb extended behind the body is simply not reliable** — worth knowing before another plate leans on one.

**THE RULING OUTSTANDING:** his head is turned far enough to put cheek and jaw in frame. `fallen-stars` hit this exactly and was adopted on the author's reading that no eyes were visible (s40). Same question, same scope: one picture, and `03` does not move either way.

**903 words to 688, every instruction intact.** What came out was restatement, not detail: the altitude stated seven times where §Altitude prescribes three instruments, the hidden face four times where the plate rule asks for two, eleven capitalised blocks where §Write for an image generator allows two or three. Also cut — `no fire anywhere near him`, a negative with nothing pulling toward it, and `not a small child and not an adolescent`, the comparison-to-nothing form §Scale between figures logged as a failure.

**AND TWO CLAUSES ADDED, because the first run diagnosed itself.** Dropping the contradictions is necessary and is not sufficient: the run put him beside the rock on a broad shelf, so the re-cut states the staging positively, which is the register a renderer obeys — *the rock stands behind his shoulder, not beside him*, and *the ground he stands on is a narrow lip; the drop begins within a stride of his leading foot.* **Both replace a banned pose with a described one.**

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy — hair and colouring only.

THE VIEWER IS HIGH ON A MOUNTAIN, a few feet behind a boy and
slightly above him, looking out and steeply down. There is no beach,
no shoreline and no water's edge anywhere near him.

Frame, stacked top to bottom: bare night sky with a few small cold
stars as tiny reserved flecks; the boy and the split rock close to
the viewer on the left, his feet at mid-frame; below and beyond them
the mountain falling away, dark and empty; along the bottom edge a
burning waterline and its double in black water.

Setting: the end of a footpath on a bare mountainside, a clear winter

deep cool wash.

The split rock is one upright standing stone in the near foreground,
roughly the height of a grown man, split top to bottom by a single
clean cleft.

The boy is nine, slight and narrow through the shoulders. The top of
his head reaches about three quarters of the way up the split rock;
his head is roughly a sixth of his total height.

HE HAS ALREADY STEPPED PAST THE ROCK AND HIS WHOLE BODY IS ON THE FAR
SIDE OF IT. The rock stands behind his shoulder, not beside him. His
leading foot is flat on the ground beyond the stone with his weight

within a stride of that foot. HIS NEAR ARM IS STRETCHED BACK BEHIND

his side. Nothing is in either hand and he carries no light. He
stands still, shoulders down, not braced, not crouched, his arms not
out for balance.

HE IS SEEN FROM DIRECTLY BEHIND: the crown and nape of his head, the
backs of both shoulders, his back, his heels. NO EYE APPEARS ANYWHERE
IN THE PICTURE. Do not turn his head toward the viewer.

Far below, where the land meets the sea, a long line of small warm
orange lights burns end to end along the shore from one side of the

the reflections trembling and slightly displaced. The lamps and their
reflections are reserved as bare unpainted paper with a warm tint at
their edges only. No single lamp is bigger than a pinprick; if one is
large enough to read as a lamp, the viewpoint is wrong.

The lights are the brightest and most finished thing in the picture,
and the sharpest step from light to dark happens at their edges. They
throw nothing on him: no warm rim, no lit edge. The boy, the rock,
the ground and the sky are dark and loose at quiet low values, lit
only by the night sky, no hard edges in any of them. The stars above

lights below.

The only skin is the one hand on the stone, and it is cool, with no
warm ochre in it. All the warmth in the picture is in the lamps and
the water.

Dark hair, cut short and uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick and
layered for a winter night and a little too large for him, in dull
oatmeal and dead olive. Heavy boots with thick pale wool socks pulled
on over them, shapeless and bunched over the boot and the ankle, both
feet in frame. The ink carries the cloth — dense scratchy linework in
the seams, the patches, the mended places and the bunched wool at his
feet, ink dominant over wash throughout the clothing.

No other figure anywhere in the frame, and no boat on the water.
```

### PLATE: the-two-pails

**RUN, NOT ADOPTED (s41).** The composition, the two mouths and the reflection-tell all came through — *the ice returns nothing and the water returns the sky* rendered exactly as specified, and the author ruled everything but the colour fine. **Two findings, and the first is general:**

1. **BANNING WARM COLOUR READS AS BANNING COLOUR.** The block said *NO WARM COLOUR ANYWHERE IN THE PICTURE — not in the sky, not on the frost, not in the water, not in the timber*, and then named the palette positively: *cold greys, dull green-greys and bare paper*. **The run came back effectively greyscale.** The positive naming lost to the ban sitting in front of it, and a colour-temperature instruction was read as a saturation instruction. **The fix is to ban the warm SOURCE — sun disc, glow — and never the warm HUE, then require the cold hues by name as a positive with something to measure against.** Written up at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §A colour ban is read as a saturation ban.
2. **It came back photographic rather than loose watercolour and ink** — the most rendered thing in the folder by some distance. Recorded as evidence for the finish question the `README` leaves open, not acted on: the author ruled the picture fine apart from the colour.

**AND THE DIRECTION IT PRODUCED (author, s41): the plate programme wants people and expressions.** An object-only plate is not what the book wants illustrating, however well it renders. See `../README.md`.

*Candidate 2.* **No anchor at all** — there is nobody in it. Ch. 5 canon: *two pails on the bench on a hard-frost night, one pail plain and one brined for the goat, set out together, found together in the iron morning... The plain pail wore its lid of ice. The brine pail moved when he rocked it, dark and obedient and unfrozen* — and the sentence that is the school: *The more salt the water carries, the deeper the cold must bite to hold it.*

**THE PICTURE THAT KILLS THE PROMISE.** Two objects and one difference, and the difference is the fact that walks over two nights later and puts its hand on the oldest thing in him. No figure, because he is not in the room when it lands.

**THE FIRST PLATE IN THE FOLDER WITH NEITHER A FACE NOR A WARM SOURCE.** Every one so far has had one or the other — a face to finish, or a fire, a lamp, a town. This one is grey on grey and has to hold on value alone. **That is the reason to run it**, and it also puts a limit under the open finish question: `THE INK CARRIES THE CLOTH` is a *cloth* lever, and there is no cloth in this picture, so whatever settles the set's consistency cannot be that clause by itself.

**THE REFLECTION IS THE TELL, and it is the whole design.** *Moved when he rocked it* is motion and a still image cannot carry it. What it can carry is what each mouth does with the sky: **the ice returns nothing and the water returns a broken sky.** Frozen and unfrozen, stated as an optical fact rather than as an adjective.

**A GREY DAWN PULLS HARD TOWARD A GOLD ONE**, so the block bans the sun disc, the glow and the warm hue separately — three channels, per §Over-writing contradicts. For the same reason the two-clause demotion is kept whole here (*no hard edges* AND *nothing competing for finish*); cutting the second is what let the last run's foreground come back rendered.

```
No reference image.

Setting: a rough plank bench against the outside wall of a small
stone mountain house on a hard-frost morning, as loose wash
suggestion — the wall in cold grey wash behind it, frozen ground
below, a pale flat band of sea far below and beyond, no detail
anywhere. Thick white rime on the bench, the ground and the wall.

THE VIEWER LOOKS DOWN INTO THE MOUTHS OF BOTH PAILS. A close view of
the bench top from above and slightly to one side, high enough that
the inside of each pail is fully visible. The two pails fill most of
the frame.

TWO WOODEN PAILS STAND SIDE BY SIDE ON THE BENCH, TOUCHING, AND THEY
ARE THE SAME PAIL — same size, same staves, same iron hoops, same
handle, same wear. Nothing tells them apart except what is in the
mouth, AND THAT DIFFERENCE IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. The left pail
is capped by one flat disc of ice: opaque, matte, pale, reflecting
nothing at all, a lid rather than a surface. The right pail is open
dark water, unfrozen, the surface just disturbed and carrying a
broken reflection of the grey sky across it. One mouth returns
nothing and the other returns the sky.

THE LIGHT COMES UP FLAT AND GREY OFF THE SEA, low and level. There is
no sun disc, no glow, and NO WARM COLOUR ANYWHERE IN THE PICTURE —
not in the sky, not on the frost, not in the water, not in the
timber. There is no skin in this image. It is cold greys, dull
green-greys and bare paper throughout.

The two mouths are the only finished things and carry the sharpest
step from light to dark — the ice pale and hard-edged against dark
timber, the water black. The bench, the wall, the ground and the
sides of the pails are held at quiet middle values, loosely washed,
no hard edges, nothing competing for finish.

Frost is bare unpainted paper: the rime on the bench boards, on the
iron hoops, along the tops of the staves.

Nobody is in this picture — no figure, no hand, no arm, no sleeve at
the frame's edge, nobody behind the bench. There is no third pail and
no other vessel anywhere in it.
```

### PLATE: the-fact-lands

*Author-requested (s41): close up, a face, an expression.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`. Ch. 5 canon: *It was two nights later, in the loft, doing his numbers, that the fact stood up and walked over to the oldest thing in the waiting part of him and put its hand on it... He lay very still... at the bottom of it was the lazy place, the one load-bearing loop, and it was this: the sea can never freeze. She always knew it. / The promise was not a promise. It was a door painted on a wall. / He did not cry. He noticed that, distantly, the way he noticed the wind change. What he felt was closer to the morning with the stone in the gate — the mountain-sized weight of a thing, and, arriving underneath it, unwanted, unstoppable, the cold beginning of leverage.*

**THE FIRST PLATE IN THE BOOK TO SPEND THE GREY** (author ruling, s41 — `../README.md`). It is rendered at full strength here, with the two-clause mechanism the portraits use: the hair demoted to strands and open washes, the sharpest step from light to dark sited inside the iris. **The chapter earns it** — this is the moment his mother's schools turn into an instrument he uses against her, and the eye that belongs to no one in the house is the right thing to be the lightest object in the frame.

**THE REFERENCE WILL FIGHT THIS PICTURE, and the block says so first.** `kael-9` was cut deliberately as *alert and direct, a boy in the middle of asking something*, and its own note reserved a *withheld, still variant* for a plate that ever needed one. **This is that plate, and it is the exact inverse of the face it is anchored on.** The override is stated in the reference line rather than left to the expression paragraph, because a face reference argues continuously and a single contradicting clause downstream will lose.

**LIGHT FROM BELOW, ARRIVING EARLIER THAN PLANNED.** The `README` holds the Ch. 8 doorway as the rematch that gates Aurelian's candle; this picture needs the same thing and needs it for its own reasons — a banked fire in the room below, reaching him over the edge of the loft floor, catching the underside of the jaw and nothing else. **If it works here, the candle is unblocked without waiting for the doorway.**

**THE INHERITED COLOUR PARAGRAPH IS CORRECT HERE, FOR THE FIRST TIME IN THE FOLDER.** *The warmth belongs to the skin alone, not the paper or the cloth* was written for portraits and every plate so far has had to override it. In this one the only warm thing in the frame **is** skin. Nothing to override.

**RUN, NOT ADOPTED (s41).**

**1. LIGHT FROM BELOW IS PROVEN.** A faint warm source entering low, catching the underside of the jaw and the neck, with the beam and the roof above going dark. `ch01-fallen-stars` had established only that an in-frame source throws nothing; **this establishes that an out-of-frame source below the subject lights a face properly.** The `README` held the Ch. 8 doorway as the rematch that gates **Aurelian's candle in Ch. 9** — that gate is open, and the candle no longer waits on the doorway.

**2. THE GREY RENDERED, AND DRIFTED BLUE.** It is unmistakably the lightest thing in the face and the two-clause mechanism carried it — but it came back a pale blue-grey where `03` and the brief both say **near-white neutral grey, cool and COLOURLESS, winning on value and never on saturation.** The block said colourless and got a hue anyway. **A named colour word pulls its own saturation with it**; the fix is to site it against a landmark — *no more colour in the iris than in the whites around it* — rather than to say colourless again.

**3. THE ARCHETYPE BAN WORKED AND THE SUBSTITUTION DID NOT, WHICH IS THE FINDING.** *Do not draw a sad child lying awake* held completely: there is no sadness in it. What arrived instead is **wonder** — eyes wide, lips parted, a face looking up at something. Mouth open where the block said *mouth closed and relaxed*; eyes wide where nothing asked for wide. **Banning one archetype delivers its neighbour.** A lit face looking upward at night means either grief or awe, and closing one door opened the other.

**4. AND THE REASON IT LOST IS THE REFERENCE, WHICH THE OVERRIDE FAILED TO STOP.** `kael-9` is cut as *alert and direct... brows slightly raised, eyes wide and fully open... a boy in the middle of asking something* — and that is exactly the face that arrived. The override was placed in the reference line, first, deliberately, precisely to pre-empt this, **and it still lost.** This is the second time a reference has beaten an explicit instruction about the face (`ch01-fallen-stars`, the turned head), and the first where the instruction was given priority position. **In a close crop, a face reference argues about expression continuously and more words do not beat it. The fix is a second reference, not a better sentence** — and the portrait system reserved exactly that: *a withheld, still variant stays available as a deliberate second file if a plate ever needs one.* It is built at `../portrait-prompt-system.md` §Kael at nine — the withheld variant.

**FOR THE AUTHOR:** the picture is off the beat rather than badly made — it is a boy at the start of wondering, and the chapter's beat is the end of a calculation. Unlike `hand-on-the-rock`, there is no adjacent Ch. 5 moment it lands on instead.

**THE ARCHETYPE IS THE RISK, NOT THE AFFECT** (`../portrait-prompt-system.md` §Known behaviours). *Not sad* on a child lying awake in the dark at night leaves the renderer with a sad child anyway, because that is what the staging means everywhere else. So the block bans the picture by name — *do not draw a sad child lying awake* — and replaces it with the positive: *draw a child who has finished a calculation.*

# Ch. 13 — The Coat · plate sheet

**Both plates composed at s45 and neither has run.** Kael is eleven turning twelve across this chapter (`07/timeline`: no pin, the birthday passes unmarked) and the burns are in its opening spring; Elarine is twelve at the autumn hall. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` and `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`.

**THE PAIR SPLITS THE CHAPTER'S TWO PRICES.** What the dawn school costs, written on a body with nobody in the frame; and what the hall costs, written on the face of somebody who did not choose to pay it. The chapter is titled for a garment nobody may draw (`../canon-brief.md`), so both plates take the price instead of the thing.

**ANCHORING COST.** Both references are head-and-shoulders. The receipts is a hand and a forearm, so it is anchored on skin alone and its anchor does almost nothing — the same position `ch12/the-hole-through-the-tree` was in. The spots of colour is a close face and its anchor is doing all of the work.

**BOTH BLOCKS CARRY THE CORRECTED AGE INSTRUMENT** (§A close-up plate and the age line, as walked back at s44): the rung **plus a ratio the framing can actually perform** — both terms in the crop, at the same depth, near enough to read against each other. Here that is the wooden sword's grip beside the hand, and the cup beside the hand. Both are the white stitch's geometry, which is the one that worked, and neither is furniture's hand-across-a-hall, which is the one that did not.

**AND THE RECEIPTS STATES NO AGE NUMBER AT ALL.** §Damage records that stated ages plus injury cause refusals; the crop has no face, so the eye-line rung has nothing to sit in; and the ratio carries the whole job on its own.

**CANDIDATE 3 IS DEFERRED, AND THE REASON IS A MISSING FILE.** Oss's face after the laugh crests is the chapter's most human beat and there is no `images/portraits/oss.png`. A plate would have to author his face out of `03/oss` — and `REFERENCES SHOW THE PERSON. PLATES SHOW THE MOMENT.` says a plate is not where a person's face gets decided; whatever it drew would become his reference by default. **The work that unblocks it is a portrait, not a plate**, and it is named here so the next session does not re-propose the plate first. He is in the second plate as a loose dark shape at the far end of a hall with no features drawn, which is the most he can legally be until that portrait exists.

**AND THE SAME BLOCK FALLS ON THE JUDGE.** `candidates.md`'s alternate — Halden at the trough, talking to the yard and not to Kael — is worth planting and has no reference either. **Aurelian's apology is the one alternate that is fully anchored** (`aurelian-14`, `elarine-14`, `kael-14`, and `ch09/aurelians-candle` proves the face): it is passed over here because it is a wide three-figure staging whose subject is a measured distance, and the folder's standing preference is the close crop on a face doing something. It stays a candidate.

### PLATE: the-receipts

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 13 canon, the first burst: *a white bite up the forearm that dropped the waster out of his hand and dropped him after it, and he sat in the frost with the arm ringing like struck iron.* And the season's tally, which is the picture: *He was burned three hundred and twenty times that spring. He counted, because counting was his. Little white-pink speckles came up on the wrist and the back of the hand, the Art writing its own receipts.*

**THE PICTURE IS ONE OF THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY MORNINGS, NOT THE FIRST ONE.** That is what makes it legal: on the first morning there were no speckles yet, and by the middle of that spring a dropped waster and a marked wrist are the same morning. Both halves are Ch. 13's own page and nothing is composed across chapters.

**IT IS FACELESS, AND THAT IS PRICED RATHER THAN ARGUED.** `../README.md` prefers the candidate with a face in it and s43 ruled the preference is not a gate. The chapter's whole argument is that the price is paid where nobody looks and never shows above the collar — **a crop that stops at the elbow is that sentence.** The second plate carries the faces.

**AND IT DOES NOT MAKE THE RESERVE ITS SUBJECT.** `ch11/the-white-stitch` and `ch12/the-hole-through-the-tree` both put bare paper at the centre of the frame and both came back correct; a third would be a habit rather than a choice. **Here the frost reserves and the marks are drawn** — each one an ink ring, not a gap in the wash — so the two whites in the picture are made by different means and cannot compete.

**THE RIGHT HAND, DELIBERATELY.** `../canon-brief.md` puts the Ch. 6 blade scar on the **left** forearm, and an image cannot decline to answer: a left-arm crop would have to draw a second family of marks beside the receipts and split the subject. The page does not say which hand held the waster, so the framing answers.

**THE ARM IS BARE BY WHERE THE CLOTH STOPS, NOT BY WHERE THE SLEEVE ENDS.** That is the fault that lost the wrists on `ch10/the-honey-and-the-bread` and it is the one thing in this block that could put the subject under a cuff.

**THE VARIANT THAT IS NOT THE BLOCK: HIS FATHER'S BOOTS.** *His father stood over him and did not help him up* is the hardest line in the chapter and it would go in the frame as two boots a stride off, unmoving, with no face. **Two reasons it is held back rather than run.** It turns a hand plate into a staging problem with an unanchored adult below the knee — and §Some moments are undrawable records the folder's one refusal, which fell on a child in restraint in a yard. A downed child with marks on him and a grown man standing over him is near enough to that neighbourhood that it is not what a first run should be spent on. **Available on request; the composition is one sentence.**

```
Image 1: kael-14.png — portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the flat ground of a mountain smallholding before sunrise on
a cold spring morning, as loose wash suggestion — frozen ground under
frost, the yard falling away into cool wash a stride behind, the far
side of it lost. Cold flat half-light before sunrise, no sun and no
shadow with an edge anywhere, and it is the only light in the
picture.

Composition: close on one hand and one forearm, seen from above and a
little to the side. The picture is cropped at the elbow, so no head,
no face and no other part of him is in the frame. The frame holds
four things only: the hand, the forearm, a wooden sword and the
frost.

WHAT THE HAND IS DOING: his right hand is flat on the frozen ground,
palm down, fingers spread and taking his weight, the whole outside of
the hand and the wrist turned up toward the viewer. The forearm runs from it
up out of the top of the frame — sword, frost, hand, wrist, forearm,
one continuous limb in that order. His left hand is out of frame.
Nothing is held.

THE MARKS ARE THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Across the back of the hand
and up the wrist runs a scatter of small round marks, pale pink and
white, flat to the skin and healed — thirty or more of them, none
larger than a grain of barley, spread from the knuckles to a hand's
width up the forearm. Each one is drawn with the ink as a small ring,
not left to the grain of the paper.

The wooden sword lies where it fell: one plain wooden practice sword
on the frost beside the hand, at the same distance from the viewer,
its grip nearest the hand and a hand's width clear of it, touching
nothing and held by nobody.

THE HAND IS A CHILD'S, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS THE GRIP: the round
grip of the wooden sword lying beside it is as long as his whole hand
from the heel of the palm to the fingertips. A small hand, short in
the fingers, the knuckles not yet come up.

THE ARM IS BARE TO ABOVE THE ELBOW: no cuff, no sleeve edge and no
cloth of any kind on the hand, the wrist or the forearm anywhere in
the frame. The frost is broken and smeared under the hand where it
went down, and stands unbroken everywhere else.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the skin is the only
warm colour anywhere, and the marks on it are pinker than the skin
around them. The frost, the ground, the wooden sword and everything
behind them are cool grey and grey-blue with no brown in them.

THE BACK OF THE HAND AND THE MARKS ON IT ARE THE MOST FINISHED THING
IN THE PICTURE and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The
frost is the brightest thing in the picture and is reserved as bare
unpainted paper. The wooden sword, the ground and the yard behind stay at quiet
middle values with soft edges and no detail, and nothing back there
competes for finish. The ink linework stays the strongest element
throughout and does not blur into the wash.
```

### PLATE: the-spots-of-colour

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`. Ch. 13 canon: *It was a crop of laughter, many throats, one animal* — and, at the far side of it: *At the far table Elarine sat straight as a mast with spots of color high on her cheeks, and the laughter was running over her too — Sedrane and the goat boy.* The teller is on the page in the same paragraph: *up on the bench-end with his hands drawing, the middle tables turned to him.*

**THIS IS ONE OF TWO CHAPTERS WHERE THE TELL IS LEGAL AND IT IS ALREADY ASSIGNED HERE.** `../canon-brief.md` reserves *spots of colour high on her cheeks* for Ch. 13 and Ch. 17 and forbids it in the canonical portrait; `candidates.md` spends it at Ch. 13 rather than Ch. 17 on the ground that here she is being spent in public **without having chosen it**, and records the non-choice so it is not re-proposed at Ch. 17. `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` deliberately wrote no cheek negation at all so that nothing would be read into that run. **This is the payout.**

**IT RESTAGES `ch12-furniture` ON PURPOSE, AND THAT IS THE ARGUMENT FOR IT.** Same hall, same midday, same laughter, one figure near and large with the room falling away behind — **and the opposite face.** Two chapters ago the boy nobody was looking at kept his face at the room's temperature; here the girl everybody is laughing at keeps everything still except the one thing she cannot. Set against each other they are a pair; the difference is written into the lighting as well as the face — furniture takes flat light from a high window at its back, this one takes it across from the side, which also gives the demotion somewhere to sit (§Name the winner, s44).

**THE COLOUR IS THE WINNER AND IT WINS ON HUE, NOT ON VALUE.** Same shape as Kael's grey, which cannot win on saturation and takes the lightest value instead. Here the two patches are the only warm colour in the entire picture and her face is the most finished thing in it — **staged rather than merely prioritised**, since §Name the winner has two runs where a small tell lost anyway.

**HER GAZE IS NAMED BECAUSE THE FAN'S WAS NOT.** `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` emptied its frame, gave its subject's eyes nowhere to go, and the eyes went to the viewer and turned a plate into a portrait. The page does not say where she is looking, so the plate answers with framing: straight ahead, level, down the length of the table at nothing, which is a girl declining to turn round.

**AND HER EYE COLOUR IS NOT IN THE BLOCK.** `../canon-brief.md`: the page has never said what colour her eyes are and must not; *a portrait may show them; a plate may not name them.* The reference carries them anyway (§What the reference carries). Noted, not re-raised: `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain`'s block does name them and that plate is adopted and stands.

**THE TELLER IS IN THE FRAME AND HAS NO FACE.** One child standing up on a bench at the far end, small, a loose dark shape with no features — which is the whole of what a plate may draw of Oss until `images/portraits/oss.png` exists.

```
Image 1: elarine-14.png — portrait reference of the girl.

Setting: the long hall of a temple at midday in autumn, as loose wash
suggestion — high windows down one wall, long plank tables and
benches running away behind her into quiet wash, the far end of it
going dark. Flat cold daylight coming across the hall from the
windows on her left, and it is the only light in the picture.

Composition: chest-up and close. One girl sits at a plank table in
the near foreground, nearest the viewer and much the largest figure
in the frame, seen in three-quarter view, bolt upright, her shoulders
square and level. The near edge of the tabletop crosses the bottom of
the frame and one plain wooden cup stands on it beside her hand.
Behind her and much further off, at the middle tables, two children
turned away up the hall, seen from behind, their heads back and their
mouths open; and beyond them, small and at the far end of the hall,
one child standing up on a bench with both arms out.

WHAT EACH HAND IS DOING: her right hand rests on the boards beside
the cup, loosely closed and still. Her left hand is in her lap and
out of frame. Nothing is held in either hand.

WHERE EVERY FACE IS LOOKING: hers is the only face in the picture,
and she is looking straight ahead, level, down the length of the
table at nothing, and not at the viewer.

THE COLOUR ON HER CHEEKS IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. High on each
cheekbone, under the eye, one patch of warm pink with a soft edge,
about the size of her own thumbprint. The rest of her face and her
throat stay pale and cool with no other colour in them.

HER FACE GIVES NOTHING BACK. Completely still, mouth closed and
level, the brows unmoved, the eyes steady, the shoulders not moved at
all. NOT embarrassed, NOT ashamed, NOT angry — and equally NOT cold,
NOT haughty, NOT bored.

SHE IS TWELVE AND SMALL FOR IT, AND THE MEASUREMENT IS THE CUP: the
eyes sit a little below the vertical midpoint of the head, nearer
that midpoint than a young child's and still below it. Her hand on
the boards is a child's, and the wooden cup standing beside it is as
tall as her hand is long.

Ink-dark hair, a flat neutral black with no blue in it, drawn back
severely and bound tight at the nape, nothing loose anywhere. Pale
indoor skin. Plain dark close-fitting clothing of very good quality,
exactly made and buttoned high at the throat. No ornament, no metal
and no badge anywhere on her.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the two patches of
colour on her cheekbones are the only warm colour in the whole
picture. Her skin is pale and cool, and the boards, the cup, the
stone, the benches, the air and all the clothing in the hall are cool
grey and grey-blue with no brown in them.

HER FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING IN THE PICTURE and carries the
sharpest step from light to dark, along the lit edge of it where the
window light comes across. The two children behind her are looser and
softer-edged with no detail drawn in them, the standing child beyond
them is a loose dark shape with no features at all, and the
rest of the hall is summary shapes at the edges with nothing drawn in
them. The daylight from the high windows and the light lying along
the scrubbed boards are reserved as bare unpainted paper. The ink
linework stays the strongest element throughout and does not blur
into the wash.
```

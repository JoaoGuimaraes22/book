# Ch. 13 — The Coat · plate sheet

**Composed at s45. `the-receipts` is adopted on its first run; `the-spots-of-colour` has not run.** Kael is eleven turning twelve across this chapter (`07/timeline`: no pin, the birthday passes unmarked) and the burns are in its opening spring; Elarine is twelve at the autumn hall. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` and `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`.

**THE PAIR SPLITS THE CHAPTER'S TWO PRICES.** What the dawn school costs, written on a body and read off the face that keeps paying it; and what the hall costs, written on the face of somebody who did not choose to pay it. **Both are chest-up closes on one face** (`../README.md`, s45). The chapter is titled for a garment nobody may draw (`../canon-brief.md`), so both plates take the price instead of the thing.

**ANCHORING COST.** Both references are head-and-shoulders and both plates are chest-up closes, so both anchors are doing the whole of their work and neither picture authors much below a collar. **This is the first Ch. 13 pair to be able to say that** — the receipts was composed as a hand and a forearm, anchored on skin alone, and the author sent it back (`../README.md`, s45).

**BOTH BLOCKS CARRY THE CORRECTED AGE INSTRUMENT** (§A close-up plate and the age line, as walked back at s44): the rung **plus a ratio the framing can actually perform** — both terms in the crop, at the same depth, near enough to read against each other. Here that is his own hand held up below his own chin, and her hand on the boards beside a cup. Both are the white stitch's geometry, which is the one that worked, and neither is furniture's hand-across-a-hall, which is the one that did not.

**AND THE RECEIPTS STATES NO AGE NUMBER, WHICH THE OTHER BLOCK DOES.** §Damage records that stated ages plus injury cause refusals, and the receipts has marks on a child in it. The rung and the ratio both work without a number; the number is the one part of the instrument that is optional.

**CANDIDATE 3 IS DEFERRED, AND THE REASON IS A MISSING FILE.** Oss's face after the laugh crests is the chapter's most human beat and there is no `images/portraits/oss.png`. A plate would have to author his face out of `03/oss` — and `REFERENCES SHOW THE PERSON. PLATES SHOW THE MOMENT.` says a plate is not where a person's face gets decided; whatever it drew would become his reference by default. **The work that unblocks it is a portrait, not a plate**, and it is named here so the next session does not re-propose the plate first. He is in the second plate as a loose dark shape at the far end of a hall with no features drawn, which is the most he can legally be until that portrait exists.

**AND THE SAME BLOCK FALLS ON THE JUDGE.** `candidates.md`'s alternate — Halden at the trough, talking to the yard and not to Kael — is worth planting and has no reference either. **Aurelian's apology is the one alternate that is fully anchored** (`aurelian-14`, `elarine-14`, `kael-14`, and `ch09/aurelians-candle` proves the face): it is passed over here because it is a wide three-figure staging whose subject is a measured distance, and the folder's standing preference is the close crop on a face doing something. It stays a candidate.

### PLATE: the-receipts

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 13 canon, the first burst: *a white bite up the forearm that dropped the waster out of his hand and dropped him after it, and he sat in the frost with the arm ringing like struck iron.* And the season's tally, which is the picture: *He was burned three hundred and twenty times that spring. He counted, because counting was his. Little white-pink speckles came up on the wrist and the back of the hand, the Art writing its own receipts.*

**THE PICTURE IS ONE OF THREE HUNDRED AND TWENTY MORNINGS, NOT THE FIRST ONE.** That is what makes it legal: on the first morning there were no speckles yet, and by the middle of that spring a dropped waster and a marked wrist are the same morning. Both halves are Ch. 13's own page and nothing is composed across chapters.

**RECOMPOSED AT s45, BEFORE IT RAN.** The first version of this block was a hand and a forearm cropped at the elbow, and the author ruled on the set rather than on the picture: *we do lots of close ups of hands... chest up shots, creative expressions, i like better* — and specified the replacement, *same scene of the receipts, but we can actually see more. Hurt but focused expression.* **The moment is unchanged. The crop widened until the face was in it**, and the hand-and-forearm version is gone rather than kept as a variant, because it was not rejected for being wrong.

**THE TELL IS STACKED UNDER THE FACE, WHICH IS THE WHOLE COMPOSITION.** He holds the burned forearm up hard against his own chest with the other hand closed around it below the elbow — so the marked wrist sits **directly below his chin, at the same depth, at the size of a hand in frame.** That answers both halves of §Name the winner at once: its limit says a tell smaller than a hand in frame cannot carry finish however it is prioritised, and its s44 correction says to stage the small thing rather than shout at it, as `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` did with four hands on a table. **Face and marks are named as the two most finished things and they are touching.**

**AND THE POSE IS WHY THE AGE INSTRUMENT CAN BE THE FACE ITSELF.** With the hand held under the chin, the ratio is his hand against his own head — **a child's hand is plainly shorter than his own face, and a grown hand is about the same length as it.** Both terms in the crop, same depth, same size, impossible to stage out of reach. **This is new and untested**, and it is written down as an instrument a chest-up can always perform, not as a rule: the folder has one run's evidence for the copper bar and none at all for this.

**HIS EYES GO UP AND OUT OF FRAME, AND THAT IS THE ONE REAL COST.** `ch10/the-honey-and-the-bread`, `ch11/the-white-stitch` and `ch12/furniture` all solved Kael's face by putting his eyes down on an object, and it worked all three times because **a reference holds no downcast version of itself to supply** (s43). **A fourth would be a habit, and this face has somewhere better to look:** up and to his left, past the corner of the frame, at the man standing over him who does not help him up. The page puts his father there and speaking. **So the gaze is named — the fan proved an emptied frame sends the eyes to the viewer — and the man is banned by name in the same block, because naming him in the gaze line is exactly the pull §Negative inventory says a negation has to have.**

**THE EXPRESSION IS THE CHAPTER'S, ONE STEP BACK FROM ITS STRONGEST WORDING.** *his eyes streaming* belongs to the first burst; this is a morning in the middle of that spring, so the block asks for eyes open and wet with the attention already back on, and not for a face in the act of crying. **The negations earn their place on the pull test:** a child, cold, and wet eyes is a hard pull toward weeping, and the picture is a boy who has stopped noticing that part.

**THE FRESH MARK IS ONE CLAUSE AND NO TISSUE.** §Damage: name the mark and where it is, do not describe what it is made of. One new mark at the wrist, red where the others are pale, the same size as the others — which is the page's own cruelty (*The burn was small. It was the size of the thread*) and the only red in the picture.

**THREE SMALL THINGS THE FIRST VERSION GOT RIGHT AND THIS ONE KEEPS.** The **right** hand, because `../canon-brief.md` puts the Ch. 6 blade scar on the left forearm and an image cannot decline to answer — a left-arm crop would have to draw a second family of marks beside the receipts and split the subject, and the page does not say which hand held the waster, so the framing answers. The **arm is bare by where the cloth stops**, not by where a sleeve ends, which is the fault that lost the wrists on `ch10/the-honey-and-the-bread` and is the one clause here that could put the subject under a cuff. And **the reserve is not the subject**: `ch11/the-white-stitch` and `ch12/the-hole-through-the-tree` both centred bare paper and a third would be a habit, so here the frost and his breath reserve while the marks are drawn as ink rings — the two whites in the picture are made by different means and cannot compete.

**THE TWO-FIGURE VERSION STAYS AVAILABLE AND IS NOT THIS BLOCK.** Drawing the father — boots, or the whole standing man — turns a plate about what the school costs into a plate about the pair of them, and it puts a downed child and a grown man standing over him in one frame, which is near enough to §Some moments are undrawable's one refusal that it is not what a first run should be spent on. **The gaze buys most of him for nothing.**

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch13-the-receipts.png`, first run (author, s45: *love it*).**

**THE STACK IS THE FINDING AND IT LANDED WHOLE.** The face and the marked wrist are in one column, the wrist directly under the chin, both at the size the block asked for, and **they are the two most finished things in the picture** — the marks are the second thing the eye goes to and nothing had to be shouted at. §Name the winner's limit says a tell smaller than a hand in frame cannot carry finish however it is prioritised, and its own answer is *the fix is the crop.* **This is that answer run as a composition rather than quoted as a caution.** The pose chain held exactly: forearm up against the chest, the left hand closed around it below the elbow, the fingers half-curled and slack, nothing held.

**AND THE UP-GAZE WORKED, WHICH GENERALISES THE s43 FINDING.** He is looking up and out past the top corner at somebody who is not in the picture, and **`kael-14`'s flat-and-level-at-the-viewer gaze did not arrive.** The rule was written as *a reference holds no downcast version of itself to supply* off three runs that all moved the eyes DOWN. This one moved them up and away and the reference lost the same way. **So the mechanism is not downcast — it is any head position the reference does not hold**, and the folder now has a fourth solve for Kael's face that is not the same solve as the other three.

**THE DEMOTION HELD IN FLAT LIGHT, AND THAT REOPENS THE s44 HYPOTHESIS IN A USEFUL DIRECTION.** `ch10/the-waterer` failed to demote a drystone wall in flat open daylight and the conclusion was **a rival needs somewhere to go**; `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` confirmed it in a dark yard. **This picture is flat, sourceless, pre-dawn light with no dark anywhere in it and the trees, the fence and the far ground all went soft and quiet.** The difference is the mist: depth haze gave the background somewhere to recede into without a single dark value. **A rival needs somewhere to go and it does not have to be darkness.** One run, directional, and it does not disturb either earlier reading.

**MISS: HE READS FOURTEEN OR FIFTEEN, AND THE NEW INSTRUMENT IS THE ONE THAT FAILED.** The hand-against-face ratio was written into the block, was perfectly stageable, and **came back at the adult proportion the block wrote against** — his hand reads about as long as his own face. So the staging fix from s44 was satisfied and the number still did not land.

**AND THE PATTERN ACROSS FOUR RUNS NOW POINTS SOMEWHERE SPECIFIC.** The one ratio that ever worked — `ch11/the-white-stitch`, eleven or twelve — measured a hand against **a copper bar**. Every failure since has measured a piece of the subject against another piece of the subject: a hand against a hand across a hall, a flat hand against his mother's flat hand, and now a hand against his own face. **An object has a size the renderer cannot argue with; a body can be scaled entire and satisfy the ratio at any age.** Written up as an open question at `../scene-prompt-system.md` §A close-up plate and the age line, and deliberately not promoted to a rule: it is one clean instance and three suggestive ones.

**ADOPTED WITH THE AGE AS DRAWN, WHICH IS THE SIXTH TIME.** `../README.md` carries the standing bias — one consistent face across the book, over accuracy in any single picture — and this run joins the candle, the roller, `vask.png`, the fan and `neris.png`.

**MISS, AND IT IS THIS FOLDER'S ERROR RATHER THAN THE RENDERER'S: THE MARKS RUN TOO FAR UP THE ARM.** `03/kael` sites them **wrist to second knuckle**. The block asked for *from the knuckles to a hand's width up the forearm*, which already exceeded that, and the run took them most of the way to the elbow. **The renderer drew what it was told.** `../canon-brief.md` did not carry the receipts at all, which is exactly the blind spot that file exists to close, and it has been repaired from `03` in the same session. The picture stands as adopted; nothing here asks for a re-run.

**AND THEY CAME BACK RAISED WHERE THE BLOCK SAID FLAT.** *Flat to the skin and healed* lost to *drawn with the ink as a small ring* — a ring reads as a rim and a rim reads as a dome, so they sit up off the skin and catch light. **If this is ever re-run, the relief has to be named and not only the colour**: no shadow under any of them, no rim, drawn as flat dots of colour. Same shape as every noun failure in §Name a thing by construction — the more concrete instruction won.

**SMALLER THINGS, NONE WORTH A RE-RUN.** The breath in the cold air did not arrive. The clothing came back warm brown-grey where the block named cool grey-blue with no brown — **the same drift as `ch08/the-one-who-did-not-laugh`, which the author kept and which is the reason colour is not a rule in this folder.** The wet eyes did not arrive either: the *hurt* half of hurt-but-focused is carried by the arm, the shoulders and the held breath rather than by the eyes, and the face reads level and watchful. The speckling on the face is the medium's granulation, texture and not canon (author ruling, s34).

### PLATE: the-spots-of-colour

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/elarine-14.png`. Ch. 13 canon: *It was a crop of laughter, many throats, one animal* — and, at the far side of it: *At the far table Elarine sat straight as a mast with spots of color high on her cheeks, and the laughter was running over her too — Sedrane and the goat boy.* The teller is on the page in the same paragraph: *up on the bench-end with his hands drawing, the middle tables turned to him.*

**THIS IS ONE OF TWO CHAPTERS WHERE THE TELL IS LEGAL AND IT IS ALREADY ASSIGNED HERE.** `../canon-brief.md` reserves *spots of colour high on her cheeks* for Ch. 13 and Ch. 17 and forbids it in the canonical portrait; `candidates.md` spends it at Ch. 13 rather than Ch. 17 on the ground that here she is being spent in public **without having chosen it**, and records the non-choice so it is not re-proposed at Ch. 17. `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` deliberately wrote no cheek negation at all so that nothing would be read into that run. **This is the payout.**

**IT RESTAGES `ch12-furniture` ON PURPOSE, AND THAT IS THE ARGUMENT FOR IT.** Same hall, same midday, same laughter, one figure near and large with the room falling away behind — **and the opposite face.** Two chapters ago the boy nobody was looking at kept his face at the room's temperature; here the girl everybody is laughing at keeps everything still except the one thing she cannot. Set against each other they are a pair; the difference is written into the lighting as well as the face — furniture takes flat light from a high window at its back, this one takes it across from the side, which also gives the demotion somewhere to sit (§Name the winner, s44).

**THE COLOUR IS THE WINNER AND IT WINS ON HUE, NOT ON VALUE.** Same shape as Kael's grey, which cannot win on saturation and takes the lightest value instead. Here the two patches are the only warm colour in the entire picture and her face is the most finished thing in it — **staged rather than merely prioritised**, since §Name the winner has two runs where a small tell lost anyway.

**HER GAZE IS NAMED BECAUSE THE FAN'S WAS NOT.** `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain` emptied its frame, gave its subject's eyes nowhere to go, and the eyes went to the viewer and turned a plate into a portrait. The page does not say where she is looking, so the plate answers with framing: straight ahead, level, down the length of the table at nothing, which is a girl declining to turn round.

**AND HER EYE COLOUR IS NOT IN THE BLOCK.** `../canon-brief.md`: the page has never said what colour her eyes are and must not; *a portrait may show them; a plate may not name them.* The reference carries them anyway (§What the reference carries). Noted, not re-raised: `ch11/the-fan-in-the-rain`'s block does name them and that plate is adopted and stands.

**THE TELLER IS IN THE FRAME AND HAS NO FACE.** One child standing up on a bench at the far end, small, a loose dark shape with no features — which is the whole of what a plate may draw of Oss until `images/portraits/oss.png` exists.

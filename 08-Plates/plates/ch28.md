# Ch. 28 — Turning Point · plate sheet

**Composed at s52 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen; Valeria is thirteen. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` + `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png` on the first, `images/portraits/valeria-14.png` + `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png` on the second. **The author was offered eight candidates and handed the pick back** (s52: *you choose*), so the two ★ entries on `candidates.md` stand as proposed.

**THE PAIR IS ONE MORNING FROM BOTH SIDES** — the inside of a killing and the outside of one. He is a boy standing in a lane with his hand out in front of him and nothing in it; she is a girl standing perfectly still while the road walks away from her in pieces. **Neither picture has a stroke in it**, and neither has a second figure in it.

**THE CHAPTER IS THE WORST MATERIAL IN THE BOOK AND THE INSTRUMENT IS Ch. 27's:** you do not draw the stroke, you draw the men. Both blocks are composed **on the second after** — `ch27/i-need-you`'s solve, and it costs these two beats nothing either, because in both cases the beat was never the act. *Then the weight went off his arm and down* is a picture of a hand; *She was rearranging the road* is a picture of a road.

**AND BOTH OMIT A BODY THE PAGE PUTS IN FRONT OF THE READER.** The man Kael kills is a second out of frame below the bottom edge, and the man the first column takes *through the middle of him* and leaves *up to the height of the roofs* is simply not drawn. **THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE binds what a plate may add, not what it must include** — the hard rule's own remedy is distance, angle, occlusion and the detail out of frame, and here it is the crop and the smoke. Both are named in their blocks as one ban each, not a stack.

**THE RED IS THE SET'S FIRST FULL USE OF THE `ch23/the-horizon` SPECIMEN ON A HAND.** Route and appearance, never substance: *there is red over the whole of the open hand … and up the forearm to the elbow, dark and wet*, dried darker in the creases where the fingers fold — which is the chapter's own arithmetic, *it had gone into the creases where the fingers folded and dried there, and it was not coming out without water and time*.

**AND THE BURN IS UNDER IT.** Ch. 26's mend is on the page in this chapter — *It was new skin. Severin had put it back on him yesterday at the eastern gate … It had been pink and clean at first light this morning* — so the forearm carries two instructions, and §Hands' rule for that is one chain in order: the skin first, then what is on it. **The whole of the plate's argument is in that stack** — the newest thing on his body and the oldest thing anyone can do to another one, on the same six inches of arm.

**HIS FACE IS THE HARD PART AND THE PAGE FORBIDS THE OBVIOUS ANSWER.** *Kael's own face was doing something. He could not tell what … he reached for his own face and there was nothing there to reach for.* So the block may not name a state and may not leave the face empty either — §Gaze's *never tell a face to do nothing*. It gets three working parts and no verdict: the lids fully raised, the mouth open and the jaw slack, the brows level and unmoved. **What the reader decides it is, is what the boy could not.**

**THE SECOND BLOCK'S SUBJECT IS AN OBJECT, AND `ch21/the-surge` IS THE WARNING.** An effect that erases the thing it runs along came back as a white-painted blade, and what fixed it was giving the effect a form. So the columns are built as construction per §Nouns — **`column` has a default and it is architecture** — torn out of the road and made of what the road is made of, broken off at the top, with the ground opened in a ring at the foot of each. And they are counted the way `ch27/the-sky` counted four burning objects and landed them all on one run: **one line per item, each with its own place in the frame, the count stated after them.**

**HER FACE COSTS NOTHING AND THAT IS THE PICTURE.** *Valeria did not stop, did not speak, did not change her pace by any amount he could measure* … *her breathing was not different.* The road is being taken apart three strides from her and the block gives her chin, lids, mouth, brows and jaw as anatomy, with the eyes on the far end of the lane past all three. `ch20/val` is the standing warning that a temperament word drifts her wistful, and `ch27/i-need-you` is the proof that anatomy holds her.

**HER HAIR IS BOUND, WHICH IS Ch. 27's STATE AND NOT YET THE CROSSROADS'.** *her hair out of its binding on one side* happens at the top of the village, minutes after this; before that the page is silent and the last thing it said was bound. **Silence is answered with the last stated fact, not with a new one.** Authoring a feature the reference carries differently is now five-for-five.

**NOTHING COMES OFF HER HANDS.** Her art is internal and the ground is what moves; a picture of a person raising the road pulls hard toward hands-out casting and a glow, so that is the one absence the block spends — §Absence, and `ch12`'s *no glow, no spark* is the precedent.

**AND HER PICTURE IS ALMOST WITHOUT COLOUR.** The only saturated things in it are the green of her eyes and the red on her arms; the burning roofs of the lane stay out of the frame, because a warm source that far off cannot light her and would only compete. `ch27/the-sky` spent its colour on fire; this one spends it on her.

### PLATE: the-hand-back

*Candidate 1, ★.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/full-figures/kael-14-full.png`. Ch. 28 canon: *He put the thread into his left hand. Shoulder, arm, hand — and then nowhere left to go, because there was no blade on the end of it any more … Then he put it through the man's chest.* … *A man is heavy. He is heavy in a way a waster and a sack and a goat are not, because he is heavy and he is also holding on. Then he stops holding on, and becomes heavy in a completely different way, and the change happens in the hand.* … *Then the weight went off his arm and down, and Kael got his hand back and stood in the lane with it out in front of him, open, not touching anything.*

**BOTH HIS HANDS ARE EMPTY AND THE PAGE PUT THEM THAT WAY.** *A hand closed on his right-hand blade at the guard … The blade went out of his fingers and across the stones and under the lip of the well. He put the left one into a knee. It stuck. There was no time, and he let it go. Nothing in either hand.* So the well-head is in the frame with one of his blades under its lip, out of sight, and the plate bans every blade — a boy who fights with two of them pulls toward one.

**THE THREADING IS NOT ON THE HAND.** It was — *The white came up along the edge of it and sat there, on new skin, in the fog, in a lane, lighting nothing* — and the page does not say it is still there when the weight goes off. Where the page is silent the plate does not invent, and the picture is stronger for it: this is a hand with nothing whatever remarkable about it, which is the whole of what the chapter finds out. **No light, no white, no glow is written into the block at all**, so nothing is put in front of the filter.

**AND THE ONE THING IN THE FRAME THAT IS NOT HIM IS THE WELL.** It grounds the crop — §The cut: a cropped figure needs a floor — and it is the object the chapter comes back to twice.

### PLATE: rearranging-the-road

*Candidate 2, ★.* Anchors: `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`. Ch. 28 canon: *Then Valeria stopped moving. She did that perhaps four times in the whole of that morning, and each time it was worse than the moving. She put both hands down at her sides and set her feet and the lane came up. / It came up in a column the thickness of a man's chest — packed earth and old stone and four hundred years of people walking on it … / Then the next one, three strides further on, and the next after that, walking up that lane ahead of her in a line.* … *She was rearranging the road.*

**IT IS THE EARTH TWIN OF `ch26/the-fan`** — one girl, side-on, hip to mid-thigh, with her whole art going out of the frame away from her and the man it is aimed at out of the picture entirely. The fan is a sheet of flame off an open palm; this is three hundred tons of road standing up, and **her hands are down at her sides doing nothing at all**, which is the difference between the two characters stated in one picture.

**THE COST IS NAMED ON THE PAGE AND IS NOT DRAWN.** *Kael had spent eight weeks of his life learning to put a thread into two fingers' width of his own forearm. / She was rearranging the road.* The plate does not editorialise the gap; it simply puts the boy's whole discipline nowhere in the frame.

full-figure reference: the charcoal sleeveless coat over the grey-blue
underlayer, the high collar, the steel vambraces.

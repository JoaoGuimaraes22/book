# Ch. 24 — The Table · plate sheet

**Composed at s48 from the scene.** Kael is thirteen, on the evening of the first bout; Aeliana fourteen; Valeria thirteen. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` on both, `images/portraits/aeliana-15.png` and `images/full-figures/aeliana-15-full.png` on the first, `images/portraits/valeria-14.png` and `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE EMPIRE LOOKING AT HIM AND FINDING NOTHING, AND THE ONE PERSON WHO LOOKS AT HIM AND SMILES.** A room built to be looked at, with a boy turned to the lamp in it like a piece of work somebody has brought in — and, an hour later, a dark corner of a path where a girl turns her head and does something with her face that he has never seen her do.

**AELIANA'S FENCE, AGAIN, AND KEPT THE SAME WAY AS `ch22/the-box`.** Her beauty may never arrive as a verdict. Here the subject is **the appraisal itself** — *she did it the way you look at a horse, or a field, or a piece of work somebody has brought you* — so what the picture is about is a person examining another person, and her Ch. 24 canon is spent exactly as the page gives it: *a lighter thing now, there was less of it, and it had still taken somebody an hour. Her hair was down.*

**THE ARM IS DRAWN AS THE PAGE HAS IT AND IN THE WORDS THAT PASS.** §Damage, as amended at s48: `slash` and `red` carried `ch23/the-horizon` where `open cut` and `blood` were refused. This block needs neither — the arm is bound in four turns of a yard-master's cloth by now, stained where it has dried — and **the older mark going up out of the cuff is Ch. 24's own property** (`../canon-brief.md`: the burn on the left forearm, wrist to elbow), drawn here for the first time. Neither block states an age.

**AND THE ROOM IS THE ONLY INTERIOR IN THE BOOK WITH ANYTHING IN IT.** Every other interior the folder has drawn is a mountain house, a study, a hall or a brick court — undyed wool, plank walls, one lamp. This one is braziers burning coals in the spring, gold nailed along a screen standing in front of nothing, and a table with three joints and out-of-season fruit going over on it that nobody is eating. **The contrast is the chapter**, and it costs the block its longest paragraph, which is the right place to spend it.

### PLATE: turn-him-toward-the-lamp

*Candidate 1.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png`, `images/portraits/aeliana-15.png`, `images/full-figures/aeliana-15-full.png`. Ch. 24 canon: *She looked at him. / She did it the way you look at a horse, or a field, or a piece of work somebody has brought you — from the top, then across, then back to the top — and she went on doing it well past the point at which a person stops.* … *"Turn him toward the lamp," the princess said.* … *Kael had already worked out what was wanted and turned, so that the light came onto the side of his face. That was somehow worse than being told.* … *"There is nothing to him," the princess said.* And the room: *whole fish laid out in a row with their eyes in. Three joints … Fruit that did not grow on this island and did not grow anywhere at this time of year, piled, and some of it was already going. Flowers cut and lying along the middle of it, out of season, wilting in the heat off the braziers.* … *in a wide silver bowl, packed round a jug and sweating into the cloth, there was ice.* … *Nobody was eating.*

**THE SUBJECT IS A BOY BEING LOOKED AT, AND HE IS GIVING NOTHING BACK** — which is the one thing this book's whole coat was built for, tested here against the highest-status gaze in the empire. *He stood the way he stood and put his hands where his hands went and did not fill the silence.*

**THE TABLE IS THE ARGUMENT AND IT IS NOT THE SUBJECT.** It runs between them, loaded and blazing, with the ice in the middle of it — and no plate, no cup and nobody eating anywhere in the frame. §The winner and the rival: the food and the gold are the rival and they are enormous, so the two faces take the hardest edges and the food stays warm, soft-edged and unfinished.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-smile

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's last beat.* Anchors: `images/portraits/valeria-14.png`, `images/full-figures/valeria-14-full.png`, `images/portraits/kael-14.png`. Ch. 24 canon: *Valeria walked out with him as far as the corner where the sea court path went up. / "I am going to miss this," she said.* … *And Valeria Vel turned her head and smiled at him. / It was not much of a smile. It was there and gone, and it did not do anything a smile is supposed to do to a face. Kael had watched her for many mornings from a treeline and stood across from her every day for a year. He had seen her satisfied. He had seen her bored. He had seen her decide not to hit somebody. He had never once seen this.*

**THE PAGE SPENDS THREE SENTENCES SAYING WHAT THIS FACE IS NOT, WHICH IS THE BLOCK'S INSTRUCTION.** *It did not do anything a smile is supposed to do to a face* — so the mouth moves and nothing else does: the eyes do not crease, the cheeks do not lift, the brows do not move. **That is the exact opposite of `ch18/caught-it-halfway`**, where the cheek had to come up with the mouth for a beginning of a smile to read; here the cheek staying down is the whole point, and the two blocks are a matched pair for anybody reading the folder for how a mouth is written.

**AND IT IS THE ONLY WARM-LIT NIGHT EXTERIOR THE SET HAS.** One lamp left behind them in the court, low and to one side; everything else black. `ch01/fallen-stars` is the night picture and it is lit by a town at distance; this is lit by a lamp six feet away and out of frame.


**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch24-the-smile.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s48: *its good, adopt*).**

**THE SMILE CAME BACK AS WRITTEN**, which was the hard part: the corners barely lifted, the mouth closed, the whole thing already going. Everything else held — her head turned back over the shoulder, her hand on the strap, the vambrace and the grey-blue underlayer, his patched wool and the cloth binding above the left elbow, eye contact as the subject with neither of them at the viewer. **And the light is the set's first close warm night source**: one lamp out of frame, a wash of it on the brick and a patch on the path, everything else black.

**WHAT DRIFTED, AND IT IS MINE.** Her eyes warm a fraction with the mouth where the block said nothing else moves — a notch fonder than *it did not do anything a smile is supposed to do to a face.* **And the author's note on the other face is the finding worth keeping: *Kael is kind of expressionless.*** The block asked for exactly that — *his face is doing nothing at all* — and the tool executed it literally. **A face given nothing renders as vacancy, and vacancy is not the coat.** `ch12/the-law-on-the-table` got this right by giving the still face one working part — *the face level, the eyes steady on the man, the jaw set but easy* — and every future Kael block should name the one thing that is working rather than saying the face does nothing.

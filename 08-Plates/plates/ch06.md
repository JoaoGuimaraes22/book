### PLATE: the-wheel

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's face.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`. Ch. 6 canon: *She sat against the wheel of a cart in the yard's dark, knees up, smaller than him or his size, he could not tell — and she was tied. Wrists behind, to the wheel, a line taken twice around and finished low where her hands could not follow it... The girl's eyes found him in the dark. They were the first stranger's eyes to ever land on him, and they went wide, and her mouth opened — and closed... there was a knot on a person, and he was the boy who untied things, and his fingers were already reading it.*

**HER APPEARANCE IS UNSPENT AND THIS PLATE MUST NOT FIX IT.** The page gives her *smaller than him or his size, he could not tell* and nothing else — no hair, no colouring, no age — and `07/planted-clues` keeps her live: **the girl's version as the story's dangerous correction.** She may arrive properly later. **So the dark does the withholding, which is framing and not invention:** she is at the edge of the light with her face turned into it, enough to read her eyes and her mouth and not enough to read a colour. Her eyes carry the beat; their colour does not.

**THE EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT, and it is named as such** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Getting expression: gaze alignment is unreliable and has to be stated as the point of the picture). *The first stranger's eyes to ever land on him.*

**HIS FINGERS READ AND HIS EYES DO NOT.** Page canon, and it is the composition: *his fingers were already reading it, finding the architecture.* So his hands are at her wrists behind the wheel and **he is not looking at them** — he is looking at her. The chain rule governs the arm (`§Describe a limb as a chain`), and the hands do the work blind.

**THE ARCHETYPE RISK IS RESCUE.** A boy freeing a bound child is an image that wants courage, resolve and a jaw set — and the page is explicit that none of that is present: *He was at the wheel without deciding. That was the truth of it... there was no moment when he chose.* Per the s41 finding that banning one archetype delivers its neighbour, the block bans the heroic register **and** its neighbours (frightened, pitying), and replaces them with the positive: a boy absorbed in a knot, doing the thing he does.

**THE TIE, NEVER THE VIOLENCE.** No blood, no marks, no men, no body, no weapon anywhere in the frame.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the dirt yard of a fishing village house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — a cart wheel, the corner of a shed, frozen mud, all
of it going into deep cool wash. Nothing else is resolved.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS A LONG HARD-EDGED BLADE OF YELLOW LAMPLIGHT LYING
ACROSS THE YARD, entering from outside the frame at one side. The two
children are at its edge, mostly in the dark beside it, caught along
one side only. WHAT THE LIGHT COMES FROM IS NOT IN THIS PICTURE: no
door, no doorway, no window, no lamp, no room and no interior is
visible anywhere in the frame.

Composition: the two children close together, seen from the side and
slightly above, cropped at the hip. A large spoked cart wheel stands
behind the girl, filling the frame behind her.

THE GIRL sits on the ground against the wheel, knees drawn up. Her
arms are taken back behind her around the wheel's rim, and one length
of rope goes TWICE around the rim and her wrists together and
finishes in a knot set low behind her, below where her fingers could
reach. The rope and the wheel are drawn precisely. She is a child of
about the boy's own size.

HER FACE IS TURNED TOWARD THE LIGHT AND ONLY HER NEAR SIDE IS LIT —
enough to read the eyes and the mouth, not enough to read a colour.
Her hair, her eye colour and her clothing stay unreadable in the
dark. Her eyes are WIDE OPEN and her mouth is CLOSED and held closed.

THE BOY crouches at her side, close, one knee down. His near arm
reaches past her body to the knot behind the wheel — shoulder, arm,
hand, rope, in that order, one continuous limb, and BOTH HIS HANDS
ARE ON THE ROPE, working it. Nothing else is in either hand. The rope
is in his fingers and is not lying slack on the ground.

HE IS NOT LOOKING AT HIS HANDS. THE TWO CHILDREN ARE LOOKING
DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE
IMAGE. His hands work the knot without his eyes.

His expression is ABSORBED AND UNHURRIED — a boy doing something he
has done a thousand times, with his whole attention on her face. NOT
brave, NOT determined, NOT heroic, NOT urgent, NOT grim, no jaw set
and no resolve anywhere in it; and equally NOT frightened, NOT
pitying, NOT horrified. Nothing in his face has decided anything.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, catching the
light — no more colour in the iris than in the whites around it.
Dark hair cut short and uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick for
a winter night.

There is nobody else in the picture: no adult, no third child, no
figure at the edge of the frame and no shape in the light. No blood,
no wound, no mark on either child, no weapon, no knife and no rope
anywhere except the one at her wrists.
```

### PLATE: carried-past-the-rock

*Candidate 3, and the chapter's landing.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`, `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 6 canon: *The split rock stood up out of the dark, and past it, against the paler dark of the sky, his mother. She did not run to them. She stood where the path ended, at the edge of the oldest law, straight and still, the way she sat at the window, and as his father carried him past the rock Kael felt her hand arrive at his head, and stay, the whole way up — one hand, resting there, riding the rhythm of his father's steps — and nobody said anything, the whole climb.* And of the carrying: *the arms were not gentle and were not rough, they were exact, and they did not shake.*

**THE DELIBERATE BOOKEND TO `ch05-past-the-split-rock`.** Same rock, same night, same child — he crossed it alone going down and is carried past it coming up, and the plate before it is the last time he was on his own feet. **The rock's size was fixed by `ch05.md`** (man-high, one cleft) and is unchanged here.

**THE ARCHETYPE RISK IS THE PIETÀ, and it is severe.** A mother meeting a broken child at night is the most heavily grooved image in Western painting, and `03` and the brief are both absolute: **Neris is not warm, not maternal, not soft**, and *her warmth is spent once in the book, on the head of an unborn child.* A tender or anguished face here is the wrong woman and would spend a thing the book has not spent. So the block bans grief, tenderness and alarm by name, and states the positive the page gives: **the face from the window.** *She did not run.*

**HER HAND IS THE SUBJECT, AND THE CROP IS WHAT LETS IT WIN.** `03`: *her hands are where the book keeps her*, and any figure must put them in frame and occupied. §Name the winner's s41 limit says a detail below about the size of a hand in frame cannot carry finish — **so this is framed close enough that a hand is large**, which is exactly what `ch04/the-master-arrives` could not do with the wrists at full length.

**HIS EYES ARE OPEN AND NOT SEEING.** *He surfaced once, partway.* The grey is spent for plates now (s41), and this is its second use — half-lidded, unfocused, pointed at nothing.

**INJURY KEPT MINIMAL AND FACTUAL** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Damage): named and located, no tissue vocabulary, and **no age stated in the block** — the reference carries it, and stated ages plus injury are the folder's one documented refusal trigger.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.
Image 2: portrait reference of the woman.
Image 3: portrait reference of the man.

Setting: a steep mountain path at night, at the point where the path
ends, as loose wash suggestion — bare rock and frozen ground, the
mountain falling away behind into deep cool wash, a few small cold
stars picked out as tiny reserved flecks in the sky. THE SPLIT ROCK
stands at the edge of the frame as a dark mass: one upright standing
stone roughly the height of a grown man, split top to bottom by a
single clean cleft, no detail in it.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS FAINT COLD STARLIGHT FROM ABOVE. It catches the
tops of things and nothing else — the woman's cheekbone and brow, the
back of her hand, the boy's face, the man's shoulder. Everything
below and behind them is deep cool shadow. There is no lamp, no fire,
no torch and no warm colour anywhere in the picture.

Composition: close, cropped at the chest, three figures. THE MAN
carries THE BOY across his body, the boy's head and shoulders lying
back over the man's forearm, the boy's face turned up to the sky. THE
WOMAN stands beside them, close, her head and shoulders against the
paler sky.

HER HAND HAS COME TO REST ON THE BOY'S HEAD AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF
THE IMAGE. Fingers spread and relaxed in his hair, the whole hand
resting with no weight in it, not gripping, not stroking, not
cradling his face. It is the most finished thing in the picture and
carries the sharpest step from light to dark, pale against his dark
hair. The rock, the ground, the sky and all the clothing are held
dark and loose at quiet low values, no hard edges anywhere in them,
nothing competing with the hand for finish.

Her other hand hangs open and empty at her side. The man's two hands
are both under the boy, carrying him. Nothing is held in any hand and
there is nothing else in the picture.

SHE IS NOT REACTING. Standing straight and completely still, her face
level and giving nothing away, mouth closed, brows unmoved — the face
of a woman sitting at a window, arriving on a night like any other.
NOT grieving, NOT tender, NOT anguished, NOT alarmed, NOT relieved,
NOT crying, no tears and nothing wet on her face; and equally NOT
cold, NOT angry, NOT accusing. She has not run and is not hurrying.
Do not draw a mother receiving a wounded child.

THE MAN IS EXACT. Carrying without effort and without care, arms
steady and level, no strain in the shoulders and no tenderness in the
hold. His face is turned uphill, away from the boy, level and
unreadable, giving nothing. He is walking; he has not stopped.

THE BOY is barely conscious: head back, the neck gone loose, his eyes
HALF OPEN AND UNFOCUSED, pointed at the sky and not seeing it. The
eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless, the lightest thing
in his face — no more colour in the iris than in the whites around
it. Dried blood on his cheek and in his hair, dirt worked into the
skin. His clothing is torn and filthy. No other wound is visible.

Long dark hair loose past her shoulders; plain coarse mended village
cloth on all three of them, THE INK CARRYING THE CLOTH at the seams
and the mended places. No ornament, no metal, no buckle on anybody.

Nobody says anything: all three mouths are closed. There is no fourth
figure anywhere in the frame and no house, no light and no building
in it.
```

### PLATE: the-wheel

*Candidate 2, and the chapter's face.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`. Ch. 6 canon: *She sat against the wheel of a cart in the yard's dark, knees up, smaller than him or his size, he could not tell — and she was tied. Wrists behind, to the wheel, a line taken twice around and finished low where her hands could not follow it... The girl's eyes found him in the dark. They were the first stranger's eyes to ever land on him, and they went wide, and her mouth opened — and closed... there was a knot on a person, and he was the boy who untied things, and his fingers were already reading it.*

**HER APPEARANCE IS UNSPENT AND THIS PLATE MUST NOT FIX IT.** The page gives her *smaller than him or his size, he could not tell* and nothing else — no hair, no colouring, no age — and `07/planted-clues` keeps her live: **the girl's version as the story's dangerous correction.** She may arrive properly later. **So the dark does the withholding, which is framing and not invention:** she is at the edge of the light with her face turned into it, enough to read her eyes and her mouth and not enough to read a colour. Her eyes carry the beat; their colour does not.

**THE EYE CONTACT IS THE SUBJECT, and it is named as such** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Getting expression: gaze alignment is unreliable and has to be stated as the point of the picture). *The first stranger's eyes to ever land on him.*

**HIS FINGERS READ AND HIS EYES DO NOT.** Page canon, and it is the composition: *his fingers were already reading it, finding the architecture.* So his hands are at her wrists behind the wheel and **he is not looking at them** — he is looking at her. The chain rule governs the arm (`§Describe a limb as a chain`), and the hands do the work blind.

**THE ARCHETYPE RISK IS RESCUE.** A boy freeing a bound child is an image that wants courage, resolve and a jaw set — and the page is explicit that none of that is present: *He was at the wheel without deciding. That was the truth of it... there was no moment when he chose.* Per the s41 finding that banning one archetype delivers its neighbour, the block bans the heroic register **and** its neighbours (frightened, pitying), and replaces them with the positive: a boy absorbed in a knot, doing the thing he does.

**RE-CUT BEFORE IT EVER RAN — four faults found by reading it, not by linting it.** The lean version passed the pre-flight clean and was still a poor prompt, which is the whole argument for judgement over instrumentation:

1. **The crop contradicted the pose.** *Cropped at the hip*, on a girl sitting with her knees drawn up, removes the knees — and the knees are the posture. Same class as `not reaching` in `ch05.md`: an instruction fighting the picture it is inside.
2. **THE COMPOSITION HID THE HANDS IT CALLED THE WORK.** Seen from the side, with the wheel filling the frame behind her and his arm reaching *past her body to the knot behind the wheel*, both her body and the wheel stood between the camera and his hands — and §Standing limitation says occlusion is precisely where these models break. **The viewer is now behind her, looking down past her shoulder at the wrists**, with nothing in front of them, and the camera position is stated as what it therefore sees (§Altitude, s41) rather than as where it is.
3. **No value hierarchy at all** — the folder's most reliable technique, simply absent, in a block that then said *the rope and the wheel are drawn precisely* and demoted nothing. Faces take the finish when nothing stops them.
4. **Two subjects, again.** The eye contact was named the subject and the knot was what the picture is about. **Resolved on channels rather than by demoting either:** her lit cheek carries the sharpest step in VALUE, the knot is in shadow and carried by INK LINE. They cannot compete because they win on different things.

**And the girl's age was anchored to nothing** — the boy has `kael-9` carrying his and she has no reference at all, so she could arrive sixteen. Now pinned by ratio and by a landmark on a third object: *her head is about a sixth of her whole height, and seated like this the top of her head comes to the wheel's hub.*

**Earlier, the pre-flight cut (kept, and it was the easy half).** 597 words and eleven capitalised blocks down to 430 and three. What came out: *no door, no doorway, no window, no lamp, no room and no interior* (six names for one absent source, now two) · *NOT heroic, NOT urgent, NOT grim* and *NOT horrified* (synonyms of the terms kept beside them) · *Nothing in his face has decided anything* (not drawable; *absorbed and unhurried* is the version that draws) · *no adult, no third child, no figure at the edge of the frame, no shape in the light* (four for one) · *no wound* and *no knife* (already banned as mark and weapon). **Every instruction survives.**

**THE TIE, NEVER THE VIOLENCE.** No blood, no marks, no men, no body, no weapon anywhere in the frame.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: the dirt yard of a fishing village house at night, as loose
wash suggestion — the corner of a shed, frozen mud, everything going
into deep cool wash.

A LONG HARD-EDGED BLADE OF YELLOW LAMPLIGHT LIES ACROSS THE YARD from
outside the frame, and it is the only light. What throws it is never
shown: no door, doorway or window appears anywhere in the frame. The
two children are at the blade's edge, lit down one side.

Composition: close, the two of them filling the frame, the frozen
ground visible beneath them. A large spoked cart wheel stands at the
left with the girl sitting against it.

THE VIEWER IS BEHIND THE GIRL AND ABOVE HER, AND THEREFORE SEES: the
back of her shoulders, the backs of her bound hands and the knot
below them, the near face of the wheel, and the boy beyond her turned
back toward the viewer. Her wrists and the knot are the nearest thing
in the picture and NOTHING STANDS IN FRONT OF THEM.

The girl sits on the ground with her back against the wheel, knees
drawn up. Her arms are taken back around the wheel's rim, and one
length of rope goes twice around the rim and her wrists together and
finishes in a knot set low behind her, below where her fingers could
reach. She is a child: her head is about a sixth of her whole height,
and seated like this the top of her head comes to the wheel's hub.

Her head is turned back and up over her shoulder toward the boy, so
her face is in view. Only her near side is lit — enough to read the
eyes and the mouth, not enough to read a colour; her hair, her eye
colour and her clothing stay unreadable in the dark. Her eyes are
wide open and her mouth is closed.

The boy crouches beyond her wrists, one knee down, leaning in. His
near arm comes down to the knot — shoulder, arm, hand, rope, in that
order, one continuous limb — and both his hands are on the rope,
working it. Nothing else is in either hand, and the rope is in his
fingers rather than slack on the ground.

THE TWO ARE LOOKING DIRECTLY AT EACH OTHER AND THIS EYE CONTACT IS
THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. He is not looking at his hands; they read
the knot without his eyes.

Her lit cheek and eye are the brightest values in the picture and
carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The knot is in shadow and
is carried by ink line instead — every turn of the rope legible in
dark scratchy linework, with no light falling on it. The wheel, the
ground, the shed and both children's clothing are held at quiet low
values with no hard edges.

His face is absorbed and unhurried, his whole attention on hers — a
boy doing something he has done a thousand times. NOT brave, NOT
determined, no jaw set and no resolve in it anywhere; and equally NOT
frightened and NOT pitying. DO NOT DRAW A RESCUE.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless — no more colour in
the iris than in the whites around it. Dark hair cut short and
uneven. Coarse undyed mended wool, thick for a winter night, the ink
carrying the cloth at the seams and the mended places.

Nobody else is in the picture and no figure stands in the light. No
blood and no mark on either child, no weapon anywhere, and no rope
except the one at her wrists.
```

### PLATE-VERBATIM: carried-past-the-rock

*Candidate 3, and the chapter's landing.* Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-9.png`, `images/portraits/neris.png`, `images/portraits/vask.png`. Ch. 6 canon: *The split rock stood up out of the dark, and past it, against the paler dark of the sky, his mother. She did not run to them. She stood where the path ended, at the edge of the oldest law, straight and still, the way she sat at the window, and as his father carried him past the rock Kael felt her hand arrive at his head, and stay, the whole way up — one hand, resting there, riding the rhythm of his father's steps — and nobody said anything, the whole climb.* And of the carrying: *the arms were not gentle and were not rough, they were exact, and they did not shake.*

**THE DELIBERATE BOOKEND TO `ch05-past-the-split-rock`.** Same rock, same night, same child — he crossed it alone going down and is carried past it coming up, and the plate before it is the last time he was on his own feet. **The rock's size was fixed by `ch05.md`** (man-high, one cleft) and is unchanged here.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch06-carried-past-the-rock.png`, run 2.** **THE BLOCK ABOVE IS THE AUTHOR'S CUT AND IS VERBATIM AS RUN**, style header included; `PLATE-VERBATIM` means the build script emits it untouched. Do not reassemble it.

**THE AUTHOR'S FOUR CUTS ALL HOLD, and they are evidence.** Removed before the run: *No other wound is visible* · *No ornament, no metal, no buckle on anybody* · *Nobody says anything: all three mouths are closed* · *There is no fourth figure anywhere in the frame and no house, no light and no building in it.* **Nothing pulled toward any of them and no stray arrived** — three were negatives with nothing behind them (§Negative inventory), and the fourth restated *mouths closed* from the paragraph above it.

**WHAT ELSE COULD HAVE COME OUT — the audit the author asked for, run on the text as it ran.** Roughly seventy words, none of which can change a pixel:

- **Abstractions whose drawable version is already next to them.** *THE MAN IS EXACT* is a note to a reader; *arms steady and level, no strain in the shoulders* is the instruction. Same for *and there is nothing else in the picture*, which names no object.
- **Restatement inside one sentence.** *He is walking; he has not stopped.* · *It catches the tops of things and nothing else* followed by *Everything below and behind them is deep cool shadow.* · *without care* and *no tenderness in the hold*, one channel, twice.
- **Not drawable in a still image.** *She has not run* — there is no way to depict not having run; *is not hurrying* is the half that draws.
- **One noun, three names.** *no lamp, no fire, no torch* — a night scene pulls toward one warm source, so one negative earns its place and the other two are padding.
- **A synonym run in the NOT list.** *anguished* is *grieving*, *accusing* is *angry*, and *relieved* has nothing pulling toward it. **The rest of that list stays** — grief, tears, tenderness, alarm, cold and stern are six different channels, and §Over-writing contradicts' limit says count the channel, not the sentence.

**And the one that must NOT be cut, so it is not cut by mistake later:** *AND THE CONTRADICTION MUST SURVIVE* reads like rhetoric and is not. `ch01-vask-kael-goat` recorded that without it the renderer resolves the attention and the expression into an ordinary tender parent.

**RUN 1 — the block did what it said, and the author changed the instruction.** Everything specified arrived: the hand resting spread in his hair as the finished thing, the split rock as a dark cleft mass at the frame edge, cold starlight and no warm colour, three closed mouths, the boy's grey half-open and unfocused. **The author's note is that the parents should be worried, looking at Kael** — where the block had both faces turned uphill and level.

**THE COLLISION, AND THE TIGHTEST VERSION OF IT.** *Worried* is a nameable feeling on two faces the book has never let be readable — `03` on Vask is absolute (*nothing in them that is anything*), and Ch. 5 gives his one exception without ever naming it. **So the amendment moves the ATTENTION and not the EXPRESSION**, which is the solution `ch01-vask-kael-goat` already proved and had adopted: *he is looking at his son and his face is giving nothing away. The attention is entirely on the child; the expression is not.* Two parents with their eyes down on a broken child, in silence, reads as worry without a canon line being spent.

**IF THAT STILL READS COLD, the next step spends Neris and not Vask** — hers is *not warm, not maternal, not soft*, which is a softer rule than his empty eyes and is the cheaper of the two to bend. **Author's call; not taken here.**

**THE ROCK READS AS AN OBELISK, AND MY OWN WORDING DID IT.** The block says *one upright standing stone... split top to bottom by a single clean cleft* — and **`standing stone` is a noun with a default**, exactly like `coat`, `dagger` and `candle`. It returned a menhir: regular sides, flat top, a cleft like a cut. The page has a natural crag *split long ago by ice or lightning* (Ch. 5), which is a different object. Fourth instance of §Name a thing by construction, and the first one this folder inflicted on itself after writing the rule.

**THE WORDING TO USE NEXT TIME IT APPEARS** (Ch. 7 stands the master beside it):

> *A natural boulder standing about the height of a grown man, irregular in outline and weathered all over, broader at the base than at the top, no straight edges and no flat faces anywhere on it. A rough fracture runs down through it, wide and uneven, opened by frost — not a cut and not a gap between two stones. It is not a standing stone, not a monument, not a menhir, not an obelisk and nothing about it is shaped or placed by hands.*

**AND THE COST OF FIXING IT: `ch05-past-the-split-rock` IS ADOPTED WITH THE OLD ROCK.** The two adopted plates would then disagree about the same object. **Not resolved here** — noted so whoever draws it next chooses knowingly rather than discovering it.

**Vask's grey has now failed a third time** — no strands at the temples, consistent with `ch03.md`'s standing note that the clause failed twice and was dropped rather than re-instructed. Recorded, not acted on.

**THE ARCHETYPE RISK IS THE PIETÀ, and it is severe.** A mother meeting a broken child at night is the most heavily grooved image in Western painting, and `03` and the brief are both absolute: **Neris is not warm, not maternal, not soft**, and *her warmth is spent once in the book, on the head of an unborn child.* A tender or anguished face here is the wrong woman and would spend a thing the book has not spent. So the block bans grief, tenderness and alarm by name, and states the positive the page gives: **the face from the window.** *She did not run.*

**HER HAND IS THE SUBJECT, AND THE CROP IS WHAT LETS IT WIN.** `03`: *her hands are where the book keeps her*, and any figure must put them in frame and occupied. §Name the winner's s41 limit says a detail below about the size of a hand in frame cannot carry finish — **so this is framed close enough that a hand is large**, which is exactly what `ch04/the-master-arrives` could not do with the wrists at full length.

**HIS EYES ARE OPEN AND NOT SEEING.** *He surfaced once, partway.* The grey is spent for plates now (s41), and this is its second use — half-lidded, unfocused, pointed at nothing.

**INJURY KEPT MINIMAL AND FACTUAL** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Damage): named and located, no tissue vocabulary, and **no age stated in the block** — the reference carries it, and stated ages plus injury are the folder's one documented refusal trigger.

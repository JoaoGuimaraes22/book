### PLATE: aurelians-candle

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/aurelian-14.png`. Ch. 9 canon: *Not the wick — the wax. The whole cup of the candle's top went up in a soft orange sheet, a fistful of fire standing off the bench, sudden enough that the boy beside him went backward off his heels; and the bench had turned to him before the flame finished standing, and for one breath, before the yard-master's hand came down over it with a wet cloth that was clearly always ready, the fire lit Aurelian's face from below, and it was the face from the roller morning with the world put right again.* And the roller morning itself: *Aurelian stood off it with his breath tearing and his face bright, a boy standing where he stood best.*

**HIS SHEET NAMES THIS AS THE BEST IMAGE OF HIM IN THE BOOK, and as a PLATE rather than a portrait** — because the portrait system's fixed lighting is soft and directional and this one is lit from underneath by a fire. It was blocked on that being possible. **It is not blocked any more:** `ch05/the-fact-lands` proved an out-of-frame source below a face lights it properly (s41), which is a different finding from `ch01-fallen-stars`, where an *in-frame* warm source rendered as a source and threw nothing.

**THE LIGHTING IS THE ARCHETYPE RISK, AND IT IS THE WHOLE DESIGN PROBLEM.** A face lit from below means menace in every visual tradition there is, and `03` is emphatic that **Aurelian is not a strawman** — *pleasant, open, easy, genuinely charming; never sneering, never smug, never cruel.* So the block has to put a delighted thirteen-year-old inside the lighting convention for villainy and hold him there. The expression is written as the subject, in the positive, with the whole neighbourhood banned: `ch05/the-fact-lands` proved that **banning one archetype delivers its neighbour**, so smug and sneering are banned alongside frightened and startled, or the face will simply move next door.

**WHAT THE MOMENT ACTUALLY IS, and it decides the gaze.** *The world put right again* is internal — a boy looking at fire he made, with everything back where it belongs after a morning in which one small nobody walked the roller alone. **So his eyes are down on the flame, not up at the bench.** A face raised to a cheering crowd is a boy being pleased *at* people, which is the reading `03` forbids; a face down on his own fire is a boy being pleased, full stop.

**HIS WARMTH IS EXTERNAL HERE FOR THE ONLY TIME.** The brief keeps *fire's warmth in his colouring, never stated* and **his ground cool — warm parchment is Aeliana's alone.** In this picture the warmth is a literal fire, so the block spends it on his face and holds the wall, the air and the frost cold behind him. The rule survives intact; it just gets its one exception drawn.

**THE TWO-CLAUSE MECHANISM, ASSIGNED CAREFULLY** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name the winner, and the s41 limit): the fire is the brightest value and would take all the finish if left alone, so it is demoted to a soft loose wash with no hard edge and no detail, and **the sharpest step from light to dark is sited on the boundary of the lit part of his face.** Brightest and most-finished are deliberately two different objects.

**NO FRECKLE INSTRUCTION, DELIBERATELY.** `03` rules freckles never for him, and the medium speckles every face regardless (author ruling, s34: texture, not a mark). Writing the negation would be fighting granulation, which the folder has already ruled against. Expect speckling; do not read it as canon.

**NO INSIGNIA IS LOAD-BEARING, not merely absent** — `07/planted-clues` reserves the Corvane insignia for the Part IV fire.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: a winter training yard at the foot of a high stone wall, as
loose wash suggestion — rough stone behind him going into deep cool
shadow, frost on the ground, no detail anywhere. The edge of a plank
bench crosses the very bottom of the frame.

Composition: close on his head and shoulders, the face filling most
of the frame, seen from slightly below. His head is tipped down
toward the flame in front of him. Nothing below his chest is in
frame.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS THE FIRE AND IT IS BELOW HIM. On the bench in front
of him a soft sheet of orange flame stands up off the top of a tallow
candle, a double handful of fire, low in the frame and cropped by its
bottom edge. It lights his face entirely from underneath: the
underside of the jaw and the chin, the underside of the brow, the
septum and the underside of the nose, the front of the throat. The
forehead, the top of the head and the hair above it fall away into
shadow. Everything behind him stays cold — the wall, the air, the
frost — and no warm colour touches any of it.

THE FIRE IS THE BRIGHTEST VALUE. HIS FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING.
The flame is soft-edged and loose, a wash with no hard edge anywhere
in it and no detail in it at all. The sharpest step from light to
dark in the picture happens along the boundary of the lit part of his
face, at the cheekbone and the jaw.

HIS EXPRESSION IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Lit up, open and
delighted — a boy standing exactly where he belongs with the world
put back the way it should be. Lips parted, the breath still coming
hard, brows raised, the whole face bright and unguarded and young.
NOT smug, NOT sneering, NOT triumphant over anybody, NOT cruel, NOT
sly, NOT knowing — and equally NOT frightened, NOT startled, NOT
alarmed. He is not looking at any person and there is nobody in the
frame he could be pleased at. He is looking at the fire.

Eyes amber, gold-brown, wide open and turned down to the flame,
catching it. The skin immediately around the eye stays cool, so the
amber holds and does not wash out into the firelight.

Dark-gold hair pushed back off the forehead, thick, dry and clean.
Clear well-fed skin with sun on it, unweathered, no dirt and nothing
worked into it anywhere.

Thirteen years old and built a year ahead of it: broad through the
shoulder and the neck for his age, the frame already going toward a
man's, and the face still soft and unfinished with the jaw not yet
come in.

Good dark wool, well cut and clean, a plain collar. No insignia, no
badge, no chain, no metal and no ornament of any kind anywhere on
him.

NO OTHER FACE IS IN THIS PICTURE. The other children are present only
as dark unlit shapes at the extreme edges of the frame, out of focus,
no features drawn — one at the left edge going backward and off
balance, away from the fire.
```

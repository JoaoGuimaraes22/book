### PLATE: aurelians-candle

*Candidate 1, the chapter's pick.* Anchor: `images/portraits/aurelian-14.png`. Ch. 9 canon: *Not the wick — the wax. The whole cup of the candle's top went up in a soft orange sheet, a fistful of fire standing off the bench, sudden enough that the boy beside him went backward off his heels; and the bench had turned to him before the flame finished standing, and for one breath, before the yard-master's hand came down over it with a wet cloth that was clearly always ready, the fire lit Aurelian's face from below, and it was the face from the roller morning with the world put right again.* And the roller morning itself: *Aurelian stood off it with his breath tearing and his face bright, a boy standing where he stood best.*

**HIS SHEET NAMES THIS AS THE BEST IMAGE OF HIM IN THE BOOK, and as a PLATE rather than a portrait** — because the portrait system's fixed lighting is soft and directional and this one is lit from underneath by a fire. It was blocked on that being possible. **It is not blocked any more:** `ch05/the-fact-lands` proved an out-of-frame source below a face lights it properly (s41), which is a different finding from `ch01-fallen-stars`, where an *in-frame* warm source rendered as a source and threw nothing.

**THE LIGHTING IS THE ARCHETYPE RISK, AND IT IS THE WHOLE DESIGN PROBLEM.** A face lit from below means menace in every visual tradition there is, and `03` is emphatic that **Aurelian is not a strawman** — *pleasant, open, easy, genuinely charming; never sneering, never smug, never cruel.* So the block has to put a delighted thirteen-year-old inside the lighting convention for villainy and hold him there. The expression is written as the subject, in the positive, with the whole neighbourhood banned: `ch05/the-fact-lands` proved that **banning one archetype delivers its neighbour**, so smug and sneering are banned alongside frightened and startled, or the face will simply move next door.

**WHAT THE MOMENT ACTUALLY IS, and it decides the gaze.** *The world put right again* is internal — a boy looking at fire he made, with everything back where it belongs after a morning in which one small nobody walked the roller alone. **So his eyes are down on the flame, not up at the bench.** A face raised to a cheering crowd is a boy being pleased *at* people, which is the reading `03` forbids; a face down on his own fire is a boy being pleased, full stop.

**HIS WARMTH IS EXTERNAL HERE FOR THE ONLY TIME.** The brief keeps *fire's warmth in his colouring, never stated* and **his ground cool — warm parchment is Aeliana's alone.** In this picture the warmth is a literal fire, so the block spends it on his face and holds the wall, the air and the frost cold behind him. The rule survives intact; it just gets its one exception drawn.

**THE TWO-CLAUSE MECHANISM, ASSIGNED CAREFULLY** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name the winner, and the s41 limit): the fire is the brightest value and would take all the finish if left alone, so it is demoted to a soft loose wash with no hard edge and no detail, and **the sharpest step from light to dark is sited on the boundary of the lit part of his face.** Brightest and most-finished are deliberately two different objects.

**NO FRECKLE INSTRUCTION, DELIBERATELY.** `03` rules freckles never for him, and the medium speckles every face regardless (author ruling, s34: texture, not a mark). Writing the negation would be fighting granulation, which the folder has already ruled against. Expect speckling; do not read it as canon.

**NO INSIGNIA IS LOAD-BEARING, not merely absent** — `07/planted-clues` reserves the Corvane insignia for the Part IV fire.

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch09-aurelians-candle.png`, first run.** The block below is what produced it and is not reassembled: it was amended after the run and the amendments were reverted when the author adopted the image.

**THE DESIGN PROBLEM IS SOLVED, and that is what this run is for.** The face is lit from underneath and **is not menacing** — open, bright, mouth open, wholly delighted, no smugness and no cruelty anywhere in it. **A boy inside the lighting convention for villainy, reading as a boy.** `03`'s *never sneering, never smug, never cruel* survives its hardest possible staging. The cool ground held with it: stone wall in cold grey-blue behind him, the warmth landing only on his face, so *his ground stays cool* survives its one literal-fire exception. No second face; the other child is a dark featureless shape at the left edge.

**AUTHOR RULINGS (s41), both against the sheet's own reading:** the **age holds** — this session flagged the face as reading several years above thirteen and the author read it as correct — and the **gaze is fine** where the sheet wanted it turned down onto the fire. Both stand as drawn, same scope as the s40 eye rulings: one picture, and nothing in `03` or the brief moves.

**ONE THING THE SHEET STILL OWES THE AUTHOR A SENTENCE ON: the wax did not catch.** What is drawn is an ordinary candle burning at the wick, and the page's beat is precisely the distinction — *Not the wick — the wax. The whole cup of the candle's top went up in a soft orange sheet, a fistful of fire standing off the bench, sudden enough that the boy beside him went backward off his heels.* A reader arriving from that paragraph sees a boy delighted at a normal candle. **Not raised again beyond this line; it is on the record and it is the author's to spend or not.**

**AND THE RUN PROVED A GENERAL RULE ANYWAY.** The word `candle` supplied its own default and beat every adjective attached to it — third instance after `dagger` and `coat`, in three unrelated domains. `../scene-prompt-system.md` §Name a thing by construction is generalised from those three and does not depend on how this picture is judged.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: a winter training yard at the foot of a high stone wall, as
loose wash suggestion — rough stone behind him going into deep cool
shadow, frost on the ground, no detail anywhere. The edge of a plank
bench crosses the very bottom of the frame.

Composition: close on his head and shoulders, the face filling most
of the frame, seen from slightly below. His head is tipped down
toward the flame in front of him. Nothing below his chest is in
frame.

THE ONLY LIGHT IS THE FIRE AND IT IS BELOW HIM. On the bench in front
of him a soft sheet of orange flame stands up off the top of a tallow
candle, a double handful of fire, low in the frame and cropped by its
bottom edge. It lights his face entirely from underneath: the
underside of the jaw and the chin, the underside of the brow, the
septum and the underside of the nose, the front of the throat. The
forehead, the top of the head and the hair above it fall away into
shadow. Everything behind him stays cold — the wall, the air, the
frost — and no warm colour touches any of it.

THE FIRE IS THE BRIGHTEST VALUE. HIS FACE IS THE MOST FINISHED THING.
The flame is soft-edged and loose, a wash with no hard edge anywhere
in it and no detail in it at all. The sharpest step from light to
dark in the picture happens along the boundary of the lit part of his
face, at the cheekbone and the jaw.

HIS EXPRESSION IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE. Lit up, open and
delighted — a boy standing exactly where he belongs with the world
put back the way it should be. Lips parted, the breath still coming
hard, brows raised, the whole face bright and unguarded and young.
NOT smug, NOT sneering, NOT triumphant over anybody, NOT cruel, NOT
sly, NOT knowing — and equally NOT frightened, NOT startled, NOT
alarmed. He is not looking at any person and there is nobody in the
frame he could be pleased at. He is looking at the fire.

Eyes amber, gold-brown, wide open and turned down to the flame,
catching it. The skin immediately around the eye stays cool, so the
amber holds and does not wash out into the firelight.

Dark-gold hair pushed back off the forehead, thick, dry and clean.
Clear well-fed skin with sun on it, unweathered, no dirt and nothing
worked into it anywhere.

Thirteen years old and built a year ahead of it: broad through the
shoulder and the neck for his age, the frame already going toward a
man's, and the face still soft and unfinished with the jaw not yet
come in.

Good dark wool, well cut and clean, a plain collar. No insignia, no
badge, no chain, no metal and no ornament of any kind anywhere on
him.

NO OTHER FACE IS IN THIS PICTURE. The other children are present only
as dark unlit shapes at the extreme edges of the frame, out of focus,
no features drawn — one at the left edge going backward and off
balance, away from the fire.
```

### PLATE: the-roller-alone

*Candidate 2.* Anchor: `images/portraits/kael-9.png` — Kael is ten here. Ch. 9 canon: *It was a drum of grey stone the length of a man, kept for flattening the yard after rain*... *"Good," the yard-master said. "Again. Alone."*... *Kael went out, because, of course, a question was standing. There was a pry-bar in the well-shed and a chock of wood by the racks, and the rest was the spring he was eight: where a boy who came up to a thing's shoulder stood so that the thing's own bigness went to work against it. The roller came back across the yard in stiff little tips and shoves, one boy walking it the way you walk a barrel, and the yard went quiet in a way it had not been for the seven. / He set the bar down. He was not much out of breath, and he heard how that sounded in the quiet.*

**RUN 1 (s41).** The lever reads: bar under the stone's edge, wooden block as fulcrum, both hands on the free end, the drum tipped and held. **And the ease held** — weight settled, shoulders down, nothing braced, no strain in the face — which was the archetype risk and the whole beat. The watching line, the three bent over their knees, no faces among them, no adult, frost as bare paper, and the cold-with-named-warm all came through.

**MISS: HE READS ABOUT THIRTEEN.** And the cause is specific rather than general — **the block gave the STONE three measurements and the BOY none.** *The top edge level with his shoulder* and *longer than the boy is tall* both size the drum against him; nothing anywhere sizes him against himself or against another person. `ch07-kael-of-what` put him beside an adult and he came back ten; `ch01-vask-kael-goat` measured him head-to-body and against a man's hip and came back four.

**So: a solo figure has no age reference in the frame, and the fix is his own proportions, not the object's** — head as a fraction of his height, limbs, the build. Recorded here, per picture, not promoted: this is the third run to drift old and the author has already judged one of the three correct.

**THE SUBJECT IS A DISPROPORTION, so it is stated as measurement** (`../scene-prompt-system.md` §Scale between figures): the top of the drum at the boy's shoulder, the drum longer than the boy is tall. Adjectives cannot carry this picture; the whole point is a ratio.

**THE ARCHETYPE RISK IS EFFORT.** A child moving a great stone is an image that wants strain, heave and triumph — and the page says the opposite twice: *stiff little tips and shoves, the way you walk a barrel*, and *he was not much out of breath.* The ease **is** the beat, and the yard goes quiet because of it. So the block bans the heroic register and its neighbours and states the positive: a boy doing a familiar chore.

**FORTY CHILDREN, WRITTEN AS FOUR AND A MASS.** The tested ceiling is four resolved figures (§Four figures), so three bent over their knees carry *the seven who did it first* and the rest are summary shapes with no faces. Same solve as `ch08/the-one-who-did-not-laugh`.

**AURELIAN IS DELIBERATELY NOT IDENTIFIABLE IN IT.** *The bright went off the face by degrees* is a real beat and it would need his reference, his colouring and a second resolved face, and it would split a picture whose subject is one boy and one stone. It is available as its own plate and is not this one.

**THE WARM IS NAMED, because this is a cold picture** (§Name both temperatures, s41). `ch05/the-two-pails` went greyscale by having no warm to hold against the cold; here the skin, the pry-bar and the chock are the warm, and they are said so.

```
Image 1: portrait reference of the boy.

Setting: a walled stone training yard at a temple on a winter
morning, as loose wash suggestion — flat flagstones with frost on
them, a long wall behind with wooden weapon racks against it, the far
corners going into cool wash. Flat cold overcast daylight, no sun and
no shadows with edges.

Composition: the boy and the stone together in the middle of the
frame, cropped just below his knees, seen from the side and a little
below him.

SCALE, AND IT IS THE SUBJECT OF THE IMAGE: a drum of grey stone lying
on its side, longer than the boy is tall, its top edge level with his
shoulder. It is one solid piece, plain and unmarked, with no axle, no
frame, no handles and no rope on it.

The drum is tipped up a few degrees on the near end and is held there
by ONE IRON PRY-BAR, its tip under the stone's edge and a small block
of wood set under the bar as a fulcrum. WHAT IS IN EACH HAND: both
the boy's hands are on the free end of the bar, one above the other,
pushing it down and across. Nothing else is in either hand and there
is no rope, no lever and no second bar anywhere in the picture.

HE IS NOT STRAINING. Weight settled, shoulders down, arms not locked,
back straight, breathing easily — the ease of somebody doing a chore
he has done a hundred times. NOT heaving, NOT braced, NOT triumphant,
NOT proud, NOT grimacing, no teeth showing, no effort anywhere in the
face or the body. His attention is on the point where the bar meets
the stone.

Behind them, at a distance along the wall, a line of children of
about his own age, standing and watching, silent and completely
still. THREE OF THEM, NEARER, ARE BENT OVER WITH THEIR HANDS ON THEIR
KNEES, still getting their breath back. No face in the line or among
the three is drawn — they are dark summary shapes, loosely washed,
with soft edges and no features. There is no adult in the picture.

THE PICTURE IS COLD AND THE WARM IN IT IS NAMED: the boy's skin, the
pale wood of the fulcrum block and the worn wooden grip of the bar
are the only warm colour anywhere. The stone, the flagstones, the
frost, the wall and all the clothing are cool grey and grey-blue with
no brown in them. The frost on the flagstones is reserved as bare
unpainted paper.

His face and his two hands on the bar are the most finished things in
the picture and carry the sharpest step from light to dark. The drum
is a large quiet grey mass with no detail and no hard edge on it, and
the watching children are quieter still. The ink linework stays the
strongest element throughout and does not blur into the wash.

Eyes near-white neutral grey, cool and colourless — no more colour in
the iris than in the whites around it. Dark hair cut short and
uneven. Plain undyed working cloth, coarse and mended and a little
too large for him, the ink carrying the cloth at the seams and the
mended places. He wears no ornament and no metal.
```

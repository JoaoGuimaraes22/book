# Ch. 33 — The Breach · plate sheet

**Composed at s53 from the scene, under the register.** Kael is thirteen, Aeliana fourteen; Vask and Severin are unpinned. Anchors: `images/portraits/kael-14.png` + `images/portraits/vask.png` on the first, `images/portraits/aeliana-15.png` on the second.

**THE PAIR IS THE CHAPTER'S TWO ENDS OF ONE NIGHT: A CHILD ARRIVES AND A FATHER IS SPENT.** Both are composed on a pair of hands. The sword goes into a boy's hand with his father's fingers closing it; a lamp is held at the height of an old man's shoulder by a girl who has never carried anything. **Neither picture has a death in it and neither has a birth in it**, and the chapter is entirely in both.

**THE DEATH IS NOT DRAWN AND THIS IS NOT SQUEAMISHNESS.** *The plate follows the page* permits it and the folder's own record advises against it: at s51 the author dropped two built and competent grim plates (`the-good-part`, `not-kind-to-me`) and adopted `aftermath` and the two fans instead. **What the chapter is about is not the sword coming off a dead man's hand — it is the four seconds before, where a man hands his son a weapon he cannot lift and tells him he will grow into it in a year.** The rest is on `candidates.md` with its framing, unspent.

**THE WHITE IS OUT OF HIM IN THE FIRST PLATE, AND THE PAGE PUT IT OUT.** *Vask stood in his own doorway with the longsword hanging and the white going out of him in front of everybody.* It comes back later, outside, and that later is not this moment. **So the man in this picture has nothing on him at all**, which is worth more than any amount of lightning: he is a tired man in a burnt jacket giving his son a sword. `ch21/the-surge` and `ch30/the-white-in-all-of-him` already own the element.

**AND THE HAND ON THE HEAD IS DELIBERATELY NOT THE MOMENT.** *He put his hand flat on the top of his son's head, once, the way he had done perhaps eleven times in Kael's life* — which is `ch31/the-hand-flat` two chapters earlier with the sexes swapped, and the set should not spend the same gesture twice in three chapters. **The transfer is the beat with the object in it**, and the object is the one Book Two is carrying.

**THE SECOND PLATE SPENDS SOMETHING THE FOLDER HAS PROTECTED SINCE Ch. 22.** `../canon-brief.md`: *her hands do nothing: unoccupied, holding nothing, at rest*, and `ch26.md` passed over a candidate on exactly that ground — that a picture of the princess's hands doing something is *an argument worth having later*. **This is later, and the page is what buys it**: she carries a lamp round a fleece for the length of a birth, is sick against a wall, comes back and picks it up again. The fence is against a decorative princess, not against this.

**AND HER BEAUTY DOES NOT ARRIVE AS A VERDICT, BECAUSE THE PICTURE IS OF A JOB.** `README.md` makes a portrait *plate* of her illegal; this is a moment plate, and every clause in it is a task — the arm out, the height held, the eyes off the floor. **Nothing in the block admires her.**

**THE BIRTH IS OUT OF FRAME ENTIRELY AND SO IS THE MOTHER.** Neris is on the floor eighteen inches from the bottom edge and is not in the picture; nothing of her, nothing of the child, nothing of the work is drawn. This is the pregnancy ruling doing what it was written to do (author, s51), and it costs the plate nothing, because **what is worth drawing here is the girl holding the light.**

**THE RED EYES ARE NOT IN EITHER PICTURE.** Neris's and Wren's both belong to this chapter and both are the kind of fact `../canon-brief.md` protects — *two body-marks of magic exist* — and neither is drawn here, because the two moments that carry them are the two that cannot be composed without putting the mother's body or the newborn's face in a frame this sheet has just kept them out of. **Kael holding his brother** is on `candidates.md` for a chapter that can hold it.

**PASSED OVER, AND WHY:** **facing the wrong way** — the boy carried down the slot over an old man's shoulder, awake, arms swinging, the sword still in hands that will not open, watching his own roof take at one corner — is the strongest single image left in Book One and it is a third plate on a sheet that has two. **Walking out with nothing in either hand** and **the mountain going** are both on `candidates.md`. **Oss on the lip** is blocked for the same reason it has always been blocked: there is no `images/portraits/oss.png`, and `ch13.md` says the work that unblocks it is a portrait, not a plate.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-sword

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch33-the-sword.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53: *great*). Ch. 33 has its two.**

**BOTH EYE-LINES LANDED AS DESIGNED, AND THE DESIGN WAS AN INVERSION.** The man is looking at his son's face; the son is looking down at the thing arriving in his hand. **That is the whole editorial of the plate and it is carried by two clauses**, and it is the third time today a gaze has held when the block gave it a target the head could turn toward — the exceptions this session are both cases where the target sat an inch from the face.

**THE SIZE IS THE SECOND SUBJECT AND THE RATIO CARRIED IT.** *From pommel to point it is nearly as long as the boy is tall* — and in the picture it is absurd on him, which is what *it is not a knife; you will grow into it in a year* needs a reader to see. §Scale's rule about a ratio and never an adjective has now worked three times this session, on an age, on a hand and on a sword.

**AND THE TWO SMALL PAGE-FACTS BOTH ARRIVED**: the cloth-wrapped bundle with two covered hilts across the boy's back, and the man's sleeve rolled above the elbow over a plain coarse working jacket with nothing tailored on it. The lit doorway is behind them at one side and the burning pens are a low orange line far off at the other, exactly as sited.

**WHAT DRIFTED, two:** the man's left hand is not closed on the flat of the blade halfway down — his other arm is out of the frame, so the picture has three of the four hands the block wrote and the sword is supported at the grip alone · and the crop went to the hip where the block said the waist. **Neither costs the beat**, because the beat is the grip changing owner and the grip is where all three remaining hands are.

### PLATE-VERBATIM: the-lamp

**ADOPTED — `images/plates/ch33-the-lamp.png`, FIRST RUN (author, s53). THE BLOCK BELOW IS THE AUTHOR'S OPTIMISED VERSION OF THIS SHEET'S**, stored exactly as run.

**AND IT PUT BACK A CANON FENCE THIS SHEET HAD DROPPED.** My block said only *the back of an old man's grey head*. `../canon-brief.md` on Severin is explicit and the reason is structural: **hair fully grey, a true neutral grey, even everywhere — no seam, streak or patch, because that shape is the drain-mark.** The author's block states it — *his hair is evenly grey throughout — no streak, no seam, no patch, nothing lighter or colder in any part of it* — and it came back even. **In a book where a white streak is a body-mark of magic, any grey or white head in frame must be told which register it is not**, and this sheet is the reminder that the fence lives in `canon-brief.md` and not in my memory.

**AN UNSTEADY ARM WAS DRAWN AS A TILTED LAMP.** Mine said *the arm is not quite steady* — a state a still picture cannot assert, which is s51's own finding about faces (*a working part must be a state the picture can hold, not an action the face performs*). Theirs puts the instruction on the object: **the lamp tilted slightly off level and the flame leaning.** It is in the picture, and it is the only reason the arm reads as tired. **The s51 rule generalises off faces and onto anything the block asks for.**

**THE RIVAL IS DEMOTED ON BOTH CHANNELS.** The hearth fire is given edge and finish as well as size — *dull orange, soft-edged and low in value, and it does not light her* — where mine gave it only size and position. The lamp wins the picture completely.

**THE REST AS WRITTEN**: the lamp held out at arm's length at chest height, warm orange up the underside of her jaw and along the inside of her sleeve with the room black a few feet away; the silver-white hair half down and half pinned, colourless and with no yellow in it; the heavy coarse cloak over the fine gown with the stain down the near sleeve; the old man's shoulder and grey head cut by the lower left corner; and **nothing whatever of the mother, the child or the work in the frame** — the seal this block was built to keep.

**WHAT DRIFTED, three:**
1. **A second small flame stands on a shelf behind her at the right.** The block said the lamp is the only light *on her*, which is true in the picture — but it did not say it is the only flame in the room, and one arrived. Where a picture is lit by one held object, the next block should say so of the frame and not only of the face.
2. **Her eyes are not on her own flame.** They are level and off to the frame's right, past the lamp. The viewer-ban held; the target did not — **the same fault as `ch32/the-white-in-her-hair`, and the same cause: the target is inside the frame but too close to the face to point a head at.**
3. The crop went to the knee where the block said the waist.

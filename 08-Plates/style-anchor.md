# 08 — STYLE ANCHOR (the RULE side of plates)

**CONTRACT:** how every plate in the book is rendered. Invariant across chapters.
**ADMISSION TEST:** *does this govern how a plate looks, regardless of which chapter it illustrates?*
**EVICTION:** none — rules are amended in place. Per-plate state goes to the plate sheet; depicted-fact state goes to 07/03; unresolved world questions a plate forces open go to `02-World-Bible/open-questions.md`.

---

## THE HARD RULE: THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE

A plate may depict only what its own chapter has already put in front of the reader. This is `THE LEDGER FOLLOWS THE PAGE` pointed at pictures, and it is not a style preference — it is the seal on 00.

Consequence, stated plainly: **an image cannot decline to answer.** Prose can withhold an eye colour for twenty chapters; a face cannot. Where the page is deliberately silent, the plate answers with **framing**, never with invention — distance, angle, occlusion, the body turned, the detail out of frame. If a plate cannot be composed without spending something unspent, the moment is wrong and a different moment is chosen.

**Live spend-state is not duplicated here.** 03 owns which details are unspent (Kael's grey is the live one). Read the sheet, then frame.

---

## THE COLOUR LAW (derived — 03/appearance-ledger, 02/magic)

The world carries exactly two body-marks of magic: **RED EYES** (Kynemir) and **WHITE HAIR** (drain). The cast is kept deliberately plain by design — *the dark room the story's two loud marks need*.

A heightened palette destroys that architecture. If everything is vivid, the two marks are not loud, and the book's entire visual signature goes flat. Therefore:

- **The plates are near-monochrome by law**, not by taste. Desaturated, narrow value range, colour rationed.
- **RED is reserved.** Never at eye-scale on a face except the bloodline. Ambient red (fire, blood, cloth) stays low and desaturated so that bloodline red remains the loudest red in the book. Elarine's fire is orange-grey heat, not a red spectacle.
- **WHITE HAIR IS DATA, NEVER STYLING.** 02/magic: drain whitens permanently, *proportional to depth*; an old Resonant's hair is a readable ledger. Whitening a head in a plate makes a claim about that person's magical history. Never add white for drama, age, or contrast.
  - **Ruthen** carries one seam and the page fixes it: "one clean seam of it, like quartz through rock." The plate renders one seam through dark hair — not a streak, not distributed grey.
  - **Severin** is fully grey and the drain-reading is *never confirmed*. Render grey. Never seamed, never white. The ambiguity is load-bearing and a plate can destroy it in one stroke.

## COMPOSITION DOCTRINE (derived — 03/appearance-ledger rendering rule)

The book renders bodies through Kael's eye: *reach, weight, where a body carries, what the hands do*. The plates inherit it exactly.

- **Middle distance over close-up. Bodies over faces. Hands are load-bearing** — if a plate has nothing for the hands to be doing, it is the wrong moment.
- **Silhouette first.** Each main-cast member owns a distinct silhouette readable at distance; the plate should survive being reduced to a thumbnail.
- **No verdicts.** Conventional beauty or ugliness arrives only as *what other people in the frame are doing about it* — never as the image's own judgement.
  - Working example: the honest plate of **Aeliana** is not her face. Her sheet forbids a narrator verdict and gives two legal routes — THE RADIUS (what a room does in her presence) and KAEL'S INSTRUMENTS FAILING. The plate is eleven thousand people standing up.

## GENERATOR COUNTERMEASURES

This section is the anchor's working job. Both candidate tools default to the precise opposite of this book, and the defaults are strong enough to survive a polite prompt. State these as explicit negatives every time.

- no heightened saturation; no teal-and-orange grade; no bloom, glow, lens flare, god rays
- no rim-lighting or glamour key; light is flat, weathered, weather-plausible
- no symmetrical or attractive-by-default faces — plain bone structure, asymmetry, ordinary skin
- no epic-fantasy poster composition; no hero stance, no low heroic camera
- no ornamented fantasy costume — clothing is working cloth, period-plausible, undecorated
- no floating particles, embers, motes, or ambient sparkle
- no visible magic effects unless the chapter put them on the page, and then at the scale the page gave them (demonstrations stay candle-scale)
- children look like children — 13 is 13, not a small adult

---

## FORMAT (working default — author may overrule)

One plate per chapter, portrait, full-page proportion. Additional plates allowed where a chapter earns them; the sheet records why.

---

## THE ONE DECISION NOT DERIVED FROM CANON — **AUTHOR RATIFICATION OWED**

Everything above falls out of committed canon. The **medium** does not, and is proposed here rather than assumed:

> **Ink and wash — desaturated, near-monochrome, strong blacks, visible dry-brush texture, no line-art cleanliness.**

Reasoning: it serves *strong silhouettes readable at distance* and *no heightened palette* natively, and it makes the colour law structural rather than a rule to be remembered — in a near-monochrome book, the first red the reader ever sees is the bloodline, and it lands like a struck match.

Overrule freely. Everything downstream inherits this, so it is worth one deliberate decision now rather than discovering it by accident in plate 1.

# 08 — PLATES (self-contained)

Everything that governs image generation lives here; `CLAUDE.md` carries one line about it. Read this file first for plate work and ignore the folder otherwise. **A plate session does not open with a list of what is owed** — nothing here is outstanding unless the author says it is.

**CONTRACT:** the reader-facing illustrations, and the method that makes them.
**ADMISSION TEST:** *does this govern how an image is generated, or record what one has depicted?*
**EVICTION:** rules are amended in place; findings, run records and superseded rules go to `archive/` verbatim. **The `plates/` sheets were retired at s58 (author) and are in `archive/plate-sheets.md` whole** — their design arguments, adoption records and drift counts with them.

## Session

*Let's continue with image gen* is enough to open one; a named chapter or picture narrows it. No cold read of the writing files — this folder is self-contained.

1. Read, in order: this file · `canon-brief.md` · `prompt-system.md` · `scene-tests.md` · `prompts/plate-candidates/ch<NN>.md` for the target chapter, if it exists · the target chapter in full from `manuscript/<book>/` · the `03` sheet of each character who will be in frame, for spend-state.
2. The target is the first chapter without two plates, unless the author names one.
   - **THE REVISITING PROGRAMME (author, s61).** A second pass over chapters that already have their two, taking one extra plate each, ten chapters to a session. **Ch. 1–10 is done** (s61) and **Ch. 11–20 is done** (s63 — ten extras in one session, the pick handed back: author, *pick one for each, go*); **Ch. 22–30 is done** (s64 — nine extras in one session, the pick handed back again). **The author opened that pass at Ch. 22 and not at Ch. 21** (author, s64: *continuing the job of making extra one plates, for ch 22-30*), and **then asked for Ch. 21 as well** (author, s64: *do ch21*), so **Ch. 21–30 is complete**. **Ch. 31–40 is the next revisiting session** and is not to be started until the author opens one. A revisiting session reads the chapter and its candidate list the same way a first pass does, and its extras are recorded in the same `## CHOSEN` list.
3. Design from the scene: propose the moment and the crop for each plate in a few lines and wait for the author's pick. Then write **every** candidate found into `prompts/plate-candidates/ch<NN>.md` — `## CHOSEN` first, `## CANDIDATES` after — write each chosen prompt into `prompts/plates/ch<NN>-<slug>.md` from the template, and hand over that path and the references to attach.
4. After the run: read the picture against the prompt, and adopt only on the author's word — copy the image to `images/plates/`, mark the CHOSEN entry `adopted`, commit, push. **What landed and what drifted goes under that entry as sub-bullets** — never into the prompt file, which is pasted verbatim and carries no markdown (s45); it moves to `prompt-system.md` only under the s42 ruling below.

## The files

- `canon-brief.md` — **the paste**: the appearance facts per character, plain text, at the top of any prompting conversation — and in native image generation the conversation is the prompt, so every line in it is in every picture's context (author, s59). It carries only what is on the person in every picture — colouring, build, bone, bearing, permanent marks — and nothing addressed to the writer: no fence, no provenance, no file pointer, no spend-state (`03` owns that, and §Session reads it). Armament, garment, grime and damage, hair styling and any state a chapter has put on a body are authored per picture out of the chapter and `03` — `02/geography.md`: a character reads by where they are and what they are doing, not by their house. A summary: `03` wins where they disagree, and the disagreement is the brief's bug. The fences are below in §Standing rulings; the brief as it stood before the cut is `archive/canon-brief-s58.md`.
- `prompt-system.md` — **the rule, and the shared components only.** The blocks used many times over — STYLE, the AGE rungs, HAIR, the half-figure override, the SUBJECT template, BASE, the closing line, the scene style line, the plate template — plus the instruments and the tool's limits. **No character's own prompt is in it** (author, s58): each of those is a file under `prompts/`, and the doc says what each one is and points at it.
- `scene-tests.md` — the scene-test prompts as run, under `### SCENE-VERBATIM:` headings, **and the single home for them**: several carry a variant beside the adopted one, which is the whole value of the sheet, so they are not split into paste-ready files. The specimens the template is cut from.
- `prompts/plate-candidates/` — `ch<NN>.md`, one per chapter: **every** candidate that chapter's design found, `## CHOSEN` first — a short description carrying the chapter's own words, pointing at the prompt — then `## CANDIDATES`, the rest, with why each was passed over or blocked (a rejected candidate stays so it is not re-proposed). **The chapter quote in a CHOSEN entry is load-bearing:** `scripts/build-docx.py` finds it in the manuscript to decide where the picture sits on the page, so an entry without one leaves its plate unplaced unless the script's `MANUAL` table pins it.
- `prompts/` — **every file in it is hand-authored and is the source** (author, s58): `plates/ch<NN>-<slug>.md`, `portraits/<character>-<age>.md`, `full-figures/<character>-<age>-full.md`. Prompt text only: no heading, no fence, no emphasis (s45). **Nothing under `prompts/` is generated and there is no build script** — `prompt-system.md` holds the shared components a NEW prompt is assembled from by hand. `prompts/README.md` carries the provenance of each file.
- `images/` — `portraits/` (face references, `<character>-<age>.png`; bare name where `03` leaves the age unpinned; a deliberate second face takes a state suffix) · `full-figures/` (`<character>-<age>-full.png`; the suffix is kept so no two files share a name) · `plates/` (the adopted plates, `ch<NN>-<slug>.png` — **these are pages**) · `scene-tests/` (references, not pages) · `held/` (runs kept for a decision — a good picture that is not the plate its prompt was written for; not pages; named for the prompt that made them).
- `archive/findings.md` — the four governing docs as they stood before s47, verbatim. Sheets written before s47 cite `scene-prompt-system.md §…` and `portrait-prompt-system.md §…`; those sections are there under their original headings.

## Hard rules

**THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE.** A plate depicts only what its own chapter has already put in front of the reader — THE LEDGER FOLLOWS THE PAGE pointed at pictures, and the seal on `00`. An image cannot decline to answer, so where the page is silent the plate answers with framing — distance, angle, occlusion, the body turned, the detail out of frame — never with invention. If a moment cannot be composed without spending something unspent, the moment is wrong and a different one is chosen. `03` owns spend-state: read the sheet, then frame.

**REFERENCES SHOW THE PERSON. PLATES SHOW THE MOMENT.** A reference — portrait, full figure, scene test — shows what someone is, depicts no scene, and page spend-state does not bind it. A plate is a moment and the rule above binds it completely. Aeliana's hair is loose in her references and up off the neck in a Ch. 22 plate. **NO PICTURE IS FORBIDDEN HERE AND THE LEGAL/ILLEGAL VOCABULARY IS STRUCK** (author, s65: *these are just illustrations*). A close-up, a face filling the frame, a plate that is a portrait in everything but its occasion — none of it needs clearance, and a chapter is simply somewhere a good picture can be got. What this rule asks is the one thing above: the state on the face comes from the chapter. The one case that needs care is Aeliana, and it is a page fact and not a fence — her beauty reaches the page as what a room does in her presence and as Kael's instruments failing, and has never once arrived as a narrator verdict (`03`), so a picture built to be about how beautiful she is would be saying a thing the book has not said.

**A SUBJECT BLOCK IS NOT CANON.** `00`, `02` and `03` are. A block that disagrees with a sheet is a bug in the block, corrected here rather than argued with there.

**REFERENCES ARE RE-CUT, NEVER EDITED.** When the body changes on the page, cut a new reference and keep the old; a plate uses the reference valid at its chapter.

**SILHOUETTE SEPARATION IS A PRODUCTION RULE.** Two characters who threaten to converge are separated on provenance and bearing before the face is touched. Tested at thumbnail size on Kael and Valeria — both thirteen, dark-haired, carrying two blades — and since used on Valeria and Elarine (bound vs loose, scholar vs uniform) and on Kael and Aurelian (village vs house, hacked vs groomed):

| | Kael | Valeria |
|---|---|---|
| build | hides its training | reads as function even at rest |
| stillness | invisible — trained not to move | **it warns** |
| hair | short, ordinary | well past the shoulders |
| blades | bundled on the back | one in each hand |
| cloth | undyed, light | dark |
| provenance | out of a shed, unsold | eleven generations of service |

## Standing rulings

- **Every plate anchors on the fourteen-year-old references** — `kael-14`, `elarine-14`, `valeria-14`, `aurelian-14`, `aeliana-15` — for any chapter (s44). One consistent face across the book over age-accuracy in any single picture; age is written into the block or the picture will be fourteen. `kael-4` and `kael-9` stay in the library and stop being the default.
- **The default plate is a chest-up with a face doing something** (s42, s45). A hand-and-object crop is the exception and has to earn itself; a faceless candidate is priced, not disqualified (s43). A close-up plate is a portrait of a *moment* — the chapter's own light and expression, taken from wherever that chapter puts that face in that state.
- **The scene-test register is the plate's starting shape** (author, s47 — *my favourite compositions, colouring, beauty are the images in scene tests*): one implied wall, soft daylight from one side, faces near and engaged with each other, colour named on the objects that have it. A named light, a demotion, a reserve, an absence list are instruments, reached for when the picture needs one and never carried by habit. The template is on `prompt-system.md`.
- **The register starts at s47, and the folder before it is history rather than precedent** (author, s51). `ch16/the-ear-on-the-brick` run 2 is the first picture on it. Earlier blocks stay on their sheets and their pictures stand; what they are not is evidence for how the next block should be written. **An instrument is argued from a post-register run or from this picture — never from a pre-register failure.**
- **THE PREGNANCY IS FRAMED OUT, NEVER DRAWN** (author, s51: *probably should have avoided illustrations with Neris' body showing — it is very weird with the guidelines and pregnancy*). She is pregnant from Ch. 21 to the end of Book One, and a plate carries it in posture, cloth and crop and **never as a described body**: no clause naming the term, the carry or the shape, and nothing below her collar authored where the frame can hold it. The hard rule already says a plate answers with framing where it cannot show — this is that, applied standing. `ch21/the-nine-things` predates the ruling and stands.
- **THE AELIANA PICTURES ARE THE AUTHOR'S FAVOURITES IN THE SET** (author, s53: *aeliana pics are the best*), after `ch33/the-lamp`. **It is a taste note and not a programme**: it does not license drawing her more often, and the page fact behind it is unchanged — her beauty arrives as the radius and as Kael's instruments failing and never as a narrator verdict (`03`) — and the two plates that earned the remark (`ch33/the-lamp`, `ch34/the-elbow`) are both pictures of her doing a job with her hands.
- **A HARD MOMENT IS NOT PASSED OVER FOR FEAR OF A REFUSAL** (author, s60: *those are the best images, we can always do them, eventually*). `prompt-system.md` §Injury already holds that **harm itself is not the barrier** and that a refusal is usually a fault in the block; this is the design-time half of it — refusal risk is not a reason to leave a moment unbuilt, unproposed or filed as blocked. Write the block, run it, and if it refuses reword once per §Injury and take a second refusal as the answer **for that attempt**. `ch39/the-iron-did-nothing` was priced as *the likeliest refusal in the folder* and left unbuilt, and the author put it back. What does block a moment is unspent canon, per THE PLATE FOLLOWS THE PAGE, and a face with no reference.
- **THE SET IS SHORT OF ACTION AND OF CLOSE RANGE, AND THAT IS THE THING TO CORRECT** (author, s65: *i feel like we have little action shots and closeups … those are really cool*). Counted at s65: of 121 plate prompts, **one** names contact or mid-action, and the fight-titled plates are demonstrations and aftermaths — `ch21/the-surge`'s own block says *nothing is happening: nobody is fighting*. The cause is two rules working as written — the default plate is a chest-up with a face doing something, and the s47 register is faces near and engaged with each other — plus a §Limits paragraph pricing action as impossible, **which the author struck in the same breath** (`prompt-system.md` §Limits). The specimen is `scene-tests.md` §`fight-kael-valeria`, made early, never promoted to a page; its recipe is there and an action plate is budgeted at several runs, not one.
- **Colour is named positively on the objects that have it, never banned** (s42, s45).
- **THE REFERENCES ARE SET IN THE BOOK, AND THAT MAKES THEM PAGES** (author, s55: *sprinkle in the different portraits we have too*). `scripts/build-docx.py` places ten `images/portraits/` references in `build/Book-One.docx` at each character's entrance, at 2.9in against a plate's 4.6in. **This spends the split above** — §The files reserves *these are pages* to `images/plates/`, and a reference is exempt from page spend-state because it is not shown to a reader. What the build HAS: `kael-4`, `vask`, `neris` in Ch. 1 · `severin` in Ch. 4 · `kael-9` in Ch. 5 · `elarine-14`, `aurelian-14` in Ch. 13 · `valeria-14` in Ch. 14 · `kael-14` in Ch. 22 · `aeliana-15` in Ch. 24, **not at her Ch. 22 entrance** — the reference wears her hair loose, Ch. 22 wears it up off the neck, and Ch. 24 says *Her hair was down.* `kael-17`, `valeria-17` and `aeliana-18` are out, being Book Two+ states; the full figures and the scene tests are unused. The anchors and their reasons are the `PORTRAITS` table in the script.
- **Kael's grey is spent for plates** (s42): a plate may show his eyes at full strength. It stays unspent on the page, which is a prose rule.
- **Findings are not automatically rules** (s42: *no need to be a rule — case by case we do adjust*). Record what happened on the sheet; promote it to `prompt-system.md` only when it has failed the same way, with the same cause, more than once and the correction was wanted every time. The pictures are judged by looking at them.
- **The programme is chapter order, two plates per chapter** (s42). The target is the first chapter in sequence without two, and `images/plates/` answers which that is.
- **THE PROMPT IS THE SOURCE, EVERYWHERE** (author, s58), replacing s45's `plates/`–`prompts/` split. A chapter has one candidate list at `prompts/plate-candidates/ch<NN>.md` and one file per prompt at `prompts/plates/`; a character has one file per reference under `prompts/portraits/` and `prompts/full-figures/`. **Nothing is generated and `build-prompts.sh` is gone** — assembly had drifted six of fourteen reference prompts away from the pictures they cut (`prompts/README.md`), which is the cost it was deleted for. The old sheets are in `archive/plate-sheets.md`, and `prompt-system.md`'s worked specimens cite them there.
- **Pictures that stand as drawn:** `vask.png` carries no temple-grey and `neris.png` reads early thirties — both ruled correct (s43, s44). **AND `neris-marked.png` READS ABOUT A DECADE OLDER THAN `neris.png`, AND THAT IS WANTED** (author, s60: *i like it reading a bit older. she has taken a big toll*) — the toll is the thing the book will not say out loud, and a reference is exempt from page spend-state, so it may carry what a plate could not. **`ch24/turn-him-toward-the-lamp` draws the wrist-to-elbow burn two chapters before Ch. 26 gives it to him** — the fault was `canon-brief.md`'s, corrected against `03` at s51, and the picture stands (author, s51). **`ch26/aftermath` carries three departures** — a stone courtyard, a charcoal tunic and no burn — and stands (author, s51). None re-cut, `03` unchanged. Do not re-raise. **`ch30/the-white-in-all-of-him` STANDS AS DRAWN WITH THE LIGHTNING COMING OFF HIM** (author, s53: *its good*) — Ch. 30 forbids it in the character's own words (*not sparking, not spilling, not one thread of it going anywhere it had not been told to go*) and Ch. 33 describes it exactly (*it came off him in threads that went nowhere … a man with nothing left cannot hold it exact*), so the picture is true of the wrong minute. **Not re-cut, not re-run, and the page is unchanged. Do not re-raise.** **AND THE FOUR s53 PLATES STAND AS DRAWN** (author, s53: *its fine*), against a recorded drift list on each sheet: **the loose crop, now at ten instances and never once corrected** · **the age reading above thirteen where no age clause was written** — the adopted pictures stand as drawn and none is re-cut, **but the author settled the method at `ch35/the-spotter` by putting §AGE's adolescent rung into the block, and it came back fourteen**; a block still to be run may carry it · **a small mark on a face falling below the scale at which it renders** · and the boy's cloth coming back ragged where the block said mended. The drifts stay recorded as evidence and none of them is a correction owed.
- **Finish is not yet consistent across the set** and the author has not settled it. The controlled pair is `ch08-the-one-who-did-not-laugh` / `-cold`; new blocks reserve bare paper by name and protect the ink from a general softening, and leave hue per picture (s43).
- **Never design "the coat."** It is the book's metaphor for Kael's concealment, not a garment — and `coat` as a noun returns a frock coat (`prompt-system.md` §Nouns). Moved from `canon-brief.md` at s59.
- **Valeria is never a one-sword identity.** Twin blades, both hands full, is what she is. The fence is against one sword and not against a count in a given frame: where a hand is occupied with something else, a plate may leave the second blade out of frame or undrawn (author, s51). Moved from `canon-brief.md` at s59.
- **THE FRAME IS NAMED, AND CAMERA VOCABULARY IS NOT BANNED** (author, s59) — `prompt-system.md` §The frame and §Light. None of the ninety plate prompts had named the frame's shape, 64 of the 79 adopted plates came back tall, and the loose crop stands at twelve recorded instances with no correction ever tried against it; the named frame is the first, and the next run under it was its test. **AMENDED AT s60 (author): the frame is named as one of the tool's five ratios and not as a shape in prose** — `prompt-system.md` §The frame carries the five and why the shape clause was retired. The old rule that a camera is written only as the surfaces it exposes carried a kneeling subject and not a standing one in the same session.
